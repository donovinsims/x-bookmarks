# How to Write Educational Content (for growing pipeline)

## Post

I started posting on LinkedIn in January 2022, and I've made over 1,000 infographics.
A lot of B2B founders are still operating with the 2024 content playbook.
Let's rewind a bit:
Around 2023 (the golden age of educational content), you could attract clients simply by sharing your expertise in content.
In 2024 and 2025, it became more competitive, so you had to add creative formats AND share unique insights.
But in 2026, AI models became incredibly good at sourcing insights and formatting, which considerably increased the volume of educational content.
So obviously, the rules have changed a lot.
I spent 6 months iterating on one simple question: how do you create great educational content?
I think I've found how to create great content at scale and increase distribution, without spending 10 hours a day on content.
This article is part 1 of a long series on the new playbook of B2B content. Here's exactly what I will share:
The new content mix for 2026/27 (and where educational fits)
How to use a content brain to ship content faster
The right educational formats to avoid audience fatigue
The foundations to write and design great educational content
So if you want to nail your content strategy: read this one.
 
The new content mix for 2026/27
First of all, before explaining how to create great content, you need to understand that you can't publish only educational content anymore. It was possible before, but the rules have changed.
 
You now need to update your content mix to win:
Educational (40%): Stop posting only educational content (reduce it from 70% to 40%) and concentrate the value in dense content (articles repurposed into posts).
Thought leadership (15%): Dedicate new formats (news analysis, screenshots, POV, opinions) to sharing your point of view on the market.
Build in public (15%): Show the progression of your journey and be as transparent as possible.
Personal brand (15%): Put a face, values, and a personal journey behind the business growth.
Offer content (15%): Use this part of the mix to sell your offers.

Step 1 - Start from your content brain
If there is one thing you need to build right now, it's your content brain. It's the most valuable asset to build and the best use case for AI. But you need the right process to do it.
Your content brain will allow you to run 4 specific jobs:
Collect, store, organize and scan your entire knowledge (prompt it any time)
Ideate content by finding the right insights (daily alerts are also good)
Ship better content and much faster, as you save time on ideation and creation
Connect to your GTM campaigns to inform them with the right context
Here's what a content brain looks like for us and for our clients (knowledge is randomized in this screenshot for confidentiality reasons):
 
 
Your content brain should have 4 distinct layers
 
1. The knowledge layer. It's everything you, your team and your company know: your past content, SOPs, sales calls, interviews and podcasts. Most people build it once and forget it, but we plug agents and connect it to the internet, so it's always up to date.
 
2. The context layer. This is the mistake I see a lot: founders have the knowledge, but not the context, so their insights land without direction. It holds your positioning, ICP, strategy and objectives, so it knows when to publish, when not to, and when to trigger an email.
 
3. The GTM brain. It acts as a senior GTM engineer: it knows when to contact someone, what signals to use, what's going on in your CRM and what objections to handle. And it keeps evolving based on the feedback and signals from the market.
 
4. The design layer. With so much AI content, design is one of the few things that still makes you stand out. That's why our senior art director builds your art direction, because a great art director is still better than AI at this job.
 
 
Your content brain is connected to ideation, creation, distribution, and GTM
 
Your content brain helps you for these 4 critical jobs:
Ideation: it reads your knowledge, your market and your signals, so you never start from a blank page. It tells you what to talk about this week, and why.
Creation: it organizes your content creation and guides the copywriting and design. But it doesn't replace the actual creation from a human.
Distribution: it helps you distribute and analyze the results of LinkedIn posts, newsletters, YouTube videos and X posts. It learns the best format for each channel and when to publish.
GTM: it connects your content to pipeline. When someone engages, it spots the signal, checks the ICP fit and triggers the right play.
 
Step 2 - Choose the right educational formats
When I published my infographic about the new content mix, another reader asked a good question:
 
I'll share examples for each type of content in the upcoming weeks. For now, I'll start by showing the best formats and channels for educational content:
1. LinkedIn infographics. By far the most useful educational format: they allow you to package insights and distribute them with a visual people love reading.
 
2. X articles. They're really useful now on X because they get good distribution (shown to the right audience). You need to constantly push them with new X posts and threads.
 
3. Substack newsletters. Your newsletter is the most important long-form format to pause, reflect, and organize your insights. It can be repurposed into videos (used as scripts) or into social posts.
 
4. YouTube expert videos. It's by far the hardest format, but when you nail it, you get unlimited distribution, subs, email signups, and leads.
 
5. LinkedIn text + photo posts. You don't always need to create an infographic or a carousel. You can add to your point with a simple image.
 
6. Podcasts. Create your own, or build a strategy to get invited to podcasts as a guest. It's a strong way to reach new audiences (of the guest or of the host) and let them discover your own socials.
7. What about Substack notes? I could show you my most viral Substack note, but on average they make 500 to 1,000 impressions. You can schedule 3 Substack notes per day, but don't expect tremendous distribution.
 
 
Step 3 - Create good content
You can have the most organized content brain and the best formats in your content plan, yet you can still produce bad content.
There are 4 levels of content quality:
 
Level 4 is AI slop: generic AI posts that repeat what everyone already says. It has a negative impact on your brand and creates marketing debt.
Level 3 is templated content: clean and correct, but the same formats, hooks and visuals as everyone else.
Level 2 is expert content: real expertise, proof from your work and a clear point of view.
Level 1 is the 1% content: a fresh piece of content in a signature format and design that only your brand could publish.
Between levels 3 and 2, there is what I call "the quality chasm". Most brands that publish never cross this chasm and stay stuck in levels 3 and 4.
 
So how do you skip levels 4 and 3, to create top quality content?
1/ Intention, passion, and expertise
I see most brands forgetting the most foundational rules of content creation for the sake of "scaling content production". They're not creating content to help anymore. They just do it because they need to.
So I want to talk about a non-technical concept I don't share enough: intention, passion, and expertise.
The intention to really help people with your content.
The passion for what you are doing and writing.
The real expertise that allows you to create good content.
2/ Quality of insights
The biggest quality factor is your insight. It's much easier to create a strong educational piece when you have proprietary data no one else has. This puts you directly in the top 1% (except if you do a terrible job at writing, designing, and distributing).

3/ Levels of complexity
People usually open socials between meetings, or just before posting something. They do not log in to study or to consume heavy educational content. That intentional learning happens more on YouTube, where they type keywords for a clear reason. LinkedIn is less intentional.
Because of this, work across three levels of complexity:
Accessible: easy and fast to consume. It grabs attention and gets high reach (hundreds to thousands). You cannot post only this, or you stay too surface-level.
Intermediate: a step deeper than accessible.
Advanced: your deepest material, for example a segmented GTM plan for 2026.
