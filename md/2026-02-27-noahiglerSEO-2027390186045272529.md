# How to Do Local SEO the Right Way (So You Stop Going Back to Angi) 

## Post

If your main source of leads is Angi, Yelp, Thumbtack, or HomeAdvisor, you don't have a marketing strategy. You're renting someone else's.

Those platforms own the customer relationship, control the lead flow, set the price, and send that same lead to 3-4 other companies to make the most money possible. You're competing on price before the customer even calls you.

Here's how to build a lead generation system you actually own.

Your Google Business Profile is the foundation.

When someone searches "plumber near me" or "roofer in Dallas," Google shows three businesses in the Map Pack before anything else. Before the organic results and before Yelp or Angi.

Those three spots get the majority of clicks. And those leads are free. Not shared with competitors, not $50-80 per lead. Just people finding your business and calling you directly.

If you're not in the top 3, you're invisible to most people searching for your service. Getting there starts with your GBP.

Here's what a properly optimized GBP looks like:

Exact match business name with your primary service (use a DBA)

Correct primary category matching your main service

Every service listed with keyword-rich descriptions

Real photos from actual jobs uploaded at least twice a month

Service area set to a realistic radius

Linked to a city-specific landing page, not your homepage

Most contractors have a half-filled profile linked to a homepage that mentions 8 cities. That's not going to cut it.

Reviews are the biggest ranking factor you can control.

Google weighs your recent reviews heavier than your total count. A company getting 15 reviews per month will outrank a company with double the total reviews but only pulling 2-3 per month.

The system is simple. Ask every customer in person before you leave the job site and hand them a QR code linking directly to your Google review page. For anyone who doesn't leave one on the spot, set up an automated text follow-up at 12 hours and an email follow-up at 24 hours. Then respond to every review within 24 hours with the service and city mentioned naturally.

If you do high-ticket work like roofing, remodeling, or restoration, don't limit yourself to asking after completed jobs. Estimates and inspections are reviewable interactions too, and they can triple your review volume since you're running way more of those than actual jobs each month.

Your website needs to work harder than a digital brochure.

Most contractor websites are built like company presentations. Here's who we are, here's our story, here's everything we do on one page.

That's not what converts. Your website's job is to make someone call you within 10 seconds of landing on it.

Above the fold you need your service and city in the headline, a tap-to-call button, your star rating and review count, and 2-3 trust signals like licensed, insured, or same-day service. Nothing else needs to be there. Not your company history, not a stock photo, not a paragraph about your values.

Below that, include your process so they know what to expect, real photos from jobs, embedded Google reviews, your service areas, and a FAQ section answering common objections.

Build individual pages for each core service. AC repair gets its own page. Water heater installation gets its own page. Each one should go deep on that specific service with a process breakdown, pricing context, real photos, and FAQs specific to that service.

Then build location pages for every city you want to rank in. Each page needs unique content about that area, not a template with swapped city names. Google can tell the difference and so can homeowners.

Internal linking needs to tie all the pages together. Service pages link to location pages, location pages link back to service pages, and this web of connections is how Google understands what you do and where you do it. It also helps your pages stay indexed and rank.

Citations tell Google you're a legitimate business.

Your business name, address, and phone number need to match exactly across every directory on the internet. "ABC Plumbing" on Google and "ABC Plumbing LLC" on Yelp confuses Google, and that inconsistency costs you rankings.

Build listings in at least 75 directories starting with Yelp, BBB, Angi, Apple Maps, and Yellow Pages. Then add local directories like your Chamber of Commerce and regional contractor associations. Every citation with consistent NAP is a trust signal that helps you rank higher.

AI search should be optimized for (despite being slightly overhyped).

People are asking ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews for contractor recommendations right now. If your business doesn't show up, you're invisible to a growing segment of customers.

LLMs don't pull from Google Maps as much... they pull heavily from news articles, press releases, brand mentions, and public social content. So the playbook is different from traditional SEO.

Publish press releases monthly through a service like EIN Presswire, create "best of" listicle content on your blog like "Best plumbers in Phoenix: Top 5 for 2026," encourage happy customers to recommend you in local Facebook groups, and sponsor local teams or events for brand mentions on .org sites.

Most of your competitors have zero AI search presence right now. A few press releases and a couple listicle articles can put you ahead of businesses that have been around for decades.

Once ChatGPT or Gemini local search crosses 10%+ of total search volume, you'll be crushing it.

What to look for if you hire an agency.

Not all agencies are the same. Here's how to tell if someone actually knows what they're doing.

Yes, I run a local SEO agency. Take this with a grain of salt (given I can be biased). This is from my findings after speaking with over 300 home service businesses about their experiences working with agencies.

They should be able to show you exactly what they're doing each month with no black box and every deliverable documented. They should not REQUIRE a long-term contract because if they need 12 months locked in to prove their value, that tells you something. And you should own everything they build, meaning your website, your GBP access, your content, and your citations all stay with you if you leave.

They should also specialize. An agency that does SEO, PPC, social media, web design, branding, and email marketing for restaurants, dentists, lawyers, and contractors is not going to give you the depth you need in YOUR niche.

Tl;dr: if you want to rank #1 on Maps and LLMs, find a LOCAL-SPECIFIC SEO agency. Someone who understands the importance of ranking in the Map Pack, how to rank GBPs, how to help set up GBPs, etc.

Ask them what their plan is for your specific market. If they can't explain the competitive landscape in your city and what it's going to take to rank there, they're going to give you cookie-cutter execution and generic monthly reports.

Realistic expectations.

SEO is not instant. Anyone who promises page one rankings in 30 days is lying to you.

Here's what a realistic timeline looks like. Months 1-2 are foundation work where your GBP gets optimized, citations go live, website gets updated, and your review system starts running. Months 3-4 are when rankings start improving and you'll see movement in the data before you feel it in lead volume. Months 4-6 are when leads pick up and your phone starts ringing more consistently. And months 6-12 is where the compounding effect kicks in as rankings solidify, review count grows, and AI visibility builds.

The tradeoff compared to ads is time. Google Ads can generate leads within a week while SEO takes months. But ads stop working when you stop paying. SEO compounds over time, and the work you do today builds an asset that generates leads whether or not you're still paying an agency.

The bottom line.

Angi, Yelp, and Thumbtack want you dependent on their platform because that's their entire business model.

Your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews, and your AI search presence are assets you own. Nobody can turn them off, raise the price, or send the same lead to your competitor. Build the foundation, be patient, and trust the process. The businesses that invest in owning their lead generation always outperform the ones renting it.
