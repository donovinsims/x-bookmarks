Fundamentals

Before we get into the fun stuff, you need to understand a few core principles.  

Remember: good copywriting is getting the right person to take the right action.

That action might be reading, replying, sharing, following, or buying. But every post should be engineered to make someone do something.

Foundational principles you need to understand:

1.     Write for a specific audience

Picture your ideal reader before you post. Get as detailed as possible:

What are they (currently) struggling with

Where do they consume content? Is 𝕏 even the place your ideal reader is?

What value do they want?

What are they feeling right now?

And so on.

Depending on your post (top, middle, or bottom of the funnel), you'll want to get increasingly detailed.

2.     One idea per post

If you have three ideas, you have three posts. Multiple ideas generally confuse the reader and the algorithm. 

3.     Every post needs a takeaway

Ask yourself: "What does someone walk away with?"

Every post needs a concrete point and takeaway. 

4.     Clarity > clever

Good writing reduces friction for your reader.

Make it as easy as possible to understand your point.

My rule: If a 12 y/o can't read it, I'm not publishing. This doesn't mean your concepts intellectually need to be dumbed down to this level, but it does mean your vocabulary, formatting, and word choice do.

Nail these four things, and you're already ahead of most people who post on 𝕏.

Hooks 101

The most important part of any written media is the hook.

Think about it, if you made it this far, it's because my hook intrigued you.

The only job of your first line is to get someone to read your second line.

Your main goal is to create a curiosity gap for your reader. You want people to feel like you're speaking to them.

There are eight types of hooks worth mastering:

Bold claim: "Copywriting is the most underrated skill on 𝕏."

Callout: "If you're a founder with under 1K followers, read this."

Curiosity gap: "I just realized something 99% of founders miss."

Clear outcome: "How to write posts like the top 1%."

Contrarian: "F*ck posting volume. You NEED to post less."

Story: "It took me 8 years to realize this..."

Number / list: "7 copywriting rules I wish I knew 6 years ago."

Proof: "This post got 2M views. I analyzed why (takeaways)."

The best writing is usually a combination of multiple of these puzzle pieces.

Rules for writing better hooks:

Lead with the most interesting thing. Cut the warm-up.

Be specific: "I grew fast" is crap. "I hit 5.8M impressions in my first month" is better. Numbers, names, and timeframes always beat vague promises.

Make it about them. The reader is always asking, "What's in it for me?"

Keep it short

Create tension. A good hook opens a loop the reader needs closed. A question, a surprise, a contradiction, the need for value.

If your best line is at the bottom, move it to the top.

Every great hook taps into at least one of these:

Self-interest: "What's in it for me?" (outcomes, money, time saved)

Curiosity: an open loop the brain wants closed

Novelty: something they haven't heard before

Emotion: surprise, fear, FOMO, excitement

Identity: "This is about people like me"

Again, the strongest hooks stack two or three of these frameworks.

Example: "If you're a founder under 1K followers (identity), this one change doubled my reach (self-interest + curiosity)."

How I write hooks (my process)

Write the post first, hook last. Once you know what the post actually says, the hook is easier

Write 5-10 versions using different hook types

Cut the weakest options immediately

Refine each line several times until each hook line rolls smoothly into the next

Cut any unnecessary words (things that say the same thing twice or add no value/point)

Structure & Formats

In a way, formatting and structure are also part of the hook too. If a post looks too daunting, people will scroll away. Again, it's about reducing friction.

Every post needs a shape. Without one, even great ideas read like a ramble.

The 6 structures I use most:

1.     Problem → Insight → Takeaway (written post)

Name a problem, share what most people miss, give them something to do.

2.     The list

Hook + numbered points + closer. Easy to digest, save and share.

3.     The graphic

Packaging a point into an easy-to-digest visual

4.    Video

5.     Article

6.    Quote Tweet

Rhythm & Readability

Understand this: Most of the time, people don't read posts. They scan them.

Your job is to make your post feel effortless so readers can commit to spending time on it.

Use line breaks. Walls of text get skipped.

Vary your sentence length. Short sentences hit hard. Longer sentences carry detail and build momentum. Mix them.

Each line flows to the next.  Each line should carry one thought. If a sentence has two "and"s and a comma, it's probably two sentences.

Keep paragraphs to 1 to 3 lines. On mobile, a 4-line paragraph looks like an essay.

Make it scannable. Numbers, lists, bullet points, arrows

Use transitions that pull people down. These are short connector lines keep people reading. Examples:

"Let me explain..."                        "But here's what nobody tells you:"

Write for the phone screen. Most of your readers are on mobile. Before posting, preview it on your phone.

Word Choice

Every word needs to earn its place in your copy.

• Cut filler: really, very, just, actually, etc.

• Use simple words: utilise → use, facilitate → help, commence → start

• Use strong verbs: "grew really quickly" → "exploded"

• Write in active voice: "Mistakes were made" → "Most creators make this mistake"

• Use "you" more than "I": always make it about the reader.

• Avoid AI slop (em dashes, it's not [X], it's [Y], etc.)

• Delete. If deleting a word doesn't change the meaning, delete.

Voice

Anyone can copy your format and hooks, but copying your voice is much harder.

• Write like you talk

• Have opinions

• Be consistent with the tones and styles you pick.

• Show personality

• Share your experiences: "here's what happened..."

• Pick your perception: smart, funny, blunt? Your voice should match how you want to be perceived.

• Don't copy someone else's voice

• Keep your quirks

CTAs & Closers

The biggest takeaway here: you need to DIRECT people on what to do next.

Want a follow? Ask for it.

Want people on your website? Give them easy access to the link.

Want comments? Ask a question or spark conversation.

Tell people what to do.

Match the CTA to the funnel

Tips:

In the replies to your best post, comment your CTA

Sprinkle CTAs throughout your posts (at the beginning, when retention is highest)

Be specific and keep your CTAs low-effort to execute

Tease what's next

Ask the readers for something (advice, what they want to see next, etc.)
