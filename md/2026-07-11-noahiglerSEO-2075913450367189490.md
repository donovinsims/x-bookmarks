# Google wiped a batch of real customer reviews from a business we work with, and we got them reinstated.

## Post

Google wiped a batch of real customer reviews from a business we work with, and we got them reinstated.

This happens more than owners realize.

Google runs spam filter sweeps, and real reviews get caught as collateral damage.

Most owners notice the count dropped, assume those reviews are gone forever, and move on.

They're recoverable. This process works for us 8 or 9 times out of 10.

Step 1. Go to the Google Business Profile help center and file a case for missing reviews. You'll get an automated email back, and it almost always says some version of "we checked and found no policy violations." This is where everyone gives up.

Step 2. Reply directly to that automated email. Do not open a new case. State plainly that these are legitimate reviews from real customers with completed jobs, and request that the case be escalated to a specialist. The words legitimate and escalate matter, because that reply is what routes the thread to an actual human instead of the bot.

Step 3. Stay on the thread until a human responds. Keep every reply short and factual, and have the reviewer names and job dates ready if they ask.

One warning. This only works for real reviews. If they were bought or gamed, escalating digs the hole deeper.

Last thing: check your review count monthly. Losing 15 reviews in a sweep can quietly move you down the map pack, since velocity is the thing Google weighs most, and you can't fix a sweep you never noticed.
