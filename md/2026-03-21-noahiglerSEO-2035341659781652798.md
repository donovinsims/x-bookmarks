# In the past month I've had calls with four different companies who had 150+ location pages on their website.

## Post

In the past month I've had calls with four different companies who had 150+ location pages on their website.

None of them were ranking. Most weren't even indexed by Google.

Here's why:

Having hundreds of location pages used to work. You'd spin up a template, swap out the city name, maybe change a sentence or two, and publish pages for every zip code within 50 miles. Five years ago that could actually move the needle.

Google has gotten significantly better at detecting this. If your location pages are 150 to 200 words with the city name swapped out and nothing else that's unique, Google sees them as duplicate content.

It doesn't index the pages. Or worse, it indexes them and then devalues your entire site because it looks like you're trying to game the system with thin, repetitive pages.

Every single one of these four companies had the same problem. Their agency built them 150+ pages using a template. Different city name, same exact content. And they were confused why they weren't ranking in any of those cities.

After a few notable algorithm updates, Google has made it clear it wants a lean, efficient website.

Here's what actually works now:

You don't need 150 location pages. You need 10 to 20 really good ones targeting the cities and suburbs that actually matter for your business. The areas where your ideal customers are, where there's real search volume, and where you can realistically compete.

Each page needs to be genuinely unique. 500 words minimum. Mention specific neighborhoods within that city. Reference local landmarks or conditions that are relevant to your service. Include real photos from jobs you've done in that area. Answer questions specific to that location.

A location page for Cedar Park should read completely differently than one for Round Rock. If the only difference is the city name in the H1 tag and the first paragraph, Google treats them as the same page.

The companies I work with that rank across multiple cities all have one thing in common. Fewer location pages, but each one is built intentionally.

If you currently have 100+ location pages and your rankings are flat... check Google Search Console > go to Pages > look at how many of those location pages are actually indexed.
