# We've generated $20 million for home service shops in the last two years.

## Post

We've generated $20 million for home service shops in the last two years.

Location pages carried a big part of that.

Here's exactly how we build them.

(Bookmark this)

---

WHY THEY WORK

Location pages do two jobs at once.

Job one is ranking in the search results themselves. Your website can show up organically for every city your trucks drive to, but only if a page exists that's built for that specific city. Someone searching from a suburb 15 miles out finds the page written for their suburb.

Job two is the one almost nobody talks about. The right location pages make your Google Business Profile perform better. Google cross-references your website to understand where you actually work, so a strong page cluster lifts the profile's map pack rankings right along with the organic ones. The site and the GBP feed each other.

---

HOW MANY TO BUILD

> Pull every city and suburb you completed jobs in over the last 12 months. Get this from your CRM or invoices, not a keyword tool.

> Rank those cities by average ticket, not search volume. A suburb with 30 searches a month and $20K jobs is worth more than a city with 500 searches and $300 service calls.

> Build 3 to 5 per month. Quality holds at that pace and collapses at 50.

> Multi-location operators run the same play, each office just gets its own cluster of suburb pages around it.

I've audited sites with 800 auto-generated location pages that rank for nothing. Google figured that trick out years ago. Fewer pages with real substance win.

---

WHAT GOES ON EACH PAGE

Swapping the city name into a template is not a location page. Every page we build has:

> An intro written about that specific area. Housing age, common problems there, how long you've served it. A plumber writing a Meyerland page should be talking about slab leaks and 1960s cast iron drains, not "we proudly serve the area."

> The service broken into sections, the same way your main money page does it.

> Proof from that city. Jobs completed there, reviews that mention the area, photos your techs took in those zip codes.

> An embedded map with directions from that city to your office.

> 4 to 6 FAQs people in that area actually ask, with the city named inside the answers.

> Service schema and FAQ schema so Google can read all of it.

That local research takes 30 to 45 minutes per page. It's the entire difference between ranking and getting filtered out as doorway pages.

---

URLS AND LINKING

> Keep URLs short. https://t.co/8kdyzcaupA, not https://t.co/POJnTqdPAw.

> Link every location page from a service areas hub and your footer.

> Every blog you publish should link to 2 or 3 location pages. Blogs are internal linking infrastructure for a local business, not lead gen.

---

HOW TO JUDGE THEM

Watch Search Console. Pages doing their job pull impressions for service plus city searches within the first month. Give each page 90 days before you call it.

Pages you build this summer will still be booking jobs in 2029.
