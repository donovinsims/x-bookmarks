# We helped a plumbing company go from almost no online presence to 40+ leads per month from SEO alone.

## Post

We helped a plumbing company go from almost no online presence to 40+ leads per month from SEO alone.

(Bookmark this if you're considering starting SEO)

Here's the local SEO process we used and why it worked:

1. Where they started.

They had a website that was basically a digital business card. Homepage, about page, contact page. No service pages. No location pages. Loading slow on mobile. Their Google Business Profile had maybe 30 reviews, a generic description, and their primary category wasn't even the best match for what people were searching.

They were getting almost all of their work from word of mouth and a little bit from Yelp. Barely any calls from Google. They knew they needed to show up on search results but didn't know how to start.

Here's what we did month by month:

2. Month 1 was all foundation work.

We fixed the GBP first because that's the fastest lever for local rankings.
> Changed their primary category to match their highest-volume search terms
> Added every relevant secondary category
> Wrote a real business description that mentioned their services and service areas naturally
> Filled out every single service with descriptions
> Uploaded real job photos (not stock images)
> Set up Google posts on a weekly schedule.

On the website side, we rebuilt their site structure.
> Created dedicated service pages for every major service they offer... drain cleaning, water heater installation, sewer line repair, emergency plumbing, etc.
> Each page targeted a specific keyword and had real content about that service. Not 200 words of fluff. Actual information a homeowner would find useful.

We also started building their citation profile. Got them listed on 40+ directories with consistent name, address, and phone number across every single one. This stuff isn't exciting but it's the foundation that everything else builds on.

3. Month 2 we focused on reviews and content.

Their review velocity was basically zero.

Maybe 1-2 reviews a month if they were lucky. We helped them set up a simple system: text every customer a direct Google review link within 2 hours of completing the job. One follow-up reminder 3 days later. Their techs started mentioning it before the text went out so customers were expecting it.

They went from 1-2 reviews a month to 8-10 within the first few weeks of running the system. By month 3 they were consistently hitting 12-15 per month.

We also started building location pages for every suburb and city in their service area.

Each page had unique content about that specific market... not copy and paste with the city name swapped.

We wrote about the types of plumbing issues common in each area, local permitting considerations, and pricing context. These pages started picking up organic traffic within a few months.

4. Month 3 is when backlinks started.

We focused on local relevance over domain authority. Got them listed in industry-specific directories. Set up partnerships with a few contractors in adjacent trades (HVAC company, a general contractor) who linked to them from their websites.

Published a few "best plumbers in [city]" listicle articles through press release distribution to get backlinks from news sites. Each listicle placement = a backlink + a citation + AI search visibility.

We also started optimizing for AI search around this time.

Made sure their FAQ content on service pages directly answered the questions people were asking ChatGPT and Google AI Mode.

Things like "how much does a water heater replacement cost in [city]" and "how long does it take to fix a sewer line." If the AI models have a source to pull from, they'll cite you.

5. By month 4-5 they started showing up consistently in the Map Pack for their main services.

Not for everything yet, but for the keywords that mattered most. Calls from Google started picking up noticeably. Their GBP insights were showing real growth in searches and direction requests.

6. Month 6-8 is when it really compounded.

The review velocity, the content, the backlinks, the citations... all of it was stacking on top of each other. They were pulling 30-40+ leads per month directly from SEO. Their Map Pack visibility expanded into the surrounding suburbs because of the location pages. They started showing up on ChatGPT and Perplexity for some of their service + city combinations.

7. Now here's the honest part.

Even though the leads were there, their close rate was pretty brutal. A lot of leads were coming in but not enough were converting into booked jobs. That's not an SEO problem... that's a sales and operations problem. We can drive the phone to ring but we can't control what happens when they pick up.

We ended up parting ways a few months back. The SEO was working, but if the leads aren't closing on the back end, the client doesn't see the ROI regardless of what the numbers say. That's a lesson we've taken seriously since then... lead volume means nothing if the business can't convert it.

The takeaway for anyone reading this: SEO works.

For local home service businesses, it's one of the best long-term investments you can make.

But it only works if the rest of your operation is set up to handle the leads. You need someone answering the phone fast, following up with missed calls, and actually closing the jobs.

The marketing is one piece.

The business has to be ready for it...
