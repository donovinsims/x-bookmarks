# 22 writing prompts for people with multiple interests

## Post

Last week I published my career roadmap for independent writers & creators. A predictable 7-step path from “barely publishing” all the way to “earning a living from creative projects only you could create.”
I’ll link it at the bottom. But today I want to focus exclusively on Step 1: Habitual Publishing. Because one reader asked a question that’s worth exploring together:
 
For many, that’s the hardest part: Writing Consistently. Publishing on a repeatable schedule, before you’re an expert, before you have a “niche”, before you feel ready. Hell, I’ve worked with writers who’ve built and sold companies for millions, spent 20 years in their field, and still struggle with this. It’s normal.
And most of them stay stuck, because they try to solve the consistency problem by:
Buying a writing course
Spending 2 more weeks on their bio
Consuming more books & videos about writing
...the last one is my kryptonite. When I go through periods of struggling to write consistently, it becomes my personal form of productive procrastination.
My way out of this is black hole is to make writing fun again: Write whatever the hell you want and publish. No overthinking it. But if you’re anything like me, that advice can lead to more overthinking than less...
 
I have 100 interests, which should I write about? What topics fit my brand? What even is my brand? What am I? How does anything exist?!?!?!!!
Motivation existentially cooked.
4 hours later, I end with a messy, half finished draft and more excuses to put off writing.
So when I read Spike’s comment, this is what I wanted to say:
The skill will come later. The brand will come later. Your best ideas will come later. Right now, the only thing that matters is writing consistently. Here’s some writing prompts to make it easier.
Below are those 22 writing prompts curated specifically for writers whose interests don’t fit into one lane. Don’t overthink them. Don’t worry about turning them into 2,000-word articles. And please, use them without AI (they’re meant to pull out your unique perspective, not a computer’s).
Pick one, write what comes up, hit publish, repeeeeeaaaaaattttt.

22 Writing Prompts for People With Multiple Interests
 
Share the experience only you can speak from:
Explain a skill you learned in a completely unrelated job that you still use every day.
Write the honest before-and-after of something you dreaded doing. Address it to the person who is still stuck on the “before” side.
Write about something you quit and the opportunities that only showed up after.
Describe a conventional milestone you skipped (a degree, a certification, a “proper” job) and the unexpected thing that happened instead.
Write about a lesson you had to live through before it made sense.
Express the opinions you’ve earned:
Write about something you believed for years that fell apart the moment you tested it.
Defend something in your life that others thing is a “waste of time. Make the case without apologizing for it.
Describe a decision you agonized over for weeks that turned out to be simple. Write about the gap between how hard your brain made it and how little the outcome required.
Write about something you changed your mind about this year.
Point at something people in your world are optimizing that barely matters, then point at the thing they’re ignoring that does the real work.
Say what insiders won’t:
Answer a question someone once asked you that you couldn’t answer cleanly at the time.
Break down something people assume is natural talent in your world but you know is learnable. Show how it’s actually built.
Admit something everyone in your world pretends to understand but doesn’t. Then explain it for real.
Share the connections only you make:
Write out the advice people in your personal life keep asking you for, even though it has nothing to do with your main work.
Write about a small moment from years ago that you still think about, and figure out on the page why it stuck.
Reframe a question people keep answering wrong because they’re asking it wrong in the first place.
Describe a connection you once made in conversation that nobody else in the room made.
Explore where your interests collide:
Describe the moment you realized two of your interests were teaching you the same skill.
Explain something from your main interest using a concept from the interest you’d never lead with.
Take a problem everyone in one of your interests complains about. Show how another of your interests solved it years ago.
Write about a rule that’s sacred in one of your interests but routinely broken in another. Then pick which world has it right.
Write about one belief that two of your most different interests secretly share. Pick something that people inside either world wouldn’t notice.
 
These prompts aren’t just a workaround for writer’s block. They’re how you build a body of work that sounds like you and includes your various interests.
Every time you write across your interests, you give people more ways to connect with you. I’ve had complete strangers become subscribers (and even clients) not because of my best writing frameworks... but because they found out I drank Ayahuasca. Or we’re both fans of Nassim Taleb’s Incerto series.
Stop trying to cut off the weird, quirky parts of yourself. They’re what make you worth reading.
