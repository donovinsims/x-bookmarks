# Your agent needs a marketing harness. Here's a full guide explaining how to make one.

## Post

A practical guide to building a marketing harness in Claude Code.

TL;DR — the one-minute version

Create a marketing brain for your agent with all the context + assets it needs. Then use that as the foundation for a harness that executes the marketing workflow you want to automate.

To set this up, the workspace needs:

brain: products, customers, positioning, brand style, and campaign history.

creative kit: searchable photos, videos, references, and templates.

workflows: how to research, choose a direction, plan, produce, and review.

research tools: so the agent can research the channels where you're marketing to decide what to create,

feedback: your corrections and actual campaign results, saved separately.

Now the agent has the context to recommend what to make and why, then carry the chosen direction through production.

The loop is: goal → research → campaign ideas → approval → production → review → results → next plan.

Gooseworks gives you a full brand brain + creative agent out of the box, but you can also set this up yourself with a simple folder in Claude Code.

Start here: give this to your agent

This asks the agent to build your workspace. It still needs connected tools and access to your sources. The sections below show what that workspace should contain.

Why a marketing brain changes the work

An agent can write copy or generate an image from a prompt. Running a campaign also requires judgment:

Which audience or problem deserves attention?

What angle fits the product and the evidence?

Should this become an ad, a video, a creator brief, or an email?

Which assets and formats express the idea?

What should the next campaign reuse or change?

Those decisions depend on context. If the context stays scattered across chats, the operator keeps supplying it by hand.

A marketing brain makes it retrievable. A harness gives the agent a process for using it.

The brain holds what the agent knows. The harness connects that knowledge to tools, workflows, and review.

The outcome is a workspace you can delegate marketing work into: research, proposed strategy, campaign plans, creative, and a record of what happened. Your role is to set direction, resolve missing context, and review the important decisions.

1. Build the workspace

Here's an example file tree worth saving:

Three parts matter:

brain/ is durable context shared across campaigns.

skills and routing explain how to do the work and which tools to use.

campaigns/ keeps each run's evidence, decisions, outputs, and results together.

These are suggested names, not a required framework. Have the agent create the actual documents and skills, then inspect them.

Keep the always-needed instructions short; point to the other files and tell the agent when to read them.

The file tree becomes useful when retrieval works. Ask the agent a product or audience question and check which source it uses.

2. Give the brain business context

Have the agent draft these .md files from your website, product pages, customer research, brand guide, existing content, and campaign records:

brand.md: what the company does, voice, constraints, and things it should never say.

products/<item>.md: offers, variants, approved claims, restrictions, source URLs, and last-check dates. Use one document per product or connect your catalog.

audience.md: buyers, situations, pain points, objections, and evidence.

positioning.md: why people choose you, angles to explore, prior concepts, and winners supported by your results.

design.md: fonts, colors, aesthetic, and reference ads or organic posts.

history.md: earlier campaigns, review decisions, and measured outcomes.

Keep confirmed facts separate from assumptions. An inferred audience pain point needs research; a repeated color in your posts may need confirmation against your brand guide.

Use Markdown or readable HTML. Keep index.md short: which file answers which question, and where the original sources live.

3. Make the creative kit searchable

An agent needs to find the right input for an idea. A folder of mystery filenames makes that difficult.

Index your remote asset library in assets.md. For each useful item, record:

A preview or durable link.

What it shows, the product or variant, and useful visual details.

Format, version, source, and usage restrictions.

In templates.md, record reusable recipes: a product close-up, scene-led ad, comparison, testimonial layout, or conversation video. Include the inputs each recipe needs and the ideas it suits.

Make selection reasoning visible:

Idea: show the bottle in a commuter's day.

Input: bottle beside a work bag.

Format: scene-led ad with a short headline.

Reason: the setting explains the use situation.

This is a fictional example. The useful part is the connection between the audience problem, concept, asset, and format. You can correct any link before production.

4. Connect tools, then define the routing

The harness needs tools for the work you want to delegate:

Research: customer sources, competitor content, public ad/social references, and trend signals.

Creation: image and video generation, editing, and reusable layouts.

Review: an HTML board, Notion artifacts, or your Gooseworks asset library.

Results: your own ad, social, or email data where access is connected.

Research tools to evaluate include:

ScrapeCreators for public social references

the watch skill for video analysis

Influencers.club for creator discovery

Orthogonal/Fiber/Apollo for lead research

In workflows/tools.md, document what's actually connected and what each tool can do. In router.md, map tasks to skills, required inputs, and output locations.

For example:

Research request: load audience and positioning; collect sources; save research.md.

Campaign request: read research and relevant lessons; save the brief and concepts.

Production request: read the approved plan and selections; save outputs and previews.

Results request: retrieve the campaign's measured data; save observations and proposed lessons.

Reuse shared actions across skills: find an asset, fetch a source, analyze a video, generate an image, or save an artifact. A workflow coordinates those actions.

5. Teach it to decide what to make

Give the agent a business goal. Let it investigate the opportunities before prescribing the creative.

The agent should:

Read the relevant brain files and lessons.

Interview you for missing goals, constraints, or business context.

Collect evidence from connected sources.

Propose distinct campaign directions and explain their fit.

Turn the selected direction into concepts and an output plan.

Ask for the reasoning behind the recommendation. “Make three videos” is less useful than an explanation of the audience problem, angle, format, and evidence supporting it.

Public competitor ads show what another brand is running. They don't establish profitability. Use those references to develop hypotheses; use your own account results to evaluate performance.

For each concept, save the hook, message, selected assets/templates, formats, and rationale in concepts.md and selections.md.

6. Run the campaign through production and review

The campaign workflow should carry the approved direction through the rest of the work:

Plan: define deliverables, claims, channels, formats, timing, and costs.

Approve: record the chosen concepts and production scope.

Produce: generate the ads, posts, videos, or emails using the selected inputs.

Review: show previews, accept corrections, and preserve versions.

Distribute: stage approved work in connected channels; publish or activate only under the permissions you've agreed on.

Measure: bring actual results back into the campaign record.

That last stretch requires publishing and analytics integrations. A generation tool alone doesn't provide it.

Review product accuracy, whether the creative communicates the concept, readability on a phone, and rendering quality.

Leave feedback against the exact version. “Use the close-up and shorten the headline” gives the agent a concrete revision to make.

The workflow should also save its current state and next action. A later session can read the campaign folder and resume from the last completed step.

7. Give it two learning loops

Save your review comments and campaign performance separately.

Review feedback tells the agent what you prefer: warmer backgrounds, a clearer product crop, language that fits your voice.

Performance tells it what happened in a test: creative, audience, offer, channel, dates, exposure, and reported results.

Keep the raw observations. Then propose dated lessons with:

The evidence and source.

Whether it's a preference or a performance hypothesis.

The audience, channel, product, or offer it applies to.

Limits and reasons to revisit it.

Before the next plan, the agent should read relevant lessons and show which ones influenced its recommendations. After a campaign, it should propose updates.

Stale learning still needs judgment. A recent result can be weak evidence; an old lesson can remain useful. Review conflicting or outdated lessons rather than treating everything saved as a permanent rule.

This improves the context and workflows the agent uses. It isn't model retraining or proof that every campaign will improve.

8. Give your team access

Push your marketing workspace to a private version control repository, such as GitHub, and invite your team.

That gives everyone access to the same brand brain and workflows. Teammates can pull the latest context, contribute updates, and review changes. Version history lets you see what changed and why.

Keep large photos and videos in your asset library; share their indexes and links in the workspace.

If you're using Gooseworks, you can just invite team members in Settings.

What you can delegate into this harness

The same foundation can support:

Ads: research angles, plan concepts, generate creative, and review results.

Social and video: research topics, plan formats, and produce content.

Competitor and trend research: collect observations and propose opportunities.

Creator discovery: build shortlists with reasons for fit.

Email and SEO: add lifecycle or search context and dedicated workflows.

Claude Code is the walkthrough here. The knowledge-and-workflow pattern can travel to Grok bot, Muse, Dots, or another persistent agent; check each one's file access, tools, and skill support.
