# How to Rank #1 on Google Maps and ChatGPT in 2026

## Post

People are searching for local services in two places now.

Google Maps still drives most of the leads.

But ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews are real channels now. Thumbtack just plugged into Claude. Angi and Yelp are integrated with ChatGPT.

Homeowners are asking AI "who's the best HVAC company near me" and the answer comes from a totally different place than Google's Map Pack.

The businesses that figure out both will dominate their markets for the next decade.

The ones who only optimize for Google will wonder why their competitors keep showing up everywhere.

I've personally ranked over 100 home service businesses #1 on search results. Here's the boring playbook that actually works to rank on Maps and AI search.

Google Maps Comes First

Most of your calls still come from the Map Pack. Get this right before anything else.

Build the GBP correctly.

Primary category set to the service that makes you the most money. Every secondary slot filled. A plumber should have plumber, drainage service, water testing service, etc. Most home service operators have 1 or 2 categories selected. The rest are sitting empty as free ranking signals.

Service area set to a 1.5 hour drive radius from the shop. Push wider than that and Google decides you don't really cover those cities and ranks you nowhere.

Physical address used wherever possible. Service-area-only profiles get crushed by physical address profiles in local search. If you're working out of the house, get a real shop or warehouse address.

50+ geo-tagged photos uploaded, refreshed monthly. Real photos of your trucks at job sites, your team doing the work, before-and-afters, finished installs. Stock photos hurt more than they help.

Services section fully completed with all your services and keyword rich descriptions.

Products section filled out completely. Most operators have never opened this section. It's a full ranking signal sitting empty with photos, descriptions, and starting prices that both Google and LLMs read.

Exact match business name (DBA).

Your business name is one of the strongest ranking signals on the GBP. Most operators don't realize they can change it legally.

File a DBA at the county level. Costs $25-100, takes a couple weeks. Your DBA can include the services you offer.

"Smith Plumbing LLC" becomes "Smith Plumbing, Drains & Sewer Repair." That's now your legal name. Update the GBP to match. Update signage, invoices, website footer, and citations.

Google requires the GBP name to match real-world signage and legal documents. The DBA satisfies that requirement.

I've had clients move from position 7 to position 2 inside 60 days off this single change.

Reviews are 40% of your ranking. Velocity matters more than total count.

Google weights the last 90 days of reviews heavier than your lifetime total. A competitor sitting on 600 reviews who stopped generating new ones 8 months ago is beatable by a profile with 150 reviews and 25 in the last 30 days.

The system that works:

QR codes on every truck and invoice. Techs trained to ask in person right after the job: "If everything looks good, would you mind scanning this and leaving a quick Google review? Takes 30 seconds." $25 paid out per review brought in per tech. Automated SMS follow-up at 2 hours. Owner personally calls 5 past five-star customers every Friday for unwritten ones. 24-hour response on every review, signed by name.

An HVAC client of mine ran this for 90 days. Went from 11 reviews a month to 38. Map Pack ranking moved from position 8 to position 2. Zero changes to the website, citations, or backlinks during that window.

NAP consistency across 80+ citation directories.

Pick one format for your business name, address, and phone number. Use it everywhere. "ABC Plumbing" on Yelp and "ABC Plumbing LLC" on BBB confuses Google. "(512) 555-1234" on one site and "512-555-1234" on another can cause problems.

Fix every existing listing. Then build out 80+ directories total. Yelp, BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Apple Maps, Yellow Pages, Foursquare, the chamber, industry-specific directories, supplier dealer locator pages.

AI Search Is the New Frontier

Now the part most local businesses have zero presence on.

When someone asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity "who's the best electrician in Denver," the AI doesn't search Google Maps. It pulls from training data and real-time retrieval sources.

Those sources include news articles, press releases, brand mentions on authoritative sites, public social content, and "best of" listicles.

If your business name doesn't appear next to your service and your city in any of those places, you don't get recommended.

The Thumbtack/Claude integration changed this.

Claude now has a Thumbtack connector with 300,000+ contractors plugged in. Angi and Yelp are integrated with ChatGPT. TaskRabbit just plugged into Claude. The marketplaces are becoming the default supply layer for AI recommendations.

If you're not on Thumbtack, Angi, Yelp, HomeAdvisor, and BBB, you're invisible to AI search before the conversation even starts.

Press releases are the cheat code.

Publish one a month. Distribution costs $50-400 depending on the service. Topics that get pickup:

> Years in business milestones

> New service area expansion

> Community involvement and charity work

> Industry certifications

> Heat wave or cold snap response coverage

> New financing options

EIN Presswire and PRWeb are cheap options (unaffiliated). They get picked up by news aggregators and indexed across dozens of sites. Each placement becomes a data point AI models pull from.

I've seen clients start showing up in ChatGPT answers for "best [service] in [city]" within 75 days of running a consistent press release schedule.

Listicle content.

LLMs pull heavily from "best of" articles when answering local recommendation questions.

Create your own. Title format: "Best [Service] in [City]: Top [Number] Companies for 2026." Publish on your blog, on Medium, or distribute through a press release. Include yourself first or second. Include real competitors with genuine pros and cons so the article reads as actually helpful and not just self-promotion.

Two or three of these targeting different services in your primary city is enough to start feeding the AI.

Local Facebook groups and Reddit.

LLMs train heavily on public social content. Every time someone posts "We used ABC Plumbing last month, highly recommend" in a neighborhood Facebook group or local subreddit, that becomes training data.

After every job, ask happy customers if they're in any local community groups. "If anyone ever asks for a [service] recommendation, we'd appreciate you mentioning us." Most are happy to help if the work was good.

One organic recommendation in a local Facebook group is worth more for AI visibility than 10 of your own social posts.

Local sponsorships.

A $300-500 sponsorship of a Little League team, local nonprofit, or community event gets you a backlink from a .org domain AND a brand mention that feeds AI training data. Two birds, one cheap stone.

Conversion Catches What Rankings Bring In

You can rank #1 on Google Maps and show up in every AI answer in your city. None of it matters if the website doesn't convert visitors into calls.

Most home service sites convert under 1%. Well-built ones convert 3-5%. Same traffic, three to five times the leads.

The fixes that move the number:

Phone number top right, large and clickable, wired straight to the dialer on mobile. No modal popups, no extra confirmation step. One client lost 30% of his calls from a popup modal between the button and the dialer.

Above-the-fold needs to deliver in 3 seconds: service and city in the headline, sticky call button, review count and rating, 3-4 trust reassurances (licensed, insured, same day service, financing available), and a real photo of the team.

Form fields cut to the minimum: name, phone, service needed. Every extra field costs about 10% of conversions.

Mobile PageSpeed score above 70. Compress images, remove unused plugins, lazy load below the fold. A site loading in 2 seconds dramatically out-converts the same site loading in 6.

Real photos throughout. Stock photos of people in hard hats destroy trust instantly. Phone photos from real jobs build it.

Financing visibility on every service page for any ticket above $5K. Roughly 60% of replacement customers want financing. If your competitor displays Synchrony or GreenSky options and you don't, they're closing deals you never even quoted.

How It All Fits Together

The boring fundamentals are what win. Almost nothing here is exotic.

For Google Maps you need a complete GBP, consistent reviews, a city-matched landing page, and clean citations. The DBA is the highest leverage tactical move most operators skip.

For AI search you layer in press releases, listicle content, local social mentions, marketplace listings (Thumbtack, Angi, Yelp), and community sponsorships.

For conversions you fix the website so the traffic that arrives actually picks up the phone.

Each layer compounds. Every review builds on the last one. Every press release feeds another data point. Every citation reinforces legitimacy. After 12 months of running this consistently, you have an asset that generates leads whether you're actively working on SEO or not.

The businesses winning local search figured this out. They're not smarter and they're not running fancy tactics. They committed to the boring stuff and stuck with it for 12 months while their competitors chased the next shortcut.

That's the whole playbook.
