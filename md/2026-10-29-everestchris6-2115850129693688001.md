# how to automate marketing with grok bot (FULL GUIDE)

## Post

we're at the point where marketing and customer acquisition can be fully automated with the right setup. here's how to set grok bot up to build your customer profile, find what's working, make the content, find leads and have it do the outreach for you.
by the end of this guide you'll have:
a customer profile built from real research
a list of places grok bot checks for them daily
every buying signal sent to you with a draft
content and creatives made from research
grok bot running all of it and improving every week
 
how it basically works
the idea is simple. grok bot has its own computer, so it can sign into tools and scrape the web, and it can run routines on a schedule. you give it a setup prompt with everything about your business, and it sets itself up as your marketing team.
it splits the work into separate jobs, like one job researching your customers, another writing the content, another finding leads, and so on. you can think of each job as a small bot with one task, and how many it needs depends on your business.
this works for any kind of business, b2b or consumer. what changes is what a conversion is for you and which channels are allowed to reach your customers. for example, a b2b service can email a company that's publicly listed its address, but a consumer app can't email strangers, so its outreach is content and ads instead. the bot works this out from what you tell it.
everything lives in one place. either grok bot builds you a dashboard with everything in it, or you connect it to notion and it runs everything from a channel there.
the whole setup is built around whatever your conversion is, let's say a booked call or a signup, not content for its own sake. so the research looks for the problems your customers actually have and the content speaks to those problems, and at the end of the week the report writes down what actually brought customers.
what you need
apify, which is a scraping service. it pulls reddit, google maps, the meta ad library, stuff like that, and grok bot calls it with an api key.
replicate for images and video, with gpt image 2.5 for images and seedance for video. (they are currently the best models)
if you sell through whop, connect that too. grok bot has a whop connector, so it can see what's actually selling and who's joining.
and if you sell to businesses, a separate email sending domain, not your main one, so it can send cold email.
the setup prompt
this is the prompt that starts everything. it tells the bot what you sell and who buys it, sets up the dashboard and the jobs, and runs everything once as a test so you can see the output.
fill in the brackets with as much as you know. the more it knows about your business and your customer, the better everything downstream gets.
 
 
start with your customer
the first thing the bot does is build your customer profile from real research. it goes where your customers already talk about the problem you solve, and pulls the actual language people use, with a link to every source.
for each type of customer it writes a profile of who they are and what they're trying to get done. this matters because the language customers use to describe a problem is usually different from the language a company uses to describe its product, and the bot writes everything downstream in the customer's version.
let's say you sell to roofers. the profile will be full of complaints about lead platforms selling the same lead to three other roofers at the same time, which turns straight into an angle like "leads nobody else gets." just an example.
then every piece of content has to get past that customer. the bot scores each draft from 1 to 10 the way the customer would. a piece that leads with your company name tends to score low, and the same piece rewritten to lead with the customer's problem scores higher. these scores are the bot playing the customer.
if you don't know who your best customer is yet, the bot can help there too. tell it to propose a handful of customer types, ranked by how well your product fits and how active those people are online, and let you pick.
 
 
research what's already working
twice a week the bot looks at what your customers are complaining about right now and what your competitors are running, and turns it into ideas and a library.
the sources are the communities for your customer and the meta ad library, which is a public list of every ad running on facebook and instagram.
each idea goes into the dashboard with an angle and a link to where it came from. the library is where it saves the ads and posts that work, explaining why each one works.
it also watches your competitors every week, and when it finds one doing something close to what you do, it rewrites your main difference so every piece says what they can't. one tip tho, scope every search to the right community, because a broad reddit search just returns random posts.
 
plan the week and write the content
the first day of the week, the bot turns the best ideas into a calendar for the week and asks you to approve it. a week is usually a handful of pieces across a couple of channels.
once you approve, it writes each piece in plain short sentences in the customer's tone, with your main difference stated every time.
the customer check catches the usual problems. a post that just lists complaints and never says what to do gets a note like "so what do i do about it?" and comes back with actual steps.
 
 
make the creatives
before it makes any images or video, the bot writes a style note from the best examples in the library. the style note says what every creative should look like, like photoreal, the product shown in the customer's actual situation etc., then every image and video follows that note, so the account basically looks like one brand.
it generates within a weekly budget you set and only for pieces you've approved. for everything else it just writes the prompts, so you can see what it would make.
 
fix the page they land on
a lot of businesses lose people on the page, not on the way to it. so the bot opens every landing page the way a customer would, coming from the post or ad that sent them, and checks whether the page repeats the promise that got them there and whether the booking or signup button is visible.
the usual leaks are a headline that doesn't say who it's for and prices that are missing, stuff like that. the bot lists every leak with a fix and drafts the fixed copy.
it also writes what it learned into the rules page as a lesson, something like "say who it's for and show real proof in the first screen of anything we publish", so the content and creative jobs follow it from then on.
 
 
find leads without getting banned
every day the bot finds people asking for exactly what you sell and writes the reply, and you post it yourself.
this is the same whether you sell to businesses or consumers.
the hard rule is that it never posts or dms on any platform itself, because automated posting gets accounts banned and a lot of communities ban self-promotion outright. so public replies are help-only, with no link and no mention of your business unless someone asks what you do.
let's say someone posts that they're unhappy with the options they've tried. the bot drafts a reply that actually helps, with real advice on what to look for, and sends you the link and the draft in chat. you read it and post it from your own account, and if they ask what you do, you tell them.
it logs every community's rules in the dashboard, and where a rule can't be confirmed it treats that community as no-link.
 
outreach, which depends on who you sell to
this is the one part of the setup that changes with your business, so the bot runs one of two versions.
if you sell to businesses, cold email is on. a company that lists its email on its own website can be contacted, and the bot can build that list and send the sequence under proper rules.
if you sell to consumers, cold email is off. you can't find a stranger's personal email, and you shouldn't email it if you could. so the outreach job runs the channels that actually work for consumers instead, like paid ads built from what the research found, or creators and affiliates who already reach your customer. the bot proposes the channels and drafts everything.
for b2b, here's how the email runs. it sends from a separate domain, never your main one. before each campaign you approve the list and the emails in chat, and only then it sends. every email has your business name and should have an easy unsubscribe. it only emails business addresses that are publicly listed on the company's own site. it starts at 20 a day and pauses if bounces go over 3% or anyone marks it as spam, and every reply comes to you.
the list gets built from google maps or a directory through apify. the bot searches your customer type in the places you want, keeps only the addresses printed on each company's own site, and removes the listings that aren't actually your customer. a sequence is usually three emails spread over a week, each one offering something small and free.

the weekly report and the learning loop
at the end of every week the bot reports what went out and what brought customers, then writes 3 to 5 plain lessons into the rules page. every other job reads those lessons before it works, so the whole setup gets a bit better every week.
if you sell through whop, this is where you should use the connector. the bot pulls real sales and new members from there, so what brought customers means people who actually paid.
 
 
the weekly schedule
every morning you get a short check-in with new leads and what's waiting for your approval, plus anything it spotted.
once a week the report and lessons come in along with fresh research, and the plan for the week shows up for you to approve. the page audit runs once a week too.
everything else runs after you approve the plan.
