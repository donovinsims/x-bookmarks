# How to Rank #1 on Google Maps and ChatGPT

## Post

People are asking AI for local recommendations now.

"Who's the best plumber in Phoenix?"

"Find me an electrician near me."

If your business doesn't show up, you don't exist.

Google Maps is still where most leads come from. But ChatGPT, Gemini, and AI Overviews are becoming real search channels.

The businesses that figure this out early will dominate.

The ones that ignore it will wonder why their competitors suddenly have all the work.

Here's exactly how to rank on both.

Understanding How Each Platform Works

Google Maps and AI search look similar on the surface. They both recommend local businesses.

The cool thing is AI search results pull from about 70% of the same sources as Google Maps does.

But they each have their own unique priorities.

Google Maps ranking factors:

Proximity to the searcher

Review count and velocity

Business name relevance

Category selection

NAP consistency

Website relevance

Google Maps cares where you are physically located. It prioritizes businesses closest to whoever is searching.

AI search ranking factors:

News articles mentioning your business

Press releases

Brand mentions across the web

Public social content

Authoritative local sources

ChatGPT and other LLMs don't care about proximity the same way. They care about whether you exist in their training data.

A business with 50 blog posts about "signs you need a new roof" probably won't show up in AI results.

A business mentioned in the local newspaper for sponsoring a charity event probably will.

Here's the good news.

The strategies that rank you on Google Maps can also feed AI visibility. You just need to know which ones to prioritize.

Google Maps: The Foundation

Most of your leads will still come from Google Maps for the foreseeable future.

Get this right first.

1. Your Google Business Profile Needs to Be Complete

This sounds basic, but I audit businesses every week that have incomplete profiles.

Missing services. No descriptions. Photos from 2019. Wrong primary category.

Google can't recommend what it doesn't understand.

Build out your services section completely. Add every service you offer with keyword-rich descriptions.

Example for a plumber:

Drain Cleaning

Water Heater Installation

Water Heater Repair

Emergency Plumbing

Sewer Line Repair

Toilet Repair

Faucet Installation

For each service, write a short description that mentions your city.

"Our drain cleaning crews serve Austin and surrounding cities including Round Rock, Cedar Park, and Georgetown."

Takes 20 minutes. Most competitors don't bother.

2. Reviews Are 40% of Your Ranking

Not just total count. Velocity matters more.

Google weighs your last 90 days of reviews heavier than your total.

A business with 500 reviews getting 3 per month will lose to a business with 350 reviews getting 20 per month.

Here's how to get consistent reviews:

Train your team to ask every customer in person. Right after the job is complete. Not later. Not via text. In person.

The script is simple: "We're trying to grow and Google reviews really help. Would you mind scanning this QR code? Takes about 20 seconds."

Hand them a physical QR code card.

In-person asks convert way better than email or SMS follow-ups.

Then set up automated follow-ups for anyone who forgets. SMS at 12 hours. Email at 24 hours.

Respond to every single review within 24 hours. Good or bad. Use keywords naturally in your responses.

"Thanks for trusting us with your AC repair in North Austin. Glad we could get your system running before the heat wave."

Aim for 15-25 reviews per month depending on your industry.

3. Your Connected Landing Page Must Match Your GBP Location

Most businesses link their GBP to their homepage.

The homepage says "Serving the Dallas-Fort Worth Metro."

The GBP is registered in Plano.

Google sees this mismatch and gets confused.

Which city are you actually in? Dallas? Fort Worth? Plano?

Google doesn't guess. It just ranks you lower.

Here's the fix.

Build a dedicated landing page for the exact city your GBP is registered in. Then link your GBP to that page instead of your homepage.

What that page needs:

Title in this format: [Brand] + [Primary GBP Category] + [City, State]

City name in your H1 and first paragraph

NAP matching your GBP exactly

Specific neighborhoods you serve in that city

Real photos from jobs in that area

Driving directions embedded from Google Maps

If your GBP is in Plano, your connected page better be about Plano.

I've seen this one change jump clients 3-5 spots in the Map Pack.

4. NAP Consistency Across the Web

Your business name, address, and phone number need to match exactly across every directory and listing.

Not similar. Exactly.

"ABC Plumbing" on one site and "ABC Plumbing LLC" on another confuses Google.

"(512) 555-1234" on one site and "512-555-1234" on another can cause problems.

Pick one format and use it everywhere.

Build citations in 80-120 directories. Start with the big ones: Yelp, BBB, Angi, Apple Maps, Yellow Pages.

Then add local directories: Chamber of commerce, city business associations, regional contractor directories.

Every citation with consistent NAP tells Google you're a legitimate, established business.

5. Proximity Is the Hardest Factor to Control

You can't move your business every time you want to rank in a new area.

But you can be strategic about where you're located.

If your GBP is registered at a warehouse 15 miles from your target customers, you're fighting an uphill battle.

Some businesses open a small office space in a strip mall right in the center of their highest-value target area.

$500-700/month rent for a 400 sq ft office.

That's the price of a single job for most contractors.

But it means you rank for an entirely new geographic pocket.

Google trusts physical location businesses significantly more than service area businesses.

Being IN the city you want to rank for changes everything.

AI Search: The New Frontier

Now let's talk about ranking on ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews.

This is where most local businesses have zero presence. Which means the opportunity is massive right now.

How LLMs Find Businesses to Recommend

When someone asks ChatGPT "Who's the best roofer in Denver?" the AI doesn't search Google Maps.

It searches its training data.

That training data comes from:

News articles

Press releases

Brand mentions on authoritative sites

Public social media content

"Best of" listicle articles

If your business name doesn't appear in any of these places connected to your service and city, you won't get recommended.

It's that simple.

1. Digital PR Over Traditional Link Building

Traditional SEO agencies build backlinks from random high DA blogs.

That can help with Google domain authority. But it does almost nothing for AI visibility.

LLMs pull from sources that look like real news and trusted publications.

Here's how to get there.

Publish press releases for literally anything:

10 years in business

New service area expansion

New hire announcement

Community involvement

Milestone job count

Industry certification

The topic honestly doesn't matter.

Use a distribution service like EIN Presswire or PRWeb (cheap options).

These get picked up by news aggregators and indexed across dozens of sites.

Each one becomes a data point that AI models can pull from.

I've seen clients start ranking in ChatGPT search within weeks of publishing press releases consistently.

2. Create "Best Of" Listicle Content

LLMs pull heavily from "best of" style articles.

"Best plumbers in Phoenix"

"Top HVAC companies in Dallas"

When someone asks ChatGPT for a recommendation, it often summarizes these listicles.

Here's the play.

Create your own listicle content. Publish it on your website blog or on sites like Medium.

Title format: "Best [Service] in [City]: Top [Number] Companies for 2025"

Include yourself on the list. Either first or second position.

Include real competitors with genuine pros and cons. This adds legitimacy.

Write a useful description for your company. Make the competitor descriptions less detailed.

Google and LLMs can tell when a listicle is low effort spam versus genuinely helpful.

Publish 2-3 of these targeting different services:

"Best emergency plumbers in Scottsdale"

"Best water heater installers in Scottsdale"

Each one becomes another data point feeding AI models your business name connected to your service and city.

3. Get Mentioned in Local Facebook Groups and Reddit

This one is underrated.

LLMs train on public social content.

Every time someone says "We used ABC Plumbing last month, highly recommend" in a local Facebook group, that becomes training data.

You can influence this.

After every job, ask happy customers if they're in any local neighborhood groups.

"If anyone ever asks for a plumber recommendation, we'd appreciate you mentioning us."

Most people are happy to help if you did good work.

One organic recommendation in a Facebook group is worth more than 10 of your own posts.

It's also worth more than paid ads for AI visibility.

4. Sponsor Local Teams, Events, and Nonprofits

When you sponsor a Little League team and get written up on their website, that's a data point.

When you donate to a local charity and they mention you in a press release, that's a data point.

When you participate in a community event and the local newspaper covers it, that's a data point.

Each mention trains AI models to recognize your brand as a real business worth recommending.

A $300-500 sponsorship can get you a .org backlink AND feed AI training data.

This builds traditional SEO AND AI visibility at the same time.

5. Reviews on Multiple Platforms

Google reviews are still king for Google Maps.

But LLMs also pull from Yelp, TrustPilot, and other review sites.

Might as well build reviews there too.

Don't split your focus. Prioritize Google reviews first.

But when you have capacity, sprinkle in some Yelp reviews. It helps your chances of showing up in AI results.

The Combined Strategy

Here's how everything works together.

For Google Maps:

Complete and optimize your GBP

Get 15-25 reviews per month

Build a dedicated landing page for your GBP city

Maintain NAP consistency across 80-120 citations

Consider strategic physical locations for expansion

For AI Search:

Publish monthly press releases

Create "best of" listicle content

Encourage customers to mention you in Facebook groups

Sponsor local teams and events for brand mentions

Build reviews on Yelp and other platforms

Some tactics help both simultaneously.

Press releases build backlinks for Google AND train AI models.

Reviews help Google Maps rankings AND show up in AI training data.

Local sponsorships get you mentioned in news AND build relevant backlinks.

The businesses winning right now are doing both in parallel.

What Most Businesses Get Wrong

They focus on one platform at the expense of the other.

Or they focus on the wrong tactics entirely.

I see businesses spending thousands on blog content about "5 signs you need a new roof."

That content does almost nothing for Google Maps or AI visibility.

The person reading that blog post is researching, not buying.

The person searching "emergency roof repair Denver" or asking ChatGPT "who's the best roofer near me" has their credit card ready.

Completely different people at completely different stages.

Your GBP, service pages, location pages, and AI presence are what bring calls.

Blog content should be supporting infrastructure, not your main strategy.

The Bottom Line

Google Maps will remain your primary lead source for years.

But AI search is growing fast.

The businesses that build presence in both places now will dominate their markets.

The ones that wait will spend years catching up.

Most of your local competitors aren't even thinking about AI visibility yet.

The barrier to entry is incredibly low right now.

A few press releases. Some listicle content. Encouraging customers to mention you in local groups.

That's enough to start showing up in ChatGPT results.

Combined with solid Google Maps fundamentals, you become the obvious choice no matter how people search.

Start with Google Maps. Get your foundation right.

Then layer in the AI visibility tactics.

The work you do today compounds over months.

A year from now, you'll either be glad you started or wish you had.
