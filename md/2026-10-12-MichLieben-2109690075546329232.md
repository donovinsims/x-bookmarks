# How to Get Your First 10 B2B Clients From Zero (FULL COURSE)

## Post

I grew my cold outbound agency to $7M ARR and ran outbound for 300+ B2B companies.

The first clients came from these same 4 steps, and step 1 only costs your time.

It works for agencies, consultants, freelancers, coaches, fractional executives and SaaS founders who sell to other businesses.

This is the full process for turning free content into people you can message and book on a call.

The 4 steps, in order:

Each step feeds the next one, so run them in this order.

The loop:

Get your name in front of buyers with a channel that costs time

Send that attention to one page that explains what you do

Message every good-fit person who saw you and stayed quiet

Repeat steps 1 to 3 for at least 3 months

What a setup that books calls includes:

Content people see every week

A page that tells them how you help

A way to catch the visitors who leave without booking

A hand-written message to every good fit who never reached out

Getting your name out there:

You have more time than money at the start, so pick a channel that costs time and almost nothing else.

The 4 options:

LinkedIn posts

YouTube videos

Blog posts and a newsletter

Answering questions in communities

I tried all four.

LinkedIn had the biggest impact in the shortest time, and it's where I tell every friend in B2B to start.

Today I'm at 82,000+ followers there.

Post on a fixed schedule.

Three posts a week is the pace I give founders who start from zero.

Write about the problems your buyers pay you to fix, in the words they use in comments and on calls.

Each post covers one problem and the steps you use to fix it.

Building the page they land on:

Every post sends people somewhere.

That place has to explain how you help within a few seconds of landing.

Where the page lives.

Build it on your own website, or as a published Notion page for free.

Your LinkedIn profile can do the job too, with your offer in the headline and the page link in your Featured section.

What goes on the page, top to bottom:

One line on who you help and the result they get

A short video of you explaining how it works

One button to book a call

Proof: a result from past work, your content numbers or screenshots of happy replies

A free resource people can grab with their email

Write the top line like this:

We help [type of company] get [result] in [time frame].

Anyone who reads that line knows in a few seconds whether the page is for them.

One button, repeated.

Every section points to the same "book a call" button, and the free resource sits at the bottom of the page.

Recording the video sales letter:

A video sales letter (VSL) is a short video where you explain who you help, how you do it and what happens next.

Our conversion 3x'd the first time we filmed one.

The 5 parts of the script:

Who it's for: the type of company and the role

The result: what changes for them after working with you

How it works: your process in 3 simple steps

Proof: one result with real numbers, from past work or a first pilot

The next step: "book a call below"

Keep it short.

Say each part in a few sentences, so the whole video stays a few minutes long.

Record it on your phone or laptop, and do it again until it sounds like you talking to a client.

Put the video right under the top line, above the button.

Catching the people who don't book:

Two tools keep page visitors in your pipeline after they leave.

1. A lead magnet.

A lead magnet is a free resource people get in exchange for their email.

Good ones: a checklist, a template, a short guide, or the exact process you use with clients.

Build it as a page in Notion.

Click Share, open the Publish tab and click Publish, and Notion gives you a public link for free.

Add a Notion form to your page that asks for an email, since Notion forms are free to create and share.

Every signup lands in a Notion table, so email each one the guide link yourself while the list is small.

Nurture the list.

Send everyone who downloads it one email a week with your best ideas and results.

beehiiv handles that for free up to 2,500 subscribers.

2. Visitor identification.

Visitor identification tools add a small script to your website and show you which companies, and often which people, visited your pages.

Finding the good-fit prospects:

Step 3 starts with a list of people who already know your name.

Where to find them:

People who reacted to or commented on your posts

People who viewed your profile (free accounts see a few recent viewers, Premium shows the full list for up to a year)

Companies and people from visitor identification

Lead magnet signups who haven't booked a call

Keep the good fits only.

Write down the type of company you help and the job titles that buy from you.

Remove everyone outside that line before you write a single message.

Remove clients, partners and anyone already talking to you.

Get a working email. The order to run:

Prospeo: paste each person's LinkedIn profile URL

FullEnrich: run everyone Prospeo missed, since it checks 20+ data providers one after another

Log which tool found each email in your sheet.

Prospeo: free plan with 100 credits a month

FullEnrich: 50 free credits to start, then $55 a month for 1,000 credits

Keep a simple sheet.

Name, company, LinkedIn URL, email, where you found them, and the post they engaged with.

That last column makes the first message easy to write.

Writing the first 50 by hand:

Write the first 50 messages yourself before you automate anything.

The message:

Hey {{first_name}}, looks like my post on {topic} landed well. Are you working on {initiative}? Happy to swap notes if useful.

How to fill it in.

{topic} is the post they reacted to, taken from your sheet.

{initiative} is the project that post is about, like hiring salespeople, launching a product or fixing their onboarding.

The question in the middle gives them something easy to answer in one line.

Where to send it.

Free LinkedIn accounts only get a few personalized connection notes a month.

So send most of the first 50 by email from a separate sending domain (setup below), and DM the people you're already connected with.

Why by hand.

Every manual message teaches you something about what gets a reply.

After 50, you'll see the topics, titles and openers that get replies, and you'll automate those next.

Log every reply.

Note which post, which title and which opener led to each reply.

Protect your main domain.

Send cold email from separate domains, and keep your main domain for the emails your clients see.

Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain.

SPF and DKIM prove the email really comes from your domain, and DMARC tells inboxes what to do with mail that fails those checks.

Stay between 20 and 30 emails per inbox per day, with 2 to 3 inboxes per domain.

Running it for 3 months:

Repeat steps 1 to 3 for at least 3 months before you allow yourself to give up.

You'll keep posting through weeks with no visible results.

I posted 30 times before I saw any traction.

Three posts a week puts post 30 in week 10.

Each week of reps makes the next posts and messages easier to write.

Stay on one channel for the full 3 months.

A second channel splits the hours you have.

Track 3 numbers every week:

Posts published

Messages sent

Calls booked

Re-use what gets replies.

A post that starts conversations becomes the topic of your next 5 messages.
