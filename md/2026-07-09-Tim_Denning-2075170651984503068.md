# You’re born to f*cking create. Life will feel boring until you start creating something.

## Post

The traditional path means you create nothing.

The average job has zero creativity and imagination. It’s why the average worker lives a life of quiet desperation – too comfortable to change, not uncomfortable enough to do anything about it.

When I worked in consulting, I’d come up with new frameworks or ideas. I’d present them to my boss.

He’d take them to his leadership meeting. Then he’d present them to the broader teams. Then the Powerpoint guys would hack my ideas to death, like murdering a toddler, and present back to me some butchered, tomf*ckery carcass of a deck.

That’s how the traditional world works.

New ideas are risky. Creation is a system. New creations are fed into a big machine that spits them back out as the same old ideas you were trying to innovate on.

This is why workers are bored.

We’ve forgotten how to create. We’re so busy following the rules or optimizing for some promotion or stock payout that’ll probably never happen, while our souls are dying inside because we create nothing.

AI has made it worse. All the boring day-to-day work is being automated which is exposing the lack of creation even more.

You’re born to f*cking create. Life will feel boring until you start creating something.

Why modern life feels boring as hell

People spend the majority of their time looking at a screen.

Or on social media being rage-baited by professional sh*t posters who just use politics and the news to piss people off for attention and to sell their $49 going viral course.

Spare time is spent watching Netflix. People would rather stay home than go outside. The pandem*c taught us to fall in love with comfort. It’s why modern fashion looks like absolute garbage.

I went to the shopping center on the weekend and every second person was wearing pajama pants, Crocs, and some baggy top. Go to a pub or nightclub, and you’ll be even more disappointed at the attire.

The core problem isn’t obvious. What is it? Dopamine.

100s of years ago, the Romans would get the same amount of dopamine in a year that the average person gets in a single day. Modern life has become p*rn.

P*rn started as nude people banging on a screen for pleasure. Then getting attention became p*rn. Then LinkedIn created career p*rn (those 'day in the life at Slack' TikToks where someone does two-tenths of f*ck-all and calls it work).

All of this life p*rn spikes your dopamine.

Too much dopamine from life p*rn makes everything feel boring, kind of like if you’re high on cocaine all the time, then when you’re sober everything feels boring in comparison (never done coke myself).

Too many dopamine spikes are making modern life feel terribly boring. Most people aren’t qualified in neuroscience to be able to see this pattern, so they live in ignorant bliss as a dopamine junkie without knowing.

Most people aren’t depressed, they’re uncreative

All the boredom leads to depression.

I shouldn’t admit this, but a big chunk of my banking career was nothing more than deep depression that I was too afraid to label as that. But now I’m unhinged and all, I should just admit it.

A lot of depression is secretly a lack of creation.

Therapists have a name for the treatment that works when antidepressants alone don't — behavioral activation. It's a clinical way of saying: go make something happen. Go create.

When you don’t create, it kills your spirit. Part of your humanity is quietly stolen from you. A creative deficit leads to feelings of “Is this all there is?” And that eventually leads to depression. Many depressed people addicted to prescription pills would be better off indulging in acts of creation than numbing their life with legal drugs.

But they don’t know any better. No one talks about the creative crisis caused by modern life. We all just live as if creating zero is normal as long as we get paid.

Getting paid isn’t enough though.

$200K a year feels like $100K a year after a few weeks.

If all you do is exist and never take the plunge back into creation, you will feel this sadness I’m describing and have a man in a white coat describe it as depression.

Don’t you dare ignore your creative calling any longer.

Your work is a form of radical

self-expression

Yet most people express nothing through their work.

This is why I’ve encouraged millions of people to write online. Not for fame, bikini babes, or head jobs – but as an act of expression. When we express we create. That act of creation fills a void in our lives that many of us have been starved of for decades.

Your expression transcends you too.

Your family and friends get to witness that creation. In extreme cases, it can even become your legacy. That’s worth fighting for, isn’t it?

Your creations express what you believe in too. They help people understand you. They allow you to create missions and share a worldview. That might seem small, but trust me, it’s absolutely addictive once you experience it.

Because too often in modern life we hide our true beliefs for fear of judgment. Or we let an employer limit our free speech out of fear we might get canceled or fired.

All of us have wild ideas we must let out into the world, or those same ideas destroy us from the inside.

A 14-year-old screwed the entire TV industry

In 1921, a 14-year-old named Philo Farnsworth is dragging a plow across a potato field in Idaho. Back and forth. Line by line.

The most mind-numbing chore on Earth.

Except this kid had been reading science magazines he found in an attic, and as he stared at the rows of dirt he’d carved into the field, his brain did something yours can do too: it made something out of the boredom.

The boy asked himself, what if you could paint a moving picture the same way you plow a field — one line at a time, but with electrons, too fast for the eye to catch?

He had no money. No degree. No lab. No permission. He was 14.

6 years later, in a bare San Francisco room, he transmitted the first electronic television image in history — beating the biggest corporations and the most credentialed engineers on the planet.

A farmhand with an idea f*cked the entire industry.

Every farmhand for a 1000 years had stared at those same rows in a field and felt nothing but tired. The difference wasn’t the field.

It was a mind that wanted to create instead of just endure.

And here’s the part that should haunt you. Farnsworth cured his own boredom by inventing the screen. Then he watched it turn everyone else into passive, glazed-over consumers. And he was so disgusted he banned his own kids from watching TV.

The creator built the thing. The consumers got addicted to it. Same object. Two completely different lives.

Creation is wealth generation

Society has taken a dark path.

Those who create are being torn down by those who create nothing. Everyone is equal, except mother nature proves nothing is equal. When you create things, you generate value. If you do that for long enough, you’ll build some form of wealth.

The opposite is true too.

If you create nothing, you will create very little value and your bank account will reflect that. When you don’t create anything you get stuck in a Hunger Games form of survival, where every day feels like you’re barely breathing.

You cannot consume your way out of this darkness, you can only create your way out.

Creation is value. Creation is money. So you must create to survive and later thrive. No one wants to hear this because deep down it’s obvious. But we forget how powerful the act of creation is because corporate culture and herd mentality of modern sheeple people in society conveniently allow us to forget.

Don’t you dare forget. Create… or drown in consumption.

There are no freaking rules

Creation used to be full of gatekeepers.

I should know. I started my career in music. Absolute suck-off-fest. Talent meant absolutely nothing. The best performers had PR, management, and a label behind them. The best music without these things went nowhere.

But no one told me that. I learned the hard way.

Thankfully, society has corrected itself. Technology has removed every single barrier. This is why traditional media and platforms like Medium have seen millions of users disappear for more open alternatives like Substack.

No one wants to ask an editor, publication, or platform for help reaching an audience. They just want the work to speak for itself. If it’s good, people will consume it. If it’s not, the only opportunity is to get better which is completely in your control.

When rules are applied to creation, you end up with AI slop.

So much AI-generated content is lifeless because AI is taught to follow rules. AI doesn’t want to piss you off or offend you so everything it creates is safe. That’s the real problem few people talk about.

Real art is dangerous. It’s unconventional, irrational, and pushes boundaries.

AI can’t do any of this. All it can do is ask me 1000 times why I didn’t reveal more details of the story I told about not wanting to live. It doesn’t understand that not every story is complete, not every essay ends on a succinct takeaway, not every opinion needs a Harvard research paper to back up the advice.

You can just create and the very act of creation is enough. Humans can interpret the creation however they want. They can take away some parts and leave others.

True art is deeply imperfect.

Rules cannot exist for art to do its job.

“Creative types” don’t exist

The label is an excuse for everyone who opted out.

We’re born to be creative. It’s not a personality type, but a decision you make if you want to live the good life and not be painfully bored every day.

Creativity and imagination create free energy inside your mind. They feed into everything you do and how you interact with others.

The people who operate at higher states of consciousness or are simply infectious to be around have secretly mastered creativity. They don't say it — either because they think it's obvious, or because they've never noticed they're doing it.

Act like you’re a creative type because you are. And if you’re not, not to sound drastic, but it’s easier for AI to replace you. Creativity is the new moat – it’s the only advantage in a new world where hard work accounts for nothing.

Become a weirdo again

The antidote to a lack of creation is to create again.

It’s how you heal from mainstream society and painful corporate culture that has made everything boring and allowed millions of humans to write “kind regards” and “let’s circle back” in emails like a pitiful corporate drone.

To create is to be weird. It’s not normal. Most people are called consumers. That’s ironically even what the government calls us. To create is to embrace your weird. It’s to be a little artsy. It’s to see opinions like a**holes – everyone has one, so what.

When you create, normies think you’ve lost the plot or they say, “Timbo, you’ve changed, man.” Damn straight. Change is the only way to heal. Unless you start to rapidly become creative again the consequences are dire. Again, because of AI.

Work that creates boredom or is mind-numbing to complete can be done by robots 24/7 for free without any complaints, sickness, or need for a holiday.

It’s obvious where the world is going.

This is your limited window of opportunity to re-enter the world of creativity before it’s too late.

Make creativity your #1 priority.

Stop f*cking putting it off. Stop saying “One day.” Stop waiting for the chaos to calm down (it won’t). Just create. Every damn day like your life depends on it (because it does, amigo, trust me).

The things you create make you come alive. They stop you from feeling bored. They cure any depression you might be experiencing. And they take you out of the quiet financial desperation millions of people suffer through.

Creation is transcendence. It’s one of the greatest feelings in the world, and I want you to experience it every day. Get addicted. Become obsessed. Go all in. Create.
