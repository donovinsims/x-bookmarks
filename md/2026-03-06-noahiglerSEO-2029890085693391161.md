# Every home service company we've helped scale past $3-5M in revenue has one thing in common.

## Post

Every home service company we've helped scale past $3-5M in revenue has one thing in common.

They're not reliant on a single marketing channel...

The average is actually three (excluding repeat customers and referrals).

I talk to business owners every week who are doing $1.5 to $2M almost entirely off word of mouth and maybe one paid channel. And that's great... until it's not. One channel dries up, one slow season hits a little harder than usual, and suddenly you're scrambling.

Most importantly, what took you to $1-2M won't necessarily take you to $3-5M.

The companies that push past that point and keep growing have diversified how they get new customers.

Here are the three channels I see most consistently across the ones that are crushing it:

1. Local SEO (showing up in local search results organically)

This is the foundation. Showing up in the Map Pack (top 3 results) when someone searches for your service in your area. Showing up when someone asks ChatGPT or Google AI Mode for a recommendation. These are high-intent leads who are actively looking for what you do, right now. Oh, and once you're ranking, this is typically the cheapest, highest quality leads available.

2. Google Ads (PPC + LSAs)

Paid search fills the gaps while your organic rankings build. Local Service Ads especially are huge for home service businesses because you're only paying for actual leads, not clicks. The companies doing this well aren't just throwing money at Google and hoping (more common than you think). They have proper tracking set up, they know their cost per lead, they know their close rate, and they're making decisions based on ROAS.

3. B2B partnerships

This is the one most people overlook. Relationships with realtors, property managers, insurance adjusters, general contractors, complementary service providers. These aren't leads you're competing for against 4 other companies. They're warm referrals from trusted sources, and they close at a significantly higher rate than any inbound channel.

I can't totally speak on how to gamify building better b2b partnerships, but I see them crush for some of our clients.

The businesses stuck at $1.5 to $2M usually have ONE of these. The ones growing past $4M often have ALL three.

The biggest lie I hear from companies  is they can "keep doing what we're doing and get more calls"... that's how you stay stuck.
