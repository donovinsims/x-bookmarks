# My clients closed $8.4M from organic last year. Here's everything I do.

## Post

I run local SEO for over 40 home service companies. Plumbers, roofers, HVAC, landscapers, remodelers, restoration, etc.

The average client has been with us for 11 months. Some have been with us for over three years.

This is the full system we run for every single one of them.

Why organic matters even if you run ads

I'm not anti-PPC. We run Google Ads for clients too.

But here's what I've watched happen over and over. A business spends $15K, $25K, even $40K a month on ads. Their cost per lead creeps up every quarter. ROAS slowly drops. And if they ever pause the spend, the leads stop that same day.

I just talked to a custom home builder who spent $180K on PPC over 16 months. The agency was bidding on informational keywords like "how much does it cost to build a custom home." They wrote blog posts with ChatGPT and called it SEO.

Total contracts closed from all of it: zero.

His son had to fly in and fire the agency himself.

With organic, the math flips. It costs more patience up front. But 12 months in, your cost per lead is dropping while your lead volume is climbing. And it never turns off.

We have a client that's been with us for over two years. Just two brothers cutting lawns. They maybe got 5 phone calls a month TOTAL. Last season they hit 286 real leads in a SINGLE month, only from organic.

Step 1: Google Business Profile strategy

This is where 80% of the leads come from for every client we work with. Not the website. Not blogs. The Google Business Profile and the Map Pack.

Most businesses set up their profile once and forget about it. They pick whatever name they registered with the state. They choose a generic category. They upload three photos from 2019 and call it done.

The first thing we do for every client is the business name. If your legal name is "Smith & Sons LLC" and you're a plumber in Dallas, you are fighting uphill from day one. A competitor named "Dallas Plumbing Pros" will outrank you for "plumber in Dallas" with half your reviews.

We help clients file a DBA. It costs about $50 in most states and takes a week. That DBA lets you legally update your Google Business Profile name to match what people actually search.

This is one of the most impactful ranking factors on Google Maps and most businesses have no idea it exists.

Then we go deep on categories, services, products, attributes, and photos. Every field filled out. Every service listed with keyword-rich descriptions. Real job site photos uploaded monthly. Review responses that include the service and city naturally.

The last part of the GBP is click through rates. Let's just say there are... ways to increase the CTR on your GBP. We run this for some clients and it adds a ~5% increase in rankings.

Step 2: Multi-location expansion

This is where it gets fun and where most agencies don't even think to go.

Every Google Business Profile has a ranking radius. Roughly 5 to 15 miles depending on competition. Outside that radius, you're invisible on Maps no matter how many reviews you have.

So if you want to cover a 30-mile metro, one profile isn't going to cut it.

We help clients open strategic new Google Business Profiles in key areas across their service zone. Real locations where they can get verified without risk.

Then we run the full SEO playbook on each profile. Same optimization, same link building, same review strategy.

We had a client open an exact-match profile in a suburb about 15 miles from their main location. Almost zero competition in that area. Within a few weeks they were ranking number one for their highest volume keyword. Their main profile handles the core metro. The new one picks up everything the first one can't reach.

After three years with some clients, we'll have six, eight, even ten profiles across a metro. Each one generates leads independently.

I've talked to companies that have 15 or 16 GBPs covering an entire metro. That kind of map coverage doesn't just generate leads. It becomes a real part of the business's valuation when it's time to sell.

Step 3: Website structure that actually helps your maps rank

Most home service websites are built to look nice. They're not built to rank.

The biggest unlock for Map Pack rankings that nobody talks about is location pages. Not one "service areas" page with a list of cities. Actual dedicated pages for each city you serve with unique content about that area.

Each location page links to your service pages. Each service page links back. This internal linking structure is how Google connects the dots between what you do and where you do it.

We also build service pages that go deep. Process breakdowns, pricing context, real photos, FAQs specific to that service. Not 200-word pages with a stock photo and a contact form.

One flooring company we audited has nearly 1,000 Google reviews and a higher rating than every competitor in their market. They rank sixth for their main keyword directly on top of their own address. Guys with 40 and 60 reviews are beating them.

Wrong GBP category. No location pages ranking. No internal linking strategy. Just a showroom profile coasting on reviews and hoping for the best.

Reviews alone do not win.

Step 4: Reviews as a system

Most contractors ask for reviews the same way. They finish a job, send a text link, and hope. Maybe one out of every five customers actually leaves one.

We treat it like a system with actual numbers behind it.

Every client gets QR code cards their techs carry on every job. We train them to ask in person before they leave the site. Not a text two days later. Face to face, right after the job is done, while the customer is still grateful.

Then we automate the follow-up. A text goes out within 2 hours with the direct review link. If they don't leave one, a reminder goes out 2 days later. That second touchpoint catches people who just forgot.

For multi-location clients, we set up a link rotator. One universal link that automatically distributes reviews across profiles based on whatever split we set. Maybe 50% to the main location, 30% to the second, 20% to the newest one. The team sends one link every time and the routing happens automatically.

Companies that take this seriously average 15 to 30 new reviews a month. That velocity matters more than total count. Google weighs your last 90 days of reviews heavier than your lifetime total.

A business with 80 reviews getting 12 a month will outrank a business with 300 reviews getting 1 a month.

Step 5: Link building and digital PR

This is the part most DIY business owners skip entirely. And it's the part that separates page one from page three.

We build three types of links for every client.

First is citations. About 60 to 70 business directory listings with identical name, address, and phone number. Yelp, BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Apple Maps, and industry-specific ones. One wrong digit in your phone number on a random directory can confuse Google. Consistency across all of these tells Google the business is legitimate.

Second is local backlinks. We do outreach to real local businesses. Other home service companies in nearby cities, suppliers, vendor partner pages, chamber of commerce sites. These are the highest value links a local business can get.

Third is press releases. We publish them monthly through distribution services. The topic honestly doesn't matter that much. New hire, community sponsorship, service area expansion, seasonal promotion. What matters is your business name appearing next to your service and your city across dozens of news sites.

This does two things. It builds backlinks that help Map Pack rankings. And it trains AI models. Which leads to the last piece.

Step 6: AI search visibility

People are asking ChatGPT for contractor recommendations right now. "Find me a plumber near me." If your business doesn't show up, you're invisible to a search channel that's growing every month.

It's still early. Maybe 3 to 5% of local search volume. But all it takes is one good update from ChatGPT or Google for that to jump to 10%.

The good news is about 85% of the tactics that rank you on Google Maps also help with AI visibility. The press releases feed directly into this. The citations show up in training data. The location pages give AI models context about what you do and where.

We also create listicle content. "Best plumbers in Phoenix." "Top HVAC companies in Dallas." Published on the client's blog, on LinkedIn, or through press distribution. Each article becomes another data point connecting the business to the service and city inside AI training data.

One plumber we work with published 3 press releases over 2 months. He now ranks first when you ask ChatGPT for a plumber in his city.

The results

This system isn't fast. I won't pretend it is.

Most clients see their first real movement in months two through four. By month six, the leads are consistent. By month twelve, organic is competing with or beating their paid channels.

A roofing company in New York came to us closing about two roofs a year from SEO. After nine months, they've closed over $750K from organic. They're projecting SEO will bring in $2M this year.

An HVAC company in Los Angeles had never done residential work. We set up their first Google Business Profile from scratch. Eleven months later, they average 300 calls a month from that single profile and have closed north of $800K.

A landscaping company went from $10K months to $75K months over three years. All organic.

These aren't outliers. This is what happens when you run the same playbook consistently and don't quit after 90 days.

The businesses we've worked with for two or three years are paying us the same monthly fee they started with while generating ten, sometimes twenty times the lead volume they had when we began.

That math doesn't exist in any other channel.
