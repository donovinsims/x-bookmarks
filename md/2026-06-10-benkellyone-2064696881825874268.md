The Foundation (Months 1-3)

Most people overcomplicate this.

You need 3 things to start:

1. A pre-approval for an SBA loan
2. A target industry that you understand
3. Understanding of basic financial statements

You don’t need an MBA, a million-dollar net worth, or a tech background.

Step 1: Finding the Right Business

This is where 90% of people fail.

They chase sexy industries instead of profitable ones.

My rule:

If it's exciting or trendy, I'm not interested.

### 5/13

Best targets:

• Plumbing businesses (recession-resistant)
• HVAC companies (an essential service)
• Accounting firms (recurring revenue)
• Car washes (predictable cash flow)
• Laundromats (simple operations)

Boring = Best

### 6/13

Step 2: The SBA Loan Strategy

Use the government's money to acquire cash-flowing businesses.

SBA 7(a) loans give you:

→ 10-year terms
→ Competitive rates
→ 90% financing (put down 10%)

### 7/13

Step 3: Finding Sellers

Target owners who are actually ready to exit.

I focus on the "Silver Tsunami" (10,000 Boomers retire daily with no succession plan).

But if you want to find the best deals?

You won’t always be able to find them listed on business broker websites…

Step 4: The Due Diligence Process

This is where I went from just looking at deals to actually closing on them.

The secret?

I stopped trusting what sellers told me and started verifying everything.

Do NOT get lazy here.

### 10/13

Step 5: The Scaling Mechanism

As you start stacking businesses, you realize you can’t do everything yourself.

The key is hiring general managers.

My best advice:

Look for businesses that already have someone operating the day-to-day.
