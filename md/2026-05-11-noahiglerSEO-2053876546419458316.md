# I turn away companies every week because their reviews will bottleneck our local SEO.

## Post

I turn away companies every week because their reviews will bottleneck our local SEO.
 
Not because the work is too hard. Because if you're 60 reviews behind your top competitor and only getting 2 a month, no amount of advanced SEO is going to outrank them in 6 months.
 
You'll just spend money waiting for reviews to catch up.
 
Here's the test I run on every prospect to figure out if SEO will actually pay off for them right now:
 
Your total review count needs to be at least half of your top ranking competitor's count. And your monthly review velocity needs to be higher than theirs.
 
If you have 80 reviews and your top competitor has 200, that's fine. You're already in the range where rankings can move. If you also have higher velocity (say 12 a month vs their 4 a month), you're in the position where every other piece of the SEO strategy actually compounds.
 
Now flip it.
 
If you have 30 reviews and they have 200, no amount of citation cleanup, link building, or location page optimization is going to bridge that gap. Google looks at the math and decides they're more trustworthy.

You'd be paying an agency to push a boulder uphill...
 
This is why the first thing I look at on every discovery call isn't the website or the GBP, it's the review math.
 
If you're not at half your top competitor with higher velocity yet, the only thing that matters for the next 90 days is reviews.
> Get your team asking in person on every job
> Set up automated text and email follow-ups
> Call past happy customers and ask directly
 
Once you cross that threshold, advanced SEO becomes the necessary if you want to DOMINATE.

Don't start SEO until you have steady reviews.
