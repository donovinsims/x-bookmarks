# I run SEO and ads for home service companies.

## Post

I run SEO and ads for home service companies.

The thing that costs them the most money isn't bad marketing. It's what happens in the 47 minutes between when a lead comes in and when someone actually responds.

I started tracking this across our client accounts about a year ago because I kept seeing the same pattern. We'd rank a company number one on Google Maps, leads would be flowing in, website converting well. Everything on the marketing side was working exactly how it should.

Then I'd look at their actual close rate and it was terrible.

The leads weren't bad. The response time was.

The average across our clients before we started flagging it was around 45 to 50 minutes for form submissions, text leads, and aggregator leads like Thumbtack. Phone calls were better because someone usually picks up during office hours. But form leads, texts, and LSA messages would just sit there.

After 5pm it gets worse...

Most companies have their CSR go home at 5 and the phone either rolls to voicemail or a generic answering service. Meanwhile between 6pm and 9pm is when the highest percentage of homeowners are actually searching for services. They get home from work, the AC isn't working, the toilet is backed up, they notice the roof damage from last week's storm. They sit on the couch and pull out their phone.

Those leads don't wait until morning. They text the next company on the list.
I did the math on one plumbing client. They were generating about 50 qualified leads a month from organic alone. Their CSRs were averaging 24 minutes on form and text leads during business hours. After hours, nothing got touched until the next morning.

Based on their average ticket and close rate, the revenue they were losing to slow and missed responses was somewhere around $25K to $30K a month. On leads they were already generating. Leads we had already paid to rank them for.
That's roughly $300K a year leaking out of a business that thinks it has a marketing problem.

I've since started running this analysis for every new client during onboarding. The number is always big. Sometimes it's $150K, sometimes it's $500K. Depends on the vertical, ticket size, and lead volume. But it's never small.

If you're spending money on marketing and your average response time on non-phone leads is over 10 minutes, run the math yourself. Take your monthly lead volume, estimate how many come in after hours or sit for more than 15 minutes, multiply by your average ticket, and apply even a conservative close rate.

That number is what slow response time is actually costing you. Most owners have never calculated it and once they do it changes how they think about everything.

Might make a tool that calculates how much $ you're missing with your current sales times..
