# Every strategy I've used to generate $20M+ in jobs from Google for our home service businesses

## Post

Every strategy I've used to generate $20M+ in jobs from Google for our home service businesses

(Bookmark this)

> File a DBA with your core service + city and make it your Google Business Profile name
> Primary GBP category needs to match whatever search has the highest volume in your market
> Fill out every single service in your GBP with keyword-rich descriptions, not just the service name
> Open your GBP hours to 24/7 so you show up when someone searches at 10pm with a broken furnace
> Get a physical location as close as possible to where your highest-value customers actually live
> Link your GBP to a city-specific landing page, not your homepage
> If you offer two different trades under one company, set up a second GBP with its own primary category
> In a big metro, set up multiple legitimate GBP locations across the area to beat the proximity radius cap
> Build a review system that pulls in 15-25 new Google reviews per month without begging or paying for them
> Train your techs to ask on-site before they leave the job with a QR code card that links straight to your review page
> Automate a text at 12 hours and an email at 24 hours for anyone who didn't leave one on-site
> Respond to every single review within 24 hours and mention the service and city naturally in the reply
> Build 80-120 citations with your exact name, address, and phone number matching across all of them
> Start with Yelp, BBB, Apple Maps, Angi, Facebook, then hit your Chamber of Commerce and local directories
> Index every citation after you build it because an unindexed citation does almost nothing for you
> Get backlinks from your suppliers and distributors, they already know you and most have a partners page
> Sponsor a local sports team or charity for a few hundred bucks and get listed on their website
> Press release once a month through EIN Presswire or PRWeb with your brand tied to your service and city
> Dedicated service page for every core service you offer, not one page listing everything with bullet points
> Dedicated location page for every city you want to rank in, each with unique content about that area
> Target keyword in your title tag, H1, meta description, and first 100 words on every money page
> Internal link everything, service pages to location pages and back, blog posts pointing to money pages
> Mobile load time under 3 seconds, compress all images, lazy load anything below the fold
> Above the fold: service + city headline, massive tap-to-call button, star rating, lead form, 3 reassurances
> Real photos from real jobs, never stock images
> Embed your Google reviews on your homepage and location pages
> FAQ section on every service page answering the exact questions homeowners type into ChatGPT
> Write "Best [Service] in [City]" listicle articles on your blog or Medium and include yourself with real competitors
> Get reviews on Yelp specifically because ChatGPT pulls from Yelp harder than almost any other source
> Get mentioned on Reddit, Nextdoor, and local Facebook groups for brand signals LLMs can pick up
> Schema markup on every service and location page so AI models can parse your business as a structured entity
> Content gap analysis against whoever ranks top 3 in your market, then make your pages better than theirs
> Post on your GBP twice a week with real job photos and link to your service and location pages
> Geotagged photos uploaded to your GBP to strengthen "near me" rankings
> Set up after-hours call handling so leads that come in at 9pm don't bounce to a competitor by morning
> Track where every lead comes from so you know your cost per lead by channel and which dollars produce jobs
> Segment your Google Ads campaigns by service type so the algorithm doesn't optimize for the cheapest click instead of your highest ticket work
> Build dedicated ad landing pages matching the exact service and city of the ad, not your homepage
> Weight 60-70% of ad budget toward your highest margin services
> If competitors aren't bidding on your brand name and you're already ranking organically for it, cut the branded ad spend and put it toward higher-intent service terms
