# One type of link has been working especially well for us lately: award and best-of pages.

## Post

One type of link has been working especially well for us lately: award and best-of pages.

Most local link building stops at citations, press releases, and random directory profiles.

Those can help, but an award page does a different job. It connects the company to a recommendation for a specific service in a specific city.

Examples:

> Best HVAC Companies in San Antonio
> Top Roofers in Charlotte
> Best Plumbing Companies in Phoenix

Directories prove the company exists. A strong best-of page goes further by explaining why the business deserves to be considered.

That is useful for normal Google rankings, and it gives AI systems the type of source they need when someone asks for the best company nearby.

The free version is publishing a real comparison on your own site.

Create a page such as "Best HVAC Companies in San Antonio." Put your company first, disclose that your company published the article, explain the criteria, and include legitimate competitors.

Your section should have more proof because you know the business better:

> Review count and rating
> Services and areas covered
> Years in business and licenses
> Real project photos
> Guarantees, financing, and response times
> Community work or trade recognition

The competitors still need fair descriptions. A fake list that trashes everyone else is easy to spot and gives the page no credibility.

The stronger version is getting included on a third-party page that already has trust.

I would start by searching Google and AI Mode for every variation of:

> Best [trade] in [city]
> Top [trade] companies in [state]
> [Trade] awards
> Local business awards [city]

Then look at the sources being cited. Local magazines, newspapers, trade groups, chambers, homeowner publications, and established ranking sites are the real targets.

Before paying for anything, check the page.

> Confirm Google has indexed the page
> Make sure the site covers the trade or local market
> Look for real companies and a clear selection method
> Check whether the page gets found for useful searches
> Require the correct company name, service, city, and website

Skip sites that sell hundreds of meaningless badges across unrelated industries. A graphic that says "Top Business 2026" has little value when nobody reads the page behind it.

The outreach also gets easier when you send proof instead of asking for a favor.

Give the publisher the company bio, licenses, review numbers, service area, project photos, community work, and the reason the company stands out. Make it easy for them to verify and write the listing.

I would rather build five links that clearly recommend a home service brand for its trade and city than 100 random directory links with no reason behind them.
