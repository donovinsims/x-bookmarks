# How to Use Trending News for Local SEO (Website + Google Profile): "It's Low-Hanging Fruit"

## Post

Tim opens with a question: could this be one of the best strategies for local SEO and for traffic to your website?

His answer is that it is up there. Two sites he shows are doing it at a huge scale, and he says very few local businesses are.

The strategy is news. A news section on your site about trending topics in your niche, then the same news on your Google Business Profile. He calls it low-hanging fruit.

Here is the whole setup:

Study two big sites that already run on news

Check which of their pages actually bring the traffic

Find news stories for your niche and turn each one into an article

Put the news feed on autopilot

Post the news on your Google Business Profile and reply to every review

I pulled every step from the recording, screen by screen.

Tim's whole video in one sign: a local business can borrow the news play from much bigger sites.

I like this one because it gives a local business something new to say every week, without inventing topics.

Prefer video? Tim walks through both sites and the whole setup here:

1. Look at two sites that run on news

Tim's first example is Teslarati. It has a whole news section about Tesla, and the goal is to bring readers in and send them on to its shop of Tesla accessories.

He shows it at about 115,000 in monthly organic traffic.

His second example is The Points Guy. It publishes news about saving money with travel points, multiple articles every day, and earns referral money from credit cards.

The Points Guy in Arvow's Site Explorer: 12M estimated monthly organic traffic, 417k ranking keywords and Domain Rating 70.

Neither one is a local business. Tim's point is that the play itself is simple enough for one.

2. Check which pages bring the traffic

Tim opens the top pages report for The Points Guy to see where that traffic lands.

The site's top pages, with a news article at 85k visits a month in fifth place and another at 51k further down.

Tim reads this as a lot of their traffic coming from news articles.

The same screen tells a more mixed story. The top page is a Google Flights guide at 7.2M visits a month, and most of the rest are credit card and loyalty guides.

So I read it as news earning a real share of the traffic, not most of it. For a local business that is still plenty.

3. Why news helps the whole site

Tim gives three reasons, in his own words:

It brings traffic to the news articles themselves

It helps your overall domain and your authority

Other sites link to news articles, so you pick up backlinks naturally

The last one is the one I would watch. A local roundup that people quote is easier to earn links with than a service page.

4. Find news stories for your niche

Tim uses Arvow for this. Inside it there is a news article feature where you type a search query and it finds stories.

His demo query is credit cards.

The Add News Article screen, where each selected story becomes its own article.

You click the stories that fit your niche, add them as articles and create them.

For a plumber, my query would be something like water bills or a new local building rule. Broad topics give more varied results, and specific ones give tighter results, as the screen says.

5. Turn a story into a full article

Tim opens a Tesla news article he made earlier with the same feature.

It came out with the SEO pieces already in place:

A meta description

H2 and H3 headings

Its sources listed

From there you add your website as an integration and publish it straight to your site.

I would still read every news article before it goes live. News moves fast, and your name is on it.

6. Put the news feed on autopilot

The next step is an AutoBlog. Tim sets one up from a news feed: you pick the feed and the keyword, and it generates and publishes to your website automatically.

Creating a campaign with Full AutoBlog selected, which runs feed, generate and publish on autopilot.

The same screen also has AutoBlog with Review, which waits for your approval before anything is published.

Tim's idea is to run several of these for different sub niches inside your niche, so news articles keep coming out.

If I were starting, I would pick AutoBlog with Review for the first few weeks.

7. Post the news on your Google Business Profile

This is what Tim calls the next level.

He searches for a plumber in Buffalo to make the point: most local businesses are not even publishing posts on their profile, let alone posts about trending news.

So you take your news articles and post them on your Google Business Profile too. Regular articles work there as well.

In Arvow you connect your Google Business Profile and automate the posts.

His reasoning is consistency. In his view, regular releases of articles, news and posts help Google rank and show your profile more, because you keep adding fresh content.

8. Reply to every review

The same Arvow connection also has review automation. You see your reviews and get automated replies based on what each review says, tailored to your brand.

Tim compares two plumbers on the map. Both have a 4.9 rating, and he calls the rest pretty equal, though one has more reviews.

One of them replies to its reviews and the other does not. As a customer, Tim says he would pick the one that replies, because it looks engaged.

He also thinks replies help SEO, because they put service words like plumbing repair back into the listing. That is his view, and the video does not test it.

I would never stuff keywords into a reply. Answer the person, and let the service you did come up naturally.

9. The rest of the toolkit he mentions

Before he wraps up, Tim runs through the other Arvow features he uses alongside this:

Site Optimizer, which audits meta titles, meta descriptions, image alt text, internal links and schema, and fixes them in bulk

To be precise about Automatic Backlinks: it is a one-way, credit-paid network of in-content links placed inside articles that AutoBlog publishes. You get 1 free link a day, then each verified link costs 15 credits, with no reciprocity, no Domain Rating matching and no vetting.

His last trick: when the monitor shows a prompt where you are weak, write an article with that prompt as the title.

Where to start

If I were a local business starting this week, I would pick two news topics my customers care about, publish one short news article on my site and the same news as a Google Business Profile post, then reply to my last ten reviews. Then I would set up a news AutoBlog with review: see Arvow pricing. Use code Tim10 for 10% off.

What I would not conclude

The thumbnail says rank number one locally. The video shows no local ranking, call or traffic result for a business that used this, so I am not claiming one.

Teslarati and The Points Guy are huge national sites, not local businesses. Their numbers show what news can do at scale, not what a plumber will get.

The same screens show Teslarati's traffic down 58% over the chart period, and The Points Guy well below its 2023 peak. News traffic can fade, so I would treat it as one channel among several.

Tim's views on review replies, keywords in replies and posting consistency are his opinions, not tests.
