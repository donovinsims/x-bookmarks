What most people missed
Web agencies have charged €2,000 - €8,000 per website for decades. The price isn't the build. The price is the sales process: ads to find leads, proposals, mockups, meetings, revisions, weeks of back-and-forth before anyone signs anything.
Claude didn't just make websites cheaper to build. It made them cheap enough to build BEFORE the sale.
That changes the psychology completely. A proposal is a promise - easy to ignore. A finished website with the client's own photos, their own menu, their own reviews quoted on the homepage - that's theirs. Saying no to it feels like losing something they already own.
The math that breaks the agency model:
Cost to build a demo with Claude: €0 (your subscription covers unlimited demos) 
Time per demo: 15 - 30 minutes 
Demos per evening: 5 - 10 
Conversion at even 20%: 1 - 2 clients per evening of work 
Price that closes small businesses instantly: €300 - 500 + €150/month  
The window right now: there are roughly 25 million small businesses in the EU and 33 million in the US. Studies consistently show around 25 - 30% of small businesses still have no website at all - and half of the ones that do have something that hasn't been touched since 2015. Almost nobody is pitching them this way. That gap is the business.
 
Part 1. Finding leads on Google Maps (the free, infinite lead list)
The lead list costs nothing and never runs out. The filter is simple:
Open Google Maps. Search "restaurant" (or barber, dentist, florist, gym, auto repair) in any mid-size city. Now look for this exact combination:  
✓ Rating 4.5 or higher - the business is good at what it does 
✓ 200+ reviews - they have real customers and real revenue
✗ No website link, or a website that looks like 2012 - they're losing customers online
High rating + no website = a business that wins offline and loses online. They have money. They have customers who would book online. They just never had anyone make it easy for them.
One evening of scrolling Maps in a single city produces 20 - 40 qualified leads. Write them in a spreadsheet: name, rating, review count, Facebook page link, what's wrong with their current web presence.
Then grab the raw materials for the demo: 5 - 10 photos from their Google Maps profile, their business description from Facebook, their opening hours, and 2 - 3 of their best customer reviews. You'll hand all of this to Claude.
One honest note that also strengthens your pitch: their photos are used for the demo only. After they sign, you replace them with photos they own or licensed ones. Tell them exactly that - "I built this with the photos you already have online; imagine it with professional ones." It turns a legal footnote into an upsell.

Part 6. Mistakes to avoid
Mistake 1: Pitching businesses with bad ratings. A 3.2 - star restaurant doesn't need a website, it needs a different chef. Only pitch winners who are invisible online.
Mistake 2: Building generic demos. The magic is recognition - THEIR photos, THEIR reviews on the homepage. A template demo converts at zero.
Mistake 3: Charging agency prices. €2,000 puts you back in proposal-and-meetings territory. €400 + €150/month is an impulse decision for a business doing €30K/month in revenue.  
Mistake 4: Skipping the recurring offer. The one-time sale feels good. The €150/month is the actual business. Always attach it.  
Mistake 5: Talking about Claude, AI, or how fast it was. The owner doesn't care. They care that customers can finally book a table at 11pm. Sell the outcome, never the tool.
