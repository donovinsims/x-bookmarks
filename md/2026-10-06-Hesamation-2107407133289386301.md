# If you have too many interests, build this AI system in 1 hour

## Post

if you have an ADHD brain, you probably have six different goals, you get overwhelmed, and you miss days without working on any one of them.
that's me. I love agents, harnesses, RL, LLM architecture, inference engineering, and alignment. the pile keeps growing, not for a lack of focus, but because I genuinely find passion and reason to follow each of them.
and that's the problem.
the moment I go deep on one, I feel like I am missing the others.
the advice is usually "just pick one thing". and I've already argued why that aged poorly.
 
the problem isn't having multiple interests, but a planning force outside your head and motivation. otherwise, motivation just becomes a daily battle in the morning inside your head, and a lot of times, it won't work.
you've probably heard that AI is the greatest gift for the neurodivergent. and that's true, but only if you can harness it correctly.
in this article, I explain the simple system I set up that works for me (and maybe for you too). I will explain:
two rules that stop your interests from competing
the five parts of the system, and why each one is there
what the research says about it
the weekly loop that keeps it alive
subscribe to my Substack so you don't miss the next one. https://multyhead.substack.com/
you're not unfocused, you're unplanned
and that's what the ADHD brain is worst at. what you need is to:
turn a vague goal (I want to become an X researcher) into an actual roadmap
decide which topic needs your attention today
remember where you left off last week
re-plan when everything falls apart (it will!)
the good news is, this is EXACTLY the boring work agents are good at now. reading a pile of context, turning it into steps, and rewriting the plan 10 times without feeling overwhelmed. 
your interests don't have to compete
 
here's what six interests look like in practice: every morning is a negotiation in your head between them on which gets today... you pick one, and the other ones still keep pulling at you.
so you switch, and switch, and switch.
and by Friday, anything has barely moved.
AI fixes this with two rules. you could do this yourself, but keeping them up week after week is just the exact kind of problem the ADHD brain has. the agent doesn't have that problem.
rule 1: use lanes, not sequences
the most natural plan that comes to mind is a queue. you finish one interest, start the next, then the next. it looks like focused, but the interests at the back of that queue don't quietly wait. they PULL.
so instead, every interest gets a lane:
build: one interest that gets most of the energy right now
reading: one that moves slowly, one chapter or paper a week
open: one side-quest slot for anything that's exciting this week
parking lot: everything else
nothing gets dropped, so no interest gets the anxiety of getting missed. it just waits with a bookmark. the AI can spread the lanes throughout your week, so that morning negotiation is already gone. 
this is what a planning force outside your head looks like. 
 
rule 2: find the spine
a lot of interests look fragmented and different from the outside. most of the time, they're not. they share common grounds, abd the agent can find them.
say you like coding, design, psychology, writing, video, etc. that looks like five hobbies that couldn't be more different, but point them at one thing, like shipping your own app, and they all turn into one direction.
code builds it
design makes it user-friendly
psychology helps you understand the people using it
writing markets it
and video gets it seen by more people
once you have that spine, going deep on one interest also powers the others instead of competing with them. 
 
if your interests are already close (mine are all AI), the spine is easy to form. if they look completely unrelated, that's exactly when you want the agent to find it for you, but of course, don't expect magic.
the Simple System You Need
1.the agent (Claude, ChatGPT, Hermes, GrokBot): this is the brain. any agent that can connect to your apps through plugins works. it reads everything and turns it into a plan. Claude, however, in my experience, is a much better option in this case.
 
2. your resources: it could be your CV, your socials, the jobs you want to apply to, the courses and papers you've saved, etc. it must be anything that shows who you are, what you know, and what you want to study. you can drop these as files, or just explain them in the prompt.
3. Notion. this is the roadmap and the memory for the agent. it holds the goal, the sources, the decisions, etc. if you don't want to rely on the agent's memory, this is the best option. the agent reads it at the start of every session and updates it along the way. obviously you can use other apps, even a .md file locally.
4. Obsidian, YOUR notes. Notion is for the agent. but Obsidian is where you store your notes. You can use Notion for yourself as well, in my case, I hate Notion for studying. I love the interconnection between notes, and the fact that everything can be stored locally and also edited by the agent running on your system.
5. A Planner (TickTick or Todoist). This is more critical than you think. planning is one thing, but having small steps to be accountable for each day is a MASSIVE difference. both of these apps have plugins that can connect to agents, to add tasks, or read the progress. I personally use TickTick, and obviously that's what I'd suggest. 
 
notice the emphasis on the "simplicity" of the plan. that's CRUCIAL. you don't need a system that adds to the friction when you're already unmotivated to do anything, you need something as easy as a prompt. 
the research: get the plan out of your head
the research behind this is older than AI. but it points one way: move the planning out of your head, onto paper, an app, or another person.
write it down
Russell Barkley, who is one of the best-known ADHD researchers, says ADHD is a problem of executive function. what he suggests is to arrange your environment so it "boosts or facilitates limited EF", like a to-do list for the day. (Barkley, 2011)
 
track your progress
A meta-analysis of 138 studies and 19,951 people found that monitoring your progress helps you reach your goals. when progress was physically recorded or reported to others, the effect was even bigger. (Harkin et al., 2016). that's ticking off tasks as done, then having the agent review them.
 
have someone check in
148 college students with ADHD who did 8 weeks of coaching improved their study and learning skills, self-esteem, and satisfaction with school and work.
 
of course, none of these studies were tested on AI, and the agent doesn't replace a coach... but what it does is automate the boring part that the research points at: write things down, track them, and have someone check in.
the weekly loop keeps the plan alive
you tick off tasks during the week
Claude reads what got done, what didn't, and all the notes you wrote in Obsidian
it updates its plan in Notion
it plans next week/days for you
 
set it up today in 60 minutes
you can do it in one sitting:
brain dump (10 min). list every interest that's haunting you, what you want to learn, become, build.
gather your resources (10 min). your CV, links to what you've built, GitHub, a few jobs you want to ideally apply to, the courses, the papers you want to learn. everything.
let the agent interview you (20 min). give it everything you got and ask it to question you to clarify any missing points. 
build the roadmap page (10 min). let the agent create its Notion page. the goal, the sources, the decisions. fix anything that might be wrong.
fill next week (or today). what I personally like to do is to set up each day's goals, rather than fill a whole week. that helps me be more flexible. 
schedule the loop. add one recurring task, for once a week, to run the weekly loop. 
 
Pinch of Salt
it's a blessing to have a mentor who can give you personal guidance and check on you. the agent won't replace that, but it's the best second option available.
the agent just suggests. you decide. you edit the roadmap. 
a vague goal gets a vague plan.
it's just a planning tool, not a treatment for anything. 
I only recently started running this, but so far it beats every other method I've tried to stay on track. I added each part one by one, after realizing what my previous systems were missing.
and to be clear, the AI doesn't learn FOR you. the hard part is still on you. the AI only takes over the boring part around learning, which for an ADHD brain is also the part with the most friction.
