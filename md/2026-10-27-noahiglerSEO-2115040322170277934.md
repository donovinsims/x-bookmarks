# If I owned a $10M plumbing and HVAC company in Charlotte, here's exactly how I'd make organic search our biggest market…

## Post

If I owned a $10M plumbing and HVAC company in Charlotte, here's exactly how I'd make organic search our biggest marketing channel:

(Bookmark this)

The Charlotte metro has about 2.9 million people, and the top of the market is stacked.

These are the review counts on some of the biggest Google profiles in the city right now:

> Morris-Jenkins: 36,800+
> Dilling Heating, Cooling, Plumbing & Electrical: 6,700+
> One Hour Heating & Air: 6,100+
> Benjamin Franklin Plumbing: 4,000+
> Horne Heating and Air: 3,400+

Morris-Jenkins is owned by Wrench Group, a private equity platform. One Hour and Benjamin Franklin are franchise brands under Authority Brands, which is PE-owned too.

You're not passing 36,800 reviews, and you don't have to.

I searched Google for Morris-Jenkins in Concord, Huntersville, Rock Hill and Gastonia, and the same profile came back each time: one address in southwest Charlotte, near Steele Creek.

That pin is about 22 miles from Huntersville, 30 from Concord and 34 from Mooresville. In a metro this competitive, one Google profile only ranks a few miles from its address.

Now look at who Google lists first in the suburbs:

> Mooresville plumbers: 65 to 653 reviews
> Concord plumbers: 108 to 944
> Fort Mill plumbers: 130 to 412
> Huntersville HVAC companies: 5 to 401, plus one from north Charlotte with 1,600+
> One Hour's Kannapolis location: 38 reviews and a 4.3

Concord is the biggest city in the metro after Charlotte, with about 105,000 people, and none of the plumbers Google lists first there has 1,000 reviews.

That's the opening. Here's the playbook I'd run:

1. Fix the tracking first
Give every source its own tracking number: each Google profile, the website and Google Ads. Each profile's number goes on all of its citations, and every sold job in the CRM gets matched to the lead that started it. The monthly report should show what organic actually sold.

2. Map the money, then map the rankings
Pull 12 months of sold jobs from the CRM and plot them by zip code. Then run a grid scan for "AC repair near me" and "plumber near me" across the whole metro. The next office goes where the good tickets are and the rankings aren't.

3. Open three real offices, and let the competition pick each one's trade
> Mooresville, plumbing and HVAC. About 50,000 people, 34 miles from Morris-Jenkins, and the top plumbers have 65 to 653 reviews.
> Huntersville, HVAC first. It grew from about 3,000 people in 1990 to about 47,000 in 2010, so a lot of the original systems in those subdivisions are near the end of their life. NuBlue has 2,400+ reviews just north in Cornelius, so the grid scan decides where in town it goes.
> Concord, plumbing first. Chad Love Services already has 4,500+ reviews on the HVAC side there, but the plumbing side is a lot softer.

I'd skip Matthews and Rock Hill for now. MTB Mechanical has 4,600+ reviews in Matthews, and Brothers Air has 9,300+ in Rock Hill.

Each office needs a lease in the business name, a permanent sign with the exact name, and its own suite number. Space them so their ranking areas meet at the edges.

4. Put the trade and the town in each name
> Mooresville: [Brand] Plumbing & Air Mooresville
> Huntersville: [Brand] Heating & Air Huntersville
> Concord: [Brand] Plumbing Concord

It has to be a real DBA. In North Carolina, that's an assumed business name certificate filed with the county Register of Deeds, and recording it costs $26. Get the name on the lease, invoices and citations before it goes on the profile.

5. Build each profile out
> Primary category: HVAC contractor or Plumber, matching the trade in the name
> Every service that office runs, each with a short description that names the town: heat pump replacement, AC repair, ductless mini splits, duct replacement, water heater replacement, tankless water heaters, repiping, sewer line repair and drain cleaning
> Open 24 hours only if a person answers. Morris-Jenkins and One Hour both list 24/7.
> Its own tracking number, with the same name, address and number on 50 core directory listings
> The website button pointed at that office's city page, with tracking tags
> Real photos from jobs in that town, and posts twice a week with a Learn More button to a service or city page

6. Win the monthly review count, not the total
Count the new reviews on the closest competitor's profile over the last 30 days, then beat that number every month.

Ramp each new profile: about 15 reviews in month one, 25 to 30 in month two, 30 to 35 in month three, then 40 to 45 a month.

The tech asks in person the moment the AC kicks back on or the water's running again:

"We're a local Huntersville company trying to grow. If you're happy with how everything turned out, I'd really appreciate a quick Google review. Just scan this, it takes about 20 seconds."

Text them the after photo to attach. Reply to every review within a day, and name the job and the town:

"Thanks for trusting us with the heat pump replacement in Huntersville. Glad we got you cooled down before the weekend."

Pay techs and CSRs about $30 a review, and put a leaderboard up in the shop every week.

7. City pages built from your own calls
When I audited One Hour's Charlotte site, the Matthews and Huntersville pages read like one template with the town name swapped. Google treats pages like that as duplicates, and most of them never get indexed.

Start with the 8 to 10 towns that matter most, like Huntersville, Cornelius, Davidson, Mooresville, Concord, Harrisburg, Kannapolis and Mint Hill:

> Title: "AC Repair in Huntersville, NC | [Brand]," with the H1 in the word order people search, "AC repair Huntersville"
> Pull 6 months of calls from each town, have AI sort them, and write up what people there actually call about
> Embed a live map of the jobs you've run there, plus reviews from customers who live there
> Write about the houses. Pre-1960s homes in places like Dilworth and Myers Park are where galvanized pipe shows up, and some 1980s and 1990s homes around Charlotte still have polybutylene.

8. Price pages
There isn't a price range anywhere on One Hour's Charlotte site. Build the pages people search before they call:

> What a new heat pump costs in Charlotte
> Water heater replacement cost in Charlotte
> What it costs to repipe a polybutylene house

Give a real range, what pushes a job up or down, and the monthly payment. Those are the pages ChatGPT and Google's AI answers quote.

9. A homepage that books jobs
Reviews and a big call button up top, online booking, the money services as big tiles, and price ranges without a form. The typical contractor site turns under 1% of visitors into leads, and ours land around 3 to 6%.

10. Local links and PR
> Join the local chambers in Lake Norman, Mooresville and Cabarrus County
> Sponsor youth sports in Huntersville and Mooresville. A few hundred dollars gets you a link from a local .org.
> Give the top realtors in each town a free first-year tune-up to hand every buyer they close with
> Send a press release a month tying your name to the service and the town
> Answer real questions in r/Charlotte

11. Google Ads while it ramps
Bid on your own name, and put the rest of the budget on installs and replacements in the towns where the new offices aren't ranking yet.

Reality check.

Morris-Jenkins has 36,800+ reviews, and you're not catching them in uptown.

Follow the ramp and a new Mooresville office has about 200 reviews by month six. That's more reviews than three of the five plumbers Google lists there today. Plan on 6 to 12 months before organic carries real weight.

Someone's going to figure this out and own the north side of Charlotte.
