# Just onboarded a home service company that's been trying to leave its agency for months.

## Post

Just onboarded a home service company that's been trying to leave its agency for months.

The agency owns their website, their Google Ads account, their Analytics, their Search Console, and the DNS on their own domain.

Firing them means starting over from zero.

This is way more common than it should be. The agency sets everything up "for you" in year one and it feels like great service. Three years later you find out none of it is yours. The ad account with all its history, the website, the tracking data, even the domain settings live in accounts you've never logged into.

And here's why agencies do it: it turns firing them into a hostage negotiation. Leave, and you lose your ad history, your analytics, and sometimes your entire website overnight.

Before you sign with anyone, or right now if you already work with an agency, confirm you own these six things:

> Your domain and DNS. Registered under your email and your credit card. This is the deed to the house.

> Your website and hosting. If the agency built it on their account or their platform, ask what happens to it the day you leave, and get the answer in writing.

> Your Google Business Profile. You are the primary owner. The agency gets manager access, never ownership.

> Your Google Ads account. The account lives under your email with your billing. Agencies connect to it through their manager account. If they "run their own account for you," all the data that makes your ads cheaper over time walks out the door with them.

> Analytics and Search Console. It's your business data. Own the accounts and grant access, instead of accepting access as a favor.

> Call tracking and your CRM. The phone numbers your customers have saved should port with you, and your lead history is yours.

Checking all six takes 20 minutes. The good agencies pass without blinking, because doing it right costs them nothing except leverage they shouldn't want in the first place.
