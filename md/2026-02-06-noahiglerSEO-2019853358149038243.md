# A contractor I work with pays each tech $25 for every Google review that mentions their first name.

## Post

A contractor I work with pays each tech $25 for every Google review that mentions their first name.

He went from 5 reviews a month to over 35.

The system is dead simple. After every job, the tech hands the customer a QR code card and says something like "If you were happy with the work, a Google review mentioning my name would really help me out."

The $25 incentive changed everything because it made reviews the tech's problem, not the owner's. Nobody had to remind them or chase them. They started asking every single customer because there was money on the table.

It also created competition between the guys on the team. One tech started averaging 8 reviews a month and wouldn't shut up about it. The other guys picked up their game just to keep up.

Friendly competition is SO underrated when motivating your techs.

The side effect nobody expected is that the reviews themselves got better. When a customer mentions a tech by name, the review reads more personal and specific. "Jake came out and fixed our AC the same day, super professional" hits way harder than "Great service, would recommend."

Those detailed reviews build more trust with potential customers reading them and they give Google more context about your services and service area.

$25 per review when your average job is $1,000+ is one of the cheapest customer acquisition tools you'll find.
