# Had a plumbing client outrank competitors with 4x his review count by filing a $50 form at the county clerk.

## Post

Had a plumbing client outrank competitors with 4x his review count by filing a $50 form at the county clerk.
 
The strongest GBP ranking signal most home service owners never touch is the business name itself.
 
Here's the legal way to put keywords in yours.
 
(Bookmark this)
 
Google's local algorithm weighs the business name on your GBP more heavily than almost any other signal. A profile named "Smith's Drain & Sewer Repair" ranks higher for "drain cleaning [city]" than one named "Smith's Plumbing" with the exact same reviews, citations, and proximity.
 
Most home service owners assume their business name is locked to whatever's on their LLC paperwork. They have more flexibility than they realize.
 
The legal way to optimize the name is by filing a DBA (Doing Business As), also called a fictitious business name. It's a $25-100 county filing that lets you operate under a different name. Google requires the GBP business name to match your real-world signage and legal documents, and the DBA satisfies that requirement.
 
Here's the structure that actually works:
 
> Original LLC: "Smith Plumbing LLC"
> DBA filed at county: "Smith Plumbing, Drains & Sewer Repair"
> GBP name updated: "Smith Plumbing, Drains & Sewer Repair"
> Verification documents: county DBA filing, signage matching the DBA, invoices using the DBA
 
The DBA is a real legal entity. It just happens to include the services you offer in the name.
 
What to include in the DBA name:
 
> Your brand name first
> The 1-2 services you most want to rank for
> Optionally a third descriptor like "Repair," "Service," or "Specialists"
 
Examples that work:
 
> "[Brand] HVAC, Heating & Cooling"
> "[Brand] Roofing & Storm Damage Repair"
> "[Brand] Drain & Sewer Specialists"
> "[Brand] Tree Service & Stump Removal"
 
What gets you suspended:
 
> Inventing a DBA you never actually file. Google audits this and the suspension is permanent.
> Adding city names to the DBA. "Phoenix Plumber" looks like keyword stuffing even with a filing.
> Changing the GBP name without updating signage, invoices, and website to match.
> Filing the DBA but keeping all your branding under the LLC name only.
 
Google's local quality team is increasingly aggressive about suspending GBPs with unsupported names. If they audit you, they want to see the county filing, signage on your truck or building, and matching invoices. With those in place, the DBA is rock solid.
 
The order of operations:
 
> File the DBA at your county clerk or state level. Varies by state, $25-100, takes 1-3 weeks
> Update your bank account, invoices, business cards, truck wraps, and website footer to display the DBA
> Change your GBP business name to match exactly
> Submit the county filing as supporting documentation if Google flags the name change
> Sync all 80+ citation directories so the NAP stays consistent everywhere
 
Common mistakes operators make trying to do this themselves:
> Skipping the actual filing and just changing the GBP name. That's keyword stuffing and gets the profile suspended.
> Picking a name with too many services jammed in. "Smith Plumbing, HVAC, Electrical, Roofing & Pest Control" reads like spam to both Google and homeowners.
> Forgetting to update the website footer and contact pages. The NAP mismatch hurts trust signals across the board.
> Only doing it on the GBP and never updating the rest of the citation profile.
 
The DBA play is the most underused legal lever in local SEO for home services.
 
If your competitors have generic names like "[Owner] Plumbing" and you're "[Brand] Plumbing, Drains & Sewer Repair" with proper documentation, you'll outrank them at the same review count.
 
Spend an afternoon on this and a $50 county filing fee. The ranking lift compounds over the next 60-90 days.
