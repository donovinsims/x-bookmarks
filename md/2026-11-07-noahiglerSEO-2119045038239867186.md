# We build home service websites that turn about 3 to 6% of visitors into leads.

## Post

We build home service websites that turn about 3 to 6% of visitors into leads.

The typical contractor site is under 1%.

This is everything we put on them, top to bottom:

(Bookmark this)

1. The first screen
Someone landing from Google decides in a few seconds whether to call you or go back to the search results.
> Show your trade, your city, your star rating and your review count right at the top.
> Put a big call button and a book online button side by side.
> Use a real photo of your team or your trucks, never stock.
> Skip pop-ups on phones, and use one main button instead of three that do the same thing.

2. More than one way to book
> Keep a call button on screen as people scroll.
> Offer online booking with real open times. A booking box we tested on one HVAC company's site booked 64 jobs and over $180K in sales in September.
> Add a "call or text" option for people who can't talk at work.
> Cut the form down to 3 or 4 fields, tops.

3. Reviews from more than one platform
> Put a review bar near the top with each platform's logo, star rating and review count.
> Lead with Google, then add the sites your local buyers actually check. On the West Coast, way more people use Yelp. My parents still do.
> Facebook, Angi and your BBB rating belong there too if you're strong on them.
> Add a live feed of recent Google reviews with the dates showing, so people can see they're current.
> Feature reviews that mention the service and the town.

4. Answers to the questions that stop people from calling
> Show price ranges for your big jobs, with no form to fill out to see them.
> List financing as a monthly payment.
> Put your license number on the page, plus any manufacturer dealer status you have.

5. Services people can scan
> Use 6 to 8 big tiles for the jobs you want most instead of a list of 40 links.
> Link each tile to its own service page.

6. Behind the scenes
> Submit a test form once a month and make sure it lands in your CRM.
> Text every form lead back within a minute, then call.
> Put a tracking number on the site and on each Google profile, so you can see your conversion rate by source.
> Make sure the site loads fast on a phone, because that's where most of your visitors are.

7. Ads get their own pages
Send paid clicks to a landing page built for that one service, with one action, not to your homepage.

One window replacement company we work with went from 1.2% to 4.8% after we rebuilt its site.
