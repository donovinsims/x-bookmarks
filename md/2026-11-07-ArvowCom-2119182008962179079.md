# How to Stop Your Google Business Profile Looking Like a Ghost (5 Parts): "Proof You're Alive"

## Post

Carvalho opens with a simple question: have you been taking good care of your Google Business Profile?

He says local businesses are getting hundreds of calls a week from their profile, and that is how they win new customers.

So he opens Google Maps in Dallas, one of the most competitive cities he could pick, searches for a plumber, and takes apart the profile ranking first:

Real photos of the business, updated all the time

The right category and the same name, address and phone everywhere

Profile posts, the feature almost nobody uses

Reviews, with a reply to every single one, even the one-star ones

Then the part he automates with AI: the replies and the posts

Here is the whole breakdown I pulled from the recording, screen by screen.

Carvalho's warning in one picture: stop posting and replying, and Google can't tell you apart from a business that closed.

He is clear that he is not affiliated with the plumber. He picked the listing only because it ranks first.

1. Show real photos, and keep adding them

The first thing you see on a profile is the images, and this one is full of real ones.

Their truck and their store

A customer testimonial and a video

The team, which Carvalho says passes a lot of authority and credibility

Their slogan and how long they have been around

Technicians on real jobs and finished services

He guesses they hired a photographer. The point is that these are clearly their own business, not stock photos.

If my profile only had a logo and two old photos, this is where I would start, because it is the first thing a searcher sees.

And they keep adding new ones. That leads to his main idea.

2. Google needs proof your business is alive

Carvalho says Google treats Google Business Profiles as living entities.

It needs constant reassurance, proof that your business is alive, active and working every day.

His reason: plenty of listings belong to businesses that closed, or whose founder died, and they just sit there. He calls them ghost profiles.

In his view, regularly updating and posting to your profile is one of the most important ranking factors, because it is how Google sees you are not one of those ghosts.

3. Get the category and your NAP right

Under the name and the reviews sit the categories. This plumber only uses plumber, but Carvalho notes you can add more, like contractor or a service type.

Then the NAP: name, address and phone number.

His rule is that it must be identical everywhere:

Your website

Your Google Business Profile

Yelp

Your local chamber of commerce and any other directory

If any of them differ, you send Google mixed signals.

4. Use the posts section

Carvalho calls posts one of the most neglected parts of a profile, because almost no local business owner posts there.

The top plumber posts about the business constantly. There are three post types:

Updates, which work a lot like a short blog post

Events, with a start and end date

Offers, for a sale or a coupon

He says Google values these a lot, and that this is one of the biggest differences between this profile and the ones at the bottom of the results.

I would start with updates. They are the easiest of the three to keep going every week.

5. Ask for reviews, and reply to every one

Reviews are the other big factor. If you are not asking past and current customers for reviews, Carvalho says start now: by email, by phone or on the spot while you do the job.

His view is that reviews compound, and that is what makes a profile rank over the medium to long term.

The top Dallas plumber's review summary: 4.9 stars from 1,932 reviews.

Then the part most businesses skip. Many have plenty of reviews but never reply, or ignore the one-star ones.

This plumber replies to every review. Carvalho sorts by lowest rating to prove it, and even the one-star reviews have an answer.

A one-star review on the same profile, with an owner reply that apologises and invites a call (phone number blurred).

He says this is the reason the plumber ranks so high. That is his read, not something the video tests, but replying to every review is a habit any business can copy.

I also like the tone of that reply: it apologises and offers a call instead of arguing.

6. Automate the review replies in Arvow

If you do not have time to answer reviews, Carvalho moves to Arvow, which he describes as an SEO platform for local business owners.

Step one is connecting your Google Business Profile, which is a single click to link your Google account.

That opens two features: review automation and profile posts.

On the reviews screen you see every review, your average rating, the number of reviews, how many need a reply and your response rate. You can write a reply by hand or have AI draft one.

To take yourself out of it, you turn on review automation. Then you pick a reply style:

Personalized AI replies, where the AI reads each review and writes an answer

Saved templates, where you write the reply once per rating, personalized with the customer's first name

Under that, you set a rule for each star rating.

Rules by rating: each star level can publish automatically, wait as a draft for approval, or get no reply.

The screen also shows the cost: each personalized AI reply costs 4 credits, and saved templates are free.

Carvalho's setup: personalized AI replies for five, four and three stars, and do not reply for one and two stars, so he handles those himself.

If you do not have time for those either, he says the AI stays polite, because you control the tone, reply length, language, reply delay and custom guidance for your brand voice.

My own starting point would be draft for approval on every rating until the replies sound right.

7. Automate the profile posts

The second feature is profile posts.

For a single post, you tell the AI what to focus on, leave the AI image switched on, and pick the post type. Events and offers need a few manual inputs like dates.

For an update, you only choose the button:

Call now, which uses the phone number on your profile

Learn more, which points to your website or booking page

Then the automation. You give it a name and pick a mode: review mode sends posts to your profile as drafts to approve, and full automation publishes them live.

Next you add context from your knowledge base and brand brief, so the AI knows your services and schedule, plus optional content guidance.

The schedule step: post every day, every 2 days, every 3 days or every week, with a default button and an AI image.

Carvalho recommends every 2 days, with call now or learn more as the default button, and images left on because they matter for these posts.

The same screen shows 5 credits per generated post: 4 for the post and 1 for the AI image.

8. What stays manual

Carvalho's claim is that this automates about 75% of your profile.

What is left is adding your own real photos and setting up your NAP. That lines up with parts 1 and 3, and I would not skip them.

Where to start

If I were starting this week, I would check my name, address and phone on the site, the profile and the top directories, reply to every unanswered review, then switch on review automation and a profile post every 2 days: see Arvow pricing.

Start the post automation in review mode so you can read the first few posts before they go live.

What I would not conclude

The description says this plumber gets hundreds of calls every week from his profile. The video never shows his call numbers, so I am not claiming that.

The thumbnail's 1.4-star profile with seven reviews is an illustration, not a real listing from the video.

Carvalho's 75% figure and his view that replies and posts are the reason the plumber ranks first are his opinions. The video shows the profile and the tools, not a before-and-after test.

Recap

Show real, recent photos of your business

Give Google steady proof you are alive with regular updates and posts

Keep the same name, address and phone everywhere

Ask every customer for a review and reply to all of them, even one-star ones

Automate the replies and posts, then add your own photos by hand
