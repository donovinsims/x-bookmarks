# Private equity owns a lot of the biggest names in home services now.

## Post

Private equity owns a lot of the biggest names in home services now.

If you run a $10M shop, there's a good chance one of these is in your market:

> Wrench Group (Leonard Green): Parker & Sons, Coolray, Morris-Jenkins
> Apex Service Partners (Alpine, with Apollo buying in): Frank Gay Services, Best Home Services
> Authority Brands (Apax): One Hour Heating & Air, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, Mister Sparky
> KKR: Neighborly (Mr. Rooter, Aire Serv, Mr. Electric), and soon A1 Garage Door, which it's reportedly buying for about $2B
> Champions Group (Blackstone agreed to buy it this year): Service Champions, Bell Brothers
> Any Hour Group (Knox Lane): Any Hour Services
> Master Trades Group (L Catterton): Len The Plumber

On sales calls, I hear the same worry all the time. A PE-backed company moves into the market, and the owner assumes they'll win on marketing because they have the budget.

They'll probably win on Google Ads. Ads are an auction, and the bigger budget can keep bidding.

You beat them with SEO. The organic map results aren't an auction. Google ranks profiles on how close they are to the searcher, how well they match the search, and how trusted they are, and fresh reviews are a big part of trust. A bigger budget can't move their office closer to your customers.

Their Google setups are also a lot more uneven than the budget suggests:

> A1 runs a real review machine: 3,806 new Google reviews in 30 days, spread across 111 profiles
> Parker & Sons added 11 on its main Phoenix profile in 30 days, and ranked outside the top 20 on 78% of the Phoenix grid for "ac repair near me"
> One Hour Heating & Air in Charlotte lists 45+ towns on its site and has 2 physical locations to rank from

How I'd beat the one in your market:

> Run a map scan from your best neighborhoods and find where their pins fall out of the top 3
> Put a real office with its own profile closer to those neighborhoods than their nearest one
> Out-review them there. Count the new reviews on their closest profile over the last 30 days, then beat that number every month.
> Build a page for every service and every town they don't cover well
> Keep your name, address, and phone number identical on your site, your profiles, and every listing. Several of the big brands we've audited don't.
