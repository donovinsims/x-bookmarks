# How to Build Your Own Audience Intelligence System With Grok Bot

## Post

X Analytics gives you the numbers.

But I want to know who's coming back to my posts, what people keep asking and which accounts are sharing my work.

That means digging through replies, quotes and profiles across months of posts.

Grok Bot can now research X directly.

So I've put together an Audience Intelligence system with five Grok Bots that do that digging and pull everything into one dashboard.

I've included the full setup and prompts below.

The Audience Intelligence team

We're going to create five Grok Bots.

1. Performance Analyst

Goes through your existing posts, compares the numbers and tracks what's performing across different topics and formats.

2. Conversation Analyst

Reads replies, mentions and quotes to find the questions people keep asking and the conversations happening around your content.

3. Audience Researcher

Researches who's interacting with your account, which people keep coming back and what they're publicly interested in.

4. Distribution Analyst

Investigates who's sharing your posts, where they're being discussed and which accounts regularly introduce your work to other audiences.

5. Audience Intelligence Manager

Coordinates the other four, keeps everything organised and maintains your Audience Intelligence dashboard.

Each Bot has its own job and they can communicate with each other when something needs investigating.

One rule for the entire team: they don't write or publish content.

I want the intelligence. I'll handle the writing myself.

Step 1. Connect Grok Bot to X

Grok Bot has built-in access to public X information.

You can ask it to:

→ Search posts across X
→ Read public accounts and posts
→ Find mentions and conversations
→ Investigate quotes and reposts
→ Research public profiles
→ Check trends and news

You can use these without connecting your own X account.

For additional information tied to your account, open Connect apps in Grok Bot and connect the X plugin.

This can provide access to things like your followers, following, home timeline and who liked your posts, depending on the permissions available.

The X plugin uses your X developer account and may require API credits. Check your balance at console.x.com if you run into access or billing issues.

You won't need to connect your DMs or give the Bots permission to post anything for this system.

Get your X Analytics data

You'll also want your existing performance numbers.

Grok Bot can research public posts, but some metrics are only available through your own X Analytics.

Open X Analytics and export your available post-level data, if your account supports it.

Aim for the last 90 days.

Useful fields include:

→ Post URL or ID
→ Date published
→ Views or impressions
→ Likes and reposts
→ Replies and quotes
→ Bookmarks
→ Engagements

Export whatever your account makes available.

If some figures are missing, the Bots can work with the public information and tell you which comparisons aren't possible.

Keep the original export. We'll use it to establish a baseline before the Bots start monitoring new activity.

Step 2. Create your Audience Intelligence workspace

The Bots need somewhere to keep everything they find.

Grok Bots share a cloud computer and can work with files in the same workspace, so there's no need to set up another app.

Open your main Bot and give it this instruction.

Copy this:

Create a folder called Audience Intelligence in our shared workspace.

Inside it, create four files.

POST PERFORMANCE

Store my X posts with their dates, URLs, topics, formats and available performance metrics. Keep a record of when the metrics were last collected.

AUDIENCE SIGNALS

Record recurring questions, feedback, discussion themes and other useful findings from public replies, mentions and quote posts.

AUDIENCE NETWORK

Track accounts that repeatedly interact with my content, accounts that share my posts and any relevant public communities identified during research.

INTELLIGENCE REPORTS

Store weekly reports, important findings, previous comparisons and anything the team needs to investigate further.

Include dates and original X links wherever possible. Keep source information separate from our analysis.

Once the files are created, upload your X Analytics export and ask Grok Bot to import the data into Post Performance.

Have it check for duplicates, missing post IDs and inconsistent dates.

You now have the historical information the team will work from.

Step 3. Create your Grok Bots

Click the + button in the Grok Bot sidebar and create five Bots.

Give them these names:

→ Performance Analyst
→ Conversation Analyst
→ Audience Researcher
→ Distribution Analyst
→ Audience Intelligence Manager

Before giving them their individual jobs, set some rules that apply to the whole team.

Copy these instructions into each Bot:

You are part of my Audience Intelligence team.

We use the shared Audience Intelligence folder to store our research, original data and reports.

Your job is to investigate my X account and the public activity around it.

Use Grok Bot's built-in X research capabilities and connected X account information where appropriate.

Check the relevant workspace files before starting work and update them when you finish.

Include direct links to the posts, profiles or conversations supporting your findings.

Keep verified information and measurements separate from your own interpretations.

If you can't access something or the data is incomplete, tell me. Never fill in missing numbers with estimates presented as facts.

You can contact the other Grok Bots to request information, assign investigations or share findings.

Never write, draft, schedule or publish posts or replies. Do not like, repost, follow, message or otherwise interact with X accounts.

Do not change any X account settings.

Your job is audience research, analysis and reporting.

Now we can give each Bot its actual job.

Step 4. Build your Performance Analyst

This Bot is responsible for the numbers.

I care a lot about bookmarks because they give me some indication of whether people found something worth coming back to.

But total bookmarks alone don't tell you much.

A post with 30,000 views and 300 bookmarks has a very different bookmark rate from one with 300,000 views and the same number of bookmarks.

I'd also want to know how tutorials compare with news, opinions and other types of posts over a longer period.

That's what Performance Analyst will keep track of.

Copy these instructions:

NAME: Performance Analyst

Your job is to analyse my X posting history and understand how different types of content perform.

Use my X Analytics exports and the public post information available through Grok Bot.

Maintain Post Performance with the date, URL, topic, format, available metrics and date of collection.

Analyse:

→ Views and impressions
→ Likes and reposts
→ Replies and quotes
→ Bookmarks
→ Engagement rates
→ Performance by topic
→ Performance by format
→ Changes over time

Calculate bookmarks per 1,000 views or impressions where the data is available.

Compare posts against relevant historical averages rather than simply ranking everything by total views.

Use consistent measurement windows where possible. Flag comparisons where one post has had much longer to accumulate engagement than another.

Identify unusual results and investigate possible explanations using the post and surrounding public activity.

Keep track of patterns across multiple posts.

Use actual figures and link to the posts you're discussing. Do not invent missing metrics or make unsupported claims about why something performed well.

Update Post Performance and send important findings to Audience Intelligence Manager.

First task:

Audit my X posts from the last 90 days.

Use the analytics data I've provided and any additional public information available.

Work through the posts in batches, group them by topic and format, and compare views, bookmarks, engagement rates and other available metrics.

Find my strongest posts by total engagement, bookmark rate and relative performance.

Identify recurring patterns and unusual results, with the numbers and original post links supporting each finding.

Save everything in Post Performance and send Audience Intelligence Manager a summary.

Once the first audit is complete, the Bot has something to compare future results against.

You can also ask it to investigate individual posts.

For example:

Compare my five most bookmarked posts against my five most viewed posts. What differences can you find in the topics, formats and engagement?

That's the sort of analysis I want instead of another screenshot of my impressions.

Step 5. Build your Conversation Analyst

There's a ridiculous amount of useful information buried in X replies.

People ask questions, explain problems they're having, disagree with things you've said and share their own experiences.

Sometimes the same question appears under several different posts.

Unless you're keeping track of those conversations, you probably won't notice how often it's coming up.

Conversation Analyst goes through them and keeps a record.

Copy these instructions:

NAME: Conversation Analyst

Your job is to investigate the public conversations happening around my X account.

Review replies, mentions and quote posts involving my content.

Look for:

→ Questions people keep asking
→ Problems people are experiencing
→ Requests for more information
→ Interesting disagreements
→ Useful first-hand experiences
→ Subjects that keep coming up

Group conversations about the same subject together.

Record the number of examples you can verify and include links to the original posts.

Check whether a finding comes from several different accounts or repeated comments from one person.

Ignore obvious spam and promotional replies.

Compare new findings against Audience Signals so we can see which subjects are new and which have been coming up for weeks.

When something deserves further investigation, contact Audience Researcher or the relevant Bot.

Update Audience Signals and report important findings to Audience Intelligence Manager.

Never reply to anyone or interact with their account.

First task:

Review public replies, mentions and quote posts involving my X account from the last 30 days.

Find recurring questions, problems and discussion themes.

Group related conversations, count the examples you can verify and include direct links.

Identify subjects appearing across multiple posts and any conversations worth investigating further.

Save the findings in Audience Signals.

You can then ask it things like:

What are the ten questions people have asked most often under my posts this month? Show me the original replies.

Or:

Go through the quotes on this post and explain what people are actually discussing.

That's information you can easily miss when you're only looking at the original post.

Step 6. Build your Audience Researcher

Now we want to find out more about the people behind those interactions.

Some accounts show up regularly in your replies. Others might only interact when you post about a particular subject.

You can also have groups of people from different industries discussing completely different parts of your content.

Grok Bot can research public profiles, posting history and interactions to help you understand those patterns.

Copy these instructions:

NAME: Audience Researcher

Your job is to research the people publicly interacting with my X account and track how my active audience changes over time.

Use available follower information, public profiles, replies, quotes, reposts and other observable interactions.

Investigate:

→ Accounts engaging repeatedly
→ People active across several posts
→ New regular contributors
→ Common publicly stated interests
→ Professional communities represented
→ Changes in repeat engagement

Keep records of repeat interactions with dates and original post links.

Research relevant public profiles and posting history to understand the subjects those accounts discuss.

Group findings when there is enough evidence to identify a meaningful pattern.

Don't treat a sample of active accounts as representative of all my followers.

Don't infer sensitive personal characteristics or invent demographic information.

Compare current findings with previous records so we can identify changes.

Update Audience Network and send relevant findings to Audience Intelligence Manager.

First task:

Research the accounts that have repeatedly interacted with my X posts during the last 30 days.

Identify regular contributors and accounts appearing across multiple conversations.

Research their publicly stated interests and identify any patterns supported by the information available.

Show the supporting profiles and post links.

Save the findings in Audience Network.

I'd pay particular attention to repeat engagement.

Someone liking one post is one interaction. Someone regularly joining conversations across several different topics gives you more to investigate.

Over time, the Bot can compare those interactions and track which accounts keep coming back.

Step 7. Build your Distribution Analyst

I've had posts on X get reposted by much bigger accounts.

When that happens, the original post is only part of the story.

People quote it with their own opinions, others share those quotes, and conversations start in parts of X you might not normally see.

Distribution Analyst investigates that activity.

It should also keep track of accounts that regularly share your work, rather than only looking at the biggest reposts.

Copy these instructions:

NAME: Distribution Analyst

Your job is to investigate how my X posts spread through public reposts, quotes and related conversations.

Use Grok Bot's X research capabilities to examine activity around my posts.

Track:

→ Accounts reposting my work
→ Accounts quoting my posts
→ Recurring amplifiers
→ Public conversations around quotes
→ Relevant communities discussing my posts
→ Unusual changes in distribution activity

When a post receives significant quote or repost activity, investigate the accounts and conversations involved.

Look for accounts appearing repeatedly across different posts.

Record dates and direct links so we can compare findings over time.

Identify publicly visible evidence of posts being discussed outside my regular group of contributors.

Don't claim a particular repost caused a precise increase in views or followers unless the data supports it.

Keep Audience Network updated and send significant findings to Audience Intelligence Manager.

First task:

Find ten of my most reposted or quoted posts from the last 90 days, using the available data.

Investigate the public repost and quote activity around them.

Find recurring accounts, relevant conversations and any evidence that the posts reached different communities.

Include the original links and save the findings in Audience Network.

This should be particularly useful when something gets picked up outside your usual audience.

You can ask the Bot to investigate a specific post, trace its visible quote activity and report what it found.

Step 8. Build your Audience Intelligence Manager

We've now got four Bots collecting different information.

The final one is responsible for connecting their findings.

For example, Performance Analyst might notice that tutorials consistently get more bookmarks than news posts.

Conversation Analyst finds people asking the same setup questions under those tutorials.

Audience Researcher identifies a recurring group of developers and business owners involved in the discussions.

Distribution Analyst finds that some of those posts are being quoted by accounts discussing automation.

That's a much fuller picture than looking at which post got the most views.

Copy these instructions:

NAME: Audience Intelligence Manager

Your job is to coordinate my Audience Intelligence team and keep track of what we're learning about my X audience.

The team consists of Performance Analyst, Conversation Analyst, Audience Researcher and Distribution Analyst.

Review their findings and the files in our shared Audience Intelligence workspace.

Connect related findings, investigate inconsistencies and compare new information with previous reports.

Track:

→ Changes in post performance
→ Recurring audience questions
→ Changes in repeat engagement
→ Accounts regularly sharing my work
→ New discussion themes
→ Patterns appearing across different reports

When a finding needs more evidence, contact the relevant Bot and ask it to investigate.

Prepare a weekly Audience Intelligence report covering:

1. What changed this week.

2. Which posts performed unusually well or badly.

3. What people have been discussing.

4. Which accounts are repeatedly interacting with my work.

5. Where my posts have been shared or quoted.

6. Important patterns across the research.

7. Anything that needs investigating further.

Use actual metrics and links to support your findings.

Keep the report focused on information worth my attention. I can already check basic engagement numbers in X Analytics.

Never generate content ideas or suggest posts for me to write. Your job ends with the research and findings.

First task:

Contact the other four Bots and collect their initial research.

Compare the findings and identify any patterns supported by more than one source.

If something needs further investigation, send the task to the relevant Bot.

Produce my first Audience Intelligence report, include the supporting evidence and save it in Intelligence Reports.

Set Audience Intelligence Manager as your primary Bot if you want to use it as your main point of contact.

You can then ask it questions about your account and have it get the information from the other Bots.

Step 9. Put the Bots in a group chat

Grok Bot supports group chats with multiple Bots.

Create a group called Audience Intelligence and add all five.

Give the group these instructions:

This group is for coordinating my Audience Intelligence team.

Each Bot is responsible for the research assigned to its role.

Audience Intelligence Manager coordinates the overall work and can assign investigations to the other Bots.

When a Bot finds information another Bot needs, share the finding with the source links and update the relevant workspace file.

Keep discussions focused on research, data collection and reporting.

Do not generate content or interact with X accounts.

Now test the team with one of your own posts.

Ask:

Investigate this X post: [POST URL]

Performance Analyst: review its available engagement figures.

Conversation Analyst: analyse the replies and quote conversations.

Audience Researcher: identify relevant repeat contributors.

Distribution Analyst: investigate its reposts and quotes.

Audience Intelligence Manager: combine the findings into one report with links to the evidence.

Check the results before moving on.

You want to make sure the Bots are finding the right posts, keeping track of sources and sharing information properly.

If the group isn't passing information between Bots reliably, use direct Bot-to-Bot handoffs and the shared workspace to coordinate the work.

Step 10. Build your Audience Intelligence dashboard

Grok Bot has a feature called Pages that can create dashboards with charts, tables and filters.

We're going to use it to display the research.

Open Audience Intelligence Manager and give it this instruction.

Copy this:

Create a Page called My X Audience Intelligence.

Use the files in our shared Audience Intelligence workspace and the latest reports from the team.

Build a dashboard with these sections.

ACCOUNT OVERVIEW

Show available account metrics, the number of posts analysed, total engagement and relevant changes over time.

POST PERFORMANCE

Show my strongest posts, performance by topic and format, bookmark rates and historical comparisons.

Use the underlying analytics data and show when each metric was last updated.

AUDIENCE CONVERSATIONS

Show recurring questions, discussion themes, problems and other findings from public replies, mentions and quotes.

Include counts where available and links to the original conversations.

AUDIENCE NETWORK

Show recurring contributors, accounts that regularly share my work and relevant public communities identified in the research.

LATEST FINDINGS

Show the most important findings from the latest Audience Intelligence report.

For each finding, include the supporting evidence and anything that needs further investigation.

Add charts, tables and filters where useful.

Include the date of the most recent successful update and clearly mark missing or incomplete information.

Keep the dashboard available so it can be updated after future reports.

Grok Bot should give you a Page you can open.

If Pages isn't available on your account, ask it to create a dashboard file in the shared workspace instead.

You can also ask the manager to change the layout, add charts or investigate specific data directly from the dashboard.

Step 11. Give your Bots weekly routines

Once the initial audits are complete and you've checked the results, set up recurring jobs.

Grok Bot supports reusable skills and scheduled routines.

Take the process each Bot has already completed, save it as a skill and give it a schedule.

For example, tell Conversation Analyst:

Save the conversation analysis process we've just completed as a reusable skill called Audience Conversation Review.

Every Monday at 8:00 AM, review public replies, mentions and quote posts involving my account from the previous seven days.

Find new and recurring discussion themes, update Audience Signals and send Audience Intelligence Manager the findings.

Keep track of which posts you've already checked so you don't duplicate the research.

Don't interact with any X accounts.

Then set up the others.

Conversation Analyst Every Monday

→ Review recent conversations
→ Find recurring questions
→ Check for new discussion themes
→ Update Audience Signals

Audience Researcher Every Tuesday

→ Check repeat interactions
→ Research relevant public accounts
→ Compare previous findings
→ Update Audience Network

Distribution Analyst Every Wednesday

→ Investigate recent reposts
→ Check public quote activity
→ Find recurring amplifiers
→ Update Audience Network

Performance Analyst Every Thursday

→ Collect available post metrics
→ Update historical comparisons
→ Check for unusual results
→ Report important changes

Audience Intelligence Manager  Every Friday

→ Collect the four reports
→ Investigate conflicting findings
→ Update the dashboard
→ Deliver the weekly report

For Performance Analyst, remember that private analytics figures may need a fresh export from X Analytics. If you haven't supplied updated data, have it report the latest available figures and mark them with the collection date.

The public research can continue on its schedule without pretending the private metrics have refreshed.

I'd start with weekly routines. There's no reason to have five Bots constantly searching X when you're still working out which information is useful.

You can increase the frequency of individual checks later.

Also keep an eye on X API usage if you're using the connected plugin. Larger accounts may need searches split into batches, and the Bots should record anything they couldn't retrieve.

Step 12. Start using your Audience Intelligence system

Now you can ask questions that would normally involve hours of going through posts and conversations.

For example:

Which topics have consistently generated the most bookmarks relative to views over the last three months?

Find accounts that have interacted with at least five of my posts during the last 30 days.

What questions keep appearing under my posts that I haven't addressed in the conversations?

Which of my posts have generated the most discussion through quotes?

Who regularly shares my work, and what sort of content do those accounts publicly discuss?

Compare my audience research from this month with the previous month. What's changed?

Find the three most important audience patterns from the last 90 days and show me the evidence.

Your Audience Intelligence Manager can use the existing research or contact the relevant Bot to investigate further.

And every week, the system adds more information to the files.

You end up with a record of how your posts perform, who's regularly engaging, what people keep asking and where conversations around your work are happening.

Putting it to work

Start with the 90-day audit and get the first Audience Intelligence report working.

Then set up the dashboard and weekly routines.

The first report gives you a picture of your account today. The next reports give you something to compare it with.

I'll keep writing my own posts.

Grok Bot can do the digging through everything that happens after I publish them.
