What Project Setup Looks Like 

For my first projects, I just started prompting. No folders, no instructions. It worked, but every new chat started from zero and way more tokens had to be used. It was productively inefficient.

Now every project starts with: 

A folder on my laptop, inside one main Developer folder

A project in Codex or Claude pointed at that folder alongside my Vault folders

Instruction files, before any code (written by the frontier model) 

Plan mode and research mode first in the most elite model, then building with Opus 5.5 or Sol 6.1 

 

The instruction files are where the compounding happens. Every project has a few short ones: what the product is or will be, what the design rules are, and how I like to work. Because every agent has my vault, it knows my identity, projects, people, decisions, sources and skills. 

My /write-like-emily writing skill was built from my full X archive (20,724 tweets since 2018) and gets updated every time I correct it. My design skills are built off a combination of Impeccable, UX/UI design skills I found on X, books, and rules I've reinforced as I built. 


Talk to Prompt is MUCH Faster 

Almost every prompt I send starts as me talking. I click the mic in the chat and ramble. Talking is much faster and allows me to critique the output while scrolling through it. It's also great while walking and prompting from my phone.

I have an idea for another side project I want to build called How much is a trillion? And I want this to be like a visual website where it shows like a stack of a hundred dollar bills. Or iPhones or apples. And how much a trillion of these would fill, whether it's like a football stadium or help me plan this out. But I want it to be highly visual, very snappy, high performance and like a 3D visual, so extremely well rendered. And I want this to be for the launch of Anthropic's IPO. Help me plan anything out and help me come up with the folders.

Can't Rush Craft: Creative Execution Matters the Most 

Getting a working version back is fast. The first build of TRILLIONS came back 25 minutes after my first prompt, and the first version of Follow the Money was ready about 30 minutes after my planning brief. 

A majority of my time is spent on creative direction, then on UX/UI across mobile + web. That is where most of the build goes. It's also the most important. This is what separates projects that get earned distribution vs. those that don't. 

The more I built, the more I noticed AI has defaults. The same few fonts (Geist), the same safe layouts, the same sounds in motion video. If you take the first output, your site will look like everyone else's first output. In a world where code becomes easier to deploy, this perpetuates the cycle of sameness. I didn't want any of my work to look like AI slop, so with every project I pushed for a new creative direction, intentionally avoided the defaults, and tried to stretch my own creative range. 

Where I Find Creative References

In all my tests, I've found AI is quite weak at creative direction and execution. It's strong at pattern matching when you feed it references and clear instructions. That means knowing where to source references and using your own human creativity alongside imagination becomes key. For me, these come from Cosmos, Instagram, traveling, art museums, and walking around outside. I've noticed I'm way more open to type, color, and design out in the streets now. 

 

I often reject the first few rounds of whatever AI gives me. Sometimes I don't know what I want until I see something that reminds me of it, so I might use AI to help me brainstorm. I'm also not a designer in practice, so I'm learning the principles at the same time I'm applying them. Similar to my /write-like-emily skill, I used some branding and design books to help build a .md skill for my agents.

 

What My Creative Process Looked Like

Human creativity persists, and I find myself using AI as a creative brainstorming mirror, but often heavily steering it. AI is incredible at executing vision, but it's rarely good at imagining (at least for now).

Say No to AI Slop

A lot of my skepticism with AI output comes from the marketing output I've seen, which can be generic, miss opportunities, and isn't always the direction I'd take. Marketing is something I have domain expertise in, so this created uncertainty for every other form of output. I'm not a designer or developer. So I asked designers. One of them taught me about grid sets, and now I check every layout against one.

I also give the agents design skills: Impeccable for spacing, margins and type, Remotion for video, and some other UX skills. I use agents to scan GitHub repos for the skill quality, and cross check if it is something I need. 

 

I also can be paranoid and ask agents to spawn subagents to review it's code and work. There have been a few times it's caught it's own mistakes. Or I've had to play with side projects on web and mobile and QA everything. 

TRILLIONS: "$1000 looks like $100." The $100 bills at one point looked bigger than the taxi. The shipping container came out 40 feet long when I'd asked for a standard 20 foot one.

sponsor me index: audits found campaigns showing $0 raised when the amount wasn't known, and creators' own bids counted as money raised

Follow the Money: it nailed the vertical scroll, but did it before it fully understood how card payments work. AI had a ton of documentation to piece it together, but I had to speak to fintech leaders and experts to get it accurate.

Building furniture: my current side project is using AI to build CAD files that will get turned into furniture. It's made mistakes on sharp edges, weight projections, and sturdiness.

 

AI sounds confident but I've found it important to do deep evals and checks. Humans with direct domain expertise are the best source. Once I wrap up a project, I do a retro and have the agent reflect back my preferences, ways of working, and push it back into my /vault so future agents can learn more quickly. 


Distribution Distribution Distribution 

Building my X presence about three years ago was one of the best career and personal decisions I've made. 

I first started posting about marketing insights, wins, and learnings; that resulted in new connections, work opportunities, direct monetization (total of $796 from X at time of writing this), and reach. I owe a lot of the big traffic spikes in each of these side projects to having an established X account. However, it's important to note: 

The algorithm can change at any moment (and it has) so it becomes a single point of dependency

You don't need to have an existing following here to go viral

Organic attention and traffic from one post on social media is not sustained

Attention is increasingly more scattered and scarce, and split across multiple channels

Every project has done the same thing: one big spike, then flat or smaller spikes if I continually double click. TRILLIONS got 24,980 page views on Nov 8. That's 92% of all its views, in one day. The launch post did 250k views on X. Honestly, this post felt like a shift in the algorithm because the views shot up 12 hours after posting, then died right after. 

There was also a recurrent pattern in type of posts that would get the most reach. No image, no video. Just text and the link. I honestly found this strange given X is incentivizing for video, but none of my videos have done as well.

Growth Requires Consistent Experimenting

Human psychology (desire, motivations, needs) stays constant but the internet will continue to change. It will change even faster as AI iterates more quickly. This does affect human behavior. I saw this firsthand last month when visiting China and realizing SEO isn't even a necessary growth tactic there. They don't search the internet with website links like we do in the west. Most of the screen time of business discovery and inquiry is via miniapps within super apps.  

Even my own AI usage has changed in two months. I used Tab more than Instinct because it could make phone calls, up until Grok Bot could do that too. I use personal agents sometimes to help with purchasing decisions. I get a daily news and work brief from my agents which is time less spent on a social network screen.

 

As the internet continues to change because of AI and technology, the more growth experimentation will need to occur too. This iterative testing mindset came from my career in performance marketing. At the third agency I worked at prior to working in crypto full time, every media buyer aimed for the revenue share rather than the base salary. Media buyers were incentivized to increase ad spend as much as possible, because that meant more revenue for the agency, and a % of that went directly into our paycheck. The only way to increase ad spend was to maintain CACs/CPAs while scaling which requires rigorous creative, audience, and channel testing. 

This performance based compensation is similar to what you see with today's clippers and UGC creators. They constantly iterate and find new arbitrage edges to gain as many views as possible to earn more.

What This All Means For Marketers, Builders, and Founders

This is a special moment in time where many people are using AI, but fewer are using the frontier models, and even fewer are using it in harnesses and workflows that truly make their work and lives better. 

Code is solved for. Making websites and apps is already easy and will become even easier. The hypersaturation of new content combined with increased output correlates with more personal brands being built. "Build your own distribution" is a consensus take, but it's absolutely wise to build your own distribution in a time like this. The catch is, quality attention becomes even more expensive and difficult to sustain as building with AI gets cheaper.

The brands and founders set out on sustaining attention are: 

Owning multiple digital media surfaces ("new media," news social pages, driving web traffic to native app downloads) 

Hiring or working with people that have their own built online presence

Focusing on IRL, in-person attention (events, cafes, dinners), and out of home 

Multi variant testing at every stage of a user's life cycle

Creating organizational context and design systems that can be scaled with AI 

Hiring ambitious young talent to experiment with neodistribution tactics: building out UGC and clipping armies, focusing on cultural news pages, helping with paid media. This is the evolution of what was once ecommerce dropshippers and previously affiliate marketers.

The best marketers I know are building out their own distribution whether that is their own personal brands to building boutique influencer networks to selling digital space on their physical phone farms, or all of the above.

AI will continue to proliferate. We are in that cusp of time where I know the technology will change user behavior, but I'm just not quite sure how, yet. The way I get through it is staying curious, using it, experimenting, and deeply, deeply caring about the craft.
