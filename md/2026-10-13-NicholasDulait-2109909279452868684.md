# Claude is killing your SEO (and you don't even know it)

## Post

Everyone is doing SEO with AI now.

Most of them are making mistakes that did not exist two years ago.

I'm about to share 5 of the biggest mistakes beginners are doing with Claude.

AI made SEO easy.

That also made the mistakes easy.

When pages were written by hand, a bad idea cost you a week. Now it costs you one prompt, repeated across the whole site.

-> If the foundation is wrong, automation just ships the garbage faster.

1. The rat mindset only works in rat markets

You will not trick Google with a homemade process. Not on keywords that matter.

Paul calls it the rat mindset. Build something cheap, publish it fast, hope it gets indexed.

It does work, in one place: markets where Google expects very little.

Countries with almost no competition on the keyword

Niche keywords with business potential that nobody targets yet

Operators who live in black hat and know exactly which gaps are open

If you are one of those, fine. Most people reading this are not.

On a searched keyword in a developed market, Google has 10 serious candidates for the top spot. It compares them.

A page of meaningless text, no images, nothing useful on it, no brand behind it, is not winning that comparison.

Before you publish anything, ask two questions:

If you landed on this page from Google, would you actually read it?

Is it more useful than the 9 pages next to it?

If either answer is no, the problem is not your publishing speed.

2. Every hack gets patched. Use it, then get out.

Search engines have one goal: give the visitor the true answer so they come back.

Every tactic that bends the truth is on a timer. Some timers are just long.

Two of them ran out recently.

City pages

You are a plumber in one city. You publish a page for every town around it.

It is half true, so it took Google years to deal with.

Antoine's agency ranked city pages in Marseille, Bordeaux, Toulouse and Paris about three years ago. Then they cut back to Paris, Lille and Lyon. Today they keep one: Lille, where they actually are.

Paul's city pages brought him leads for three or four years. They still hold. They bring in far less than they used to.

Fill-in-the-blank city pages were not a mistake in 2023. They are one now.

Ranking yourself first

"The 10 best tools for X". You are number one. Your competitors are underneath.

Antoine ran this play and says the window was 6 months to a year. Then the curve turned.

The part nobody expects: LLMs read that page and cite every tool on it, except yours.

You do not say you are the best on your own site. Someone else has to say it.

The rule I take from both:

Exploit the window while it is open

Assume it closes in 6 to 12 months

When it closes, rewrite. Neutral rankings on your site, praise earned on other people's

3. Ten pages a day on a new site is the number one trap

AI lets you build a site in an afternoon and fill it by the evening.

That is exactly why fresh domains die now.

A new site has no trust yet. A wall of pages arriving at once looks like the thing Google's spam policies were written for.

In March 2024 Google added scaled content abuse to its spam policies. It applies no matter whether the content came from automation, humans, or a mix of both.

Volume is not the violation. Unoriginal content at volume is.

Paul's ceiling for a new site:

From day one: 3 or 4 pages a week, at most

Every page: one a person would actually read

Before scaling: wait until Google starts sending clicks on its own

His own content site took about a year to reach that point. Only then did he open the throttle.

We publish 2 articles a week on the ChatSEO blog. Not 10 a day.

4. Not being a brand is a ranking mistake now

Put yourself in Google's seat for a second.

Hundreds of thousands of new sites to sort every day. Which ones are real?

The cheapest signals to check come first. Is there a legal notice? An About page? A named author? That is a few lines of code to fetch, much cheaper than analysing a brand's social presence.

So the cheap signals are the floor. Not having them sorts you with the junk sites.

The on-page set, in order:

About page. Who you are, where you come from, the team.

Legal notice. Company name, address, contact.

Author box under every article, linking to the writer's real profiles.

Structured data. Organization on the site, Person on every author.

Paul's estimate: 15 to 20 minutes with an AI that has access to your site.

Paste this:

Audit my site for brand signals and fix what is missing.

1. Create or rewrite the About page: who we are, the team, our story.

Ask me for any fact you do not have. Never invent one.

2. Make sure a legal notice page exists and is linked in the footer.

3. Add an author box under every article: name, one-line bio, links

to their LinkedIn and X.

4. Add Organization schema sitewide and Person schema for each author.

5. List every page you changed.

Line 1 matters most. An About page with invented history is worse than none.

Social accounts and a community take months. Start them if the project is serious. The on-page floor takes an afternoon, so there is no excuse for skipping it.

5. Not plugging AI into your CMS is the new mistake

If your site is yours and the stakes are reasonable, an AI should already be editing it.

Remember the self-ranking fix from section 2?

Done by hand, that is an afternoon per listicle. Done by an AI connected to your CMS, it is one prompt:

Go through every listicle and comparison page on the site.

Wherever we rank ourselves first, rewrite the ranking so it is

neutral: order by the criteria stated on the page, and move us to

the position we would honestly earn.

Keep each page's structure. Show me the diff before publishing.

Then the real gain: you never explain it twice.

Save these rules for every new page we create:

- Add the author box and Person schema

- Link to 3 existing pages on the same topic, with descriptive anchors

- Never rank ourselves first in a list

That is the whole difference between a power user and everyone else. The process is written once.

This is not a contradiction of section 1.

Scaling garbage with AI is the mistake. Producing quality with AI in a tenth of the time is the opposite of it.

Where I am wrong, or at least not there yet

This piece is mostly Paul's and Antoine's experience, not a test. Two operators, a handful of sites, no control group.

The city page and self-ranking declines are real to them. Neither of them gave me a number, so I am not giving you one.

The exception in section 1 is real too. Keywords with business potential that nobody targets still exist. A mediocre page can rank there. It just does not make a strategy.

And the honest bit about tools.

Paul has tested the new automated SEO tools. The ones he tried make every mistake in this article: brand new sites, a flood of informational articles nobody reads, no author, no brand signals.

We want ChatSEO to run SEO on its own one day. We are not there. Building an SEO tool that is actually good takes years, and our to-do list is long.

What I would do on any site tomorrow

Run the two-question test on my last 10 pages

Find every hack I am still running and assume it dies within a year

Cap a new domain at 3 or 4 pages a week until Google sends clicks

Ship the About page, legal notice, author box and schema in one afternoon

Connect an AI to the CMS and save every rule I explain twice

In that order, because automating the last step before the first four just scales the mistakes.

Test. Retire. Slow down. Prove you exist. Automate.
