# I've spent the last 3 weeks tearing apart websites from One Hour, Roto-Rooter, and other massive home service brands.

## Post

I've spent the last 3 weeks tearing apart websites from One Hour, Roto-Rooter, and other massive home service brands.

This is the service-page structure worth copying.

(Bookmark this)

ABOVE THE FOLD

> The H1 names the exact service and market in plain English.

> A short subheadline explains the outcome, availability, and strongest trust point.

> One real job or team photo proves this is a physical company.

> The phone number is visible, clickable, and connected directly to the mobile dialer.

> A short primary CTA matches the visitor's intent: Call Now, Schedule Service, or Get an Estimate.

Directly below it, add a trust strip with the two to four details that carry the most weight: Google rating and review count, years in business, license information, warranty, financing, or a real guarantee.

Twenty generic badges look like decoration.

THE PROBLEM

Next, confirm that the homeowner landed in the right place.

For an AC repair page, cover the actual symptoms:

> Warm air, weak airflow, short cycling, leaks, frozen coils, and a system that won't turn on.

For a window replacement page, explain fogged glass, drafts, rot, broken seals, frame damage, and rising energy loss.

These sections match the language a homeowner uses before they know the technical diagnosis.

THE SERVICE

> What the technician inspects

> Which repairs or replacement options exist

> How scheduling and arrival work

> What happens after the estimate

> Warranties, permits, brands, and financing

Add starting prices when you can.

A diagnostic fee, minimum charge, rough price range, or financing example gives homeowners a useful answer before they call. Useful pages tend to rank and convert better than pages that dodge every money question.

PROOF

An AC repair page needs AC reviews and repair photos. A generic testimonial about friendly office staff provides less buying confidence than a review describing the exact job.

Use two to four service-specific reviews, real job photos, a short project example from the target market, and the warranty or certifications that actually apply.

Add a clear repair-versus-replace section for high-ticket work.

Tell the homeowner which conditions support a repair, when replacement becomes the better financial decision, and what the next diagnostic step looks like. This is where a strong page earns trust before the call.

LOCAL PROOF

Name the cities the team truly serves, show recent work from those areas, and link to the relevant location pages. Don't paste a list of 80 cities into the footer and call it local SEO.

FAQs should answer timing, service fees, warranties, financing, brands, emergency availability, and what the homeowner needs to do before arrival.

The final section repeats the action with fresh proof beside it. On mobile, keep a sticky Call Now or Schedule button within thumb reach.

TECHNICAL CHECKS

> A short readable URL

> One clear title and H1

> Self-referencing canonical

> Crawlable text instead of important copy trapped inside images

> Internal links from the homepage, service hub, related services, and relevant locations

> Call tracking, form events, and booked-job attribution

Open the finished page on a phone, tap the number, submit the form, and confirm both leads reached the CRM with the correct page attached.
