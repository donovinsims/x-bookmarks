# 23 Secrets of a $30M Influencer Marketing Program

## Post

I ran the day-to-day ops and marketing that took Cal AI from $0-$30M+ ARR in 18 months.
In that time, I personally evaluated 10,000+ creators while signing over 300 influencer partnerships that helped us scale profitably and eventually sell to MyFitnessPal.
If you’re trying to run an influencer marketing program to blow up your app, this is a must read.
 
1. The comment section tells you everything
Comment sections that convert have multiple threads of conversations and real responses.
Avoid the ones that are full of emojis and entertainment audience signals.
When the audience talks to each other, they buy.
Read the comments before any other metrics.

2. You pay for trust, not reach
UGC buys a swing at the algorithm.
Ads buy reach.
Influencers buy the trust they've built with their audience.
If you're not buying trust, run ads. They're cheaper.

3. Not all views are created equal
We paid a mukbang creator a fortune and got nothing back.
People who watch someone eat 10,000 calories don't want to count their own.
The views were real, but the audience was dead wrong.
Stick with creators in niches with high intent followings.

4. The biggest creators convert the worst
Past a certain fame, everyone knows it's a sponsored post.
You pay a premium for trust and get none of it.
The bigger the name, the weaker the conversion.

5. Followers mean nothing
I've seen millions of followers pull 10,000 views. I've seen 15,000 followers pull millions.
You want creators who are still relatable and talk to their audience like a friend.
Price on expected views, never on audience size.

6. The money is in the mid-tier creators
Too expensive for new companies, too small for whales.
Big brands can't measure attribution so they overpay for household names.
The smallest creators overprice because every brand messaging them is clueless about deal structure.
You get squeezed on both ends, so stick to the middle.

7. Set your break-even before you spend
Set a target cost per thousand views to stay profitable.
Measure every deal against it.
Know your number before you negotiate, otherwise you're gambling.

8. Pay flat fees, keep the upside
It’s industry standard, and if a video does ten million views, you owe nothing more.
Ads charge you for every view.
Keep your asymmetrical upside at all costs.

9. Seamless integration is the whole game
The second people know it's an ad, they scroll.
The product should slide into content the creator already makes.
Hard-sells always die.

10. The creator's own average is your scoreboard
Compare their video for you against their normal views.
Performed like the others? Integration worked.
Tanked? Your product stuck out.

11. Let the creator cook
They built an audience because they're good at content, so let them do their thing.
Trying to micro-manage or overscript videos for them is the biggest mistake you can make.

12. Function over form
Creators don't want a beautiful slide deck.
They want a Google Doc they can read in two minutes and execute on.
Spend your time elsewhere on revenue generating tasks.

13. Speed is the only moat
If you move faster than everyone else, you will most likely win.
If you think it will take a week, try to crank it out in a day.
Infuse speed into every aspect of your operation or get left behind.

14. Check the supply before you pick a niche
Are there enough creators here, and are they available to work with you?
If you find a niche with only a few hundred creators total, chances are they’re already working with your competitors.

15. One landmark deal makes every deal easier
Your first viral video pays off twice: it converts, and it makes every signing after it easier.
You're now the brand that top creators work with.

16. Reverse-engineer every hit
When a video overperforms, your job isn't to celebrate. It's to find out why and do it again.
Run that playbook and format for every creator partnership until it stops working.

17. When you're stuck, sign more creators
Nearly every growth problem has the same answer: more creators, and current ones posting more.
Not reporting, not branding, not strategy.
The lever is almost always distribution.

18. Volume is a system, not hustle
Build an automation machine: VA outreach, contracts, scheduling, tracking, all automated.
Want more creators? Build the system, don't add unnecessary hours.

19. Spend your hours where the views are
It’s the same effort to manage a creator pulling a million views as one pulling thirty thousand.
Sign and manage the creators with the most upside.

20. Stop chasing attribution you can't see
When scaling at first, you can match post times to download spikes.
But when doing real revenue, the spikes vanish.
Trust the aggregate. Don’t waste hours on building a perfect tracking system.

21. The only thing you can't survive is apathy
Positive comments and negative comments both convert.
People arguing about your product are engaged with your product.
Indifference is what kills you, so you better provoke a feeling.

22. Prune the program like a tree
Develop scoring for every creator.
The ones that convert, you keep and renew. The ones that don't, you cut.
A profitable program is a roster that’s always raising your floor metrics.

23. Treat ads like lighter fluid
Only run paid ads when your revenue is consistent enough to bring on an A-player to solely manage that growth channel.
And by that time, you have enough proven organic angles that can be spun up as ads.
