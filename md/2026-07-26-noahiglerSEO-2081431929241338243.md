# We're relaunching a flooring company's entire website this month.

## Post

We're relaunching a flooring company's entire website this month.

Most home service sites lose rankings after a redesign because of what happens in the 48 hours around launch.

Here's the exact launch checklist we use.

The redesign itself isn't the risk. Google doesn't care that your site looks better. The risk is that the new site changes your URLs, drops pages, or hides content, and Google re-crawls it like a stranger's website.

The checklist:

> Map every old URL to a new one with 301 redirects. Every service page, every location page, every blog post that ever earned a link. A page that dies without a redirect takes its authority to the grave.

> Keep the URL structure identical wherever possible. The safest redirect is the one you never needed.

> Carry over your title tags, H1s, and page content before you touch anything visual. Redesign the wrapping, not the words. You can improve the words later, once rankings hold steady.

> Submit the new sitemap to Search Console the hour you launch, then request indexing on your money pages by hand.

> Crawl the site weekly before launch and after. You're looking for broken links, pages that vanished, and anything blocking Google from crawling. Catching it in week one is a quick fix. Catching it in month three is a recovery project.

> Watch Search Console daily for two weeks. A small dip while Google re-crawls is normal. Pages falling out of the index is not, and you want to know which one is happening on day 2, not day 60.

Worth saying plainly: even with every box on this list checked, a relaunch usually costs some traffic for a stretch. The good news for this flooring company is that a lot of what they could lose is old blog traffic that has never produced a single lead. Trading visits that never turned into calls for a site built to convert is a trade worth making.

We're running this exact list right now. It's boring and mechanical, and it's the reason a redesign becomes an upgrade instead of a restart.
