# Google is shutting down the Local Services Ads dashboard.

## Post

Google is shutting down the Local Services Ads dashboard.

Starting in August, home service LSA accounts get migrated into Google Ads as a new Performance Max campaign type built around pay-per-lead.

Here's what's changing, what to do before your migration date, and my theories on why Google is doing this.

WHAT STAYS THE SAME

> You still pay per lead, not per click. Valid calls and messages only.

> Your ads still show only on Search and Maps, in the same positions as today.

> Still no keywords. Targeting runs off your service categories and service areas.

> Your Google Verified badge carries over, and if you're already verified there's no insurance or license reverification.

WHAT'S CHANGING

> The LSA dashboard goes away on your migration day. The login redirects straight into Google Ads, and your old performance reports do not come with you.

> Google's automated bidding sets your cost per lead from here on.

> One cost target per campaign, across every service. If you want plumbing priced differently than HVAC, you now need a separate campaign for each trade.

> Weekly budgets become daily budgets. Google divides your weekly number by 7, and monthly spend caps at about 30 days' worth of that.

> BBB callouts are gone. You pick at least 6 structured callouts in the assets tab instead. (TBD)

> Your lead inbox moves into Google Ads under Conversions, with the same charged status, dispute form, and lead downloads.

> Your Google Business Profile now feeds your ads directly. Name, address, and hours sync one way from the profile into Google Ads, and changing your name or address can pause your campaign for 24 to 48 hours while Google reviews it.

THE TIMELINE

August: the first wave of US home service advertisers. Plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, and moving.

Late 2026: service-area businesses without a storefront and accounts with custom setups.

2027: everyone else, including non-US accounts.

You get an email 14 days before your date, a reminder a week out, and Google says to expect up to two weeks for performance to settle after the switch.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOUR HOME SERVICE BUSINESS

> Export everything now. Reports, lead history, what each service costs you per lead. Once the dashboard redirects, that data is gone and you'll have no way to prove whether the new system charges you more.

> Get your Business Profile right before your window. If you've been putting off a DBA filing or an address change, handle it now. The new campaign gets built from whatever your profile says, and changing your name or address after migration can pause your ads for up to two days.

> Multiple trades under one account? Decide your structure early. One campaign means one cost target across everything you do. Splitting by trade gives you control back, but it also splits your budget and your data.

> Do not panic in the first two weeks. Google says performance takes up to two weeks to stabilize after migration. Don't pause it and don't rebuild it during that window, because you'll just restart the learning period and drag the mess out longer. Give it the full two weeks unless something is seriously broken.

MY THEORIES

Theory 1: Cost per lead is probably going up in competitive markets, and Google has a very good reason to want that.

Before this change, you could set a max cost per lead and Google couldn't charge past it. That limit is going away. Google's system sets the price now, and prices follow demand. During a July heat wave or the week after a storm, expect to pay more for the same lead. You can still set a target cost per lead, but a target is not a limit, and Google can go past it when demand spikes. The monthly cap only protects your total spend, not the price of each lead.

Now look at the timing. Google just reported its first quarter of negative free cash flow since going public in 2004. They're not losing money. The ads business is making more than ever. But they're spending it faster than it comes in, about $45 billion on AI data centers in the last three months alone, with plans to spend around $200 billion this year and even more next year. They're borrowing money and selling stock to cover the gap. When a company is burning cash at that pace, every product gets squeezed for more revenue, and ads are the machine that pays for everything. Removing the cap on what a lead can cost you, at this exact moment, fits that picture a little too well.

What you can still control is simple: answer every call and keep reviews coming in weekly, because Google still wants to send leads to businesses that handle them well.

Theory 2: Google plans to show these ads in more places eventually.

They didn't pick the Performance Max name by accident. Right now these campaigns only run on Search and Maps. But Performance Max is the campaign type Google built to push ads onto YouTube, Gmail, and websites across the internet. My guess is that somewhere down the road, Google starts offering to run your lead ads on those extra surfaces too. If that option ever shows up in your account, be careful with it. More placements usually means worse leads, and in a pay-per-lead system that means paying for more junk.

Theory 3: A lot of owners are going to stop managing their own accounts.

The old LSA dashboard was simple. Any owner could log in, check leads, and adjust the budget in two minutes. Google Ads is not simple, and most owners hate being in there. My guess is a big chunk of set-and-forget accounts slowly get worse after the move, because the owner stops logging in and nobody catches drifting budgets or junk leads. The businesses that actually watch their account after the switch will take leads from the ones that don't.
