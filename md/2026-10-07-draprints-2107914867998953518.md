# the complete guide to running linkedin outbound...

## Post

we've sent over 4 million linkedin inmails  with 20%+ reply rates across 30 industries.
that's across 140+ b2b companies, run on 100+ real linkedin profiles.
this guide covers everything: the profile, the list, the ways to send, the message, the follow-ups, staying safe, handling replies, and scaling up.
 
why linkedin works so well for outbound
 
why linkedin works so well for outbound:
a decision maker gets hundreds of cold emails a month, 
and only a handful of cold linkedin messages.
your message sits next to real conversations.
a linkedin message lands next to messages from colleagues and clients, 
while a cold email lands next to newsletters and promotions.
the reply rates show it.
in our data, inmails get around 8% replies on average, compared with around 1.2% for cold email in 2026.
what one account can do.
a design agency hired us to find new clients.
we ran their outbound from a single linkedin account for 18 days, 
with no personalization in the messages at all.
92 interested leads came in, including big companies that had been out of their reach until then.
 
step 1: set up the profile
every person you message will look at your profile before replying.
what the profile needs:
a photo where your face is clear and well lit
a headline that says the result you create, with a number if possible
an about section written as "i", a few short paragraphs long
your work history, with real dates
a custom profile link with your name in it
500+ connections
a few recent posts or comments
the open profile setting switched on, so anyone can message you back
 
step 2: build the list
 
use sales navigator.
run an account search to get your companies, then find the right people within them.
use lots of job title variations, since companies call the same job different things.
filters that find people more likely to reply:
changed jobs in the last 90 days
active posters from the past month
recently promoted
each of those has got us around 3 times the replies of a normal list.
check who has an open profile.
open profile members accept inmails from anyone, connected or otherwise.
the message button on their profile tells you whether it's on, so record it for every lead during list building.
screen the list.
before any messages go out, 
let a model check every company's website against your buyer profile and note why it passes or fails.
reports usually come from people who got a message meant for someone else.
 
step 3: pick how you'll send
inmail.
a direct message to someone outside your network, paid for with sales navigator credits.
it arrives straight away, with no waiting for anyone to accept anything.
credits come back when the person replies within 90 days, even if the reply is a no.
connection request, then a message.
send a connection request, and message them once they accept.
each account can send around 100 requests a week, so use them on the people worth it.
event attendees.
sign up for linkedin events in your market, and you can message the attendees without connecting.
linkedin groups.
join the groups your buyers belong to.
being in the same group lets you message other members directly, and the group itself gives you something in common to mention.
connection request notes.
keep the note to one line about why you want to connect, with no pitch.
save the offer for the message after they accept.
voice notes.
after someone accepts your connection request, send a 30 second voice note with their name and one specific thing about their company.
sequences built around voice notes have got us around 40% responses, since a voice stands out in an inbox full of text.
 
step 4: write the message
 
the subject line is their first name.
a first name only subject line has taken our inmails past 50% replies, since it reads like a personal note.
keep the message short.
the best cold message we've ever sent was 19 words long.
it covered 3 things: our service, the kind of client we work with, 
and a question asking if they wanted to hear more.
the structure:
a real, numbered result from a company in their position
one short question
use proof from their industry.
a result from a staffing firm, sent to other staffing firms, reads as proof they can relate to.
small markets get more personal messages.
when there are only a few hundred possible buyers, spend more time on each one.
one of these small, highly targeted campaigns got a 46.88% reply rate.
write several versions.
sending the exact same message to thousands of people gets flagged.
write 3 to 5 versions of each line and rotate them.
 
step 5: follow up
send 2 more messages:
after 3 or 4 days, share another client result
after 7 to 10 days, ask a quick question about their timing
after that, stop and move them back into your list for a later campaign.
each follow-up should add something new.
a follow-up that only says "just bumping this" is easy to ignore, 
so give each one a fresh reason to reply.
 
step 6: send safely
linkedin watches how an account behaves, and sending safely matters as much as sending a lot.
what gets accounts flagged:
actions faster than a person could do them
exactly the same gap between every action
a whole day's messages sent in a few minutes
logging in from different countries
the identical message sent to thousands of people
a low connection acceptance rate
people reporting your messages
how to stay safe:
spread activity across the working day
vary the timing between actions
do less at weekends
use several versions of each message
log in from the same place each time
reply rate affects how much you can send.
linkedin now looks at how often an account's messages get answered when deciding its sending limit.
well answered accounts keep their volume, and ignored ones get throttled.
so good targeting and a good message protect your account.
what happens if you push too hard.
the penalties go in order: a warning, a short lockout, an id check, and a permanent ban last.
if an account gets a warning, stop using it for 2 weeks, then restart slowly.
 
step 7: handle the replies
 
reply fast
in our campaigns, 45 out of every 100 replies answered within 5 minutes become meetings.
leave them a day and it's 3 out of 100.
one person owns each reply.
shared inboxes let replies fall through the cracks.
call interested people.
a quick call books the meeting and checks they're a real fit.
offer a specific time.
"does tuesday at 11am work?" gets a quicker answer than a calendar link.
what a full campaign produced for one of our clients.
a marketing agency hired us to find new clients.
we targeted companies with 50 to 500 employees whose marketing leaders had posted recently or had just hired for marketing roles, 
which gave us 5,308 people.
over 5 weeks, 3,819 inmails went out for them.
that brought 374 replies, 75 interested leads, 30 calls on the calendar, and 2 signed retainers.
 
step 8: scale beyond one account
each account can only send so much, so more volume comes from more accounts.
what each account needs:
to belong to a real person, with real history
a complete profile
its own login location, kept the same
3 to 4 weeks of warming before it sends at full volume
how to warm an account:
week 1: activity only, meaning connecting with people in your field, reacting to posts, and sharing one post
week 2: a few more connection requests, plus normal chats with anyone who accepts
week 3: add a small number of cold messages
week 4 onward: hold a steady pace
stagger the start dates, so each account begins on its own day.
keep a spare account warming, so you can replace one if it gets a warning.
inmailer, our own software, handles the pacing across accounts and sorts every list by open and closed profiles before anything sends.
 
step 9: combine linkedin with email
some people barely use linkedin and live in their email.
there's no way to tell from the outside.
so run both on the same prospects:
a linkedin message first for senior people
3 days on, an email about that same problem
another linkedin touch for anyone who viewed your profile
a final email with a different angle
in our data, linkedin alone gets around 15% responses and email alone around 3%.
run together, the combined response is close to 35%.
 
step 10: the numbers to track
 
every week, for each account:
the share of connection requests accepted, ideally 30% or more
reply rate
any warnings or restrictions
every week, for each campaign:
reply rate for each type of company
interested replies
meetings booked
how fast replies get answered
what to change first when numbers drop:
warnings or low acceptance: slow down and check the targeting
low replies: change the list
replies but no interest: change the result you lead with
interest but no meetings: reply faster and call more
