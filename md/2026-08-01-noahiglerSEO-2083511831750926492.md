# The wealthiest neighborhoods in your city can't see your Google profile.

## Post

The wealthiest neighborhoods in your city can't see your Google profile.

Profile rankings die 2 to 3 miles from your address, and the money usually sits just past the ring.

Here's the play that gets around it.

Google draws a proximity radius around your profile's address. Inside it, you can rank in the map pack. Outside it, someone closer wins, no matter how good your reviews are. And in most metros, the neighborhoods with the biggest budgets sit exactly where your radius runs out.

Your website doesn't have that limit. Organic page rankings don't care where your office sits, which means the website can hunt in territory the profile will never touch.

The play:

> Pick the 2 or 3 highest-value neighborhoods within range of your crews. The areas where a job runs double what it runs everywhere else.

> Build one page per neighborhood per premium service. "Roof replacement in Highland Park" style, targeted at exactly what those homeowners search.

> Write each page so it could only be about that neighborhood. The home styles and ages, the specific streets you've worked, jobs completed there with photos. Google can smell a find-and-replace template, and so can a homeowner.

> Start with one neighborhood and win it before expanding. Early wins earn the authority that makes the next pages rank faster.

The bonus most people miss: wealthy customers also search the big city terms, and every neighborhood page you win feeds authority back to your main city page. So the play captures the high-ticket searches directly and lifts everything else while it does it.

Every trade has a zip code nearby where the same job pays double. Most companies run ads at it and hope. Almost nobody builds the pages that own it.
