# Roto-Rooter just showed what happens when a business has to buy more of its lead flow.

## Post

Roto-Rooter just showed what happens when a business has to buy more of its lead flow.

Free internet leads fell 13.1% year over year.

Paid leads rose 7.3%.

Total leads still dropped 1.6% because the free side fell faster than the paid side grew.

Paid now makes up 59% of all Roto-Rooter leads. It was 54% a year ago and 44% 18 months ago.

The company-owned residential business still grew revenue. Gross margin improved. Adjusted EBITDA margin slipped, and higher internet marketing costs were one reason.

Most owners watch cost per lead. I would add another number to the dashboard:

Paid leads as a percentage of total leads.

Say a company gets 100 leads a month.

Eighteen months ago, 44 were paid. Now 59 are paid.

The Google Ads CPL can stay exactly the same and the business still becomes more expensive to grow because a larger share of every month's demand has to be bought.

The direction of that mix matters. Paid share rising because the company is growing fast is one thing. Paid share rising while total leads stay flat or fall means the business is spending more just to defend the same board.

That is the part a normal ad report will miss.

Every $3M to $15M home service company should track:

> Paid leads divided by total leads
> Blended acquisition cost across Ads, LSA, agency fees, and other paid channels
> Revenue and gross profit by source
> Organic, referral, repeat, and maintenance-plan share
> Branded search growth and how many customers already knew the company

Keep Ads running when they are profitable.

Use paid search to capture demand now, then keep building SEO, reviews, referrals, maintenance plans, and brand so Ads do not have to carry a larger percentage of the board every year.

If paid share keeps climbing, I would look at review velocity, branded search, organic visibility, repeat work, and the referral system before blaming the ad account.

Roto-Rooter has one of the strongest brands in home services and is still dealing with this shift.

A smaller operator has less room to absorb it.
