# The ONLY Grok Bot Guide You'll Ever Need (My 6 Bot Team, Explained Simply)

## Post

I've been running Grok Bot every day since August. Months later, my timeline is still full of people who are confused by it, or using it for the wrong things.

They set it up, ask it a few random things, and get stuck on the same three questions. What should I actually automate? When do I use Grok Bot and when do I use Claude? And what are all these features even for?

I run my whole business with zero employees. No assistant, no researcher, no VA. What I do have is a team of 6 Grok Bots, and they message me like employees would. One finds my next post before I'm awake. One tests my apps every night. One sorts my sponsor pitches and another one tries the product before I say yes.

Here's the honest part first: there's nothing Grok Bot can do that other agents can't. I've run most of them. The difference is that it deletes all the setup that stops people from ever getting an agent running. You don't configure software, you hire a team.

In August I wrote about my team of 8 Grok Bots. After a few months of running them every day, I cut it down to 6. This is the updated version, with everything I learned along the way.

So this is the whole thing. How to think about it, every bot on my team and what it does, the features that actually matter, how it fits with Claude, and what to watch out for. Steal all of it.

Think Like a Manager, Not a User

Most people treat Grok Bot like a chatbot. They create one bot, give it no real job and ask it random things for a week. A bot with no job becomes a general helper, and a general helper is just a slower version of the chat apps you already have.

Treat it like hiring instead.

Fewer bots is better. Every bot keeps its own memory by default, so the more bots you have, the more you repeat yourself and the messier it gets. Same as real life: you don't want 100 people reporting to you. You want a small team of strong people you can train.

I learned this the hard way. I started with 8 bots, then merged two jobs into one and dropped the ones I didn't need anymore. The team got better when it got smaller.

One bot, one job. Every time I gave a bot two jobs, it got worse at both.

Give every bot anti-jobs. A short list of things it must never do. "Never send anything without my OK." "Never invent a number." This one line saves you from most disasters.

Name them so you know the job at a glance. "Jason" tells you nothing. Go for short and obvious: Chief, Newsroom, App Doctor.

Start by importing your memory. Go to Settings, then General, then Start import. Paste their prompt into the AI you use most, paste its answer back, and Grok Bot knows you and your business from day one. If you don't have that history, tell it to interview you one question at a time.

Then let your main bot hire the rest. You don't have to create every bot by hand. Tell your main bot what you need and it creates the new bots with their own jobs and puts them in your sidebar. There's also a bot designer template in the official marketplace whose only job is designing other bots. Tell it the job and it builds the bot with one goal and its anti-jobs already in place.

Align before every big job. I have a skill that makes a bot ask me numbered questions with its recommendation for each before it starts anything big. A generic prompt for a big job gets you an output that's off and wastes your usage.

My Team of 6

1. Chief: my chief of staff

The bot I actually talk to. It reads every other bot's chats, because your bots can see each other's conversations with you.

Every morning at 8 it sends me one list: what each bot did since yesterday, what's waiting on me, and my email digest. One line per email, with the important ones on top marked with an emoji. Any time I type /inbox, I get the same list.

This is the bot that changes how the whole thing feels. I don't manage 6 bots. I manage 1, and it manages the rest.

On Sundays it does two more things. It gives every bot a score out of 10 and tells me the one fix that would help the weakest bot most. A weekly report card for my team. And it looks at what I keep asking for by hand and suggests new routines. Most people only automate the jobs they think of. This finds the ones you didn't.

2. Newsroom: finds my next post before I'm awake

Every morning at 7 it scans X, Reddit and YouTube for the 3 biggest AI waves from the last 48 hours. For each one I get the link, why it's moving, and 3 angles from what I'm actually running in my businesses. Then 10 hooks for the best angle, all on one page. It waits for my pick before it drafts anything.

The "wait for my pick" part matters. Without it, bots get eager and start producing things you didn't ask for.

It also runs a quiet routine every 15 minutes on the X accounts of the big AI labs. It only messages me when something big launches, so I can post about it the same day while everyone else is a day late.

Grok Bot can search X natively, because X and xAI are the same company. That's a huge edge for anyone who creates content.

And the most important rule: it drafts from my best posts and keeps a list of every line I reject. AI should do the research, not the talking. My content still has to sound like me.

3. Strategist: knows my customers better than I do

A bot that runs every day remembers everything it finds, so it gets more useful every week. I want that memory working on the decisions that actually move my business.

My Strategist reads what my app users say everywhere: App Store reviews, Reddit threads, TikTok comments, replies under my tweets. It goes through my competitors' 1 to 3 star reviews too, because people are most honest when they're annoyed. From all of that it built 3 customer profiles with their pain points in their own words.

Every week it tells me what competitors shipped and what's trending before it's obvious. And whenever another bot hits a big decision, it checks with Strategist first.

The result is copy that reads like it was written from inside your customer's head.

4. App Doctor: tests my apps every night

My studio ships mobile apps, and testing is the part everyone quietly skips when a deadline is close.

App Doctor walks through each app every night like a brand new user who reads nothing. It's a quiet routine, so it only messages me when something breaks or confuses it, worst problem first.

The first time I ran this, it messaged me asking how to get into the first screen of the app. That question was a finding by itself. If a bot can't figure out the entry point, neither can a user.

Once a week it also reads my analytics, tells me which screen people leave on, and lists users who look like they're about to cancel, with a win-back email drafted for each one. Every email waits as a card until I say OK.

5. Deal Desk: every sponsor deal, from pitch to payment

Every new email in my Gmail triggers it. It sorts sponsor and collab pitches into "want to work with", "maybe" and "skip", using rules I wrote from my own past deals: the kinds of products my audience loved, the ones that flopped, and what I will never promote. It drafts a reply for each one as a card.

It also keeps a ledger of every deal, with brand, amount, terms and due date. When a payment is late, it drafts a polite follow-up and reminds me every 3 days until the money lands.

Chasing money was the task I dreaded most. Now it's a bot's job.

6. Product Explorer: tries the product before I say yes

This is the one people don't believe.

Deal Desk hands every "want to work with" pitch straight to Product Explorer. If it needs an account, it asks me to sign in once on its computer. Then it takes over: it explores every menu, plays with every feature, builds something small and stress tests the whole thing.

Then it gives me a one page report: what the product actually does, what surprised it, what it's genuinely good at, where it broke, and a clear verdict. Worth my audience, or skip.

The first real run was for a design platform I was testing for a campaign. The report it came back with became the backbone of the whole campaign. I used to lose entire evenings to tools that turned out to be demos wearing a landing page. Now a bot loses those evenings for me.

If a bot can't find the value in your product in an hour, my audience won't either.

The Features That Make It Run Without You

Most of the magic is in features people never turn on.

Routines vs quiet routines. A routine is a job your bot runs on a schedule in the cloud, even with your laptop and phone closed. A quiet routine only messages you when something needs you. Once you have a few bots, quiet routines are what keep you sane. If every run pings you, you've just created a new job for yourself.

Event triggers. A routine can also wake up the moment something happens: a new email, a Slack message, a form on your website. That's how Deal Desk works.

Cards. Every email or message a bot drafts waits as a card you can edit before it goes out. Keep this on. Bots draft, you decide.

Pages. The chat is a bad place to read a report. Ask for a page and you get a report, tracker or dashboard you can share with a link.

Handoffs and group chats. Bots can pass work to each other in plain words, like Deal Desk to Product Explorer. For project work, put a few bots in one group chat so they can all see each other's replies.

The shared brain. People assume all their bots share one memory. They don't. Each one keeps its own, and only a few facts like your name and time zone are shared. So ask your main bot to put what every bot should know (who you are, your voice, your rules) into the shared brain folder.

Teach it by showing. Your bot has its own computer. Open its computer view, press take control and click through a task once. That's far easier than describing every click, and it turns straight into a skill.

Skills. If you type the same instructions twice, save them as a skill. That's all /inbox is.

Its own inbox. Your team can claim an address at mail.grokbot.com to sign up for tools and newsletters without touching your personal email. You get one per account and you can't rename it, so pick the name carefully.

Talk instead of typing. Hit the mic, brain dump the whole job, and ask the bot to repeat back what it understood before it starts. It can reply as a voice memo too, which is great when you're on the go.

Keep Your Usage From Running Out

The number one complaint about Grok Bot is running out of usage. A few things fix most of it:

Ask your bots which one used the most this week and how to cut back. They can see your usage.

Turn routines that do the exact same steps every time into plain code that needs no AI. Your bot can audit your routines and find them.

Use quiet routines, and give every bot a narrow job. A bot with a vague job will happily burn credits doing vague work.

Grok Bot + Claude

This isn't Grok Bot vs Claude. They're different kinds of agents.

Claude Code is for deep work. You sit with it and build something hard: an app, a system, an article. Grok Bot is the always-on team. It watches, patrols, alerts, drafts and follows up while you're doing something else.

So Claude builds, and Grok Bot runs.

Most of what I share inside my community, The AI Head Start, is the Claude side: systems where you paste one link and Claude sets the whole thing up for you. Grok Bot is the team that keeps things running after.

And they work together. You can let Grok Bot work on your own computer, so it can reach your files and read your Claude skills and bring them into its own memory. The cleanest setup is one machine (a Mac mini works) with both installed, so your bots and Claude share the same files.

What to Watch Out For

Usage drains fast once you have a few bots and routines. Check it weekly.

Each bot has its own memory until you set up the shared brain, so you'll repeat yourself at the start.

All your bots share one computer, so a login you give one bot is available to every bot. Treat it like giving keys to the whole team.

Don't automate judgment. Automate the scanning, clicking, testing and drafting around it.

Everything that prompt does is already in this article: the rules, every bot's job, its routines and its anti-jobs. Copy them one bot at a time and you'll have the same team by the end of the week.
