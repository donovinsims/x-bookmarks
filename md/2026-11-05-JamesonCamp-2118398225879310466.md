1. Find What Your Audience Wants

My most-saved post of the year was a Grokbot workflow.

It said you could pull $20k a month building websites for penny stocks, and showed how to point Grokbot at OTC Markets and SEC filings to find the tiny public companies still running a website from 2004.

It got 2,235 bookmarks on 94,502 views. About 1 in 42 people who saw it saved it.

Look for that kind of ratio in your niche. Somebody bigger in your niche posted on your topic this month, and their bookmarks and replies show you what people wanted more of.

2. Write Posts in the 4 Formats That Get Saved

I went through my most-saved posts and four formats kept coming up.

Each of them gives the reader something specific to take away.

The opportunity: "You could pull $X a month doing Y for Z." The $20k-a-month penny stock websites post above: 2,235 bookmarks. A post about a guide on how to do a handstand making $80,000 a month: 783.

The receipt: hours, cost in tools, and what it built. My post that opened "10 hours of work. $200 in tools.": 1,159 bookmarks.

The tool setup: the tool, then steps they can follow today. A walkthrough of GPT Sol and Astra for browser work: 1,778 bookmarks.

The big-number explainer: a figure people can't picture, made concrete. My post that measured billionaire spending in Ferraris (a mega yacht is 1,400 of them) hit 455,983 views.

Don't chase views for this. My most-viewed post of the past year was a story about a tuk tuk driver in Cambodia asking if I wanted to go shoot guns.

558,865 views. Only 644 bookmarks.

The penny stock websites post got more than three times the saves on a sixth of the views. The saves come from posts that teach something, and the people who save a post are the ones who give you an email.

The receipt is the easiest one to start with, because you only need something you actually did. My "10 hours of work" post was a favor for a friend. Her coaching business had 12k+ Instagram followers and no email list at all, and in about a day I had Claude write 20 newsletters in her voice.

Stage 2 of the agent picks the format for each topic and drafts two posts in your voice, ending on a line that makes people want the detailed version.

3. Build Only for the Posts That Take Off

Writing a guide, a page and three emails for every post would eat your week.

So the agent waits. It checks each post you publish at 1, 3 and 6 hours and compares it to your own median.

When a post is pulling more bookmarks per 1,000 views than your median, you get a message with the link, its numbers and a guide idea.

You reply "go" or ignore it. Nothing gets built until you say so.

A post that takes off gets the full funnel while people are still finding it. The rest never cost you anything beyond writing them.
