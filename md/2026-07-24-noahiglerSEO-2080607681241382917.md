# Google Ads will happily spend your whole budget whether the account is set up right or not.

## Post

Google Ads will happily spend your whole budget whether the account is set up right or not.

For a home service business, five settings decide your cost per booked job. The rest is noise.

Here's every part of the account, graded.

---

WHAT DECIDES YOUR COST PER JOB

> Campaign type. Plain search campaigns. Not smart campaigns, and be very careful with Performance Max, which loves to spend a local budget on clicks that can never become jobs. Search gives you control over every keyword, and at local budgets that control is the whole game.

> Match types and negatives. Run exact and phrase match, then build your negative keyword list like it's a second job. "Free," "DIY," "jobs," "how to," and every city you don't serve. A shocking amount of local ad budget leaks out through searches that were never going to hire anyone, and negatives are how you plug the holes.

> Location targeting. Target only where your crews profitably drive, and set it to people IN your locations, not people "interested in" them. That one dropdown quietly bills owners for clicks from other states.

> The landing page. Send AC repair clicks to an AC repair page with the number at the top, reviews, and a short form. Sending ads to your homepage is paying full price for a maybe.

> Conversion tracking. Track calls from ads and form fills, then feed Google what actually booked. The algorithm optimizes toward whatever you count, so count jobs, not clicks.

---

WHAT EATS YOUR TIME INSTEAD

> Optimization score. It's a sales meter for Google, not a health meter for you. Plenty of great accounts sit at 80%.

> Auto-apply recommendations. Turn them off today. Google will "helpfully" add broad match keywords and raise budgets while you sleep.

> Ad strength ratings. An "Average" ad with the right keywords outperforms an "Excellent" ad with the wrong ones. Write ads that name the service, the city, and the offer, and let the rating say whatever it wants.

> Constant bid changes. Check the account weekly with your booked-job data in front of you. Daily fiddling just resets the learning and buys you nothing.

---

Get the five levers right and the account gets boring in the best possible way. The phone rings at a cost per job you know by heart, and you stop logging in every morning to find out what Google spent while you slept.
