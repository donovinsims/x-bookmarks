# how to sell ai agents to local businesses (full guide)

## Post

implementing ai for small businesses is a massive opportunity right now, and i want to walk you through the whole thing in this article. how the market works, how you get into it from zero, and a full real build that one of our clients pays us $3,000 a month for.

so first, why this is wide open.

take any business in your town that makes real money. a manufacturer, a contractor, whatever. all of them run on manual work. orders come in through email and someone retypes them into a spreadsheet. someone turns that spreadsheet into invoices by hand. the owner knows it's painful, he's been dealing with it for years.

and nobody is fixing it for him. big software companies build for enterprises and the local agencies sell him things like websites and google ads. so the manual work just stays there.

and here's the thing that surprises me every time i'm on a call with one of these owners. he doesn't know any of this can be automated. he's not skeptical about ai, he just has no idea this exists for a business like his. when you tell an owner that the process he just described, the one eating fifteen hours a week, can run on its own, he's genuinely shocked. in a lot of these businesses, the work you'd be automating is a couple of employees' entire job.

so the market is huge, the buyers don't know this exists, and basically nobody is selling to them. we build these systems for clients full time at my agency, and i can tell you there's not much competition right now. and claude just shipped its new model this week, the most capable one they've ever released, and it's best at exactly this kind of long multi-step building work.

now, how you actually get in. because you can't walk into a local business and say "i build ai agents." the owner has no idea what that means, and he's been burned by software salesmen before. you need to come in with something he already knows he needs.

the easiest thing i've found is creatives.

every owner knows he should be posting on social media but he doesn't post, because making content is work and he's busy running the business. that's your way in.

the offer is simple. fifty creatives a month, made from his actual business, his building, his products, his branding, delivered automatically, $99 a month. and the reason this works is that image models got really cheap. fifty branded images costs you almost nothing in api spend, so even at $99 you keep nearly all of it. you're not competing with the agency charging $2,000 a month for social media management. you're selling something cheap enough that he doesn't think twice and useful enough that he doesn't cancel.

the pipeline is scrape, generate, send. pick a niche, and if you can't pick one, just ask claude for ten local industries where owners care about their image and never post, and go with whatever's near you. apify will scrape every business in that niche in your area, names, addresses, photos, all from public listings. you run that through an image model and generate a creative that's clearly theirs. then you send it.

email works fine, attach the sample with two lines of text. but a physical postcard converts way more. nobody gets real mail anymore, and an owner holding a card with his own storefront on it has never seen anything like that. scribeless and lob both print and mail postcards through an api, so the physical mail is just another step in the pipeline. just remember the postcard thing, it comes back later in this article.

some owners will sign up at $99. you don't want to be manually sending fifty images a month to each one, so you build a small tool that sends every client a new creative every day automatically. that's a small buildout with claude, on the same stack i'm about to show you in the example.

and to be clear about what the $99 is. ten clients is $990 a month, that's not the business. the point is that once a business is paying you anything, you're not a stranger anymore. you're the guy who does their ai stuff, and they see your work in their inbox every morning. when you ask for fifteen minutes on the phone, you get it.

and on that call, you ask one question. what do you do manually every day that you hate, and he'll tell you everything. the double data entry, the inbox checking every morning, the invoices someone builds by hand. somewhere in there is a process that follows the same rules every time and eats hours of payroll every week. that's your build, and that's where the real money is.

so let me show you a real one, start to finish. this is a system we built for a client who manufactures and sells high end golf carts. this is the actual process.

when we started working with him, every lead he'd ever gotten was an inbound referral. he wanted more leads, and his ideal buyers are wineries, churches, resorts, places with big properties that move people around all day.

here's the system. it finds wineries with the google places api. it pulls imagery of each property with google's satellite and street view apis. then it takes his actual golf cart, renders it parked on their actual property with their logo on the side of the cart, and mails that image to the winery as a physical postcard. so the winery owner opens his mail and sees his own vineyard with a golf cart that has his logo on it sitting in it.

and the client doesn't touch any of it. an agent runs the loop every day. he gets a dashboard where he can see the leads and trigger workflows, and an app connected to it, so when he has a question he doesn't ask me, he asks the agent, and it answers because it has context on the whole system.

he pays us $3,000 a month for this to run. the base of the system took me about an hour to build with claude code. here's the short version of how.

i build in cursor with the claude code extension. you install it, log in, and claude is inside your project taking actions for you. have it install the supabase, vercel and fly.io clis first and log into each one, because once claude has those it creates your database, deploys your app and manages your backend straight from the terminal instead of you clicking around dashboards. then you prompt it with the system description i just gave you, plus the stack. next js with react and tailwind for the dashboard, clerk for auth, supabase for the database, vercel for the frontend, and fly.io for the backend, because vercel's timeout kills long-running image generations.

two things worth knowing from this build. first, claude put an old image model in its plan. the models don't know the newest models, which is funny, so whenever an api is involved, go find the current docs yourself and paste them in. second, the image generation has to be an edit model fed with real references, photos of the actual cart from multiple angles, the satellite view of the property, a few street view angles, and the winery's logo. the more real inputs you give it, the more the output looks like an actual photo taken on their property, and that accuracy is the whole product. a generic cart on a generic lawn gets thrown out. their cart with their logo on their property gets shown around the office.

from there it's mostly handing claude api keys while it works. google's key for the property data, the image model's, and apify for scraping each business's contact email and logo. the logos get a quality check, claude's vision looks at every one and rejects anything that won't render clean on the cart, and that one check is the difference between outputs that look like magic and outputs that embarrass your client. the postcards go out through scribeless, an agent runs the whole loop daily, you invite the client to the dashboard and the app, hand it over, and that's pretty much it.

an hour of building, $3,000 a month. and you can charge an implementation fee on top, five to ten thousand is normal for a build like this.
