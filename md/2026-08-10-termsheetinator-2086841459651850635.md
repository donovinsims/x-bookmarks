# 1/ The best prospects for AI implementation usually aren’t posting about needing AI.

## Post

1/ The best prospects for AI implementation usually aren’t posting about needing AI.

They’re already spending money to compensate for a broken workflow.

Here’s how to identify the problem, find the buyer, book the meeting and structure the deal:

2/ Start with observable signals.

Look for SMBs that are:

• Hiring receptionists or dispatchers
• Receiving “no one answered” reviews
• Running paid ads
• Opening new locations
• Offering emergency or after-hours service

Each signal points to operational leakage.

3/ Build the list around one workflow - not “SMBs that need AI.”

Example:

Multi-location home-service companies hiring dispatchers while generating inbound leads.

Source them through Google Maps, LinkedIn, job boards, company websites and customer reviews.

Apify + Claude can do most of this work for you.

4/ Then find the right job titles/person who owns the problem.

Enrich their contact information through AI-Ark, GetLeads or use the email you get from Google Maps scraper, then verify every email before sending with MiliVerifier or Reoon.

5/ Don’t offer “AI implementation.”

Offer the result:

“We’ll turn your after-hours and overflow calls into booked jobs.

Without adding dispatchers or losing those opportunities to a competitor.”

The AI is the mechanism. The recovered revenue is the offer.

6/ Your email can be this simple:

“Hiring a dispatcher while running ads?

We’ll turn your after-hours and overflow calls into booked jobs.

Without adding dispatchers or losing those opportunities to a competitor.

Can I give you a call to discuss?”

7/ The purpose of the meeting isn’t to demonstrate every feature.

It’s to calculate the cost of the existing problem:

Monthly inquiries

× missed or delayed responses
× close rate
× gross profit per customer

Now the implementation can be compared against recoverable profit - not software costs.

8/ Start with one workflow.

Define:

• The trigger
• The information collected
• The decision rules
• The handoff point
• The system of record
• The success metric

A narrow implementation is easier to approve, measure and expand.

9/ Structure the initial deal as a fixed-fee installation:

• 50% at kickoff
• 25% when the system is configured
• 25% after acceptance testing

Document the baseline, acceptance criteria, integrations, usage assumptions and client responsibilities before building.

10/ Add a paid 12-month Performance Warranty & Care Plan.

It should cover:

• Monthly system-health reviews
• Failure and conversation audits
• Bug fixes
• Prompt and knowledge-base maintenance
• Vendor/API monitoring
• Minor workflow optimization
• Response-time commitments

Usage fees remain separate.

11/ Protect the scope.

The warranty maintains the installed system. It does not include:

• New workflows
• New locations
• Major integrations
• Material process redesigns
• Unlimited revisions

Those become separately priced expansion installations.

12/ Every monthly warranty review becomes structured account development:

“What failed?”
“What changed?”
“Where is the team still doing repetitive work?”
“What is the next workflow worth automating?”

Maintenance protects the first installation. The review uncovers the second.

13/ At renewal, complete an annual architecture review.

Benchmark the system against newer proven models, tools and integration options.

Then offer:

• Warranty renewal
• Model or infrastructure upgrade
• Additional workflow installations
• Multi-location rollout
• Reporting and compliance enhancements

14/ The commercial model becomes:

Signal-based prospecting

→ fixed-fee installation
→ paid 12-month warranty
→ monthly improvement conversations
→ expansion installations
→ annual renewal and modernization
