# How to make people give a fuck

## Post

(and how to make millions from it)

Most people don't give a fuck about your ideas...
Why should they? They've got their own problems, their own plans, something they're trying to get and someone they're trying to impress. Then you arrive expecting their attention because YOU have something to say
But give them a reason to suspect they're missing something... suddenly there's something they want explained
What am I getting wrong? How long has this been happening? What could I do about it?
You can take a subject they would've ignored ten seconds ago and make them ASK you to keep going
That's the skill we're talking about
You'll use it to get people to hear out an idea, follow an explanation, read what you write, give you a chance before deciding you're another idiot wasting their time
And yes, there are some pretty fucked ways to use it
You can make people scared of a problem they don't have, embarrassed about a life they were enjoying, desperate to buy something they didn't need
Once you understand how this works, you'll also start catching people doing it to you
1 / Start with something they already understand
How can you make someone care about blood sugar?
You could open with a long explanation of what it does, how the body regulates it, why people should learn about it
Good luck getting someone who never asked for a biology lesson to sit through that
Try starting here:
"Waking up 2-3 times a night to piss and thinking it's because you drank water before bed"
Someone who does that can recognize the situation immediately
They know the walk to the bathroom, checking the time, calculating how much sleep they've got left before the alarm goes off
And they'd quite like to stop doing it
But someone who sleeps perfectly can understand the situation too
Drink water before bed → get up to piss during the night
Seems obvious enough
Now suggest that the obvious explanation might be missing something, and they can get curious about what else could be happening
They don't need a sleep problem to find that mildly interesting. They don't need to give a fuck about blood sugar either
Someone recognizes their own night, someone else wants to understand a strange thing the body might be doing
Both have a way into the subject
Before you start explaining, finish this sentence:
"Someone would care about this because it could help them _____"
"Understand blood sugar" gives them a subject to study
"Figure out why their sleep keeps getting interrupted" gives them a reason to study it
And if they don't personally need the information, ask what they'd find surprising about it
Do this with whatever you know: who could use it, what would it change for them, and what could make someone outside that group curious?
If your answer is still "they'll be more informed", keep going until you can name something they'd gain, avoid or want explained
2 / Make them question the explanation they already have
That opening also contains an explanation almost anyone can follow
"I drank water before bed, obviously that's why I keep getting up"
Now imagine you can show that the water doesn't explain a particular person's repeated waking, and there's evidence for another cause
You've taken an answer that felt settled and made it worth questioning
"Wait, if it isn't the water, what is it?"
The person who lives with the problem wants to know whether they've been trying to fix the wrong thing
Someone who doesn't have the problem can still want to know why the obvious answer failed
That's a reason to hear the rest
For your own subject, write down these three things:
→ what keeps happening to this person?
→ what do they currently blame it on?
→ what evidence could make them reconsider?
That last question matters, you need something more useful than "everything you've been told is a lie" 😂
The useful part is that the person now has a question they want answered. The next part of your explanation has a job to do
3 / Make the explanation easy to follow
Keep returning to the person's night as you explain it
Getting out of bed, barely peeing, going back to sleep, waking up again
Those details help someone picture the situation before you bring in the technical explanation
Someone who experiences it might think "how the fuck does he know exactly what happens to me?"
Notice the order: familiar experience first, explanation second
If you introduce a technical term, explain what it means + why someone needs it at that exact moment
Blood sugar? The amount of glucose, a sugar your body uses for energy, in your blood
Now they can follow the next sentence
Don't introduce five more terms before doing anything with the first one
I would write the explanation in a rough sequence before trying to make it sound good:
what happens → what might explain it → what supports that explanation → what someone can do next
Every part should move them along that sequence
And give them a useful next step, so they can do something with the explanation
In this example, a practical step would be recording when they wake, what they drink and when, and how often + how much they pee, then discussing persistent symptoms with a clinician
Show what that record looks like, so they can actually use it. "Pay more attention to your health" tells them fuck all
When you're speaking, the same order works: describe the situation, explain the relevant part, then follow the question you get back
Don't dump every fact you know into the conversation just because somebody finally listened
4 / Here's where it gets fucked
Someone can describe your problem so well that you start trusting their answer before they've proved it
Take the same bathroom example: someone could confidently declare that blood sugar crashes are causing it, then offer food + supplements as the fix
Those symptoms alone don't establish that diagnosis or prove the fix applies
But the person listening can slide from "he described my night perfectly" to "he must know what's causing it"
That leap is doing a lot of selling
Add a story about someone who struggled for years, changed their routine and finally slept through the night
You've given them an ending they might want for themselves
It still doesn't prove what caused the improvement or that the same thing will happen to them
This is the part people abuse
Describe someone's frustration → make your explanation feel like a discovery → offer relief from the problem you've just explained
If the explanation is invented, you can sell someone an answer to a problem they don't actually have
And because the opening felt so personal, they might not notice how little evidence came after it
So when you use a story, show what actually happened + what supports your explanation. A confident sentence about hormones won't do that work for you
Making shit up to sound certain gives the buyer a reason to stop trusting you as soon as they check
5 / Build the whole thing in order
Here's how I'd prepare to explain the blood sugar idea
First, choose who needs to care
Start with someone whose sleep keeps getting interrupted and who wants to understand why. Keep the situation simple enough that someone outside that group can follow it too
Then, name what they want
To get through the night + feel rested in the morning
Find the moment they recognize
They're walking to the bathroom again and checking how many hours they've got left before work
Open with that moment
"Up for your third bathroom trip and already calculating how fucked you'll feel at work tomorrow?"
Now you've given them a scene they can recognize without knowing anything about blood sugar
Give them a reason to want the explanation
"What is actually waking you up?"
That is the question the next part needs to help them investigate. Start with what they can observe, then explain what could account for it
Introduce the subject where it belongs
If blood sugar is relevant to the explanation you're giving, show why + what evidence would help establish that connection
You don't get to force your favourite answer into every problem
Finish with something they can use
Show what the record looks like: bedtime, drinks, waking times, bathroom trips
Explain what each part helps someone investigate, and what the record alone can't tell them
That's the same process you can use on your own material
Choose a person → name what they want → find the situation → write the opening → explain → give them a next step
Try your opening on someone unfamiliar with the subject and ask what they'd expect you to explain next
If their answer has nothing to do with what you explain next, fix the connection
6 / Now turn it into money
Some people will enjoy the explanation and move on
Others have the exact problem you described + want help dealing with it
Say you're applying this to a clinician's service that reviews someone's symptoms and history, then provides written findings + next steps
The person with broken sleep has a reason to consider that service. The person who was just curious can still enjoy the explanation, remember you or share it with someone who needs it
That's the commercial connection you need to build
They have to recognize a problem they have, believe you can help, understand what they're buying and see why dealing with it now is worth the money
Here's how I'd work through that using the same example
First, make the free explanation useful on its own
Give the reader something they can understand or do before they pay
The tracking example does that. They can start recording the problem, and they know why guessing from memory might leave things out
If your free post is three screens of "this is more complicated than you think, DM me", you've barely given them anything to judge you on
Then show what the paid work adds
A post can explain a common situation to thousands of people
If you're adapting this for a clinician's service, the paid work could include reviewing one person's history, medicines, symptoms + relevant results
Show the buyer what they receive: who reviews their information, what the review covers, what the written findings look like + whether there's a follow-up
A sample with identifying details removed gives them something concrete to inspect
They should be able to explain what they're paying for without repeating "it's personalized"
Make the offer continue the same conversation
For that clinical service, a concrete offer could be:
"A clinician-reviewed assessment of your symptoms + history, with written findings and a follow-up to discuss your next steps"
The reason to care was broken sleep. The offer should help them address that same problem
Don't spend the whole post on their night and then suddenly pitch a generic course about becoming your best self
Give them a real reason to act
If the problem keeps disrupting their sleep, they already have a reason to get it looked at
Show the price, the process and who the service is suitable for
The reader should know what happens after they click or message you
Use the same idea in organic content + paid ads
For organic, the recognizable night gets someone interested, the explanation gives them a reason to stay, and the offer gives the right person a next step
For a paid ad, shorten the same sequence: recognizable situation → what the viewer will learn → where they can see the full explanation
Sending someone to more detail lets them judge the work before deciding whether to pay
Then count suitable enquiries + actual customers
For illustration, 50 reviews at $300 each = $15,000 revenue
If acquiring those customers cost $3,000, you've got $12,000 left before paying for the reviews, support + the rest of the business
Those are example numbers, the actual cost of delivering the service matters just as much as the number of people who buy
And a DM asking for help isn't a sale, track the whole path
Keep finding new things to explain
The next post could show what a symptom record reveals, what a reader should bring to an appointment, or what an anonymized review actually contains
Each gives people something different they can use + another way to judge the quality of the work
The same approach applies when you sell software or a high-ticket service: show the situation your buyer recognizes, explain something useful, demonstrate the work, then offer to do it for them
