# Most home service sites convert under 1%. Ours convert at 3-5%.

## Post

Most home service sites convert under 1%. Ours convert at 3-5%.
 
Same exact traffic, but 3 to 5x the leads coming through the phone and form.
 
Here's exactly what to fix on yours.
 
(Bookmark this)
 
I audit a lot of home service websites. The pattern is almost always the same. The traffic is fine, but the phone just isn't ringing as often as it should be.
 
Most owners assume the fix is more traffic. They're paying $2-4K a month to bring more traffic to a site that's leaking 90%+ of the visitors it already gets.
 
The cheaper fix is almost always the website itself.
 
Above the fold is where 80% of conversions are won or lost.
 
This is the part of the page someone sees before scrolling. Within 3 seconds of landing, they need to know what you do, where you do it, that you're trustworthy, and how to call you right now. If any of those are missing, you're losing the lead before they ever scroll.
 
Here's what needs to be in that zone:
 
> Headline: "[Service] in [City]" or "[City]'s Trusted [Service]." Not "Your Trusted Home Service Partner Since 2003."
> Phone number top right, large and clickable. Wired to the actual dialer on mobile, not a modal that pops up. Make it sticky as people scroll.
> Subheadline with the trust stack: review count and star rating, years in business, license number, service area.
> Primary CTA button. "Call Now" or "Get Free Quote" with the phone number visible.
> Hero image of real techs, trucks, or a job. Not a stock photo of a smiling family (see this so often).
 
Phone visibility alone is the single biggest conversion lever.
 
I had a client lose 30% of their inbound calls because the phone number on mobile was wrapped in a modal. Tap "Call Now" → modal opens asking for confirmation → 30% drop off before the dialer ever launched. Wire the phone number to a clean tel: link directly to the dialer. No modal, no popup, no extra step.
 
Form fields kill conversions faster than anything else.
 
Every field you add to a form costs you about 10% of the conversions on that page. Most home service sites I audit have forms asking for name, email, phone, address, service needed, best time to call, how they heard about you, and a comments box. Eight fields is the death zone...the form is asking for more than the customer wants to give in exchange for a quote.
 
What the form should actually ask:
 
> Name
> Phone
> Service needed (dropdown)
> Address
 
That's it. You're not running a credit application, you're trying to get them to call back. Get the phone number and the rest happens on the call.
 
Mobile speed is non-negotiable.
 
Below 50 on Google PageSpeed costs you 2 to 3 spots in the Map Pack. Below 70 and you're losing 20-30% of your mobile visitors before the page even loads.
 
Most home service sites I audit are running PageSpeed in the 30s and 40s on mobile.

The fix usually comes down to:
> Compressing images. Hero images are usually 2-3MB and need to be 200-400KB
> Removing unnecessary plugins and tracking scripts
> Using a real CDN, not just whatever the host bundles in
> Lazy loading anything below the fold
> Minimizing third-party widgets like chat boxes, social embeds, and review aggregators
 
A site that loads in <2 seconds converts dramatically better than the same site loading in 6.
 
Reviews and proof need to live everywhere on the page.
 
A lot of sites have a single testimonial section halfway down the page. That's not enough. The visitor needs to see proof at every scroll depth.
 
Where reviews should appear:
> Above the fold: review count and star rating ("4.9 stars from 312 Google reviews")
> Hero section: 2-3 short pull quotes from real reviews (dynamic from the GBP)
> Service pages: dedicated section pulling reviews that mention that specific service

The trust signal compounds. By the time they scroll to the form, they've seen proof 5 or 6 times.
 
Photos and before-and-afters do conversion work nothing else does.
 
Stock photos hurt more than they help. Visitors can spot them instantly and the trust signal flips negative.
 
What to use instead:
> Real photos of your team in branded shirts on real job sites
> Trucks parked in driveways with the homeowner's house in the background
> Before-and-after shots for the jobs where it makes sense. Drain cleaning, panel work, AC installs, ductwork, roof replacements
> Photos of license documents, manufacturer certifications, and insurance certificates
 
The site should feel like a physical business with real people, not a template a marketing agency dropped a logo into.
 
Financing visibility belongs on every service page.
 
For replacement-level tickets ($5K and up), roughly 60% of customers want financing. If your competitor displays the Synchrony, GreenSky, or Wisetack logo on the service page and you don't, they're closing deals you never even got to quote.
 
Make the financing offer visible:
> Logo of the financing partner above the fold on service pages
> "0% financing available" or "Payments as low as $89/month" callouts
> A separate financing page linked from the main nav
> Pre-qualification widget embedded on high-ticket service pages
 
Sticky elements keep CTAs in front of them constantly.
 
A scrolling visitor should never be more than one tap away from calling you. What to add:
 
> Sticky header with phone number visible at all times on mobile
> Sticky footer bar on mobile with "Call Now" and "Book Online" buttons
> Floating action button on long pages
 
This sounds aggressive on paper. In practice it adds 15-25% to call volume on most home service sites.
 
Common mistakes I see when owners try to fix this themselves:
 
> Making the form longer to "qualify" leads. You're not qualifying them, you're losing them.
> Hiding the phone number to "force" people to fill out the form. They just leave instead.
> Auto-playing video in the hero section. Kills mobile speed and annoys the visitor.
> Pop-ups on first visit. Triggers an immediate back button on mobile.
> Live chat widgets that take 4 seconds to load and cover the phone number.
 
Run these fixes properly and you'll add 50-200% to your inbound call volume in 30 days. Same SEO, same traffic, dramatically more leads.
 
If your phone is quiet right now and you're paying for SEO every month, the conversion problem might be bigger than the traffic problem.
