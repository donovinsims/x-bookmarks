# If your agency is writing you 4 blog posts a month and calling it SEO, you're paying for the wrong thing.

## Post

If your agency is writing you 4 blog posts a month and calling it SEO, you're paying for the wrong thing.
 
Blogs are not how home service businesses get phone calls.
 
Here's what actually drives leads, and what blogs are actually for.
 
Plenty of home service sites I audit have a blog full of posts like "5 Signs You Need a New Roof" or "How to Tell If Your Drain Is Clogged."
 
Generic, evergreen content that ranks for informational keywords if it ranks at all.
 
The writing is fine. The problem is that the people searching for those queries aren't ready to buy.
 
No one searches "5 signs you need a new roof." Furthermore, if someone finds your article, they might be 6 months from buying, or never. Their credit card isn't out.

Compare that to someone searching "emergency roof repair Denver" or "drain cleaning [city]" or "AC repair near me." That person is buying today. Their phone is in their hand. They're calling whoever shows up first in the Map Pack.
 
Blogs target the wrong searcher.
 
What actually drives leads for home service businesses:
 
> A properly built Google Business Profile. This is where 85% of your SEO leads come from in plumbing, HVAC, electrical, etc.
> Service pages on your website, one per service offered, written for the buyer keyword. "Drain cleaning [city]" not "How to know if you need drain cleaning."
> Location pages for every city you actually serve, written from scratch with real local content, landmarks, and city-specific issues (like hard water or slab foundations).
> Citations across 80+ directories with identical NAP.
> Real backlinks from suppliers, trade associations, news features, partnered companies, and local sponsorships.
> 20+ fresh reviews coming in per month.
 
That's where the calls come from. None of it has anything to do with a blog post.
 
So what are blogs actually for?
 
Two things: topical authority and internal linking.
 
When you publish a well-written blog post about, say, "tankless vs tank water heaters," Google sees that you've covered a topic adjacent to your services. That signals topical relevance, which helps your service pages rank slightly higher. The blog post itself probably won't rank for anything valuable. But it can (over time) prop up the pages that do.
 
The other use is internal linking. A blog post about "common drain issues in older homes" is a perfect place to link to your drain cleaning service page and your top 5 location pages. Those internal links pass authority and tell Google what your money pages are about.
 
Used this way, blogs become infrastructure for the rest of your SEO. They were never going to be a lead channel.
 
If your agency sells you blogs as a lead-gen deliverable, they're either lying or confused. Either way, you're paying $500 to $2,000 a month for content that won't put a single phone call on the schedule.
 
Stop measuring blogs against the calls they bring in. Start measuring whether they help your service pages rank.
 
If your money pages are weak and your blog is full of "5 signs" content, your SEO budget is upside down...
