# I've run over 400 local SEO audits for home service shops.

## Post

I've run over 400 local SEO audits for home service shops.

The business ranking #1 almost always has 80+ indexed citations while the one hiring me has under 5.

Here's why citations matter and how to build and keep them indexed.

---

THE PART EVERYONE MISSES

A citation only counts if Google has indexed it. Half the listings from cheap citation packages never get crawled, which makes them worthless.

Test yours right now. Paste the URL of your Yelp or BBB listing into Google. If it doesn't come up, that citation does not exist as far as rankings are concerned.

---

THE STACK, IN ORDER

> Data aggregators first. Data Axle, Foursquare, and the feeds that push your info to hundreds of smaller sites. Fix these before anything else so bad data stops spreading downstream.

> The core listings. Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, BBB, Facebook, Nextdoor, Angi. These index within days because Google crawls them constantly.

> Industry specific. Houzz and Porch for remodelers, BuildZoom for contractors, ACCA for HVAC, your state license lookup. These carry relevance the generic directories never will.

> Geo specific. Chamber of commerce, local news business pages, city directories, the "best of" lists in your town. Competitors skip these because they take actual effort, which is exactly why they separate you.

75+ indexed citations is the floor in a competitive market. Pull the top competitor's count and beat it.

---

NAP DISCIPLINE

Your Google Business Profile is the source of truth. Whatever name, address, and phone number sit on the GBP goes on every citation, character for character.

That includes tracking numbers. If you run one on your GBP, which you should be doing if you actually track your SEO, then that same tracking number goes on the citations. The goal is a perfect match with the profile.

---

THE AI SEARCH ANGLE

Language models pull heavily from Better Business Bureau, local chambers of commerce, and Yelp when they recommend businesses.

So go past NAP on those three. Write a real description, load in your services, add products and photos wherever the platform allows it. Every field you fill out is more material an LLM can quote when someone asks ChatGPT for a plumber in your city.
