"Trade the trend" expresses a preference. You still have to decide what you will actually do on Friday at 8:30 when CPI prints.
For my week, that means: a fundamental bias on every asset I touch, a scenario map for every high-impact event, and a rule for which environment I'm allowed to trade in. Write the brief in this order:
Purpose: what should this trade achieve — day trade, swing, or skip entirely?
Event risk: which high-impact releases hit this week, and when do I stand down?
Bias: what is the fundamental direction on this asset right now, and what would change it?
Environment: is price in balance or imbalance?
Location: where did the majority of transactions actually take place?
Execution: what exactly is my entry, invalidation, and target?
Checks: what would make this trade unusable even if it wins?
Every line should make a checkable decision or protect one.
A useful trader names the scenario, gives the right expectation to each outcome, and explains the relationship between the print and the move. "CPI hot → dollar up" binds direction to data. "The closer the print lands to the extreme of the expectation range, the larger the repricing" adds a relationship you can inspect in the chart afterward.

Worked example: a CPI day, mapped before it happens
The job on CPI day is not to guess the number. The job is to map every plausible outcome to a reaction before the release, so the decision is already made when the data drops.
CPI is not one expectation, it's a range. Consensus might sit at 3.4%, but the market's real expectation has a minimum and a maximum band around it. Think of three scenario zones, and remember the rule that drives everything:
The closer the actual print lands to the extreme edges of the expectation range, the larger the market impact. A print in the dead center of expectations gets faded. A print at or beyond the extremes gets trended.
The scenario map I write before every CPI (plaintext)

SCENARIO A — AT OR BEYOND THE MAXIMUM (≥*.*%, the hot extreme):
Market impact: HIGH. This is an extreme print, maximum repricing.
Reaction: DXY breaks above its pre-release high aggressively, gold sells off
hard, indices drop on rate-fear.
Play: DO NOT fade the first move. Wait for the first 5–15 minute impulse to
complete, then trade the shallow pullback in the direction of the impulse.
Invalidation: if the impulse fully retraces within 30 minutes, the market is
absorbing it, stand down, the move is a liquidity grab.

SCENARIO B — DEAD CENTER (3.4%, consensus):
Market impact: LOW. The market already priced this.
Reaction: initial whipsaw both directions, then mean reversion back into the
pre-release range.
Play: this is a range day. Fade the extensions back to the volume profile's
point of control. Never trade the middle.

SCENARIO C — AT OR BEYOND THE MINIMUM (≤*.*%, the soft extreme):
Market impact: HIGH. Another extreme, maximum repricing, opposite direction.
Reaction: DXY sells off, gold rips, indices rally on cut-hope.
Play: same as Scenario A — trade the pullback, not the first candle.
Note: a soft extreme against the current bullish-USD bias FLIPS my weekly bias.

STANDING RULES:
- No entries in the 10 minutes before the release.
- First move after the release is information, not invitation.
- Extreme print + acceptance beyond the extreme = trend day. Trade it like one.
- Center print = chop day. Shrink size or don't trade.
What survived: the three-scenario map removes 90% of in-the-moment decision-making. When the print hit the soft extreme on my last CPI, I didn't hesitate — Scenario C was already written, the bias flip was already acknowledged, and I traded the pullback in gold with a clear invalidation.
What missed: the map doesn't tell you how far the impulse runs. On one CPI, the first move ran so far that the "shallow pullback" never came, and I missed the entire trend trying to buy a dip that never appeared. The repair: if the impulse never pulls back, the trade is missed — do not chase. Missing is a valid outcome.
Use the technique: write the scenario map before the release, with the minimum and maximum expectations explicit, and grade every print by how close it lands to an extreme. The extremes are where the market impact lives. The center is where accounts go to die.
Give every session an environment: balance or imbalance
 
Complexity becomes useful when you can explain which environment price is in. This is where auction theory comes in, and specifically the volume profile, which shows you where the majority of transactions actually took place. That is the single most honest piece of information on your chart, because it's not an opinion. It's what everyone actually did.
Before any entry, I answer one question: is price in balance or imbalance?
Balance = price has been accepted in a range, volume is distributed across it, the profile is fat. The market is agreeing on value.
Imbalance = price is moving away from a high-volume area with acceptance at new prices, the profile is thin. The market is rejecting old value and searching for new value.
Worked example: the balance day
If the profile says balance, my playbook is the range playbook. And the most important rule in the entire document:
In balance, you play the ranges, but never the middle of the range.
The middle of a balance area is where transactions are heaviest, the point of control sits there, and price has no edge in either direction. It's a coin flip with a spread. The edges of the range, the high-volume node's boundaries, the places where the auction ran out of participants before,  are where the risk/reward actually lives.
ENVIRONMENT CHECK — BALANCE:
- Overnight and London sessions built a clear range on ES, roughly 30 points wide
- Volume profile shows a fat, bell-shaped distribution with a clear POC near the center
- Price has returned to the POC three times and been rejected from the edges twice each
- MRKT bias: neutral-to-bullish, but no high-impact news for 6 hours, no fundamental pressure to break the balance

PLAY:
- Long only at the range low, where the profile shows the volume shelf
- Target: the POC first, then the opposite edge only on a momentum shift
- Hard rule: NO trades within the middle third of the range. If price is at the POC, I am flat. I would rather be bored than be the liquidity.
- Invalidation: acceptance beyond the range edge (two 5-minute closes outside, with volume). Then the environment has flipped to imbalance, stop trading the range immediately and switch playbooks.
What survived: the edges paid. Both range-low longs worked to the POC, and sitting out the middle kept me out of three fakeouts.
Worked example: the imbalance day
ENVIRONMENT CHECK — IMBALANCE:
- CPI printed at the soft extreme yesterday; the auction broke the prior balance and never returned, a profile with a single high-volume node below and thin volume above = price accepting new, higher value
- Overnight pullback held at the top of the prior balance area, old resistance,
  now being tested as support, with a low-volume node just above it
- MRKT bias (updated post-CPI): bullish indices, dovish repricing intact, next
  risk event not until Tuesday

PLAY: shallow pullback and continuation
- Entry: on the retest of the breakout area / first high-volume node below
- The pullback should be SHALLOW, in imbalance, deep pullbacks are a warning, not a gift. The market in control doesn't give you 50% retracements.
- Target: the next low-volume node above (price moves fast through thin areas and slows at fat ones)
- Invalidation: acceptance back inside the old balance area. If price re-enters
  and holds, the breakout failed and my bias for the day is wrong. Stand down.
What survived: the shallow pullback came, held at the volume shelf, and price ran the low-volume node to the next high-volume shelf exactly as auction theory describes. The MRKT bias gave me the confidence to hold through two minor dips instead of scalping out early..
Use the technique: the volume profile tells you where the majority of transactions took place. Balance or imbalance tells you which playbook is legal. The edge of the range or the shallow pullback tells you where the trade lives. The middle of the range and the middle of a move are where you donate.
