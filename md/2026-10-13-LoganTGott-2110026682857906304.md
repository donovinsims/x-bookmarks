# How to Figure Out Exactly What to Post (In 1 Afternoon)

## Post

Tell me if this sounds familiar...
You finally decide you're going to start posting.
You know it's good for your career. 
You've seen what it's done for other people. 
You're kind of excited about it (for once lol)
Then you actually sit down to write something and you have ZERO idea what to say. 
Everything you think of feels too obvious, too boring, or like something somebody else already said better.
So you tell yourself you'll figure it out tomorrow. 
Tomorrow turns into next week, next week turns into "I'm just not a content person," and a year goes by without you posting anything.
After my last article, a lot of people messaged me and commented saying "Okay, but what should I post now?"
 
Fair question lol. I spent that whole article telling you why to post and never told you what to post.
So this is the follow up. It's the same process I walk founders through when we're figuring out their content, and if you sit down and go through it in order, you'll walk away knowing what to post and with a plan for the next few weeks.
Grab your notes app. You're going to need it.
I. You Don't Know What to Post Because You Think It Has to Be Impressive
"Share something small every day." - Austin Kleon
The average people is NOT short on ideas. They're filtering every idea through a standard that's way too high.
They think every post needs to be some huge insight. 

→ Perfectly formatted. 
→ A nice video or clip attached to it. 
→ Something nobody has ever said before.
And since nothing they think of meets that bar, they post nothing.
But look at the people you actually follow and keep up with. 
I'd bet most of what they post is pretty normal. Their thoughts on something in their industry. What they did this week. What's working, what isn't. Something they noticed that nobody else seems to be talking about. The thing they're building that's kind of falling apart right now.
That's what people find interesting.
And when you post that stuff consistently, it makes you someone people trust, because they get to watch how you think and what you're doing over and over again, until eventually they feel like they know you.
The big ideas will come. I've had plenty of them show up in the middle of random posts I almost didn't write. But you don't get to the big ideas by waiting for them. You get there by posting consistently, learning how to take what's already working on your platform and put your own stuff into it.
You're not (necessarily) trying to go viral. You're trying to build an audience of people who care about what you do.
II. The Stuff That's Obvious to You Is Exactly What People Want to Read
When I started posting at 15, I figured nobody would care what some kid thought about making money online ( and so I stayed anonymous for over a year)
I was wrong though, and I think most people are wrong about their own content for the same reason.
Once you know something well, you forget what it was like to not know it. It stops feeling like knowledge and starts feeling like common sense. So you assume everyone else already knows it too, and you don't post it.
But the person who's 2 or 3 years behind you doesn't know it. They're stuck on the exact thing you figured out a while ago, and they'd love for somebody to just explain it to them.
That person is usually who finds you first.
You don't need to be the smartest person in your industry. You just need to be a few steps ahead of somebody and willing to say what you see from there.
Which brings us to the actual process.
III. The Protocol
There are 3 places your content comes from:
What you know well
What you're actively working on
What you enjoy
Most people only pull from 1 of these, usually the first, and then run out of things to say after a few weeks.
We're going to go through all 3, find where they overlap, and turn that into a plan. Set aside an afternoon. Don't hand these questions to AI (Unless it's already trained on you). The entire point is getting what's in your head out onto the page.
Part 1: What Do You Know Well?
Answer these (just open your notes app and use Wispr Flow):
What do people always come to you for?
What questions do friends, coworkers, or clients keep asking you?
What's something you used to be bad at and got good at?
What mistakes do you see beginners in your field make over and over?
What does everyone in your industry get wrong that kind of bugs you?
I like the last one a lot because if something in your industry annoys you, you already have an opinion on it, and opinions are what make people remember you.
Part 2: What Are You Actively Working On?
This is the list that makes sure you never run out of things to say, because it refills itself constantly.
Answer these:
What are you building, fixing, or testing right now?
What went wrong this week, and what did you do about it?
What's working better than you expected?
What did a client, customer, or coworker say recently that surprised you?
What did you change your mind about in the last few months?
When I started, a lot of what I posted was just what was going on that week. What worked, what didn't, what I was trying next. I wasn't following any strategy, I was pretty much just documenting.
And people love this, because it's happening live. They get to watch you figure things out, which builds way more trust than a perfectly packaged lesson ever could.
Part 3: What Do You Enjoy?
If you only post about what you think you're supposed to post about, you're going to quit. Maybe not in week 1. But by week 3 or 4 it starts to feel like homework, and nobody does homework for a year straight.
Enjoying it is what keeps you going. It also shows up in the writing. People can tell when you care about something, because the posts get more "technical" and you naturally have more opinions.
Answer these:
What could you talk about for an hour without getting bored?
What do you read, watch, or listen to when nobody's making you?
What topics do you bring up with friends even when it's not relevant lol?
If you had a free afternoon to learn about anything, what would it be?
Part 4: Find the Overlap
Now look at your 3 lists and circle anything that shows up in more than once.
For me it looks something like this. 

I know content, outbound, and funnels really well. I'm running an agency and building this stuff for clients every day. And I enjoy talking about AI, business, and how people build careers online. 

So almost everything I post sits somewhere in the middle of those.
Whatever's in your overlap is your core topics. That's what you want people to think of when they see your name.
Even if there's not direct overlap between the topic, you're still free to talk about it too. Just talk about it a little bit less!
IV. Borrow the Formats That Already Work
"Escape competition through authenticity." - Naval Ravikant
So now you know what to talk about. 
The next step is figuring out how to say it.
Go look at what's getting traction on your platform right now. 
Scroll through people in and around your industry and save the posts that are clearly doing well. Pay attention to how they're built. 
The hook, the length, whether it's a story or a list or a hot take, whether there's a visual, stuff like that ( I love to have Claude break down hundreds of posts a month to understand what's working and how I can replicate it)
Then take your topics and put them into those formats.
You're borrowing the structure of what works and filling it with your own experience and opinions.
This is where a lot of people mess up in 1 of 2 directions. Some skip it completely and wonder why nobody sees their posts. Others copy the topic along with the format and end up sounding like everyone else in their feed.
The format can be borrowed. What goes inside the post MUST be yours.
V. Run the Experiment
Post every day for a few weeks.
Pick from your overlap, use the formats you saved, and post. 
Don't overthink the formatting. 
Don't wait until you have a video for it. 

JUST GET OUT THERE AND POST.
At this stage you're kind of running an experiment on yourself, and the only goal is getting enough posts out that you can see what people respond to.
Then go back through everything and look at 3 things:
- Likes
- Comments
- DMs
All 3 matter.
Likes tell you people are interested in the topic. Comments tell you it made people think or want to say something. DMs tell you someone liked it enough to want to talk to you directly. Together, they're your proof that people care and that you're starting to build authority.
Find your best posts and figure out why they worked. Was it the topic? The format? The opinion?
Then find new angles on them. If a post about a hiring mistake did well, what's the next hiring story? What's the opposite take? What would you tell someone who's about to make the same mistake?
This is how a niche forms for most people. You don't pick it on day 1. You keep leaning into what's working until people start associating you with it.
VI. Turn It Into a System
You now have everything you need.
Pull out a new page and write down these 6 things:
Core topics: what's in your overlap
Side topics: what only showed up on 1 list
Formats: the posts you saved that are working on your platform
Winners: your best posts from the experiment, based on likes, comments, and DMs
New angles: fresh takes on your winners
Schedule: 1 post a day
This is your content plan.
Lean hardest into your winners and new angles. Keep mixing in whatever you're working on that week so it stays current. Throw in the stuff you enjoy so you don't burn out.
Every few weeks, run the experiment again. Look at what worked, update your winners, find new angles. The more you do it, the better your list gets, and the easier posting becomes, until eventually you're not sitting there wondering what to say anymore because you have more ideas than days to post them.
The 1 thing I'd keep in mind through all of this is to not blend in.
There are so many people posting generic stuff that anyone in their industry could have written, and nobody remembers any of it. 
The only thing you have that nobody else can copy is how you think and what you're doing, so say what you believe and post the messy version of your work.
If you read my last article and still haven't posted anything, do parts 1-4 tonight, pick 1 thing from your overlap, and post it tomorrow morning.
It doesn't need to be good,  you've just got to start!
