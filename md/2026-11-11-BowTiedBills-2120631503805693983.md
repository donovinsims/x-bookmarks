# How To Create An SEO Optimized Website... (In 10 Steps)

## Post

Around 70 to 75% of the local businesses I take on need a new website before anything else.

The old one has structural problems that cap how far it can ever rank, however many reviews and links we add on top.

So I've built a lot of websites with 1 goal: rank in Google and turn the visit into a phone call.

Before the agency, I worked on ranking and search algorithms as an engineer at FAANG.

Since then, my team has ranked 50+ law firms at #1 on Google.

This is the exact order we build in.

It works for a law firm, a dental clinic, a med spa or a contractor, and every step ends with a short checklist you can hand to whoever builds your site.

Here are the 10 steps:

Step 1: Map the searches that pay you

Step 2: Build a structure Google can follow

Step 3: Design for the phone first

Step 4: Write the 3 lines that matter most

Step 5: Give every service its own page

Step 6: Give every town its own page

Step 10: Connect it to Google and turn visits into calls

Step 1: Map the searches that pay you:

 

(My team builds SEO optimized websites for local businesses, so DM me "WEBSITE" if you want one built for yours)

Every website I build starts as a spreadsheet, before anyone opens a design tool.

It lists every search a paying client types into Google, matched to the page that will answer it.

How to build the map:

Column 1: every service you sell, one row each

Column 2: every town you want clients from

Column 3: the search a client would type, like "dog bite lawyer [town]" or "emergency dentist [town]"

Column 4: the page on your site that answers that search

A personal injury firm might end up with 6 services and 12 towns.

That's 6 service pages, 12 town pages and a clear answer for every search that brings in a case.

Leave out the searches that bring the wrong people:

A page called "how to file your own divorce papers" attracts people trying to avoid paying a lawyer.

Those visitors were never going to hire you.

Every page on the map should answer a question someone asks right before they pick up the phone.

The checklist:

One row for each service you want more of

One row for each town you want clients from

Every search on the map leads to one page, either a service page or a town page

No pages built for people who'll do it themselves

Step 2: Build a structure Google can follow:

Google finds most pages by following links from pages it already knows, and on a new site that usually starts at your homepage.

So the structure of the site is the map Google uses to understand what you do.

The structure I use for a local business:

Homepage: who you are, what you do and where

Service pages: one for each service, linked from the homepage

Town pages: one for each town, each linking back to the main service pages

Supporting pages: about, reviews, FAQ, contact

Every important page should be reachable in 3 or 4 clicks from the homepage.

In my experience, a page buried 6 clicks deep ranks worse, and Google may never find a page that nothing links to.

Keep the URLs plain:

yourfirm.com/dog-bite-lawyer

yourfirm.com/car-accident-lawyer

yourfirm.com/locations/springfield

Short, readable URLs with the service or the town in them show the visitor what the page is about before they open it.

Link pages to each other on purpose:

Each town page links to the services you offer there.

Each service page links to the towns you serve.

These internal links pass relevance between pages, and they help a visitor who landed on the wrong page find the right one.

The checklist:

Every service and town page linked from at least one other page

Plain URLs with the service or town in them

Every page reachable in 3 or 4 clicks from the homepage

A menu that shows your services and your locations

Step 3: Design for the phone first:

 

Most of your traffic is on a phone, and Google now looks at the mobile version of your site when it decides how to rank it.

So the phone version is the real website.

My team designs every page for mobile and desktop separately in Figma before building anything.

A desktop page squeezed down to fit a phone almost always buries the phone number.

What every mobile page needs:

A call button that stays on screen as the visitor scrolls

A contact form someone can fill in without scrolling

Text big enough to read without zooming

Buttons big enough to tap with a thumb

The test that takes 2 minutes:

Open your own website on your phone right now.

Time how long it takes to find the phone number and call it.

If it takes more than a few seconds, the person searching after a car accident has already called someone else.

The checklist:

Separate mobile and desktop designs, approved before the build

A sticky call button on every page

A form that fits on one phone screen

Tested on a real phone

Step 4: Write the 3 lines that matter most:

On every page, three pieces of text carry more weight than anything else in the structure:

The meta title: the blue link people see in Google

The meta description: the text Google often shows under it

The H1: the main heading on the page

Each one needs the service and the town in it, written the way a person would say it.

How I write each one:

Meta title: the service, the town and your firm name, in around 60 characters, which usually shows in full on a phone

Meta description: what you do, for whom and the next step, in plain words

H1: 1 per page, with the service and the town in it

What it looks like for a town page:

Meta title: Car Accident Lawyer in Springfield | Smith Law

Meta description: Hurt in a car accident in Springfield? Smith Law handles your claim from the first call. Free consultation, 24/7.

H1: Springfield Car Accident Lawyer

H2s and H3s organize the rest of the page, but these 3 lines move rankings the most.

The checklist:

A unique meta title on every page, around 60 characters

A meta description on every page that ends with the next step

One H1 per page, with the service and the town

Step 5: Give every service its own page:

 

Google ranks pages, and a page built around one service is a much stronger answer for that search.

A homepage that lists 10 services in one paragraph is a weak answer to all 10.

Why it matters:

Someone with a dog bite injury searches for a lawyer who handles dog bites.

If dog bites is a single line on your homepage, Google has no page to show them.

Give dog bites its own page, and you have something that can rank for that exact search.

What I put on every service page:

The meta title, description and H1 from step 4

Who the service is for, and the situations it covers

What happens after someone calls you, step by step

The questions clients ask about this service on the phone, answered on the page

Reviews from clients who used this service

A call button and a booking option

For a family law firm, that's:

Divorce

Child custody

Alimony

Child support

Someone searching "child support lawyer" has already decided they need one, so that page is one of the most valuable on the site.

Bilingual, where your market calls for it:

For an immigration firm on the East Coast, we built every service page in English and Spanish.

The Hispanic market is underserved in a lot of cities, and most competitors only optimize in English.

That firm went on to rank #1 at all 225 points on its statewide grid, with calls going from ~32 to 104 a month in its first 4 months.

The checklist:

One page for every service on your map

Each page answers the questions clients ask about that service

Reviews and a clear next step on every page

A second language version where a large part of your market speaks it

Step 6: Give every town its own page:

Rankings drop the further someone is from your office.

You can't rank in the map pack for "divorce lawyer [big city]" downtown from an office 40 minutes away.

Town pages are how you show up in the surrounding towns that are close enough to serve.

What a town page needs (the version that ranks):

An H1 with the service, the town and your firm name

A Google Map of that area embedded on the page, showing your business profile

Details only someone local would know, like which courthouse handles the cases or the landmarks nearby

Reviews from clients in or near that town, where you have them

Internal links back to your main service pages

What gets a town page ignored:

Copying the same page 20 times and swapping the town name.

Google sees 20 pages saying the same thing and has no reason to rank any of them.

Each town page needs something on it that's only true of that town.

The checklist:

One page for every town on your map

A local detail on every page that couldn't be copied to another town

A map embed on every page

Links from each town page to the matching service pages

Step 7: Write for the person who'll pay you:

 

Search engines care what your website says and who's saying it.

The design matters to the visitor, but Google ranks the page on what it says.

Write for buyers:

Every page should help someone who's about to hire you make the decision.

The traffic ticket firm I worked with skipped "how to fight it yourself" content and wrote about red light cameras instead: where the new cameras are, what the fines cost and what the firm does about a ticket.

That's content for someone holding a ticket right now.

Keep it simple to read:

A person searching for a lawyer after an accident is stressed and reading on a phone.

Write in short sentences and plain words, and put the answer near the top of the page.

Answer the question first:

Put the most common question about the service at the top of the page and answer it in the first 2 or 3 sentences.

That's what the visitor came for, and a clear answer near the top is easier for AI tools to quote.

Show who's behind it:

Put real photos of your team and office on the site, with their names, credentials and client reviews.

A stranger deciding who to trust with a legal problem wants to see the people.

The checklist:

Every page written for someone close to hiring you

The main question answered in the first few sentences

Short sentences, plain words

Real photos, names and reviews on the site

Step 8: Add schema that matches your Google profile:

Schema is code on your page that tells Google exactly what your business is.

For a local business, that's things like your name, address, phone number, hours and the services you offer.

Google documents this as "LocalBusiness" structured data and asks you to use the most specific type that fits, like "Dentist" or "LegalService".

The rule that matters most:

The name, address and phone number in your schema have to match your Google Business Profile exactly.

The same goes for every directory listing.

Google trusts your listing a little less for every mismatch it finds, so one old phone number in your schema works against you.

What I include:

The business type that fits you best

Name, address and phone, word for word as they appear on the profile

Opening hours

The services or practice areas

The areas you serve

How to check it:

Run the page through Google's Rich Results Test before it goes live.

It shows you which parts of the schema Google can use and flags errors in them.

The checklist:

Schema on the homepage and every location page

Name, address and phone matching the profile exactly

Hours matching the profile and every directory

Tested in the Rich Results Test before launch

Step 9: Make it fast and clean:

 

A slow page loses the visitor before it finishes loading.

Google measures this with 3 numbers it calls Core Web Vitals.

The 3 numbers, with Google's "good" thresholds:

Largest Contentful Paint: the main content loads within 2.5 seconds

Interaction to Next Paint: the page responds to a tap within 200 milliseconds

Cumulative Layout Shift: the layout stays put while it loads, with a score of 0.1 or less

You can check them for free in PageSpeed Insights, though INP only shows once the site has enough real visitors.

The technical cleanup:

My team fixes all of this in the first month of every engagement:

Broken links

404 pages

Oversized images

Missing alt text on images

Missing meta titles

A photo straight off a phone can be several megabytes, so every image gets compressed before it goes on the page.

The checklist:

Core Web Vitals in the "good" range on mobile, once there's enough real visitor data

Every image compressed and given alt text

Zero broken links and zero 404s on internal links

Every page has a meta title

Step 10: Connect it to Google and turn visits into calls:

A finished website still has to be found, and every visit still has to turn into a call.

Connect it to Google:

Set up Google Search Console and verify your domain

Submit your sitemap there, so Google has a list of every page you want crawled

Check the Page indexing report over the next few weeks to make sure your pages are being indexed

Set up Google Analytics and call tracking, so you can see which pages bring in calls

A page Google never indexed can't rank, however good it is.

Turn the visit into a call:

After a car accident, people call 5 firms.

Whoever picks up first wins.

That's why I embed a calendar booking right on the site.

A booked 15 minute call takes them off the market, because they're not going to call 3, 4 or 5 other firms once they've booked.

Free tools that bring in leads:

A calculator can rank for its own searches and capture the lead before they call another firm.

Personal injury: a case value calculator, since everyone on a no win no fee case already has a number in their head

Family law: alimony, child support and child custody calculators

This doesn't work for immigration or criminal law, because there's nothing to calculate.

The checklist:

Search Console verified and the sitemap submitted

Pages showing as indexed

Analytics and call tracking live

A booking calendar on the main service pages

A calculator, if your practice area has something to calculate

After launch:

 

The website is the part of SEO that takes longest to move.

Changes to your Google Business Profile can show up in the map pack within a couple of days.

A website has to be crawled again, compared against everyone else and ranked again, which usually takes 2 to 4 weeks for each change.

So I track a few numbers every month:

Calls and form fills from the website

Clicks and impressions in Search Console

Rankings for every service page and town page

Which pages bring in the calls, from call tracking

Then I build more of whatever is working: more town pages where calls are coming in, and more service pages for the cases you want more of.

Design the phone version first, with a sticky call button

Put the service and the town in the meta title, the meta description and the H1

Give every service its own page, and every town its own page with local details

Write for buyers, and answer the main question near the top

Add schema that matches your Google profile exactly

Get Core Web Vitals into the "good" range and clean up the technical issues
