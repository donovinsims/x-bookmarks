# If I owned an HVAC company today and wanted to dominate Google Maps in my city, here's exactly what I'd do for the firs…

## Post

If I owned an HVAC company today and wanted to dominate Google Maps in my city, here's exactly what I'd do for the first 12 months.
 
(Bookmark this)
 
First thing I'm doing is studying the demand cycle.
 
HVAC is one of the only home service trades with two peak seasons. Summer drives AC repair and replacements. Winter drives furnace and heat pump emergencies. Spring and fall are tune-up season, which most operators don't market successfully. 

That's a huge missed window because tune-ups are the cheapest way to load up your future replacement pipeline.
 
Around 75% of my leads are coming from local search. Map Pack, Google Ads, and Local Services Ads. Aggregators, social, and referrals barely move the needle once you're ranking locally. The whole 12-month plan gets built around owning local search because they are the warmest, high-intent leads out there.
 
I'd pull up Ahrefs and look at the money keywords for my market:
 
> "AC repair near me"
> "AC repair [my city]"
> "HVAC contractor [my city]"
> "furnace repair [my city]"
> "heat pump installation [my city]"
> "AC tune up [my city]"
 
Then the high-ticket long tail:
 
> "AC unit replacement [city]"
> "HVAC system installation [city]"
> "ductless mini split installation"
> "ductwork replacement [city]"
 
The repair searches are constant lead flow. The replacement keywords are the $8K to $30K tickets. Both matter, but a single closed replacement lead can pay for three months of marketing.
 
Day one I'm rebuilding the GBP from scratch.
 
This is the single biggest lever in HVAC. If I only fix one thing this month, it's this:
 
> Primary category set to HVAC contractor. Every secondary slot filled with the right ones: air conditioning repair service, heating contractor, furnace repair service, air duct cleaning service, gas installation service, heating equipment supplier. Most HVAC profiles I audit have 1 or 2 categories filled. Filling out every relevant slot is a free ranking signal.
> Business name optimized through a legal DBA. I'd file a fictitious name like "[Brand] Heating and Air Conditioning" and verify it with Google. Not a trick, an actual legal filing.
> Service area set to cities within a 1.5 hour drive of the shop. Push the radius wider than that and Google decides you don't really cover those cities and ranks you nowhere.
> Physical address used wherever possible. Service-area-only profiles get smoked by physical-address profiles in local search. If you're working out of your house, get a real warehouse or shop address.
> 50+ geo-tagged photos uploaded, refreshed monthly. Real photos of techs, vans, install jobs, panel work, ductwork, condensers in driveways. Two photos per job, ops person uploads on a set day each month.
> Every service listed individually with 100+ word descriptions. AC repair, AC installation, furnace repair, furnace installation, heat pump installation, mini split installation, ductwork, indoor air quality, maintenance plans.
> Products section filled out with every main service. Most HVAC operators have never even opened this section. It's a full ranking signal sitting empty, with photos, descriptions, and starting prices that LLMs and Google both read.
 
Reviews are where I'd put consistent energy month after month.
 
Here's what nobody tells home service owners about reviews. My competitor sitting on 600 reviews who stopped generating them eight months ago is completely beatable by me with 150 reviews and 28 in the last 30 days. Google weighs recency as much as total count.
 
So my system runs like this:
 
> QR code stickers in every truck and on every invoice, linked to my Google review URL
> Every tech trained to ask in person right after the install or repair: "If everything looks good, would you mind scanning this and leaving a quick Google review? Takes 30 seconds."
> $25-50 paid out to the tech per review
> Automated SMS follow-up at 3 hours for anyone who didn't leave one on site
> 24-hour response on every review, positive and negative, signed by name
> A monthly call from the owner to past five-star customers asking for any unwritten reviews
 
An HVAC company we work with went from 8 reviews a month to 34 in 60 days running this system. That single shift moved them from position 11 to top 3 in their primary city.
 
Website gets restructured to actually rank.
 
Most HVAC sites are built backwards. One "services" page with bullets for AC, furnace, heat pump, mini split, and ductwork all crammed together. Zero location pages. A homepage that says "your trusted HVAC partner since 1998" and not much else. Google can't rank a Swiss Army knife page for 5 different keywords.
 
Here's the structure I'd build:
 
> Homepage focused on the primary city plus the main service combo (AC + heating)
> One dedicated service page per service offered. AC repair, AC installation, furnace repair, furnace installation, heat pump installation, ductless mini split, ductwork, indoor air quality
> Each service page 1,200 to 1,800 words minimum with FAQs, photos, pricing context, financing options visible, and proper schema
> One location page for every surrounding city I actually serve, written from scratch and never spun from a template. Local landmarks, neighborhood names, climate-specific issues like high humidity, hard water on coil pans, or older homes with undersized ductwork
> Internal linking connecting every service page to every location page so Google understands you offer every service across every city
> NAP identical across every page, footer, contact form, and schema markup
> Mobile PageSpeed score above 85
 
Conversion rate matters as much as the rankings.
 
Phone number top right with click-to-call wired straight to the dialer on mobile. Form fields kept to name, phone, and service needed. Trust signals above the fold like review count, NATE certification badges, manufacturer certifications (Carrier, Trane, Lennox), license number, financing partner logos, and years in business. Every extra form field costs about 10% of conversions.
 
Most home service sites convert under 1%. Well-built ones convert 3 to 5%. Same traffic, three to five times the leads.
 
Citations and backlinks are how I'd build trust faster than my competitors.
 
For citations, I'm getting listed on at least 80 directories with identical NAP. Yelp, BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Nextdoor, Yellow Pages, Foursquare, Thumbtack, the local chamber, ACCA member directory, manufacturer dealer locators. One mismatch like a missing suite number or a different phone format hurts trust with Google.
 
Backlinks are where home service businesses get scammed...
 
A vendor offering 500 backlinks for $99 is selling you a Google penalty. Real link building in a competitive market costs $500 to $3,000 a month.
 
The links worth building for HVAC:
 
> Manufacturer dealer pages. Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Mitsubishi mini split dealer locator pages all link back to dealers
> Supplier pages like Ferguson HVAC, Johnstone Supply, and Gemaire dealer profiles
> Trade association memberships. ACCA, RSES, BPI, NATE
> Chamber of commerce, state HVAC contractor board
> Local news features through HARO, Qwoted, or direct outreach. Heat wave advice, cold snap prep, and energy bill stories all get pickup
> Youth sports sponsorships and local nonprofits ($300 to $500 gets a .org backlink)
> Partnered company links. Direct outreach to plumbers, electricians, and roofers in your market for legitimate cross-link partnerships. Almost nobody does this and the links are highly relevant
> University trade program partnerships
> High traffic home improvement blogs
 
Press releases are the cheat code for AI search.
 
I'd publish one a month:
 
> Heat wave call volume report
> Annual breakdown of most common HVAC failures by season
> A new financing option for replacement systems
> Charitable furnace replacement for a local family in winter
> Storm response coverage for HVAC damage during weather events
 
What matters is the business name showing up next to "HVAC" and the city name across news sites and aggregators. That's how AI search starts citing you.
 
Most HVAC competitors aren't thinking about AI search yet. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude are getting asked "who's the best HVAC company in [city]" thousands of times a week, and the businesses showing up in those answers are the ones with consistent press history, strong GBP signals, and listicle placements.
 
90-day AI sprint:
 
> Month 1: 2 press releases, 3 useful answers in r/[mycity] and r/[mycity]Homeowners as the owner (not the company), pitch 3 local "best HVAC company in [city]" listicles
> Month 2: 2 more releases, 3 more Reddit posts, follow up on the listicles, get listed on Thumbtack and Angi (both now feeding Claude and ChatGPT directly)
> Month 3: audit which LLMs are citing me and double down on what's working
 
We ran this exact sprint for an HVAC company and started showing up in ChatGPT answers for "best HVAC in [city]" within ~75 days.
 
The conversion lever most HVAC operators leave on the table is the maintenance plan.
 
This isn't really an SEO move, but it's the highest-leverage thing the SEO traffic feeds into. A maintenance plan customer is worth 4-6x a one-time repair customer over their lifecycle. They become 80% of the replacement leads when the system finally fails. Every form submission and inbound call should have a clear path into a maintenance plan offer.
 
Pair that with financing options visible on every service page. The average HVAC ticket above $5K means roughly 60% of customers want financing. If your competitor displays their financing options on the service page and you don't, they're closing deals you never even quoted.
 
Here's the timeline of actual results:
 
Month 1: foundation built. GBP rebuilt, all service and location pages live, 80+ citations cleaned, review system running with techs trained.
 
Months 2 to 4: ranking movements start. Keywords I wasn't ranking for at all start appearing in positions 10-20. Reviews ramping past 25 a month.
 
Months 4 to 7: Map Pack movement. Top 5 in primary city for primary keywords across a decent radius.
 
Months 7 to 12: top 3 in primary city Map Pack. Long-tail and high-ticket replacement keywords kicking in for organic. AI search citations appearing.
 
Smaller markets with weaker competition compress this to 4 to 6 months. Going against PE-backed regional HVAC giants in Phoenix or Atlanta stretches it closer to a full 12 months.
 
The biggest mistake I'd avoid is hiring a cheap agency and expecting any of this to actually happen.
 
Run the full stack properly for 12 months, hit it harder during shoulder seasons when your competitors get lazy, and you'll own your city.
