# The home service industry has a weird divide right now.

## Post

The home service industry has a weird divide right now.
 
Half the operators I talk to are running $5M+ businesses on an old Samsung phone and a paper schedule book.
 
The other half are spending $80K on tech stacks, AI dispatchers, automated review platforms, branded apps, and quoting software, and doing $700K a year.
 
I've been thinking about why.
 
The $5M flip phone guy has the fundamentals locked. Phone gets answered fast. Tech shows up when he said he would. Customer gets asked for the review every single time. He'd be doing $8M with the right tools.
 
The $700K guy is buying tools to skip past the fundamentals. Hoping software will fix something a process should fix first. AI dispatcher routing calls his CSR isn't trained to close. Automated review platform asking for reviews from customers his techs never asked in person. Branded app for customers who don't know his company exists yet.
 
The pattern I see in operators who scale: they nail the fundamentals manually first, then layer in tech to multiply what's already working.
 
The pattern I see in stuck operators: they buy tech hoping it'll do the work the business hasn't figured out yet.
 
Same tool, opposite outcome. Depends entirely on what's underneath it.

I guess my message is that tech won't fix your business, it will amplify what you already have.
