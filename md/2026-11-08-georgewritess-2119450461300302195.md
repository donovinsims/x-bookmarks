# Learn how to write scientifically addictive hooks in the next 7 minutes:

## Post

After generating 2.4 billion views online, it's time to teach you how to do the same.

A successful hook will stop your ICP for about a sec, make them see themselves in it, create a gap that can be filled with your copy and lastly include a point that they didn't expect.

Which is why before I post anything, I run every hook through 5 ideas backed by attention research.

And before we get into them, keep in mind that eye tracking studies found that people read the first few lines of a page, then skim down the left side and only catch the first couple of words on each line.

On an average page, they read just 20-28% of the words. A hook gets even less time, so every single word you add has to earn its place.

So by the end of this article, you’ll have:

My top 5 core ideas for writing hooks that hypnotize the reader

A simple rule for each idea.

Weak vs strong hook examples you can copy

Open your notes, Wispr Flow, or your notebook and lock in:

The 5 things I check in every hook

Forget the generic hook advice like “be specific or curious ”- we’re going inside your ICP’s head, and by the end, some of you will call these techniques manipulative.

1.  Mirror your reader

People are selfish, so 9/10 times they stop for themselves rather than your topic, which means our job is to reflect them.

In listening studies, about 1/3 of people heard their own name in a conversation they were told to ignore, and memory research shows that people remember info way better when it’s about THEM.

So our goal is to show the reader’s identity, or a specific moment from their life, inside the hook.

Example:

Weak hook: “Most people quit the gym, here’s how to stay consistent.”

Strong hook: “If you lied to yourself about hitting the gym again, you’re teaching yourself that your word means nothing. Here’s how to fix it:”

2. Let them half guess the answer

Surprisingly, most hooks fail because they’re giving away TOO LITTLE or TOO MUCH.

When researchers asked people general knowledge questions in a brain scanner, their curiosity peaked when people were about 50% sure of the answer and nuked when they had no idea.

A reader who cannot guess at all keeps scrolling, and a reader who already knows the ending does the same.

Thats why I show the hook to 3 people and ask them to guess what the post says.

If all 3 guess right, I am giving away too much, and if none of them can guess at all, I add one more piece of context.

3. Put one surprise in the line

The eye goes to whatever the brain didn’t predict.

On your feed, the reader’s brain is predicting your next line from the last 100 posts they’ve seen on the topic, so one unexpected detail is what guarantees a second look.

Again the rule is simple

Add one unexpected detail and keep everything else simple, so it stands out.

4. Use the exact numbers

A precise number is more believable than a round one, even when both are true, because a round figure reads like a guess, while an exact figure reads like someone counted, so it seems more legit.

In a 2006 consumer study, people treated claims with sharp numbers such as 11 as factual, and claims with round numbers such as 10 as more likely to be estimates.

5. Make it readable in one breath

Lastly, and most importantly, make sure it’s DEAD simple and short to read.

People’s scrolling speed is increasing  rapidly, so there’s no time to confuse them or make them read it twice to understand it. They’ll get annoyed and scroll away.

So the process I follow to nail that has 2 steps:

I read the hook out loud at normal speed, and if I run out of breath before the end, I cut it until I don’t.

I paste it into the Hemingway Editor, a free readability tool, and keep swapping long words for short ones until it scores grade 6 or lower.

The habit that changed my results

In my first 8 to 12 months as a writer, I used to spend around 2 hours writing the body of a post and finish the hook in just 2 minutes.

Since then, I spend about 50% of my writing time on the first line, writing 10 versions and posting the one that passes all 5 checks.

Now I see 90% of creators and “writers” making the same mistake I did. They use a shit AI-written hook and wonder why their posts don’t perform.

Writing hooks is a scientific process, and AI has made everyone forget what a good hook looks like and how to create one.
