# Most home service websites convert UNDER 1%.

## Post

Most home service websites convert UNDER 1%.

The ones we build are closer to 6%.

Same traffic but 4x the booked jobs.

It comes down to getting a handful of boring things right. Here's the whole playbook for a home service homepage.

1. The hero section has to answer three questions in under two seconds.

What do you do, where do you do it, and how do I reach you. Big clear headline naming the service. The city or region right under it. A phone number that's visible, not buried, because a lot of older customers will read it off to their spouse instead of tapping it. And a real photo of your actual work, never a stock photo of a smiling stranger in a clean uniform. If someone has to hunt for what you do, they already left.

2. Put trust signals where they can't be missed.

Your Google rating and review count up top. Licensed and insured, locally owned, years in business, any guarantee you offer, as simple icons near the headline. Someone is about to hand a stranger their address and their money. Show them other people did it and were glad. This is subconscious credibility and it moves conversion more than any color or font choice.

3. Make booking stupid easy and give them more than one way to do it.

Some people want to call right then. Others would rather fill out a quick form, and a good chunk want to book online at 11pm without talking to anyone. Give them all of it. A tap-to-call button that sticks to the screen as they scroll, a form that asks for the bare minimum, and online scheduling if you have it. Every extra field and every extra click costs you people.

4. Build it mobile-first, because that's where they are.

Most of your traffic is on a phone, standing in a kitchen looking at a leak. If the page loads slow or the buttons are tiny, they bounce to the next company. Fast load, big tap targets, the phone number and CTA always within thumb reach.

5. Your ad traffic needs its own landing pages, separate from your SEO pages.

This one trips up almost everyone. SEO pages need content to rank, so they're longer. Ad pages need to convert, so they're stripped down to one service, trust signals, and a single clear action. Send paid traffic to a page built to convert, not to a homepage built to rank. They do different jobs, so they need different pages.

That's the main goal on a home page and every location page. Clear offer, trust, dead-simple action, fast on mobile.

We rebuilt a site for an electrical company doing about $40K a month and their leads roughly doubled. We don't even run their marketing lol.

Stop redesigning for looks... just build it to convert.
