# One Hour Heating & Air in Charlotte has about 6,000 Google reviews.

## Post

One Hour Heating & Air in Charlotte has about 6,000 Google reviews.

Morris-Jenkins has about 34,000.

If I ran One Hour, I wouldn't try to win that review race. Here's what I'd do instead.

First, what they do well. They're a 4.8, BBB accredited with an A+ since 1989, and they have separate pages for AC repair, AC installation, and maintenance. One Hour is a national franchise brand under Authority Brands, and the Charlotte location is locally owned.

The gaps are all in how they cover the metro.

1. They're not running the multi-location playbook

Their website lists about 45 towns they serve across North and South Carolina, from Hickory down to Chester, SC. They have two physical locations: Charlotte and Conover.

A Google Business Profile only ranks a few miles from its address. So for most of those 45 towns, One Hour is invisible in the map pack no matter how many reviews they have. Fort Mill and Rock Hill are on their list with no pin anywhere near them.

Compare that to A1 Garage Door. A1 runs 15+ profiles across Phoenix alone, each one placed to rank top 3 in its own part of the metro. That's a big reason Google Maps is one of their best lead sources.

As a brand, One Hour is as big as A1. Its franchisees did about $330M in 2024, per the brand's franchise disclosure document. The franchise model makes this harder, since each owner only covers the area in their agreement. But inside an area the size of Charlotte, the same playbook applies.

What I'd do:

> Pull a year of sold jobs from the CRM and map them by zip
> Run a grid scan for "AC repair near me" across the metro to see where they drop out of the top 3
> Open the next location where good tickets and weak rankings overlap. If the franchise agreement allows it, I'd look at the South Carolina side first.
> Give it a real office, its own tracking number, and its own location page

Morris-Jenkins can't be close to every searcher either. A One Hour pin in the right suburb, getting steady reviews, has a real shot at the top 3 there.

2. Their city pages are the same page with the name swapped

They have about 36 city pages. The Matthews and Huntersville pages read like one template with a different town name dropped in.

That doesn't work anymore. Google sees a stack of near-identical pages, treats them as duplicates, and most of them never get indexed. There's nothing on the Matthews page a homeowner couldn't get from the Huntersville page, so Google has no reason to rank either one.

A location page stands out when it has information only that company has. Here's how I'd build each one:

> Pull every inbound call from that area in the last 6 months, have AI sort them, and build a database of what customers there actually call about
> Write up those problems, how you solve them, and what's different about that area
> Embed a live map of the jobs you've run in that town
> Embed reviews from customers who live there
> Cover the local weather and what it does to the equipment there
> Add real job photos, local price ranges, and the neighborhoods you serve
> Start with the 8 to 10 towns that matter most, then add more once those are indexed

3. Two websites, three phone numbers

There's a second One Hour site listed at the same Charlotte street address with a different phone number. Between the two sites and their BBB listing, I found three different numbers.

Google checks your name, address, and phone number across the web to confirm you're a real business at a real location. When those don't match:

> Google has less confidence in the profile, which weakens it in the map pack
> Directories, AI tools, and customers can pick up the wrong number
> Calls ring to numbers you aren't tracking, so you can't tell which marketing is working
> Two sites at one address compete with each other for the same searches

I'd pick one site, give each location one tracking number, and make every listing match that location's Google profile exactly.

4. No pricing anywhere

No cost guides, no calculators, no price ranges. A page on what a new AC costs in Charlotte, with real local ranges, is one of the easiest pages to rank right now. It's also the page replacement buyers search for, and the kind of page AI tools pull from when someone asks what a new system costs.
