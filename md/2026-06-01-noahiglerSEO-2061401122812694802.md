# An HVAC company called me 7 months ago ready to shut off his entire ad budget.

## Post

An HVAC company called me 7 months ago ready to shut off his entire ad budget.

He was paying over $600 per lead on Google. For a residential service call, that math kills you. Two more bad quarters and the whole channel was gone.

We just pulled his dashboard. $1.27M in the last 90 days, almost all of it from search and paid.

We didn't do anything clever. We ran the same two ideas Hormozi drills into every operator, except built for home service. Here's the whole thing.

First, fix the offer before you scale the leads. For a home service company, the offer IS the website experience. About 99% of contractor websites convert under 1%. If yours is one of them, more traffic just means you're paying retail to lose customers faster.

The fix isn't another redesign. It's killing friction:

> A 6-word headline that names the outcome, not the category ("90-minute same-day AC repair" beats "HVAC Services in Dallas")
> Reviews, before/afters, the owner's name, the guarantee, all above the fold
> Online booking with real calendar availability
> Instant estimate or pricing ranges so they don't have to call to find out if they can afford you
> A sticky mobile bar with call/quote/book that follows them down the page

Get a home service site from 1% to 4-5% and you pull 3-5x the revenue out of the exact same traffic. Before you touch a single keyword.

That's why the website comes first. If you get pumped up about ranking and skip this step, you're just funneling more $40 clicks into a page that takes 30 seconds to figure out.

Second, show up three times before your competitor shows up once.

There are three spots on a Google search that matter: the AI answer at the top, the map pack, and the organic results. Most contractors fight for one and leave the other two wide open. That's the whole opening.

For the map pack, the biggest lever isn't reviews. It's a name match. A company called "Smith HVAC" will outrank "Smith Brothers" for "HVAC near me" even with fewer reviews, because the keyword is in the name. That's a doing-business-as filing done carefully (mess it up and you get suspended, so this is not a DIY weekend project).

After that it's the boring stuff that compounds:

> Primary category set to the exact service ("Air Conditioning Repair Service," not just "HVAC Contractor")
> Every service loaded in as a service AND as a product, so AI search has structured data to pull from
> Geotagged job photos uploaded every week
> And the real engine: review velocity. Total reviews matter, star rating matters, but the number you get month after month is what actually tells Google and the AI engines you're trustworthy. 30+ a month in a competitive trade.

The review system that gets you there is dead simple. Tech finishes the job, asks in person before leaving, hands over a QR code. Customer scans, leaves the review in 20 seconds, tech gets a bonus. No review? Text at 12 hours, email at 24, owner calls at 48. That's how you get 15-30 a month instead of one when you remember.

For AI search specifically, the move is structured Q&A content, citations across 80-120 directories with an identical name/address/phone, and a real service page for every service plus a location page for every city. Then internal-link them together. Most agencies are still writing "top 10 HVAC maintenance tips" blogs that rank for nothing and book nobody.

Third, and this is where the $600 leads stopped being a problem: stop optimizing for cost per lead. Optimize for LTV to CAC.

Cost per lead is a vanity number. Here's the math. A $600 lead that books a $400 drain clear loses you money. The same $600 lead that books a $20K system replacement is a 33:1 return. Same lead cost. Completely different decision.

Most operators are out there killing their best campaigns because the cost per lead looks scary, when it's actually an LTV problem. The cheapest leads are almost always the worst customers. The ones worth the most usually cost the most to get.

So we segmented his account by service tier instead of dumping everything into one "HVAC" campaign. Isolated replacements from tune-ups from service calls. Let Google's learning phase run about 60 days. Then doubled the budget on the high-LTV stuff.

We did get his cost per lead down, from $600 to about $150 in one of the most competitive markets in the country. But that wasn't the needle mover. The needle mover was making the math right so he could outspend every competitor on every click and still print.

Three things to take with you:

1. Order matters. Website first, then SEO and AI search, then ads. Run traffic into a broken site and you lose money no matter how good the traffic is.
2. Show up in all three search spots. Your competitors are handing you two of them.
3. LTV to CAC, not cost per lead. The expensive lead that buys the big job beats the cheap lead that buys nothing.

(None of this is a secret. It's just that almost nobody does all three at once, consistently, for two years straight. That's the entire moat.)
