# how to run outbound from your teams linkedin accounts (zero bans)

## Post

we run hundreds of real linkedin profiles for our clients, and we've gone 18 months without a single ban.

those accounts serve 140+ b2b companies across 30+ industries.

your own team's linkedin accounts are the best outbound asset most companies already own, and this is how to use them safely.

what one well-run account can carry.

on a campaign we ran for a client, one of our profiles sent 1,935 inmails within 7 days.

400 people responded, and more than 200 of those responses were positive.

why your team's accounts work so well:

they're real people with real history.

linkedin trusts accounts that have been active for years, with real connections and real posts.

prospects trust them too.

when a prospect checks the profile, they see a real person at a real company.

each account adds capacity.

linkedin limits how much one account can send, so 5 team members can reach 5 times as many people.

each person has their own network.

your head of sales, your founder, and your account managers are each connected to different people.

step 1: get the team on board:

outbound from someone's account only works if they're happy with it.

what to tell them:

what messages will go out from their account, and roughly how many a day

who will read and answer the replies

that the messages will be written in their voice, and they can read every one

that sending will stay at safe levels, so their account stays healthy

what to agree on:

whether they answer replies themselves or pass them to a central person

how quickly replies get answered

what happens if they leave the company

get each person's clear agreement before connecting their account.

step 2: choose which accounts to use:

the best accounts to use:

have been active for at least a year

have 500+ connections

belong to people with titles that fit your buyer, like founders, sales leaders, and senior managers

have posted or commented in the last few months

match the sender to the buyer.

a founder's account works well for messaging other founders and executives.

an account manager's profile works well for messaging managers and team leads.

step 3: get each profile ready:

every prospect will look at the sender's profile before replying.

each profile needs:

a clear photo of their face

a headline that says what the company does and the result it gets

an about section, written in first person

their current role at your company, with the company page linked

a few recent posts or comments

link every account to your company page, so prospects see a real team when they click through.

step 4: connect the accounts safely:

this step is where most bans come from, so take it slowly.

keep each account in one place.

linkedin notices when an account suddenly logs in from a new country or a new device.

each account should always connect from the same location as the person who owns it.

use a tool built for this.

a proper outbound tool gives each account its own stable connection that matches the owner's location, and paces the activity like a person would.

inmailer, our own software, does this and spreads sending across the working day for every account.

keep each login with 1 person in 1 place.

step 5: warm each account up:

even an old account needs warming if it hasn't been used for outbound before.

the warmup, over 3 to 4 weeks:

week 1: a handful of connection requests daily, somewhere between 5 and 10, to relevant people, plus some likes and comments and zero cold outreach

week 2: connection requests go up, and the owner chats normally with new connections

week 3: the first cold messages go out in small numbers, each worded differently

week 4 onward: a consistent daily pace

give every account its own start date, a day or 2 apart.

watch the acceptance rate in week 1.

if fewer than 20% of connection requests get accepted, the targeting is off, so fix it before sending more.

step 6: split the list between accounts:

give each account its own set of prospects.

2 team members messaging the same person looks spammy and gets reported.

how to split the list:

by region, so each person owns an area

by industry, so each person owns a type of company

by seniority, so senior accounts message senior buyers

keep one master list that shows which account has contacted which prospect.

step 7: set safe limits for every account:

keep each account well inside linkedin's limits.

around 100 connection requests a week at most

messages spread across the working day

lighter activity at weekends

no sudden jumps in volume

reply rate decides how much you can send.

the more your messages get answered, the more linkedin lets that account send, and accounts with poor response get held back.

good targeting and relevant messages protect every account on the team.

how many team accounts you need:

work it out from how many people you want to reach each month.

the maths:

decide how many prospects you want to message each month

set a safe number for each account, starting low and raising it once the account is warm

divide the first number by the second

add 1 or 2 spare accounts for any that need a rest

for example, 4,000 messages a month at a safe 500 per account needs 8 accounts, plus 1 or 2 spares.

where the extra capacity comes from.

open profiles and event attendees can be messaged without using up connection requests, so point most of each account's volume at them.

save connection requests for the most valuable prospects.

step 8: write messages in each person's voice:

the message structure:

a result, with a figure, from a business like the prospect's

one short question

match the sender.

a founder can write about the company's vision and results.

a sales rep can write about the specific problem they solve.

write several versions.

sending the identical message from 5 accounts to thousands of people gets flagged.

give each account its own versions.

let each person approve their messages.

they'll be more comfortable, and they'll spot anything that sounds off.

what several accounts can do together.

an ai automations agency had been running linkedin outbound for months with a near-empty calendar.

we changed their offer to a free automation built for the prospect, and ran it across 2 of their profiles.

we sent 1,083 inmails over 9 days.

25.8% replied, 35 were interested, and 14 booked calls.

step 9: decide who answers the replies:

option 1: the account owner answers.

best when the owner is a founder or senior person, since prospects expect to talk to them.

option 2: one person answers for every account.

best when team members are busy with other work.

that person answers in the account owner's voice and hands over once a call is booked.

whichever you choose, reply fast.

speed changes everything here.

we see 45% of replies turn into meetings when answered inside 5 minutes, and 3% when answered the next day.

hand over properly.

before the call, the person taking it should know who the prospect is, what they were sent, and what they said.

when someone leaves the company:

plan for this before it happens.

what to do on their last day:

stop all outreach from their account

move every open conversation to another team member, with a short note to the prospect introducing them

remove their account from your outbound tool

update your master list so their prospects get reassigned

the account belongs to the person, so it leaves with them.

that's why the master list and the conversation history need to live in your own crm.

step 10: check every account every week:

what to check:

connection acceptance, which needs to stay over 30%

reply rate

any warnings from linkedin

any messages marked as spam

what to do if an account gets a warning.

linkedin escalates in 4 stages: warning, temporary lock, identity check, then a ban.

the moment an account gets a warning:

stop all outreach from it for 2 weeks

restart at a lower volume, with only your best-fit prospects

check its acceptance rate daily for the first week back

an account that gets a second warning within a month stops outbound for good.

the rules that kept us at zero bans:

across 100+ profiles and 18 months, these are the rules we hold to every time:

every account belongs to a real person who agreed to it

every account logs in from the same place every time

every account is warmed before cold messages start

every account stays well inside linkedin's limits

every list is screened, so messages only go to people who fit

every account gets its own message versions

every warning means 2 weeks off, straight away
