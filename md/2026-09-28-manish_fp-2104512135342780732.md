# 5 Distribution Lessons You Can Steal From Zuckerberg's Playbook

## Post

Meta is giving away its newest AI product for free, and that free version is the business plan.
The product is Muse, an AI assistant that does tasks for you: booking travel, answering emails, shopping, cancelling subscriptions you forgot about.
If you look closely into this, Mark is trying to own the whole distribution network of Agentic AI ecosystem, this article will tap you inside the key resources and roadmaps which will help you maneuver your SaaS into a positive direction
1. Put your money where your mouth is
Make trying it free, and earn when the customer wins
 
Muse is free up to a generous weekly limit, and Meta says that covers most of what people need. Heavy users can pay $20 or $100 a month. The main plan is different, though. When Muse helps you buy something or save money, Meta takes a small cut, and Zuckerberg says that cut should come from the businesses involved, not the user.
His reasoning is simple, If you want everyone to have an AI assistant, everyone has to be able to afford one. Eight days after launch, Meta’s Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang, who founded Scale AI, announced invite codes: when a friend signs up with yours, you both get extra free usage, for up to 20 friends. TechCrunch reported that Muse then beat ChatGPT’s early numbers, with more downloads and daily users in the US and Canada than ChatGPT had over the same period after its launch.
 
You don’t need Billion Bucks  to copy this. Make the first step cost nothing then reward people for bringing friends, and charge for the result your customer actually cares about.
2. A rising tide lifts all boats
If you can’t own the category, grow it
 
Years ago, Google built its own custom data center technology and kept it secret. Meta later built its own version, but keeping it secret gave no advantage because Google already had one. So Meta published its designs for free. Other companies started using them, suppliers began making parts to Meta’s designs, and those parts got cheaper and better. Meta says this saves it billions.
It is still running the same play. In August, Zuckerberg announced Meta was releasing Muse Glimmer, an AI model anyone can download and run on their own computer for free.
If a part of your product won’t set you apart, giving it away can make you the default choice. People adopting you for free is the cheapest growth there is.
3. Don’t put all your eggs in one basket
Never let someone else control your distribution
 
Meta’s apps run on Apple’s and Google’s phones, and those companies set the rules. In 2024, Zuckerberg guessed Meta could be twice as profitable if it owned those platforms itself. Then, twelve days after Muse launched, Amazon blocked it from shopping on Amazon.com after Meta declined a request to remove the bot. Even a company with over three billion daily users can be shut out of a channel overnight.
If one marketplace, one app store or one search engine brings you most of your customers, they control your business. Build at least one channel you fully own, like an email list or a community.
4. When in Rome, do as the Romans do
Show up where people already are, in the way they expect
 

In 2012, Facebook’s phone app was basically its website squeezed onto a phone. It was faster for Meta to build, but it felt slow and clunky to use. Zuckerberg admits it was a mistake. Meta stopped building new features for about a year just to rebuild the app properly, while more people moved to phones every month. His take on that period: When you're in a losing situation, you often already know what you need to do. The difficult part is having the courage to actually do it.
The rebuild also produced ads that look like normal posts in your feed, which became one of the most successful ad formats ever. Muse follows the same lesson. It works inside WhatsApp and connects to the Instagram and Facebook accounts people and businesses already use. Nobody has to learn a new place to go.
 
Every new habit your product asks for is a customer you might lose.
5. Good artists copy, great artists steal
Copy what works, then make it easy for everyone
 
Meta invented the News Feed in 2006. It didn’t invent Stories, but it adopted the idea anyway, because as Zuckerberg puts it, there are more smart people outside your company than inside it.
Muse is the same move. Earlier this year, tech-savvy people bought Mac Studios just to run an AI assistant called OpenClaw at home. Zuckerberg told Heath that billions of people will never do that, so Meta built a version that runs in the cloud and that he can hand to anyone in his family. Meta has openly said Muse was heavily inspired by OpenClaw, and it is now the most downloaded free app in the US.
The idea that will grow your product has probably already worked for someone in a nearby market. The winner is whoever makes it simple enough for everyone else.
 
 
Early stage founders think about distribution after the product is built.
The unique about Zuckerberg is that he decides it before launch, starting with the price.
