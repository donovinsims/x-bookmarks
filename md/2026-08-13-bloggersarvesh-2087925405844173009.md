Twelve months earlier, the owner was sitting down with his numbers, deciding which technicians he could afford to keep.

The process
Here's the shape of it before I go deep on each part:
Money pages: Building one page per service per city, written for someone in a crisis 

Internal linking: Wiring the site together so Google can actually see those pages 

Google Business Profile: Winning the map pack, which sits above everything else 

Content: Local blog posts that exist to push the money pages up 

Link building: Real placements only, from sites that get read 

Keyword selection: Ignoring volume completely and chasing intent
Before I break those down, one thing has to be clear:
The goal of local SEO isn't rankings. It's the phone ringing.
Someone's AC dies at 9pm in July. They pick up their phone. They call one of the first three results they see.
The fourth company doesn't exist. Doesn't matter how good their techs are.
Everything below is built around that one fact.
1. Money Pages
Their entire site had one page for services.
It was called "HVAC Services." A tidy bulleted list of everything they do.
Useless.
That page can't rank for anything because it isn't about anything. And a homeowner in a panic has no reason to trust it.
So I deleted it.
Then built one page per service, per city:
Emergency AC Repair in Dallas - Same Day Service, Upfront Pricing
That's the whole formula. Name the city. Say when you'll show up. Say something about price.
Those are the three things a person needs to know before they'll dial. So they go in the title tag, the H1, and above the fold.
Four cities and six services means 24 pages. 
Yes, really. Write them properly. Don't spin them.
2. Internal Linking
Their site was a collection of dead ends.
Services didn't link to blogs. Blogs didn't link to services. Nothing pointed back to the homepage except the logo.
So I connected everything on purpose:
AC repair page → compressor blog post → maintenance plan → furnace replacement → homepage → back into the city pages
Pages that had been sitting unindexed for a year started ranking within weeks.
Nothing about those pages changed. The rest of the site just finally pointed at them.
This is the most ignored lever in local SEO. It's free. It takes an afternoon.
If you do one thing from this article, do this one.
3. Google Business Profile
Most HVAC companies fill this out once and never open it again.
That's backwards. 
In home services the map pack sits above every organic result. You can outrank the entire page and still lose the call.
 
So we treated it like the main asset:
Real job photos. Units going in, crews on roofs, before and afters. 
No stock images. Homeowners can spot stock instantly and it makes you look like every other company.
Reviews on a system. The tech asks at job completion, on site, phone in hand, before he leaves. Not an email three days later. Nobody opens those.
Then the boring stuff everyone skips: primary category, secondary categories, every service listed out individually, real hours, attributes, and a description that isn't copy-pasted from the homepage.
That work alone put them in the 3-pack.
4. Content
Most HVAC owners think blogs are a waste of money.
They're mostly right, because most of what gets written for them is filler by someone who's never seen a condenser.
We wrote posts like:
Why Dallas Summers Destroy Your AC Compressor (And How to Avoid a $4,000 Replacement)
Here's the part people get wrong:
I wasn't writing these to get traffic. I didn't care how many people read them.
They exist to prove we know what Texas heat does to equipment, and to give us an honest reason to link into the repair and replacement pages.
The blog feeds the money pages. That's the job.
5. Link Building
No Fiverr packages. No fake press releases. No 400-directory blast.
Every HVAC company stuck on page three bought that same directory package from the same guy.
What we did instead: 
local home improvement blogs and regional sites that have real rankings and real readers.
Fewer links. Slower. From places an actual human visits.
6. Keyword Selection
The owner wanted to rank for "best HVAC company."
Every owner wants that one. It sounds like the trophy.
It's worthless. Nobody types it with a wallet out.
We went narrow instead:
AC Repair Dallas 
Furnace Replacement Plano 
Emergency HVAC Repair Fort Worth
Terrible search volume. Every keyword tool will tell you these are too small to bother with.
They also convert better than anything else, because the person typing them has already decided to hire somebody. They're only choosing who.
Low traffic. High revenue. That trade is the whole game in local.

That's the full process.
