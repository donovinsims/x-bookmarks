# A garage door repair company came to us stuck at position 8.

## Post

A garage door repair company came to us stuck at position 8.

They had been working with a corporate agency (you know who I'm talking about) for 2 years.

120 reviews, solid website, but they couldn't crack the top 3-5.

The first change we made helped jump their position to 2 in a few weeks.

Their GBP was linked to their homepage.

The homepage talked about all 12 cities they serve.

Google couldn't tell which city they were actually in.

We built a dedicated landing page for their GBP city (and other main locations they serve).

> City name in the H1
> Unique location content
> Specific neighborhoods mentioned
> Driving directions from a local landmark

Their GBP started showing up in the map pack for about 20 square miles within weeks.

The thing is, this doesn't always work right away.

Someone recently commented this on another post of mine, and I totally agree.

Your homepage has years of authority built up.

A brand new location page has none of that.

If you're just starting out, linking your GBP to a locally optimized homepage often works better.

But long term, a dedicated location page wins.

It tells Google exactly what city you serve.

I recommend starting with your homepage optimized for your primary city.

Build a location page in the background.

Once it gains some authority, make the switch.
