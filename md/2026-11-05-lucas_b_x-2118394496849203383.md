step 4: the 6-job content mix

this is where most founders kill their own account.

they find one thing that works and post only that.

all long-form value, or all short takes, or a lead magnet every day.

every post has one job, and an account that books calls needs all 6 running at once:

reach without proof gets you followers who NEVER buy.

proof without reach gets you 200 views on your best result.

the mix we run on every account:

THE WEEK

step 5: articles, the reach engine

an X article gets pushed to people who don't follow you.

and someone who reads 2,000 words of you is half sold before the call.

one client came to us with 19k followers and articles averaging about 3.3k views.

we took over and started posting 3 a week.

his articles now average 50k+, and the biggest ones have pulled 178k, 154k and 139k.

how many:

we used to post 5 articles a week per account.

reach dropped on every one, because the audience got used to seeing them.

3 a week is the number we've found optimal (for now). each one gets room to travel.

the title does 80% of the work:

most of your audience will never open the article, so the title has to sell the click on its own.

the title molds that keep winning for our clients:

"how to [result] (FULL COURSE)": promises it's the last guide they'll need

"breakdown of [famous company]'s $[valuation] strategy": borrows a name the reader already trusts

"the most valuable [topic] article ever written": people click to see if it's true

"how we [result] for [number] clients": proof built into the grammar

the cover is one number big enough to read on a phone.

writing it:

open with your credentials, never with a definition.

then a "what's inside" list, then sections in the order the reader does the work.

every section needs a "what it looks like when it works": who it was, what changed, the number after.

end with a CTA: a DM keyword or a booking link (step 8).
