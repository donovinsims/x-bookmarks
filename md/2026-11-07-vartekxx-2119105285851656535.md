# GrokBot Token Usage: how to never hit your limit again in 10 step

## Post

100% used. 4 days until reset. No reset coming.

Sound familiar?

That's where Grok Bot users woke up on November 1. Musk's answer: the bot was "unsustainably" expensive to run.

The old limits aren't coming back. But most of your pool was never spent on real work. It goes to routines waking up for nothing, Bots re-reading weeks of chat, and four Bots answering one question.

Fix those 10 leaks and the same pool lasts the whole week.

Grok Bot is the best agent most people have used. It has its own computer, signs in to your tools, and runs jobs on its own while you sleep.

That is also why it eats usage. Every reply, every routine run, every Bot-to-Bot message costs something. And since the limits got tighter, a setup that used to fit in a week now runs out in two days.

01. Understand what actually changed

Over Halloween weekend (Oct 31 to Nov 1), users across X, Reddit and the Cursor forum reported their weekly meter jumping to 100% with days left before reset.

Some saw it go from 50% to 100%, others from 35% to 78%, a few with almost no Bot activity.

One user hit 100% with four days left and asked Elon Musk for a reset.

No reset was promised, and SpaceXSI has not published new limit numbers.

The part most people miss: it's one pool

On The X Subscription, Grok chat, Grok Bot and Cursor all spend from the same weekly pool. An hour of chatting with Grok or a long Cursor session leaves less for your Bots.

02. Find your meter and audit what burns it

Before cutting anything, look at the number. The meter shows one weekly percentage and your reset date.

It does not break usage down per Bot or per routine, so you have to find the heavy jobs yourself.

Now make the GrokBot tell you what it is doing all week. Send this to your Primary Bot. It coordinates your other Bots, so it can collect their lists too.

03. Slow down your routines

Biggest hidden cost

SpaceXAI's docs say it plainly: each routine run consumes usage, and that includes the Test button.

A routine that checks something every 15 minutes runs 96 times a day, 672 times a week, even when there is nothing new.

Lauren Tan (@poteto) - SpaceXAI team member gave the same advice on X: hourly or a few times a day is enough for most routines.

04. Swap schedules for event triggers

Schedule wakes your Bot even when nothing changed. A trigger wakes it only when something actually happens.

Cursor engineer put it this way: a cron wakes it up even if there's nothing to do.

05. Move long jobs to a fresh Bot

According to Cursor staff on the forum, every reply re-reads the Bot's whole conversation. Most of it is cached, but a Bot you've talked to for six weeks carries all of that into every answer and every routine run.

There is no manual "compact" button yet. Summarizing only kicks in near the model's limit.

The fix: when a Bot has run one job for weeks, have it write a short handoff note, then give the job to a fresh copy.

06. Stop the group chat tax

SpaceXAI's docs: every Bot reply counts toward usage, including replies in group chats and messages Bots send each other.

Put four Bots in one chat, ask one question, and you can pay for four answers. A Grok Bot lead's tip after their last efficiency update was short: beware group chats.

07. Write tasks with a finish line

Grok Bot picks its own model, and the docs say usage depends on the nature of the task and the effort it takes.

A vague task like "look into our churn" has no end, so the Bot keeps exploring. A task with a scope, a finish line and a stop rule ends sooner.

Cursor's own help page says the same thing: start with small, tightly scoped tasks.

08. Use app connections instead of clicking

When a Bot does a job through its computer, it opens the site, takes screenshots, finds the button, clicks and scrolls, and every step is more work.

When the same job goes through a connected app or an MCP server, it's one direct call.

Power users on the Cursor forum report this as one of the easiest savings. SpaceXAI hasn't published numbers, so treat it as a strong rule of thumb.

09. Turn repeat work into skills

If you explain the same weekly report every Monday, the Bot figures it out from scratch every Monday. A skill is a saved set of steps the Bot reuses, shared across all your Bots. You show it once, then call it by name.

10. Cap your spend and plan for 100%

When the weekly pool runs out, one of two things happens. With extra usage off, your Bots stop until the reset.

With it on, they keep going on paid credits, with no cap unless you set one. Decide which one you want before it happens.

When you're at 80%, switch to low-usage mode
