# How To Master treg (full course + use cases)

## Post

Yesterday, my research agent made 68,413 tool calls for $53.49. It checked 22,313 candidate companies, put about 3,970 on sheets, and found 1,037 work emails (731 Valid, 306 Risky) at 527 of them. $52.35 of that ran through one treg balance.

What I'm about to share is how to save you thousands of dollars in data acquisition so bookmark this post if you want to use the prompts sharing at the end of this article later on.

For comparison, Clay's Launch plan is $185 a month before a single row runs, and by Clay's own docs its 2,500 credits cover about 125 to 416 fully enriched records. That gap is why my whole lead stack runs on treg now.

This covers what treg is, how to set it up, what it does well and where it's weaker, and the six bots I run on it for research, with prompts you can paste in today.

I. What treg Actually Is

treg (treg.to) calls itself an OpenRouter for agent tools. Your agent holds one token, asks for a job ("find a work email from a name and domain"), reads the price, and calls it.

A few things make it useful:

One key for the catalog. Its homepage lists 4,100+ tools across 124 providers on Nov 4: SEO data, people and company enrichment, social profiles, ad libraries, scraping, and image and video generation.

Search by job, not by vendor. For one job it shows every provider side by side with the success rate and speed treg measured on real calls, plus the price.

Routed tools for common jobs. Finding or verifying an email, scraping a page and web search each have a treg. tool that, per its docs, tries your own keys first, then the cheapest provider per hit, and moves on when one misses.

No subscription. A prepaid balance at the provider's own rate, 0% markup, $1.00 free to start. On tools priced per success, a miss settles at $0.00.

Your own keys win. Register a key you already pay for and those calls are never metered.

Caps. A per-call ceiling header (X-Treg-Route-Max-Cost), and agent tokens an admin can limit to certain tools and give a cap.

It plugs into the agent you already use. Claude Code, Codex and Cursor through a CLI, or claude.ai and ChatGPT through an MCP connector.

II. How to Set It Up

This takes about 15 minutes.

Pick your door. In a local coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor), the docs give two commands: curl -fsSL https://treg.to/install.sh | sh, then treg login (GitHub, Google or an emailed code). In a hosted assistant like claude.ai, add https://treg.to/mcp/ as a custom connector and approve the sign-in.

Run treg once. It prints your account, team, balance and next commands. A new verified account gets $1.00 of credit once.

Give the agent its own token. An admin can mint one with treg org agent-new and limit it. Keep it in the agent's environment, never in a prompt or a shared file.

Connect the keys you already pay for. If you have a Hunter or Apify plan, connect it and those calls stop touching your balance.

Make one real call before you automate anything. Search the catalog by job, read the price, call it on 20 rows, check the output by hand.

Start a call log. Mine is a local database called leads.db: tool, cost, result, every call. Every number in this article comes from it.

1 rule I'd set on day 1: every paid tool gets a cap before it gets a loop. Hunter spent $4.66 of its $4.85 in two days before I capped it.

III. What It Does Well

Where it's clearly better than the average stack (a Clay table, an Apollo seat, a Hunter plan, or a Zapier chain gluing them together):

You pay per call, not per month. Clay, Apollo and Hunter charge a floor whether your agent runs or not (Clay Launch $185/mo, Apollo Basic from $49/mo, Hunter Starter from $49/mo, list prices checked Nov 3). treg charges when the agent calls. A Clay record holds more fields than my email column, so read that as the shape of the gap, not a like-for-like price.

Cheap sources get a fair shot. treg people search cost me $0.35 for 1,212 calls, and 45 brokers have an emailed person only because of it. A seat-based plan makes you pick one vendor up front.

The agent picks on evidence. Success rate, speed and price for every provider of a job, measured by treg. A Zapier chain hard-codes one vendor per step and never tells you there's a cheaper one.

Failures are loud. When my balance ran out on Oct 26, 1,090 lookups were refused with a clear error. Nothing quietly came back empty.

Where it's weaker:

No UI for a teammate who doesn't work in a terminal. Clay wins there.

A prepaid balance stops hard. Plan top-ups before big runs.

Direct calls to one provider don't fail over. Only the routed tools do. My bot tries about 20 providers through treg, and that order is code I wrote.

It can't pick your market. If your filters are vague, you pay for thousands of rows you'll never send to.

It went public on Nov 2. The launch post claims 85% cheaper than Clay and 25x faster. Those are treg's claims, and the runs behind them aren't published. My numbers are below.

IV. The Lead Bots I Run on It

Each one has what it does, when it runs, and a prompt you can paste into Claude Code or any agent with treg connected. My market is prop firms and CFD brokers. Swap in yours.

1. Company Finder

What it does: Searches for companies that match your market, reads their sites, and keeps only the ones that fit your rules. For me it checked 22,313 candidates (1,939 prop firms, 3,540 CFD brokers). On the CFD tab, BlitzAPI found 2,920 brokers and 370 made the sheet. crawl4ai read the sites (27,269 calls, $7.01, pages loaded 81% of the time).

When it runs: Once per new market, then weekly for new entrants.

2. The Yes/No Gate

What it does: Before any paid step, asks one cheap yes/no question: right kind of company? does this person still work there? Every no is a paid lookup that never happens. Mine ran on Jev, outside treg: 17,552 decisions for $1.14.

When it runs: In front of every paid step.

3. People Finder

What it does: Finds the right people at each kept company, free sources first. It found 1,794 people at 771 companies. On the CFD tab, BlitzAPI's employee finder gave 308 of the 495 people kept and treg people search gave 187.

When it runs: After the gate, per company.

4. Email Finder and Checker

What it does: Finds a work email for each person, then checks it before anything gets sent. The finder cost $12.90 for 2,654 calls and returned an address in 77% of answered lookups. The checker (MillionVerifier / Trykitt) cost $2.57 and removed 109 bad addresses in one pass.

When it runs: Right after People Finder.

5. Signal Scout

What it does: Flags who to contact first. Meta Ad Library through ScrapeCreators flagged 82 firms with live ads ($2.02). Two DataForSEO calls flagged 291 firms with rising traffic ($0.64).

When it runs: Weekly, on the kept companies.

6. Spend Auditor

What it does: Reads leads.db and ranks every tool by cost per usable result, not cost per call. This is how I found five paid tools that didn't earn a place for my niche: $11.49, 21% of spend.

When it runs: After the first 20 rows, after 1,000 rows, then weekly.

V. Distribution: Finding the Creators

The lead list is half of it. The other half is the people your market already listens to.

7. Creator Finder

What it does: Profiles creators in your market and keeps only the ones that meet your rules. Mine profiled 1,390 creators and 62 met my rules.

When it runs: Monthly, or before a launch.

VI. Chain Them Together

Once each bot works alone, run them as one chain on one sheet: Company Finder, Gate, People Finder, Gate, Email Finder and Checker, Signal Scout. The Spend Auditor reads the log at the end.

Two rules hold the chain together. Free first, then the gate, then the paid step. And never run a long chain on a laptop: I closed the lid and a 21-hour run paused for 16.7 hours. Put it on a server, a cloud agent, or anything that stays awake.

VII. Your First Week

Don't build all seven at once. Do it in this order:

Day 1: Install treg, take the $1.00 credit, connect any keys you already pay for, and start leads.db.

Day 2: Run Company Finder on 20 companies. Check every row by hand and fix the rules.

Day 3: Put the Yes/No Gate in front of it and run 100 companies. Count how many paid lookups the gate saved.

Day 4: Add People Finder and Email Finder and Checker on the kept companies. Set a per-call ceiling first.

Day 5: Run the Spend Auditor. Cap or cut whatever ranks last.

Day 6: Add Signal Scout and sort the sheet by who's spending.

Day 7: Top up before the full run. Mine ran dry on Oct 26.
