# How To Build A 7-Step Plan To Get Your Business Into ChatGPT

## Post

See where competing businesses appear in AI answers and use that research to plan your next website changes. Here’s the process I used.
I do a full walkthrough on this over on my YouTube channel.
I recently audited Exam Centre Birmingham, a client’s business that takes bookings for exams in the UK.
Its website was being cited in AI answers. But in the answers I reviewed, the business itself was rarely being named.
That distinction changes what you work on. A page can supply information for an answer without the business being recommended to someone looking to book.
So I wanted to find the questions that mattered to this business, then work out which website changes to make first.
You’ll learn:
How to choose buyer questions worth tracking before writing more content.
How to find useful pages your competitors have that your website is missing.
Which AI-generated recommendations to check before adding them to your plan.
How to measure changes against a recorded starting point.

1. Record Where You Show Up
Start with your own domain and the country your customers are in.
For this client, I selected the UK. Comparing its performance with results from unrelated countries would give me work that had little to do with filling exam places.
In the walkthrough, I connected Semrush through Claude’s Connectors menu. Then I created the website project in Semrush and started a Site Audit, with a limit of 500 pages.
The completed crawl covered 219 pages. While it ran, I opened the AI Visibility Overview to see where the website already appeared.
Keep business mentions separate from page citations. A mention puts the business into the answer’s text. A citation references a page as a source. Neither, by itself, tells you that someone became a customer. Semrush explains the report here.
Save the starting figures with the date and country. Record which AI platforms the results cover, too.
Now you have something to compare future results against. Next, you need a useful set of competing businesses.
2. Choose Your Actual Competitors
Check the suggested competitors before accepting them.
In this audit, some of the suggested domains belonged to exam boards. Another belonged to the client. Those were not the businesses I wanted to compare with an exam centre selling bookings.
I searched for other exam centres and replaced the suggested domains with relevant providers.
Choose businesses a customer could realistically buy from instead of you. For a local service, that includes checking where they operate.
Then compare where those businesses appear in AI answers. Open the results behind the numbers so you can see what they are being mentioned for.
A provider appearing for a relevant booking question gives you a useful example to study. A large organisation appearing for general education questions tells you something different.
That is why I move from the competitor comparison into the actual questions.
3. Find Questions That Could Bring Customers
In Topics and Prompts, I looked at the questions behind the results and read the full answers.
Some topics had volume but little connection to booking an exam. A person asking for help with a maths calculation has a different need from someone looking for an exam centre.
Informational content still belongs in the plan when it helps a future customer make a decision. But the highest-volume topic does not automatically deserve your next article.
I exported the topics and prompts as separate CSV files, then gave both to Claude. I wanted it to separate people looking for a centre from broader education searches.

4. Track Those Questions Before Making Changes
Once I had a useful shortlist, I added the highest-priority prompts to tracking.
Claude had suggested a second group covering people who could need an exam centre. I kept the initial tracking focused on the first group: people looking more directly for the service.
Use the relevant country and select the AI platforms available in your tracking setup. In the video, I set the business name and UK location before starting the campaign.
The point is to keep checking a defined set of questions. Prompt Tracking lets you inspect the sources appearing for those questions as well as changes in visibility.
Look at the answers alongside the charts. Record whether the business is named, which pages are cited, and which competitors appear.
Keep those settings consistent when comparing results. An answer to one prompt is one observation, so look for repeated appearances over time.
That tracked set also gives you a more focused collection of pages to research.
5. Study The Pages AI Uses
Open the cited sources for your selected questions and compare them with your own website.
Look for information a buyer needs that your pages do not provide. For the exam centre, that means checking whether the site clearly explains the exams it offers and how candidates can book.
Use those findings to decide whether an existing page needs improving or a useful page is missing.
I also asked Claude to analyse the backlinks through the Semrush connection. That gave me possible sources of relevant coverage to investigate alongside the content work.
For this business, education organisations and relevant directories were worth reviewing. A competitor’s website appearing in the sources did not make it a realistic place to request a link.
Check the individual opportunity before putting it on your outreach list. The organisation needs to be relevant, and your business needs to qualify for the listing or coverage.
You now have specific content work and possible third-party opportunities. The site audit helps you decide how those fit with the technical fixes.
6. Choose What To Fix First
When the crawl finished, I asked Claude to combine the audit with the research already in the conversation.
The useful output was a list of actions I could review against the actual website.
For Exam Centre Birmingham, the suggestions included fixing the homepage H1 and rebuilding the contact page. There were also exam-date pages that needed internal links so visitors could reach them from elsewhere on the site.
I checked AI crawler access, too. The audit showed green ticks for the checks displayed, so I could move on to the other findings.

7. Make The Changes And Measure Again
The video ends with the audit and action plan. Implementation is the next stage.
For this client, I’d start with the selected website fixes, then work through the content improvements. That includes giving exam pages useful, distinct information and adding internal links to relevant pages.
I’d also check that the business details match across its website and relevant listings, then review the external coverage opportunities individually.
Keep a record of what changed and when. Once the changes are in place, return to the prompts you started tracking.
Check whether the business appears more often and whether the improved pages get cited. Review Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools separately for search impressions and clicks.
Those measurements answer different questions. More citations do not automatically mean more enquiries, so keep checking what happens after people reach the website.
The plan gives you a starting order for the work. The results help you decide what to improve next.
