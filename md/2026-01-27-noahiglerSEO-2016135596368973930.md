# If I Owned a Remodeling Company in Seattle, Here's How I'd Rank #1 on Google

## Post

If I Owned a Remodeling Company in Seattle, Here's How I'd Rank #1 on Google

There's roughly 3,500 people searching for remodeling services in the Seattle metro every month.

Kitchen remodels, bathroom renovations, whole home projects. That's a lot of high-ticket jobs sitting on the table.

When you search "remodeling contractor Seattle" right now, the Map Pack is competitive but not impossible.

The top businesses have pretty weak review counts ranging from about 50 to 100 reviews. Review velocity is moderate at around 3-8 per month. A couple of them have incomplete GBP profiles with missing services and outdated photos.

Here's what's interesting about Seattle specifically.

The metro sprawls across a bunch of distinct cities and neighborhoods that function as their own markets. Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond, Tacoma, and Everett all have enough search volume and population to support their own ranking strategy.

Most remodeling companies set up one GBP in Seattle proper and try to serve the whole region.

That's a mistake.

Proximity to the searcher is one of Google's biggest ranking factors. If your GBP is in Capitol Hill and someone searches from Bellevue, you're probably not showing up.

Here's the strategy I'd run.

Start With an Exact Match Business Name

None of the top competitors are using this effectively. Most are just "[Brand] Construction" or "[Owner Name] Remodeling."

I'd register a DBA with the format: [Brand] Kitchen and Bathroom Remodeling Seattle.

This gives you a real brand identity while also getting the SEO advantage of having your target keywords in the business name. Google gives a noticeable ranking boost when your business name matches what people actually search.

Filing a DBA in Washington costs under $50 and takes about a week to process. Completely within Google's guidelines as long as it's your legal business name.

Build Out the GBP Completely

Most remodeling companies have bare minimum profiles. A few photos, maybe a description, and that's it.

I'd add every service I offer with keyword-rich descriptions. Kitchen remodeling, bathroom remodeling, whole home renovation, basement finishing, ADU construction, and aging-in-place modifications.

Seattle has a huge market for ADUs specifically because of the housing density regulations. I'd make sure that's prominently featured.

Upload fresh photos from actual jobs at least once a month. Real before and afters from Seattle homes, not stock images. Google can tell the difference and so can customers.

Review Velocity Would Be My Priority

The top competitor is getting maybe 10 reviews per month. If I can hit 10-20 consistently, I'm going to climb fast.

Here's the thing about remodeling though.

You're only completing maybe 5-12 jobs per month depending on project size. That limits your review potential if you only ask after completed projects.

The move is to treat consultations and estimates as reviewable interactions too.

You're doing 20-30 free consultations per month. Even if they don't hire you, they still got value from the meeting.

Train your team to ask after every consultation where the homeowner seemed happy with the interaction.

"If the consultation was helpful today, would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? Takes 30 seconds."

Hand them a QR code right there.

Then set up automated follow-ups via text at 12 hours and email at 48 hours for anyone who doesn't leave one on the spot.

Respond to every single review within 24 hours. Work in keywords naturally.

"Thanks for trusting us with your kitchen remodel in Queen Anne. Glad we could help bring your vision to life."

Website Structure Matters More for Remodeling Than Most Industries

Remodeling is a high-ticket purchase. People don't just call the first business they see. They research, compare, and visit websites before making a decision.

Your homepage needs to convert.

Above the fold you want your service and city in the headline, a big call button or lead form, your star rating and review count, and trust signals like licensed, bonded, and insured.

But for remodeling specifically, you also want a portfolio section prominently featured. Before and after galleries from actual Seattle projects. People want to see your work before they call.

Build dedicated service pages for each type of remodeling you do.

Kitchen remodeling gets its own page. Bathroom remodeling gets its own page. ADU construction gets its own page. Each one should go deep on that specific service with photos, process breakdown, pricing context, and FAQs.

Then build location pages for each city you want to rank in. Seattle, Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond, Tacoma, and Everett all need their own pages with unique content about that specific area.

For Seattle specifically, I'd mention neighborhoods like Capitol Hill, Queen Anne, Ballard, Fremont, and West Seattle. Talk about the housing stock in each area.

Seattle has a ton of Craftsman homes from the early 1900s that need specific renovation approaches. Bellevue has more modern construction.

This isn't filler content. It's showing Google and potential customers that you actually understand the local market.

Citations and Local Links

Build profiles in about 80-100 directories with consistent NAP.

Start with the big ones like Yelp, Houzz, Angi, HomeAdvisor, BBB, and Apple Maps. Then add local directories like the Seattle Chamber of Commerce, Bellevue Chamber, and any regional contractor associations.

For remodeling specifically, Houzz is huge. A lot of homeowners use it to find contractors and browse project photos. Make sure your Houzz profile is fully built out with a complete portfolio.

Get links from local suppliers you work with.

Your cabinet supplier, countertop fabricator, and flooring distributor probably have "preferred contractor" or "partners" pages on their websites. Ask to be included. Pay if you have to.

Digital PR for Map Pack Rankings and AI Search Visibility

This is where most remodeling companies have zero presence. Which means the opportunity is wide open.

Digital PR used to just be about building backlinks. Now it directly impacts whether AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews recommend your business.

When someone asks ChatGPT "who's the best remodeling contractor in Seattle," it doesn't search Google Maps. It searches its training data.

That training data comes from news articles, press releases, brand mentions on authoritative sites, and public social content.

If your business name doesn't appear anywhere connected to "remodeling" and "Seattle," you won't get recommended.

Here's how I'd build that presence.

Publish press releases consistently, maybe once a month. The topic almost doesn't matter. You could announce a milestone like completing your 100th kitchen remodel, write about a community sponsorship or charity involvement, or put out something about a new service offering like ADU construction or aging-in-place modifications.

Use a distribution service like EIN Presswire or PRWeb. They're affordable and get your release picked up by news aggregators and indexed across dozens of sites. Each one becomes a data point that both Google and AI models can pull from.

Get mentioned in local news outlets.

Seattle has a bunch of neighborhood blogs, local business journals, and community news sites. Pitch yourself for stories about home renovation trends in the Pacific Northwest, ADU regulations in Seattle, or how homeowners are adapting older Craftsman homes for modern living.

Journalists need sources. Most contractors never think to reach out.

Sponsor local events and nonprofits.

Seattle has tons of community events, home shows, neighborhood cleanups, and charity organizations. A $300-500 sponsorship often gets you a mention on their website with a backlink, sometimes a press release, and occasionally local news coverage.

This builds traditional backlinks that help your Map Pack rankings AND trains AI models to recognize your brand as a legitimate business worth recommending.

I've seen businesses start showing up in ChatGPT results within weeks of publishing press releases consistently. Most local competitors aren't even thinking about this yet. The bar is incredibly low.

The Multi-Location Play for Long-Term Dominance

Once your main Seattle GBP is ranking top 3, consider opening a small office in Bellevue or Kirkland.

A 400 square foot office in a strip mall runs maybe $750-1,000 per month in those areas. That's the cost of one small bathroom remodel job.

But it means you can rank in an entirely new geographic pocket.

Register a new DBA like "[Brand] Kitchen and Bathroom Remodeling Bellevue." Set up a new GBP at that address. Build separate citations. Split your review generation between locations.

Now you're ranking top 3 in Seattle AND top 3 in Bellevue.

You've doubled your addressable market for the cost of rent and some extra SEO work.

Timeline Expectations

Months 2-4 you'll see movement in the data. Rankings improving, visibility increasing.

Months 5-8 is when you're pushing into the top 3 and lead flow becomes consistent.

Months 8-12 is when you're dominating and can start thinking about expansion to other cities.

The businesses that win in Seattle's remodeling market are the ones that treat each major suburb as its own opportunity and execute consistently for 12+ months.
