# The Full Guide to Landing AI Consulting Clients

## Post

There was a stretch of years where every business on earth realized they needed a website, and the people who got paid were the ones standing there offering to build them. A lot of those guys were average developers at best, but it didn't matter because demand was growing so much faster than supply that just being positioned in front of it was the only playbook needed.

We're in that exact window with AI right now.

Every business owner knows they need to do something with AI. Even several years after its introduction, 90% of them have no clue what that something is, and almost none of them have a person they trust to figure it out with them. So the demand is massive and the supply of competent implementers is still tiny.

That's why landing clients in 2026 comes down to positioning way more than grinding. If you're standing in the right channel for your stage, you fall into clients. I've watched it happen with our own clients and with members inside our Inner Circle.

But positioning only picks the spot. Winning still looks the same as it does everywhere else in business, which is doing the same stuff every single day for a long stretch of time. Trying a channel for 30 days proves nothing. The owners crushing it picked one channel and ran it daily for months, way past the point where most people would've called it dead and moved on.

When someone tells me this market is saturated, it's almost always one of two things. Either they're standing in a channel that requires stuff they don't have yet, or they've been channel hopping every few weeks and never gave any single one enough reps to pay out.

So let's go through every channel available to you. There are two kinds, and once you see the split it becomes obvious where you should be standing.

Niche-agnostic channels

The commonplace advice is to pick a niche before you do anything, so owners spend 3 months debating dental vs e-comm instead of talking to a single business. What they don't realize is that some channels already come with the niche built in, and you get to skip that whole decision.

Warm network

Your niche here is people who already trust you.

I'm talking about old coworkers, former employers, friends who run businesses, anyone from your life before AI. The trust already exists with these people, so the only thing this channel asks of you is one real result you can share when you reach out.

What makes this channel so strong right now is that everyone in your network is already curious about AI. They've been waiting for someone they actually know to bring it up. A simple message about a result you got, sent to 40 people who recognize your name, will do more for you than a thousand cold emails to strangers.

Most owners skip this one because it feels too easy. Meanwhile it's where most first $10K+ clients actually come from.

In-person workshops

With workshops, your niche is being local.

You get a room of business owners in your city together, teach for an hour, and build something live in front of them. Chambers of commerce, industry meetups, and coworking spaces are all looking for someone who can speak on AI right now because their members won't stop asking about it.

For that hour, you're the most credible AI expert those owners have ever met in person, because you're probably the only one they've ever met in person. Everyone else pitching them is a stranger on the internet. The trust that takes months to build online happens in one session, and your pipeline walks out of that room with you.

Upwork

On Upwork, your niche is being where the buyers already are.

Business owners with money in hand are typing "AI automation" into that search bar every single day. So when you position yourself as an AI automation developer on the platform, you're standing directly in front of people who are actively looking to spend. You don't need to pick an industry or build an audience first, because the platform is doing the demand generation for you.

This is where I started. I was building Airtable and Zapier systems for whoever needed them, and those projects taught me more about how businesses actually run than any course could have. The clients you land here also become your first case studies, which matters a lot for the channels later in this article.

Niche-required channels

Cold email, cold calling, and LinkedIn outreach all live in this bucket.

These channels work, and they're how you scale past what the channels above can produce. But they carry a hard requirement the first group doesn't, which is that you're interrupting a stranger, and strangers only stop for specificity.

A message that says "we help businesses with AI" will not work. A message that says "we help law firms automate client intake" gets read, because the reader knows within a second whether it applies to them. And when they check your profile afterward and see proof you've done it before, that's when the call gets booked.

So these channels multiply positioning you already have instead of creating it for you. Come in with a defined niche, a clear offer, and a real result behind you and they'll fill your calendar. Show up empty handed and you'll swear cold outreach is dead, when really you just brought no niche to a niche-required channel.

Content

Content is its own category because it's the one channel nobody can take from you.

Your Upwork profile gets undercut eventually. A cold email angle gets copied the week it starts working. Even this gold rush window closes once supply catches up to demand. But years of documented builds, real client results, and opinions with your name attached turn into an asset that no competitor can copy at any price.

My advice is to be posting on at least one platform no matter what stage you're at. Sharing my thoughts and results on LinkedIn and X have changed my life. Which platform depends entirely on where your niche actually hangs out. If you're going after agencies, e-comm brands, or other founders, they live on X. If you're selling to corporate operators, professional services, or mid-market executives, LinkedIn is where they scroll. Pick the one that matches your buyer and post there daily before you even think about a second platform.

And know what you're signing up for. The first couple months will feel like you're talking to nobody. Then it starts compounding, and it quietly makes every other channel on this list convert better, because every prospect you touch anywhere is going to look you up before the call. What they find either sells you or buries you.

Paid ads

Paid ads exist and some agencies run them well, but I'm not covering them in this article. Once the channels above are producing, ads become a real conversation. Before that point, you're paying to send cold traffic at positioning that can't hold it yet.

The point

The AI services market right now rewards showing up in the right spot, same as the website era did. The people collecting clients today picked a channel that matches what they actually have and planted themselves in it.

If you have no niche and no results yet, the agnostic channels are wide open for you. If you have real positioning and a result behind you, the niche-required channels will scale it. And either way, you should be posting.

Windows where demand outruns supply this badly don't stay open forever. The website one closed too.
