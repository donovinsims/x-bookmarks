# If I owned a tree service company in Dallas, here's exactly how I'd rank #1 on Google Maps and ChatGPT:

## Post

If I owned a tree service company in Dallas, here's exactly how I'd rank #1 on Google Maps and ChatGPT:

(Bookmark this strategy)

There's ~3,200 people searching for tree services in Dallas each month.

That's 8 figures sitting on the table annually.

But here's what makes Dallas different from most markets.

The DFW metro sprawls across 40+ miles.

Plano alone has 290k people. Frisco has 220k. Arlington, Irving, Garland, Richardson, etc. are all massive.

Each suburb/city is basically its own market.

Most tree service companies make the mistake of setting up one GBP in central Dallas and trying to serve everyone.

That kills your rankings before you even start.

Proximity to the searcher is one of Google's biggest ranking factors.

If your GBP is in Oak Lawn, you're not ranking for someone searching in Plano.

Period.

The 3 businesses ranking in the Map Pack right now:
> Live Oak Tree Service - 967 reviews (20/month)
> Green Pine Tree Services - 595 reviews (10/month)
> True Arbor Trees - 165 reviews (1/month)

Live Oak is dominating with solid review velocity.

But look at True Arbor.

Weaker reviews and they still rank for a solid 25 square mile area.

That tells you that certain pockets of this metro are totally winable.

Here's the strategy I'd run:

1. The multi-location play (this is the real move in Dallas).

You're not going to rank across a 40 mile metro from one location.

Instead, set up strategic GBPs in 2-3 high-value suburbs.

Rent small office spaces in areas like:
> Plano - 290k population, high home values, mature trees everywhere
> Frisco - 220k population, fast-growing with new construction
> Richardson - older neighborhoods with established oaks needing maintenance

A 400 sq ft office costs $500-700/month.

That's the price of a single tree removal job.

But it means you rank for an entirely new geographic pocket.

Each location needs:
> Unique NAP (name, address, phone)
> Separate citations built for that location
> Dedicated landing page on your website
> Its own review generation funnel

This is how you own the DFW metro instead of fighting for one slice.

2. Exact match business name.

None of the top 3 are using exact match names.

If I'm starting fresh in Plano, my GBP name is: [Brand] + Tree Service Plano.

You don't need a separate company for each location.

Just set up DBAs - filing one costs under $50 in Texas and takes a week.

3. Review velocity across locations.

Live Oak is getting 20 reviews per month.

That's your benchmark.

Here's how to split it across multiple GBPs:

Assign specific crews to specific locations.

Crew A works Plano jobs, asks for Plano GBP reviews.

Crew B works Frisco jobs, asks for Frisco GBP reviews.

Train them to ask in person right after cleanup.

The homeowner is staring at their yard with the tree gone or cleaned up.

"We're a local Plano business trying to grow. If you're happy with how your property looks, would you scan this and leave us a quick Google review? Takes 20 seconds."

Then SMS follow-up at 24 and 72 hours.

Respond to every review within 24 hours.

Work in keywords naturally:

"Appreciate you trusting us with the oak tree removal in West Plano. Glad we could get it cleared before storm season."

4. GBP optimization.

Build out your services section completely:
> Tree removal
> Tree trimming
> Tree pruning
> Stump grinding
> Stump removal
> Emergency tree removal
> Storm damage cleanup
> Arborist consultation
> Oak wilt treatment
> Lot clearing
> Etc.

For each service, add a description mentioning your target city.

"Our tree removal crews serve Plano and surrounding cities including Richardson, Allen, and McKinney."

Post job photos consistently (ideally once a week but monthly is fine).

Real before/afters from actual jobs in that specific suburb.

5. Location pages that actually rank.

Build dedicated pages for each GBP location:

> Reliable Tree Service in Plano, TX | [Brand] (location page linked to Plano GBP)
> Trustworthy Tree Service in Frisco, TX | [Brand] (location page linked to Frisco GBP)
> Professional Tree Service in Richardson, TX | [Brand] (location page linked to Richardson GBP)

Each page needs unique content.

Richardson has older homes with aging trees near the UTD campus.

Write about actual differences. Don't copy/paste page content with city names swapped.

Include driving directions, local neighborhoods you serve, and a map embed.

5. Digital PR and local backlinks (this is huge for Maps AND AI search).

People are starting to ask ChatGPT and Gemini for contractor recommendations.

"Who's the best tree service in Plano?" is a real search now.

These LLMs pull answers from news articles, press releases, and brand mentions across the web.

If your company name shows up nowhere, you don't get recommended.

Digital PR fixes both problems at once.

It builds backlinks that help your Map Pack rankings AND trains AI models to recognize your brand.

I recommend doing PR monthly.

To get local links, you can start by sponsoring local events.

Little League teams in Plano, charity 5Ks in Frisco, HOA community days are all pretty easy and affordable.

A $300-500 sponsorship gets you a .org backlink (worth).

You should also "partner" with adjacent businesses. Reach out to landscapers, fence companies, and pool builders. You refer them jobs after tree removal and in return they link to your website as a "partner".

Join local chambers.

Plano Chamber, Frisco Chamber, Richardson business associations.

Make citations for each location separately.

Shoot for 80-100 citations per GBP.

Hit the big directories: Yelp, Angi, BBB, Apple Maps, HomeAdvisor.

Make sure the name, address, and phone number is identical everywhere for each location.

======

The companies that win in DFW are the ones treating each major suburb as its own market.
