# How to automate lead gen and book demos with Opus 5.5 (Full Course)

## Post

i'm going to walk you through the lead gen setup i run with AI agents, from the hands-off version to the full Claude build on Opus 5.5, step by step...
because outbound changed shape a while ago, and now every message has to win two things at once: the right person AND the right week
a perfect-fit prospect who isn't thinking about your problem this month will ignore a perfect message, and your list runs dry faster than you'd expect
and before we touch a single tool, you need to hear the one rule that decides whether any of this is worth your time
a lot of people chase shiny numbers in outreach, more connection requests, higher acceptance rates, a reply rate to screenshot... and none of it counts if the replies don't turn into booked meetings
so if a prospect said "sure, tell me more" tomorrow and you'd have no calendar link, no clear offer and nobody fast enough to answer, fix that first, because every hour you put into this article is wasted until you do

the easy route where an AI agent runs the whole loop for you

how to take a campaign apart and fix it
the weekly loop that keeps it all moving
a four-week plan so you're not staring at an empty chat

starting from zero with no outbound
quick detour (again sorry) for anyone reading this with no outbound behind them yet, because everything after this section assumes you already have an agent running
the goal here is small on purpose, one offer, one kind of buyer, one LinkedIn account
so before you open any tool, write down what you sell and what a win means for you, whether that's a booked demo, a discovery call or a trial signup
that line becomes the first thing Claude reads later, so it's worth getting right now
then pick one problem your buyer already has, and the words they'd use when they complain about it in public
those words become your keyword signals, and they're worth more than any job title filter, because a title tells you who someone is and a complaint tells you what's bothering them this week
if your LinkedIn account is new or quiet, go slow, start around 5 to 10 connection requests a day and raise it gradually, because ramping too fast is how accounts get restricted
(nobody books a meeting from a restricted account)
building our setup in Claude
the build runs on MCP, the open standard that lets Claude work inside your other tools
 
Claude can't log into LinkedIn on its own, so the work splits cleanly: Claude does the thinking and writing, Gojiberry does the finding and sending and keeps you inside LinkedIn's limits, and you approve and take the calls
connecting them takes about two minutes and no code:
in Gojiberry, go to Settings → API and create an API key
 
in Claude, open Customize → Connectors, click + 
 
and choose Add custom connector
 
name it Gojiberry and paste
 
 
click Connect
 
paste your API key, authorize
 
in a new chat, click + and switch the Gojiberry connector on
 
you'll need a Claude Pro account for this
i keep everything in one Claude project, and that project is the whole brain of the setup, so it's worth getting right
inside it, i keep three documents Claude reads every time:
a brief with the offer, the average deal size, the last five customers who signed, and what counts as a win
a scoreboard with the current numbers for each signal: leads found, replies, meetings
a running log where every week adds a new entry and never deletes an old one
the brief is the one you'll edit the most, because Claude only judges a lead as well as it understands what you're selling
the log is the one that saves you later, because three weeks from now you won't remember why you changed a message, and Claude won't either unless it's written down

one note so you don't get stuck: grouping leads into a list and attaching that list to a campaign happens in the Gojiberry app, so plan for a couple of clicks there
i pick Opus 5.5 for all of this, because the whole loop lives or dies on judgment calls about who deserves a message this week, and that's where i want the strongest model i have
for the approval step, turn on Review Mode in the agent's settings, so every lead and message waits for your yes before anything goes out
plenty of teams switch it off once they trust the output, but keep it on for your first few weeks
find the one signal worth your week
this is where the rule turns into actual work, because Claude's first job is to find ONE signal where more leads would turn into more meetings
i know it's tempting to switch on every signal on day one, but a pile of weak signals floods you with weak leads and hides the one that pays, so we go narrow
open the Insights tab first, it shows leads generated per day, which signals are active, what each one produced, and your top-performing signals per agent
 
then ask Claude to line those up against the replies marked Interested in your Uni-Box, signal by signal
what you're looking for is a signal that already produces replies from people who fit your ICP, but isn't feeding the agent enough leads yet
i call that the money signal, because it's the one place where more volume flows straight into meetings instead of into a bigger lead count
a signal that brings in hundreds of leads and zero replies is a trap, and Claude should flag it as one instead of celebrating it
so every signal gets one of three calls, keep it, keep it once you fix one thing, or drop it
and whatever Claude decides, ask it to explain the call with the numbers it looked at
a few warnings before you trust early numbers:
the system needs several days to test signals and learn which ones perform, so changing things in the first week makes it impossible to tell what's working
a new agent's reply counts are tiny, so one enthusiastic reply can make a signal look better than it is
marking leads as good or poor fit in Contacts teaches the agent, so do it, because it's the fastest feedback you can give
by the end of this step you should have one signal, one audience, and a short written reason for why that combination is the bet
take the campaign apart and fix it
now Claude inspects the campaign behind that signal, and i think of it as a full checkup in four passes, starting with the boring stuff that breaks everything else
first, targeting, which means the ICP is narrow enough to be useful
use Additional Criteria to tell the agent what to prioritize, like "prioritize fintech" or "deprioritize agencies"
use Mandatory Keywords as a hard filter, and include synonyms, because a lead who writes "outbound" when your keyword says "outreach" gets wrongly thrown out
set exclusions for service providers, freelancers, consultants and anyone marked Open to Work
second pass is the reason for reaching out, because a message with no believable "why now" reads like every other cold message in their inbox
signals give you that reason, and the detected intent should be phrased the way a person would say it, like "exploring outbound automation tools", never "i saw you liked a post"
this is also where Claude goes beyond LinkedIn
Gojiberry's native signals watch LinkedIn behavior, so if your buyers show up in podcast guest lists, conference speaker pages or funding news, ask Claude to research those on the web and push the people it finds into Gojiberry as contacts
the rule i give Claude is that every lead comes with the source it came from, and if it can't find the person's real LinkedIn profile, it says so instead of guessing
third pass is the message itself, and the structure that works for me has four one-line blocks:
what you noticed, the exact signal
why you're the one saying it
an open question, no pitch
an easy out, so not replying feels fine
ask Claude for three variants under 60 words each, pick one, and keep links out of the first message, because early links hurt reply rates and look like spam
ChargeMate's co-founder does exactly this, he has Claude write messages in batches of five or ten, pushes them into Gojiberry, and always opens with curiosity over a pitch, which took his reply rates 5 to 8x higher than his old Apollo-based outbound
 
fourth pass is the one this whole article hangs on, the path from reply to booked meeting
check that automation stops the moment someone replies, make the Uni-Box Interested filter the first thing you open every morning, and make sure replies reach you fast, through the Slack alert or your HubSpot or Pipedrive sync
GTE Localize runs it this way, automation starts the conversation and a human takes over at the first reply, and they booked more than 100 meetings across March and April
 
at the end of the four passes, Claude writes everything into one report, ranks the fixes, and recommends ONE change to make first
run the loop every week
this is the part that turns a one-off audit into a system, and it's also where AI gets dangerous if you let it react to everything
the loop i run is short, and i run it every Monday in the same Claude project with the same prompt:
pull last week's numbers per signal into the scoreboard
compare them with the numbers from before the last change
check whether anything broke, like a paused agent or a disconnected account
recommend one change with the numbers behind it
wait for my yes before touching a message, a signal or the ICP
write what happened and what changed into the log
one change at a time sounds slow
it is, on purpose, because if you change the ICP, the first message and two signals in the same week, you'll never know which one did the work
LinkedIn outreach is slow by design anyway, since messages only go out after a connection request is accepted, so a change you make today may take a week or more to show up in replies
and when Claude judges a change, it looks at replies and meetings together
a signal that doubled its lead count and brought zero extra meetings goes in the log as a miss
a message that got fewer replies but turned more of them into calls goes in as a win, no debate
i also keep the weekly prompt frozen during a test, since changing the prompt mid-experiment makes this week's read incomparable with last week's
your first four weeks
if you were starting from zero today, i'd lay out the first month like this, and i'd follow it in order instead of skipping to the fun part
1/ in week one, you connect everything and set the baseline
that means the Gojiberry agent with your ICP and a few strong signals, the Claude connector, and Review Mode switched on
you write the brief, decide what a win is, and let the agent run without touching the settings
2/ in week two, Claude finds the money signal and runs the four-pass checkup on that campaign
you read the report, push back on anything without numbers behind it, and agree on the one change you'll make first
3/ week three is shipping week
Claude drafts the change, you review it, you make it in Gojiberry yourself, and the log records the date and exactly what moved
4/ in week four, you start the weekly loop and give that change a couple more weeks before calling it a win or a miss
after that, you pick the next change on the same campaign or the next signal, and the system keeps going with you making one decision a week instead of doing all the prospecting yourself
to run this well, you only have to be honest about which signals book meetings and which ones only fill a lead list
