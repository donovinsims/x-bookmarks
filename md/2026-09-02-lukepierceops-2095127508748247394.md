# The New AI Agency Org Chart: A Full Walkthrough

## Post

There was a hiring sequence every ai automation agency owner knew by heart.
Land your first handful of clients and drown, then hire an offshore developer to do the building. Coordination breaks, so then you hire a project manager. Clients feel neglected because you and the PM are both facing inward, so then you hire a client success manager.
Three hires before you saw any margin at all. Nobody questioned it because the structure existed for a reason: building was slow. When a build took a team 3 weeks, you needed a layer of people whose entire job was coordinating the humans doing the building.
That constraint is gone. I've built AI and automation systems for 90+ companies over 4.5 years, and builds that took my team weeks in 2024 ship in days now. Agencies still running the old org chart are paying three salaries to coordinate around a bottleneck that no longer exists.
The new structure has 2 roles. This is the full walkthrough of how to build it, in order.
Phase 1: Fire yourself from the building
The first person removed from the building is you.
You stay in the client meetings, the emails, and the project management. The only thing you step away from is the building itself.
A lot of owners do the exact opposite. Building feels like the real work, so they hire someone for client communication and keep coding. That is backwards. Anyone can do the building now, and nobody else can hold your client relationships, so the building is what you hand off first.
The move is hiring 1 or 2 part-time developers. That's your tech bench. Every build flows to them while you keep running everything else on every account.
What makes or breaks this phase is the structure around the bench. Every client project gets a shared channel where progress shows daily. The bench runs a daily sync, escalates blockers within minutes instead of days, and closes each day with a report on what shipped. Skip that structure and projects die quietly, because you handed off the building and lost sight of it at the same time. Get it right and this phase alone transforms the business. You go from operator buried in code to the person running every client conversation while execution happens behind you.
Phase 2: Make the hire that replaced two jobs
Eventually your roster maxes out, and it has nothing to do with build capacity. The bench can absorb more work. The ceiling is you, because there's a limit to how many client relationships one person can run well.
The old playbook says hire a project manager here. Don't do that.
Hire an FDE. A forward deployed engineer. One person technical enough to speak to the builds and strong enough client-side to own the relationship. They take the full client-facing role for your next wave of clients, everything you were doing, and they hand the building off to the same bench you already built.
This single role replaced both the PM and the CSM. Those were always one job split in half, and the split is where information went to die. The client tells the CSM something, the CSM relays it to the PM, the PM translates it for the developers, and by the time it reaches the person building, it means something else. The FDE ends the telephone game. The client talks to one person who knows everything about the account.
The role only became viable once building got fast enough for one person to oversee a full roster of projects while running every client conversation. That's where the tools are now.
Phase 3: Repeat until you're only doing growth
When your FDE's roster maxes out, add another FDE. Same playbook, same shared bench. Nothing about the structure changes as you grow.
This is what breaks the old math. The old structure added headcount across three layers with every new wave of clients, so margin stayed flat or got worse as you scaled. Here, a new wave of clients costs one hire, because every FDE drains into the same shared bench. Margin gets better as you scale.
Your own job changes along the way. Phase 1, you're running clients with a bench behind you. Phase 2, you're running clients alongside an FDE. Phase 3, you're running sales and growth while FDEs run the clients and the bench runs the builds.
That's the whole org chart. FDEs running the clients, a shared bench running the builds, and nothing in between.
Why this works now and didn't in 2024
All of this traces back to one shift: the build got commoditized.
When building was the bottleneck, org charts were built around managing builders. Now the bottleneck is the relationship: knowing the client, mapping their processes, deciding what to build and in what order. So the structure reorganized around the person who owns that, and everything else collapsed into a bench.
The execution details still decide whether this works: when exactly to make the FDE hire, how to comp them, the daily systems that keep quality high when you're two steps from the code. Those numbers and templates are what we work through inside the Inner Circle. But the model itself is what you just read, and the direction of the industry is set.
The org chart every agency owner was taught to build was designed around slow builds, and builds aren't slow anymore. The middle of the agency got deleted.
