# A garage door repair guy doing $350K a year booked a call with me last week.

## Post

A garage door repair guy doing $350K a year booked a call with me last week.

No website until last year. Every single customer for 6 years came from word of mouth and his Google Business Profile.

He told me he set up his "Google Maps website" when he first started and never touched it again. We checked on the call and I confirmed: he never added services, never posted photos, never responded to a single review. Just had his phone number listed and let the calls come in.

And it worked because he's in a smaller market where most of his competitors aren't doing much either. He has 100-something reviews, which was enough to stay visible when there are only 5 or 6 other options in town.

Then last year he finally put up a basic website.

Nothing fancy... homepage, a few service pages, a contact form. Cost him $1,500.

His calls from Google jumped about 30% within a couple months.

Not because the website was amazing. Because Google finally had something to connect to his profile. Service pages gave Google more context about what he actually does. And people who clicked through from his GBP could see real info instead of just a phone number.

He said he felt stupid for waiting so long. Six years of leaving leads on the table because he thought a website wouldn't matter for a one-man operation.

The wild part is his GBP still isn't optimized. No service descriptions, old photos, no services as products, etc. If he actually dialed that in he'd probably 1.5-2x again.

A lot of solo operators think marketing doesn't apply to them because they're small.

In my opinion, it's the opposite.
