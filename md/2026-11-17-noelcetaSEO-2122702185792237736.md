# 561K Google AI impressions in 90 days. 24 actions to give Fable 5.2

## Post

561K impressions in Google's AI features in 3 months. Daily went from about 700 to about 17K.

That's Google's own Search Console report, on one of our own sites. Not a third-party "AI mentions" score.

You don't need 232 tactics. You need a short list, ranked by clear rules, that you can run on your own data.

Below: 24 moves in tiers, with the rules for each. Every S and A move has a prompt for Claude. No tool to buy.

What we shipped, in order

This is what went live on that site while the line climbed. It's a timeline, not proof that any one item caused any one jump.

Mid-Aug (about 700 a day): one H1 per page (one page had 11), stronger titles on the main landing pages, Organization, Article and FAQ structured data. Whole site prerendered: 229 pages, server response 0.1 to 0.2s (was 0.5s+, and 1.3 to 3.1s on non-English pages). Hub navigation changed from JavaScript buttons to real links.

Late Aug (about 1K to 2K): homepage rebuilt around the main search. Hard facts in the hero, live stats from our own database, headings written as the questions people search, FAQ from 10 to 17 questions. A guide section under the main hub. Our 136 one-to-one comparison pages rebuilt: a clickable matrix on the hub, a verdict and a next step on each, FAQ schema.

Late Aug to mid-Sep (about 2K, then 3.5K): everything translated into 11 more languages (12 in total) with hreflang. Every English title and description de-duplicated and rewritten.

Late Sep (steps to about 6K around Oct 3 to 5): long URLs consolidated to short ones with redirects, related posts under every post, 16 new hub pages of 1,500 to 1,900 words each.

Oct (about 9 to 10K, then 13K): a free interactive tool with a score table and live averages from our own users' results. Five short quizzes. 3 more languages on a staged rollout (15 total). Content routes fully static and cached at the edge.

Early Nov (16 to 17.5K): homepage title and description updated with exact facts.

No paid ads ran. The blog posts went out on a schedule the whole time. That's the backbone of the tiers below.

How to use this

In Search Console, open Performance > Search results, set the last 3 months, and export. Keep Queries.csv and Pages.csv. For the translation move, also keep Countries.csv.

Open Claude (Claude.ai or Claude Code). Paste the Base rules below, then one tactic prompt, then attach the files. Add your site URL, brand name and 3 to 5 facts about what you sell.

Pick one tier and work it for a week. Don't jump around.

Base rules (paste first, every time):

 

How we ranked it

S: fastest to do, and it works on pages Google already shows. Do first.

A: big effect, but weeks, not days.

B: depends on your niche. Check the fit.

C: do once. Hygiene.

Skip: popular, we don't do it.

S tier: do first

1. Write headings as the questions people search, then answer in one direct line. Add an FAQ with matching schema.

Why S: 2 to 3 lines per page, no new page. We did this on the homepage and hubs.

 

2. Fix titles, H1s and descriptions. One H1 per page, main search in the title, no duplicate metas.

Why S: 10 minutes a page. We did all three.

 

3. Add a better section for searches stuck at position 8 to 20. Not a new page.

Why S: closest to page 1 for the least work.

 

4. Fix the page Google already shows for your best money search.

Why S: one cause per page, and it already has impressions.

 

5. Refresh pages that lost 30% or more of their clicks. (Export with Compare: last 3 months vs previous period.)

Why S: Google already trusted them.

 

6. Link with real links, not JavaScript buttons. Then link from pages that get traffic to pages that make money.

Why S: crawlers follow links. We changed hub navigation to real links.

 

A tier: big effect, slower

7. Publish first-party data nobody else has. A free tool, calculator or stats page fed by your own users' results.

Why A: Google has nothing else to quote. Ours had a score table and live averages.

 

8. Build one-to-one comparison pages for every pair in your category. A matrix on the hub, a verdict and a next step on each.

Why A: we have 136 and rebuilt them all in August. Slow to build, easy to scale.

 

9. Translate your proven pages into languages Search Console already shows impressions from.

Why A: demand exists before you write. We went to 15 languages in stages. Add hreflang.

 

10. Give each hub a guide section under the grid. What the parts mean, how to choose, FAQ, breadcrumb schema.

Why A: hubs hold everything together. We built 16 hubs of 1,500 to 1,900 words each.

 

11. Build the pages you're missing.

Why A: new pages take weeks, but this is where growth lives.

 

12. Consolidate duplicate and long URLs with redirects. Keep one page per search.

Why A: stops two pages splitting one search. We moved long URLs to short ones.

 

B tier: depends on your niche

13. Get links to pages whose content is already right. Why B: pays only after content is fixed.

14. Answer real questions on Reddit and Quora as a real person. Why B: slow, and only where your buyers ask.

15. Local business: match name, address and phone on every listing. Why B: local only.

16. Publish on a schedule. Why B: we did it the whole time. It compounds, but slowly.

17. Programmatic pages, only with a unique data table each. Why B: same text with a new city doesn't count.

C tier: do once

18. Make pages static and fast. Why C: ours went from 0.5s+ to 0.1 to 0.2s. Nobody ranks for speed alone.

19. Add structured data that matches the visible text. Why C: Google says no special schema is needed for AI features.

20. Submit a sitemap and check indexing. Why C: catches silent problems.

21. Set hreflang right and scan translations for broken text. Why C: easy to ship wrong.

Skip: popular, we don't do it

22. Parasite SEO. Why skip: Google's site reputation policy targets third-party content published mainly to borrow the host's ranking signals.

23. llms.txt. Why skip: Google's docs say you don't need AI text files for AI Overviews or AI Mode.

24. Mass AI pages that say nothing new. Why skip: Google's scaled content abuse policy covers many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings.

How to track it

Search Console > Performance > Search results > Generative AI features (Beta). Search type: Web (text). That shows Google. It doesn't show what ChatGPT or Perplexity say about you. We built MentionFlow (mentionflow.ai) for that. It's ours.
