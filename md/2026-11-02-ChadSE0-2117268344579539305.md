before you start: the export every prompt reads

### what it did first: it refused to write a second page
this is the step most prompts skip, and it is the one that saves you
chatseo.app already has a page for that search, /blog/free-ahrefs-alternative. in the last 3 months Google showed it 276,054 times. it got 53 clicks. average position 17.2
a prompt that just "writes a page about free ahrefs alternatives" would have made a second one. two pages on one search split everything Google gives you, and usually both sink

then it did the thing a good SEO does before touching a page that already gets traffic: it listed what the page must not lose
142 queries in the export contain "ahrefs" plus "free" or "alternative": 23 clicks, 129,046 impressions
every query with a click got a line saying where the new version covers it (title, a heading, the FAQ)
the old page had 13 jump links that Google shows on their own in the results. it kept 9 of them, the 9 that carried 118,466 of those impressions, and told me which 4 it dropped and why
and it found the angle i did not see. "ahrefs open source alternative" had only 39 impressions, but 4 clicks, a 10.26% click rate, the best of the whole group. the old page never mentioned open source. the new one has a section on it
that is what reading your own Search Console first gives you. not a keyword list. the searches people already click you for
### then it read Google's first page
10 results. for each: what type of page, its title, and whether the exact search is in the title
 
what it found:
the first page is lists, forums and a video, plus an AI Overview on top
no "People also ask" box for this exact search
not one of the 10 has "free ahrefs alternative" in its title. the closest uses the plural
chatseo.app is not in the top 20 for it
that last one hurt to read and it is the most useful line of the report. 276K impressions, and not in the top 20 for the exact search. the page gets shown for long variants at position 12 and worse, where almost nobody scrolls. more on that in Part 5
then it wrote what every result has (the bar) and what none of them has (the opening):
the bar: a list of 8 to 20 tools, free and paid mixed, a year in the title, prices per tool
the opening: nobody gives a source and a date for every price, nobody mentions the open source option, nobody puts the free limits side by side as numbers, and nobody says that Google Keyword Planner asks for billing details
 
that list became a section of the page, word for word. a page that answers what the first page misses is the only kind worth writing when you start at position 17
### then it checked every price on the vendor's own page
this is step 4, and it is why i trust the page more than the one it replaced
it opened each tool's pricing page and wrote down the free plan, the limit, the paid price, the url and the date. anything it could not read stayed out
what that changed:
it removed 7 claims from the old page that it could not confirm. one was the Ahrefs Enterprise price, written $1,490 on our page. it is $1,499
it left out Ubersuggest, because the prices would not load
it left out Google Keyword Planner, because Google's own help page says you need billing information to use it
Mangools only gets one line, because its pricing page did not load either. the report says so
an AI that writes "Ahrefs costs around $99" from memory is how a page loses trust. the prompt makes it impossible: no url, no number
### the page it wrote
8 tools, a comparison table with nothing but checked facts, one section per tool with a first-person verdict, the "what other lists miss" section, a 6-question FAQ, 7 [SCREENSHOT] markers where my own captures go, and a sources list at the bottom with every url it read
 
two things i like:
the first 3 lines answer the search. "the best free Ahrefs alternative is Ahrefs Free itself". no intro, no "in today's digital landscape"
ChatSEO is 7th of 8. the prompt says never rank your own product first, and it obeyed. a list where the author is number one reads like an ad, to readers and to Google
two things i would fix before publishing:
the comparison table is wider than the page on desktop, so the last column gets cut. a 2-minute fix, but it shipped broken
the 7 [SCREENSHOT] slots are empty on purpose. the captures are your proof that you used the tools. an AI cannot take them for you, and a page without them looks like every other list
### before you publish: the review prompt
i found those two flaws by reading. then i wondered if a second fresh session would find them without me. so i wrote a review prompt and gave it nothing but page.html
 
the verdict: FIX FIRST. 2 checks passed, 5 failed
 
it found the cut table, and measured it: 762px wide in a 718px box. it loaded the page at desktop and phone width to check. then it found things i had missed completely:
the page says "each number links to the page it came from". no number is linked, and the sources list is plain text. the page's whole selling point is its sources, and they are not clickable
one line says OpenSEO is "the only option" for competitor research without a subscription. the table on the same page says three other tools do it
a math error: $349 a year is $29.08 a month, not $29.10. and the same wrong number is repeated in the FAQ code Google reads
three superlatives with no proof: "the best free crawler", "the broadest free feature set", "the most direct way"
our own trial length is marked "not published". from the co-founder, that reads absurd. the fix it gave is to publish it on our pricing page
that is 15 minutes of fixes that would have cost the page its trust. the writing prompt and the review prompt are two different sessions on purpose: the one that wrote the page is the worst judge of it
-> run the review on every page before it goes live
-> fix what it lists, run it again, publish only on PUBLISH
it also gave me 5 internal link sentences, with "free ahrefs alternative" as the anchor. Part 3 does that job much better, so i will show it there
### how to use it on your site
-> if you have a search in mind, put it in {{KEYWORD}}. if you do not, leave it empty and the prompt picks from your own export
-> run it once per page, never on 10 searches at once. one page a day is the max, two a week is plenty
-> read the report before the page. if step 1 says a page already exists, you are rewriting, so keep the old URL
-> publish, then request indexing for that URL in Search Console
-> wait. a page needs weeks to settle. judge it after a month, not after a day

Part 5: what 1.27M impressions does not prove
i opened with 1.27M. here is what that number is, and what it is not
the 16 pages Claude Code wrote for chatseo.app, from July 2025 to the end of October 2026:
1.27M impressions
1.11K clicks
an average position of 17.4
a 0.1% click rate
the biggest one, the free Ahrefs alternative page, has 618K impressions of those. in the last 3 months alone: 276,054 impressions, 53 clicks, position 17.2
an impression is not a view. Google counts one every time your page is in the results someone loads, even at position 17 on page 2 where nobody scrolls. most of those 1.27M are exactly that
so what does the number prove? that Google takes these pages seriously enough to test them on hundreds of thousands of searches. a thin AI page does not get shown 618K times. it gets ignored
and what does it not prove? that they win. at position 17, they mostly do not. 53 clicks in 3 months on 276K impressions is a page on the edge of the first page, not on it
this is exactly why the Part 1 prompt starts by reading the page you already have. the money is not in writing a 17th page. it is in moving the pages you have from position 17 to position 5. that is what the rewrite is for, and that is why it refuses to start a second page

-> every page gets a human read before it goes live. yours
