# How to Sell Grok Bot as a Service

## Post

I accidentally sold my first Grok Bot.
 
A cafe owner saw what I was doing with mine and asked if I could help him set one up.
I said I'd show him.
He basically said he'd rather pay me to do it.
Fair enough.
And I think there are going to be loads of businesses like that.
They don't want to spend their evening learning Grok Bot.
They have work they want taken off their plate which is why I get messages like this all the time on X.
  whic
So if I was going to sell Grok Bot as a service, this is how I'd do it.
Sell the job
I wouldn't start by offering:
Custom Grok Bots for businesses.
Most business owners don't care.
Start with a job they already understand.
A cafe might need something that:
→ Reads catering enquiries
→ Checks the information
→ Pulls the right pricing
→ Prepares the reply
→ Asks the owner before sending
A consultant might want something that:
→ Reads new leads
→ Researches the company
→ Checks whether they're a fit
→ Prepares the follow-up
→ Updates the CRM
An agency might want something that:
→ Checks client accounts
→ Finds anything unusual
→ Creates the weekly update
→ Flags what needs attention
That's what you're selling.
A job they currently have to do themselves.
Find something they already do manually
I think this is the easiest place to start.
Ask someone:
What do you keep doing every week that you wish you didn't have to?
You'll normally get a better Grok Bot idea from that than sitting at home trying to invent businesses to automate.
Look for things like:
→ Repetitive enquiries
→ Follow-ups
→ Reports
→ Admin
→ Checking things
→ Moving information between apps
→ Updating records
→ Looking for changes
→ Preparing the same stuff over and over
The boring jobs are probably the easiest ones to sell.
Nobody needs convincing that something they hate doing is annoying.
Watch them do it
Before I built anything I'd get them to show me the job.
Actually do it.
Screen share.
Open the apps.
Show me what comes in.
Show me what you check.
Show me what happens next.
And every time they say:
“Usually I do this...”
“But if this happens...”
“Sometimes I need to check...”
Write it down.
Those little things are the job.
A simple process can suddenly have 15 rules once you watch someone actually do it and you can use what you learn to teach Grok bot.
 
Work out what the Bot needs
Once I've watched the job, I know what the Bot needs access to.
Maybe:
→ Gmail
→ Calendar
→ CRM
→ Stripe
→ Slack
→ Google Drive
→ Analytics
→ Some internal system
It doesn't need access to everything.
Just whatever somebody currently opens to finish that job.
For the cafe, maybe that's just email, their pricing document and calendar.
That's enough.
Decide what it can do without asking
This is one of the first things I'd agree with the client.
What can the Bot just do?
What needs approval?
What should always go back to a person?
For example:
Do automatically
→ Read enquiries
→ Research
→ Check records
→ Update notes
→ Prepare reports
Ask before doing
→ Send an important email
→ Change a booking
→ Issue a refund
→ Spend money
→ Change pricing
Never do
Anything the owner isn't comfortable handing over.
You can always give it more freedom later.
I'd rather start slightly boring and have it work.
Build it in their account
I'd build the Grok Bot inside the client's account.
Their Bot.
Their apps.
Their logins.
Their data.
If we stop working together tomorrow, they still have what they paid for.
I think this also makes the offer easier to explain.
I'm not selling them access to some platform I've made.
I'm getting paid to build their setup for them.
Write the instructions around the actual job
Once I've seen the process, creating the Bot becomes much easier.
I'd cover:
JOB
What are you responsible for?
START
What causes you to do something?
CHECK
What information do you need?
RULES
What changes depending on the situation?
ACTIONS
What are you allowed to do?
APPROVAL
What needs the owner first?
PROBLEMS
When should you stop and ask for help?
FINISHED
What should be true when the job is done?
Nothing fancy.
It just needs to know the job properly.
Use their real examples
I wouldn't test it on examples I made up.
I'd ask for actual work they've already done.
Give me:
→ Five real enquiries
→ Five old leads
→ Five reports
→ Five support tickets
→ Five invoices
Whatever matches the job.
Then run those through the Bot.
We already know what the human did.
Now we can compare.
Where did the Bot get it right?
Where did it make the wrong decision?
What did we forget to tell it?
That's normally where another rule appears.
Test the annoying examples too
The easy stuff isn't what worries me.
I want the example where someone forgot half the information.
The weird request.
The customer who's angry.
The duplicate record.
The thing that doesn't fit the normal process.
The one the owner looks at and says:
“Ah, this one's f*cking annoying.”
Give the Bot that.
I'd rather break it myself than have the client's customer do it next week.
Charge for the build
I wouldn't charge per Bot.
One Bot might do something worth thousands every month.
Another might save someone 30 minutes.
Completely different value.
I'd look at:
→ How much time the job takes now
→ How often it happens
→ How complicated it is
→ How many apps are involved
→ What happens if it goes wrong
→ How much of it the Bot can actually handle
Then quote the build.
They're paying me to understand the job, build it and get it working.
Not for the number of prompts I wrote.
Then offer ongoing support
Once it's working, I'd give them two options.
Take it.
It's yours.
Or pay me to keep looking after it.
The business will change.
Prices change.
Processes change.
People leave.
New edge cases appear.
Apps get swapped.
The Bot that worked perfectly six months ago might need changing.
So the ongoing service can be pretty simple:
→ Check it's still working
→ Fix problems
→ Update instructions
→ Add new rules
→ Improve what it can handle
Then when they find another repetitive job, build the next one.
Getting the first client
I wouldn't start with cold outreach to 5,000 random businesses.
I'd start with people who can already show me the problem.
Local businesses.
People I know.
Existing clients.
My audience.
Someone running a small company.
Then I'd ask one question:
What do you keep doing manually that you wish somebody else would handle?
If they give me something Grok Bot could realistically do, I'd show them.
You don't need an hour long presentation about agents.
Take one piece of their actual work.
Run it.
Show them the result.
That's basically what happened with the cafe owner.
He saw what was possible and wanted it done for him.
The offer
I'd probably keep the actual offer this simple:
Send me one repetitive job in your business.
Show me how you currently do it.
I'll build a Grok Bot inside your account that can handle as much of that job as possible.
You'll approve anything important.
Once it works, it's yours.
That's enough.
The whole process
If I was doing this for a client tomorrow:
1. Find one job
Something they're already doing manually.
2. Watch them do it
See the actual process.
3. Write down the rules
Especially all the annoying exceptions.
4. Work out what it needs access to
Only connect what it actually uses.
5. Decide what needs approval
Agree this before it starts doing anything.
6. Build the Bot
Give it the job, rules and access.
7. Test old work
See whether it handles real examples properly.
8. Test the weird stuff
Try to break it.
9. Run it with approval
Let the owner check important actions first.
10. Hand it over
Then either leave it with them or keep maintaining it.
That's how I'd sell Grok Bot as a service.
I wouldn't try to convince businesses they need “AI agents.”
I'd find something they're already sick of doing.
Then offer to build the thing that does it for them.
