# Google Maps + Claude = $800 per client. $10k+/month. full playbook from absolute zero.

## Post

open google maps right now. type "plumber" and your city. scroll past the first three results. count how many businesses have no website.

there are probably 8 to 15 of them on your screen right now. each one is an $800 invoice. the website takes 40 minutes to build (15 once you've done it a few times). you don't write code. you don't design anything. you type a prompt, AI builds it, you press send. done.

$800 sounds like a lot for 40 minutes of work. it's not. the plumber makes that back in one job. the roofer makes it back 15 times over. the HVAC guy makes it back before lunch on monday. your fee is the cheapest thing they'll spend money on this month, and it makes them the most.

4 clients a month: $3,200. add $400/month in recurring services per client and by month three you're at $5,000-7,000/month. by month six, $10,000+. all from typing a job title into a search bar you've used a thousand times.

8.9 million businesses in the US have no website. not broke ones. a roofer pulling $15k per project who can't be found online. an HVAC company with 200 five-star reviews and no way to book. a dentist with a six-week waiting list and a site that looks like a powerpoint from 2009. 97% of their customers google them before calling. 62% of their calls go unanswered, and those callers don't try again, they call the next guy.

the money is on the floor. google maps drew you a map to it. literally.

the skill takes one saturday to learn. the first client takes one weekend. the pipeline is 8.9 million businesses long and maybe a few thousand people on the planet are working it. the rest of this article is the exact playbook. every step, every tool, every script, every dollar amount.

how it works (the whole thing)

google maps shows you the broken business. AI builds them a website in 40 minutes. you send the owner a link before they asked for one. they see their name, their reviews, their services, looking better than anything they've ever had. they don't imagine what they'd get. they're looking at it. they say yes because they've been meaning to do this for five years and you just did it for them.

no portfolio. no degree. no ad spend. no audience. you don't pitch, you don't explain, you don't convince. you show. that's the whole trick.

picking your niche

home services: HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, pest control, landscaping. a single HVAC job pays $300-800. roofing pays $5,000-15,000. your $800 fee is a rounding error on their next customer. also: dental, auto repair, salons, law firms. avoid e-commerce, franchises, anyone where the decision maker isn't the person you're talking to.

one niche. one city. go deep before you go wide.

step 1. find the leads (15 minutes)

open google maps. search "HVAC [your city]" or "plumber near me" or "roofer in [wherever]." skip the top 3-4 results. they already have their act together. the leads you want sit just below.

the pattern: decent reviews (3.5 to 4.5 stars), established business, but something visibly broken. no website link. a site that loads in 9 seconds and looks like a craigslist ad. zero responses to reviews. no photos.

two or more signals = prospect.

for each one, write down: business name, rating, review count, website (or "none"), and one specific thing you noticed. "47 reviews, no website." "4.2 stars, hasn't replied to a review in 3 years."

that detail is your pitch later.

you can do this manually for 10-15 leads. or install Instant Data Scraper (free chrome extension). it reads the google maps results and dumps names, ratings, phone numbers, websites into a spreadsheet. 100+ businesses in 90 seconds. aim for 25-30 leads per niche per city.

step 2. build the site (40 min → 15 min after a few.)

grab from their google maps profile: business name, service list, location, and their best 3-5 customer reviews. paste it into Claude with this:

write complete website copy for [Business Name], a [type] company in [city]. use their real reviews below as testimonials. include: a hero section with their main service and a "get a free quote" button, a services section listing everything they offer, a testimonials section with the real reviews, an about section emphasizing years of experience, and a final call-to-action to call or book. tone: professional but warm, like a neighbor who happens to be great at their job.

their services: [paste from google maps] their reviews: [paste 3-5 best reviews]

Claude gives you full page copy in 2 minutes. take that into Lovable, Framer, or Bolt. type "build a landing page for an HVAC company" and paste the copy in. you have a live, hosted site with a real URL in under 40 minutes.

when the owner sees it, it has their reviews, their services, their city. it looks like someone studied their business. because you did. for about 6 minutes.

step 3. send the pitch (under 70 words)

hey [first name], built a quick site mockup based on what i saw on your google profile. noticed you've got 47 solid reviews but no website linked, so you're invisible to anyone who doesn't already know your name. here's a 30-second preview: [link]. if it's close to what you'd want, happy to set it up. if not, no worries.

no mention of AI. no "digital marketing agency." no "quick question" subject line. the link does all the selling.

send via email for most niches. SMS for contractors and trades. instagram DM for salons and restaurants. follow up once after 4 days, once more after 7. then move on.

if you want faster results, call. cold emails get 1-2% reply rates. cold calls to qualified prospects book at 10%+.

don't script it word for word. just know your opening:

"hey, is this john? i was looking at your google listing this morning and noticed you don't have a website linked. is that something you've been meaning to fix, or is it not a priority right now?"

if they say "yeah, i've been meaning to," offer a free mockup. if they say "not interested": "totally fair, just wanted to flag it since the businesses above you all have sites and are getting the calls first." move on.

call your top 15-20 leads. email the rest as a fallback.

step 4. close the deal (15-minute zoom)

when they reply interested, get them on a zoom. share your screen. walk through their mockup. then ask: "what would you change?"

now they're pointing at things. "can we add emergency service here?" "different photo there?" they're mentally owning the site. they're not deciding whether to buy a website. they're deciding whether to buy THIS website.

quote them on the spot:

"so this is what i'd build for you. $800 one-time, live within a week. i'll handle the domain, hosting, everything. want me to put it together?"

then shut up. let them fill the silence.

if they say "let me think about it," try: "totally fair. what would you need to know to decide today?" half the time, they just had one small question they didn't want to ask.

if they ask "how much?" before you've shown the mockup, don't blurt a number. "depends on the setup. some of this is one-time, some ongoing. easiest way is i look at your setup with you for 15 minutes and send you a number that actually fits. does tomorrow at 10 work?"

if yes: send an invoice within the hour via Stripe or PayPal. take a 50% deposit. start the same day. deliver within 48-72 hours. speed kills doubt.

step 5. deliver the site

domain on Namecheap or Cloudflare ($10-12/year). host on Vercel, Netlify, or Lovable's built-in hosting. contact form via Tally or Formspree (both free). make sure the phone number is clickable on mobile. test on your phone. text the owner: "your site is live at [url], here's how it looks on mobile."

total delivery: 2-3 hours of work.

step 6. stack recurring revenue (this is where $10k happens)

selling websites alone can push $5,000-8,000/month. what gets you to $10k is services that bill monthly and take almost no time.

service A: review management, $300-500/month per client.

only 5% of businesses respond to their reviews. 97% of customers read those responses. a one-star improvement = 5-9% more revenue for the business. you're selling math.

every morning, open google maps for each client, check new reviews, and paste them into Claude:

write a professional, warm response to this review for [Business Name], a [type] in [city]. thank them by name if mentioned. address any complaint directly. under 100 words. sound human, not corporate.

takes 20-30 minutes per morning for 10 clients. then set up the review engine: go to the client's Google Business Profile, click "ask for reviews," and grab their direct review link. shorten it with Bitly. the client sends this link to every happy customer after a completed job. via text, not email. text open rates are 98%. email is 20%.

give this exact template to the business owner: "hey [customer name], thanks for choosing us today! if you have 30 seconds, a quick google review would mean a lot: [link]." no app, no fancy CRM. just a text after the job.

within 90 days they go from 12 reviews to 40. from 3.9 stars to 4.4. a half-star increase means roughly 20% more revenue for them, so your $400/month fee looks like a rounding error. they never cancel because the proof is sitting right there on google.

service B: missed-call text-back, $200-300/month per client.

62% of calls to service businesses go unanswered. 85% of those callers never try again. they call the competitor. a simple automation sends a text within 30 seconds of every missed call: "hey, sorry we missed you, how can we help?" set this up in GoHighLevel or OpenPhone in 15 minutes per client. the owner does nothing. leads stop disappearing.

10 clients on reviews + missed-call at $400/month average = $4,000 in recurring revenue that shows up whether you sell a single new site or not.
