# internal secrets behind 3-4 MILLION linkedin dm's sent...

## Post

140+ companies. 30 different industries. between 3 and 4 million cold dms sent.

(and a TON of cold emails sent as well)

replies and booked calls from morgan stanley, salesforce, comcast, and linkedin themselves.

a VP at morgan stanley replied in under four hours.

here is what that volume of data tought us...

1. the list does more work than the copy

we have run identical copy against two different lists and watched the reply rate TRIPLE on the better one.

so the list gets built first, and it gets built properly.

where the leads come from:

public job feeds, meaning greenhouse, lever and ashby endpoints that show who is hiring right now

government registries and filings, which show contracts won, lawsuits filed, and visas sponsored

event attendee lists on linkedin, which are exportable and full of intent

niche directories and review sites where buyers describe their own problems

then every profile gets qualified by a model BEFORE it ever enters a campaign.

the qualification prompt checks specific fields:

seniority, so you hit the person who owns the budget

headcount band, since a 12 person team and a 400 person team buy differently

industry match against the icp

a live buying signal in the last 90 days, meaning a raise, a hire, a contract, a launch

disqualifiers, meaning lending, insurance, b2c, and anyone under the revenue floor

the model tags each profile keep or drop, with a written reason attached to every call.

we run this across lists in the HUNDREDS of thousands, at a fraction of a cent per profile.

then we read the drop reasons in bulk.

when 4,000 leads get dropped for the same reason, the icp definition was wrong, and you fix it before spending a single send.

a tight list of 5,000 beats a loose list of 50,000 across every industry we have tested it in.

2. the inbox where these messages land

a decision maker's work email takes 200+ cold emails a month. their linkedin inbox holds a handful, and they read it themselves.

the doors we use to get in there:

open profiles, which accept an inmail with no connection request and zero credits spent

inmail credits, refunded whenever the recipient replies inside 90 days

event attendees, messageable for free after you register for the same event

event invites, which run on a separate weekly allowance of around 1,000

the refund rule reshapes the economics entirely.

a reply returns the credit, including a rejection, so answered messages cost NOTHING.

open profiles are why a small outbound team can reach a fortune 100 executive directly.

a large share of senior people leave the setting on, and their inbox stays quiet enough that a relevant message gets read the day it arrives.

that is the route a message takes to a morgan stanley VP, and why it came back the same afternoon.

3. what millions of sends taught us about the message

every element below was tested across industries until the pattern held.

the rules that survived:

keep it under 400 characters, because longer messages get skimmed and dismissed

open the first line on their situation, using the trigger you found during qualification

name the problem in their language, describing the symptom they feel day to day

make one small ask, meaning an opinion, a teardown, or a document you can send them

keep links out of message one, since they are the strongest spam signal you can attach

ask a single question, so the reply requires one decision

the small ask carries the most weight of anything in that list.

a call request costs a stranger 20 minutes of their scarcest asset. an opinion costs them 30 seconds of typing.

the second one gets answered, and the call comes out of the conversation that follows it.

on inmail, the subject line decides the open. plain and specific beats clever, every time we have tested it.

follow up cadence that works:

follow up one, three to four days later, carrying a new angle or a piece of proof

follow up two, seven to ten days after that, referencing something fresh at their company

then the thread closes and the lead goes back to the list for a later quarter

each follow up carries something NEW. repeat bumps into a silent thread generate reports, and reports kill accounts.

4. the operations behind the volume

millions of dms only happen when the accounts stay alive, and that is an ops discipline.

what every sending account needs:

age and real history, with profiles created months before they carry any load

a warmup ramp over three to four weeks, starting at a trickle and climbing gradually

one clean, consistent ip per account

a photo, a headline, a filled out profile and some posting history, so a click on the profile finds a plausible person

activity dripped across working hours with randomized gaps

volume distributed across the fleet, with each profile sitting well inside its own limits

acceptance rate under 20% marks an account as a nuisance. spam reports do far worse damage.

both trace back to sending messages to people who had no reason to hear from you,

which is why qualification protects the account AS MUCH as it lifts the reply rate.

a warning from linkedin means that account leaves rotation for two weeks. profiles die when people ignore that and keep sending.

there is one more thing 140+ campaigns made clear.

the lead flow is only half the machine.

speed of response on the client side is the other half.

a lead that sits for two days goes super COLD, and a booked call that nobody follows up on is a wasted lead.

so the handover matters a lot.

replies get worked inside the hour, positive replies get a human immediately, and every thread has an owner.
