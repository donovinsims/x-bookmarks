That's the whole run. Here's what came back.
Move 1: The founder's feed is the engine, not SEO
Receipt: Gojiberry gets under 1,000 US search visits a month (est.). It peaked at 959 in March 2026 and fell to 598 by October. 84% of that is people searching the brand name.
Meanwhile: $2M ARR (founder post) → $4.5M ARR (cofounder post). Both self reported.
The biggest post in the whole run: @romanbuildsaas Sep 18. "Comment JEV for early access." 491K views. 4,124 bookmarks.
What it means: a $4.5M ARR claim on tiny search traffic. The growth is coming from attention, not Google.
 
The prompt:
Pull gojiberry.ai's monthly US organic traffic for 24 months and its brand share. Add the founders' top X posts by views and any revenue claims, on one timeline.
Why it works: every comment boosts the post and hands you a warm lead.
Do this week: post your next feature with "Comment [word] for early access." DM every commenter yourself.
Move 2: Name your launch after the hottest AI agent
Receipt: Gojiberry, Jul 14: "Meet Claude Fable for sales." 275K views.
Oct 22–29: five launches in eight days. Claude Duet. Attio app. Chrome extension. Lead database. "Sales OS for Grok Bot."
Postiz founder @wickedguro, Aug 26: "Grok bot is insane!" 157K views.
Both sites have a page for every big AI agent. Postiz has about 26 per agent.
What it means: they borrow attention someone else already paid for.
The prompt:
Map every page on gojiberry.ai and postiz.com by folder and flag pages named after AI agents. Then list both founders' X posts from the last 6 months with views, and mark the launches.
Why it works: people already search for and share those names.
Do this week: pick the agent your users already use. Ship "[Your product] for [agent]" and launch it under that name.
Move 3: Build free tools that catch other people's searches
Receipt: Postiz has 249 free tool pages. They bring in 86% of its search traffic (est.).
The top one is a Discord font generator. A social scheduler, winning on Discord fonts.
Organic went 29K → 47K → 57K/mo from July to September 2026 (est.).
Gojiberry does the same: free sales generators and ~40 "X alternatives" pages.
What it means: the product pages barely get search traffic. Free tools do the work.
 
The prompt:
Pull the top 1,000 US keywords postiz.com ranks for. Group the traffic by URL folder (/tools, /blog, /compare...) and give me each folder's share. Show the top 10 pages.
Why it works: buyers don't search for you, they search for a small job, so do it for free.
Do this week: list 5 small tasks your users google right before they need you. Ship one as a free, no login tool page.
Move 4: Copy what your users already made go viral
The best twist in the whole run.
Receipt (as reported in a July 2026 article): a user wrote an X Article about Postiz + OpenClaw. 7.2M views. 700 trials a day.
The founder copied the format. His own Article got ~500K views with ~200 followers.
Then he paid X creators $60–$350 per repost. Reported result: $17K → $143K MRR.
What it means: Postiz's best growth move wasn't Postiz's. A user found it. The founder scaled it.
The prompt:
Find the most viewed X posts about Postiz the founder didn't write, plus articles and videos on how it grew. Date each one on the growth timeline.
Why it works: a user's post is proof the angle lands; yours are guesses.
Do this week: search X for your product name. Rewrite the best user post's angle as your own long post, and credit them.
Move 5: Two quiet moves that pay for themselves
Affiliates before ads. Both run affiliates on Tolt, months before any paid ads showed up.
Gojiberry since Nov 2025: 30% recurring, forever; its page claims 800+ affiliates. Oldest live Meta ad: May 18, 2026. Postiz since Apr 2025; its Google ads ran May–Jun 2026.
Their own product as the growth machine. Gojiberry's cofounder: "We used our own tool to grow our own tool." 5 LinkedIn accounts, 30 invites + 30 DMs a day each (their claim).
What it means: both only pay when something works. Affiliates cost nothing until a customer pays. Your own product turns every result into a demo.
The prompt:
When did gojiberry.ai and postiz.com add affiliate, pixel and analytics tools? When did their oldest Meta, Google, LinkedIn and TikTok ads start? Then list every growth tactic the founders name on X, marked as their claim.
Do this week:
Launch an affiliate program at 30% recurring. Send it to your 10 happiest users.
Run one week of your own growth through your own product. Post the numbers, good or bad.
The gap nobody's watching: AI answers
I asked ChatGPT 4 simple buyer questions per company.
Gojiberry: not named once. Not for "best AI SDR tool", not for "best Clay alternatives". Artisan, Clay and Apollo took the spots.
Postiz: missing from "best social media scheduler" and "best Buffer alternatives". Then you ask about open source: "My top recommendation is Postiz."
$3.5M ARR claimed, and invisible to ChatGPT. 0 of 7 answers across both of my runs. The other one only shows up when the question is its niche.
What it means: if you don't own a category, ChatGPT won't hand you one. Pick a narrow category you can own and make it your name. 
 
The prompt:
Ask ChatGPT 4 simple buyer questions in gojiberry.ai's category: "What is the best AI SDR tool?", "What is the best LinkedIn outreach automation tool?", "What is the best tool to find warm B2B leads?", "What are the best Clay alternatives?" For each answer, tell me whether Gojiberry is named, at what position, and who is named at #1 to #3.
