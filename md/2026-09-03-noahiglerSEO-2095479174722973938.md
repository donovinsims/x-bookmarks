# I pulled an Austin HVAC map scan that should bother every big shop in the market.

## Post

I pulled an Austin HVAC map scan that should bother every big shop in the market.

A business called Austin AC Repair had 24 reviews and was outranking eight-figure brands with 18,000+.

The full local brand stack looked like this:

> Legal business name that says the service and city
> Google profile using that real name
> Matching exact-match domain
> Legitimate local office
> Website focused on AC repair in Austin
> Citations repeating the same business information
> Reviews and job photos tied to that market

People give the domain too much credit.

The biggest Maps advantage comes from the exact-match business name. Google sees "Austin AC Repair" and gets a strong relevance signal before it reads the rest of the profile.

The domain, page titles, citations, and local content reinforce the same service and market.

I'd consider this when:

> Entering a new city with little brand equity
> Launching a real new division
> Buying or building a group of local brands
> Competing against large companies with generic names and weak local relevance

I'd stay away when the company already has a powerful brand, the location is fake, or the plan is twenty copied websites with no real operation behind them.

To scale it:

1. Pick one service and market with strong demand and margin.
2. Register the DBA or local trade name.
3. Buy the matching domain.
4. Secure a real operating location.
5. Build a unique site, profile, citations, and tracking.
6. Collect reviews from work completed through that operation.
7. Repeat after the first location produces real jobs.

At scale, you end up with a portfolio of local brands with separate profiles, tracking, and P&Ls.

The hard part is building a real local operation around each one.
