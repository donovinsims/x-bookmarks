# We rebuilt an electrician's website and the owner just emailed saying they hit #1 on Google for their main service plus…

## Post

We rebuilt an electrician's website and the owner just emailed saying they hit #1 on Google for their main service plus their city.

Super cool owner.

Here's the part worth paying attention to.

They didn't get there with a big backlink campaign or a massive review count. The site itself did most of the work, because we built it to rank from the first wireframe instead of bolting SEO onto a finished design.

This is the process we run on every site we build.

Keyword research and URL mapping happen before anyone touches design. We list every service worth its own page and every city worth targeting, then map each keyword to exactly one URL. The sitemap is the strategy. Most websites get designed first, and then someone tries to sprinkle keywords on top afterward. That order is backwards and it's why most home service sites never rank for anything past their own name.

That was exactly this company's situation. Their old site was a template job from an overseas builder that came bundled with the domain. It ranked for nothing past their own name and pulled in calls for every low dollar job in town while the work they actually wanted went to competitors.

Then every core service gets its own page, built around the work that actually makes money. For this company that meant electrical panel replacements, backup power systems, and EV charger installs. Each page describes the job in plain English, shows photos from their real work, and answers the questions customers ask on the phone before booking.

The main city gets a dedicated page with the service plus city in the title tag, the H1, and the first paragraph, with the same name, address, and phone number as the Google Business Profile. Surrounding towns each get their own location page with content actually written about that town.

Then the technical layer that nobody sees but Google grades:

> Every page internally linked from the homepage so it all gets crawled
> A clean sitemap submitted in Search Console so nothing sits unindexed
> Local business schema sitewide and FAQ schema on the service pages so Google and the AI models can parse exactly what you do and where
> Page speed tightened up, since a slow site bleeds rankings and calls at the same time
> Mobile first with a sticky call button, since about 80% of visitors are on their phone

There's no secret ingredient, just structure and completeness that almost no local competitor bothers with.

Backlinks and reviews absolutely take you further, and we build both for every business we work with. But in a lot of local markets the competing websites are so thin that a properly structured site on its own puts you in the top results. This electrician is proof. New site, right structure, and a few months after launch they're #1 for the exact search that brings them work.
