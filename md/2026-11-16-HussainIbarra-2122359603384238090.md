# How to become dangerously well-spoken (use these 5 strategies)

## Post

There is nothing that is more dangerous than knowing how to speak well.

If you know how to get your point across, you can do anything. You can land the job you want. Find the partner you want. Sign the client you want. Get the sponsorship you want.

Your success in life entirely depends on how well spoken you are.

I’ve struggled with social anxiety my entire life.

Every new school year, until 7th grade, whenever the teacher would ask me to introduce myself in front of the class, I’d burst out crying because I’d feel so overwhelmed.

I’d go weeks in schools where I wouldn’t speak to anyone. I was socially awkward and very introverted.

I thought that was just the way life is.

You’re either naturally good at speaking, or you’re not. Some people become politicians, while others get addicted to playing video games.

But growing up, I realized that just because I struggle with social anxiety, it’s not an excuse to be socially awkward or to say I can’t hold a conversation.

After all, everything can be learned. The same way you learned how to walk as a kid, or ride a bike, or write your name, you can also learn how to speak and overcome your social anxiety.

And now, I’m lucky enough to have given talks in front of 400+ people, built an audience of 52,000 followers, and people pay to listen to me speak and write.

Which is nuts to think about...

And look, by no means have I beaten my social anxiety for good.

I still find myself having to climb a mountain just to start a conversation, sometimes even with the people I know.

(Just ask my friends and they’ll confirm this)

But at least now, I can speak to people and I’m not as awkward as I once was (or at least I hope so).

So today, I wanna share with you some strategies that have helped me the most.

Let’s begin.

I - Build your knowledge base (practice thinking)

You can only speak at the level you are knowledgeable in.

If you want to speak with depth and meaning, you need to think deeply about your ideas, perspective, and opinions.

Most people never think about or question their ideas, perspectives, and beliefs.

When you speak to most people, you’re not speaking to them. You’re speaking to the news, the media, the content, the memes, and the propaganda they consumed. Most people’s worldviews are shaped by memes which is scary to think about.

They hear an idea, subscribe to it, then go to war with anyone who disagrees with them (even though they never actually questioned what they believe in).

You’re not articulate because you haven’t thought deeply about what you want to say.

You need to question your mind.

The best way to do it?

Writing.

You get to explore, question, dissect, analyze, develop, and test your ideas before you start speaking. So when you speak to someone, you already know what you want to say and how to say it.

When you write, you’ll quickly realize 1) just how little you know about a topic and 2) how most of your ideas aren’t your ideas. You’re just regurgitating what you heard from social media, the news, and the people around you.

If you want to take things a step further, you can share what you’re learning on social media to collect feedback.

You can use social media as a tool to think in public. You’ll get better at articulating your thoughts, know how to say something in an impactful way, and show people the value your ideas carry.

I wrote thousands of social posts, hundreds of newsletters, and did dozens of live workshops. I know what ideas perform well and how to say them in an impactful way.

I really only have 3-5 ideas that represent my personal brand (and by extension a lot of my personality). It’s not surprising to see that whenever I speak about those ideas with others, they find them fascinating.

I’ve spent countless hours refining them.

Those are the ideas that will get you discovered.

Those are the ideas that people want to hear.

Those are the ideas that will bring in new listeners, readers, and even introduce new friends to you and what you stand for.

You don’t need to memorize entire speeches and books, or random quotes, or have the entire knowledge of the encyclopedia in your mind to speak well.

You just need 5-9 of your biggest and best ideas that can be connected to almost any topic. So when it’s time to speak, you already know where to start because you thought about it and said it hundreds of times.

Every person you look up to keeps repeating the same talking points over and over again because those are the ideas they questioned the most and have done well in free markets (social media).

II - 5 strategies to become well-spoken

1) Write for clarity

Most people tell you to journal.

I’m not a fan of journaling. I tried it a few times and didn’t really see any benefits from it.

But I am a fan of writing (if you didn’t already know).

Writing is the same as journaling, but it’s less about you and more about your ideas.

When you write, you literally put your thoughts, experiences, and beliefs under the spotlight.

What does writing have to do with speaking?

It helps to understand what speaking actually is.

Speaking is nothing other than your thoughts said out loud.

Writing is the same but instead of making weird noises with your mouth, you put them on paper (or screen).

To become well-spoken, you must think clearly.

The best way to practice clear thinking is writing. It’s a safe and controlled environment where you can get practice.

You can write in a physical notebook, your notes app, or have a dedicated writing app where you save all of your ideas and writing in one place so you never forget them.

2) Sit in silence

The foundation of good speech is good thoughts.

If you’re drowning your mind with content, you have no room left for good thoughts to surface.

This is why all your great ideas come to you when you’re in the shower or right before you go to bed. It is the only time your mind has to think.

I’m not saying that you should quit society, renounce everything, go live in the mountains, become a monk, meditate for years, and achieve enlightenment. You have a job, a family to take care of, and responsibilities to meet.

What I am saying is that you need to create time blocks for yourself to think. It could be during your morning coffee, the time you journal, going for a walk, on your way to work, or going to the gym.

For me, my time to think is in the gym or while running.

My point is: do something that preserves your peace.

Let your mind digest all the information it has consumed. Only then, when you find a sense of peace, will your speech have a stronger foundation to work from.

3) Get comfortable with silence

Most people are uncomfortable with silence.

When the conversation goes quiet for 2 seconds, your mind starts to think that there’s a problem and so you try to fill the void with something, leading you to rush to say something.

Now your mouth moves faster than your mind. You stumble on your words, and you’re now self-conscious.

It’s a mess.

My friend, silence is your friend.

You know what you want to say. You just need to give yourself a few seconds to let the right words come to you.

“But Hussain, I feel awkward during that moment of silence”

Fair.

Personally, I love to do 2 things.

First, explicitly tell the other person I need time to think. This shows that you’re taking the conversation seriously and you value the other person (because you are putting extra effort into the answer you’re about to give).

Second, repeat their question as a question or you can ask them to be more specific.

This does two things:

It gives you a brief moment to think about your answer.

The other person might explain their question, or specify what they meant, which makes answering them easier.

And if you don’t have anything to say?

Tell them.

Normalize not having an opinion on every topic or an answer to every question.

Most people think this makes them look like they’re unintellectual, but from my experience, I see it as the opposite.

If someone tells me that they don’t know, I immediately think they’re smarter because they know where their knowledge ends. The ignorant doesn’t know what they don’t know.

4) Command your pauses

Modern society killed pauses.

Everyone wants everything instantly. People are watching videos in 2x (sometimes 4x) speed. Their brains are fried.

But if you want to become well-spoken and articulate, you need to know when to pause during your speech. I’m still trying to get better at this.

If you look at all the well-spoken figures in history, they know when to pause.

Most people think pausing while speaking is a sign of low intelligence. It’s not. It’s the opposite.

Pausing is a simple, yet extremely effective way of adding more impact to your speech. It shows you value the words you choose to speak and that you’re calculated and intentional with your language.

It also signals to the listener to pay attention to what’s about to be said. And if you know when to pause during your speech, then it’s easier to command respect and to influence others.

5) Use frameworks to speak

Thinking well is a prerequisite for speaking well.

Frameworks are training wheels for your mind.

There are 2 frameworks I always fall back on.

i) The Spiritual Guru’s Framework

I learned from listening to spiritual gurus. If you ever paid attention to how they speak, you’ll notice they all speak in the same way.

It has 3 parts:

Claim — you start your speech by making a claim about something you believe in, a lesson, or anything really.

Explanation — explain what you mean by what you just said. Give your reasoning and perspective.

Examples — drive your point home by giving examples.

You’ll see me use this method in my newsletters. It’s the most effective and straightforward method I’ve found.

ii) The Micro Story

Humans love stories.

The human mind is a storytelling, story-generating machine.

The clouds you see do not look like anything. It’s your mind that’s lying to you, telling you that it looks like your 5th-grade math teacher.

Humans can’t help but pay attention to stories.

Once you master this framework, you’ll know how to make anyone interested in any topic in seconds.

The foundation of a story is transformation (taking someone from point A to point B).

If you want to make it more interesting, you can add a valley (big problem) or a hill (big benefit).

For the sake of this newsletter, we’ll add a valley.

Problem (point A) — state a problem that you and your listener have experienced or observed before

Amplify (valley) — illustrate how that problem spills over to their other areas of life if it’s not solved.

Solution (point B) — give the solution to the problem. This can be a lesson, a new perspective, or actionable step(s) your listener can take to solve their problems.

If you’re familiar with copywriting or marketing, you’ve seen people talk about this framework before.

It’s one of the simplest (and most effective) frameworks you can learn.

If you’re paying close attention to the way I write my newsletters, I use the Micro Story to outline my newsletter, then I use the guru’s framework to fill each section.

It works like magic.

Before I end, I want you to think of these as building blocks you can use to get better at speaking (or even writing).

It helped me massively in my life.
