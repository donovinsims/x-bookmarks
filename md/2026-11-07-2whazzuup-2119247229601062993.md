# 200 Backlink Opportunities for SEO: Directories, Launch Platforms, Communities & More

## Post

A practical link-building resource for niche website owners, SaaS founders, affiliate marketers, and independent publishers.

Most backlink lists are filled with the same recycled directories.

You submit your website, wait for approval, and hope something happens.

But backlinks aren’t all created equal.

Some platforms help customers discover your business. Others let you publish useful resources, list software, contribute to communities, or earn editorial mentions.

I’ve organized 200 backlink opportunities into 10 categories so you can focus on the platforms that actually fit your website.

These are places to investigate—not 200 guaranteed backlinks. Some are free, others have paid tiers, and many require a qualifying business, product, project, or piece of content.

Let’s get into it.

How I’d Turn These 200 Opportunities Into an Actual Link-Building Workflow

The goal isn’t to submit to 200 websites.

The goal is to identify the placements most likely to put your business in front of the right people.

Here’s a repeatable system.

Step 1: Pick Your Best-Fit Categories

Start with two or three categories relevant to your website.

For a SaaS business, that might be software directories, startup launches, and relevant developer resources.

For a local tutoring company, it might be local listings, educational resources, and legitimate community partnerships.

For an affiliate site, it might be original-resource publishing, relevant discussion communities, and editorial outreach.

Step 2: Build a Shortlist of 20

For each prospect, evaluate:

Relevance: Does this website reach your intended audience?

Eligibility: Do you have the required product, business, expertise, or content?

Cost: Is there a free route, or are paid features necessary?

Placement: Can an accepted submission actually display your website URL?

Audience: Could real visitors discover and click your listing?

Don’t let high Domain Rating override relevance.

Step 3: Prepare Your Submission Materials

Create a reusable folder containing your website URL, concise business description, detailed description, logo, screenshots, founder biography, and relevant project examples.

Have multiple descriptions tailored to the audience rather than pasting identical promotional copy everywhere.

Step 4: Submit Five Quality Opportunities a Week

A manageable weekly target:

Monday: Research and qualify five prospects.

Tuesday: Prepare submission materials.

Wednesday: Submit or pitch.

Thursday: Follow up where appropriate.

Friday: Check approvals and update your tracker.

For platforms that need original content or participation, replace submissions with the work needed to contribute something valuable.

Step 5: Track What Actually Happened

Your backlink tracker should include:

What to record

A platform accepting a submission does not automatically mean you earned a backlink.

Visit the published page and check the actual link.

Step 6: Double Down on What Works

After a month, compare your results.

Which platforms approved submissions?

Which ones sent visitors?

Which ones exposed your product to the right audience?

Which activities created new relationships or additional editorial mentions?

Put more time into the channels generating useful outcomes.

One Important SEO Warning

Google explicitly identifies low-quality directory links, automated link creation, and manipulative link schemes as potential link spam.

So don’t interpret this article as instructions to build 200 artificial links overnight.

Use the list for discovery, not mass submission.

A useful listing, an original tool people reference, or a genuine editorial mention can be worth far more than a hundred pages created only to hold your URL.

That’s the difference between building backlinks and building a website that earns attention.
