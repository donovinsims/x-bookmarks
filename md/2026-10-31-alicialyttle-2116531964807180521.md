# This prompt will find the money plan hiding in your resume...

## Post

This prompt will find the money plan hiding in your resume...

(Paste it into your AI, then paste your resume minus ID numbers & personal details. No voice profile yet? Delete "my voice profile" in phase four. Thank me later.)

----------------------------------------------

Create this system: a personal intelligence blueprint that finds what people would pay me for, based on what I've actually done, not my job titles.

Interview me before you conclude anything. Build on what you learn instead of starting over.

## Core outcome

Create three connected layers:

1. A blueprint of who I am, what I'm actually good at, and my real constraints
2. A story bank where every story ends on a lesson
3. Three income ideas that fit me specifically, ranked, with why each one fits me

All three layers must use what you learn about me, not generic advice.

## Non-negotiable principles

- Read my resume the way a business advisor would, not the way a hiring manager would.
- Judge me on what I've done rather than what I've called it.
- It has to be specific to me. Otherwise, who cares?
- Ask for stories one at a time.
- Don't start until you tell me you've got it.

## Phase one: blueprint

Before we start, here's what we're building together.

Over the next few prompts we're going to create my Personal Intelligence Blueprint: a working profile of who I am, what I'm actually good at, what my real constraints are, and what kind of income opportunity would fit my life.

We'll build it in three passes:
1. You read my resume and tell me what you actually see in it
2. You interview me to fill in everything the resume doesn't say
3. You write the Blueprint itself

Once it's written, hold onto it. Use it for everything I ask you from here on out, and remember it for future conversations so I never have to explain myself twice.

Don't start yet. Just tell me you've got it, and I'll paste my resume.

## Phase two: read my resume

When I paste my resume, read it the way a business advisor would, not the way a hiring manager would.

Tell me:
- What I'm actually good at, based on what I've done rather than what I've called it
- The things on here I've clearly undersold or buried
- Skills I have that people pay for privately, outside of a salary
- What this resume tells you about how I think and work

Be specific and point at the evidence. Then ask me the questions you still need answered to give me proper advice.

## Phase three: story bank

After the blueprint, I want to build a story repository: the personal stories I can use in my own marketing so it sounds like a person and not a brochure.

Based on everything you know about me, ask me for stories in specific categories: a time I fixed something nobody else could, a moment I felt stuck, a time someone thanked me for something I thought was ordinary, and the moment I decided I wanted more.

Ask for one at a time. Two or three sentences each. Then organize them into a story bank with a note on what each story is good for.

For every story, add the lesson it teaches and the objection it helps someone get past. Keep it quick and make the point.

## Phase four: income ideas

Then, using my voice profile, my Personal Intelligence Blueprint, and my stories, give me three income opportunities that fit ME specifically, not generic side hustle advice.

For each one:
- What it is, in one sentence
- Why it fits me in particular, referencing what you know about me
- What I'd realistically charge
- What it demands: hours per week, money to start, how long before the first dollar
- The honest risk, or the reason it might not work

Run each through these four:
- Speed to first dollar: Could I get paid within 30 days, or is this a six-month build?
- Fits my actual life: Does it survive the hours I really have, not the hours I wish I had?
- Uses what I already have: Am I selling a skill I own, or one I'd have to go buy first?
- Somebody already pays for it: Can I name three people who pay for this today?

Then rank them for someone in my exact situation and tell me which one I should test first and why.

## Phase five: pick one

Then ask me five questions to help me pick one of those.

The worst thing to do is not make a decision.
