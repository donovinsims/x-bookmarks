# 10 Bots That Work While You Sleep(Prompts Attached)

## Post

Why now
Agents are leaving the chat window and moving into the tools you already use. Gartner expects a third of enterprise apps to ship with agentic AI by 2028, up from under 1% in 2024.
So treat them like a team: ten bots, one job title each, and one approver. You.
How to install a bot
Reply under the post and tag the @bot, or paste its prompt into Grok Bot. It asks its setup questions once, then runs on its own schedule.
10. Personal X Assistant
Your researcher, writer and hook specialist for X. It learns your voice from your last 50 posts, scans 20 accounts you learn from and hands you one queue a day.
Runs: daily at 08:00 · Replaces: a social media assistant · Never: posts for you
 
 
9. Competitor Monitor
It snapshots every rival once, then checks pricing pages, changelogs and job boards against that baseline every day. The weekly report holds only what changed, each line with its source.
Runs: daily check, weekly report · Replaces: a market analyst's weekly · Never: pads a quiet week
 
 
8. Lead List Builder
Every lead comes with a public, dated signal and a link to prove it. No evidence, no row. It stops at 50 and drafts a 4-line opener for the best ones.
Runs: on demand, stops at 50 · Replaces: hours of manual prospecting · Never: guesses emails or sends
 
 
7. Group Chat Manager
Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index counts 275 interruptions in an average workday. This bot reads Telegram, Discord, Slack and WhatsApp for you and turns them into two digests.
Runs: digests at 09:00 and 18:00 · Replaces: reading every group chat · Never: sends without you
 
 
6. Subscription Auditor
In a C+R Research survey, people guessed they spend $86 a month on subscriptions. They actually paid $219, and 42% were paying for something they no longer used.
Runs: monthly · Replaces: a yearly money audit · Never: cancels or pays by itself
 
 
5. Content Repurposer
It pulls your outlier posts and rewrites each idea natively for X, LinkedIn, Threads and Instagram, inside each platform's limit.
Runs: weekly · Replaces: a repurposing freelancer · Never: publishes
 
 
4. Morning Brief
McKinsey found that interaction workers spend 28% of the week on email and nearly 20% looking for information. One page at 07:30 takes the first chunk back.
Runs: every day at 07:30 · Replaces: a chief of staff's morning · Never: replies or accepts for you
 
 
3. Grok Swarm
One orchestrator splits your question into tasks, spawns up to 10 specialist bots, runs them in parallel and merges their work into one report. Conflicts are shown, not averaged.
Runs: on demand, up to 10 agents · Replaces: a small research team · Never: hides a disagreement
 
 
2. Build-Anything Agent
Describe a tool in plain words. It asks 5 questions, writes a one-page spec for you to approve, builds on its own cloud machine and shows a real run before it hands anything over.
Runs: on demand · Replaces: a contract developer for small tools · Never: deploys or spends without your OK
 
 
1. Second Brain
Send it anything you save. It reads it in full, links it by idea and brings it back when you work on that topic.
Runs: always on, weekly review · Replaces: a notes system you never update · Never: shares your notes
 
 
 
Where to start
Two bots a week, starting with the ones that give you time back. Tonight: paste bot #4.
4 tips for better output
Show, don't describe. Three posts you like teach a bot your voice faster than a paragraph about it.
One job per bot. Two jobs in one prompt make two worse bots.
Watch the first week. Edit its drafts and tell it why. Add what you learn to the rules tag.
Make your own. Copy any prompt here, keep the tags, change the job.
