# how to force claude to create digital products that actually sell

## Post

12 months from now, two versions of you exist.
version a is still on chapter 3 of the ebook you started in january. you've rewritten the intro 14 times. you keep googling "how to structure a lead magnet." the doc is still called "untitled document."
version b shipped 7 products this year. one of them did $8,700 in a month. faceless page. wrong-dimension banner. product he didn't write.
same starting point. different trajectory. the split happens at exactly one point — the moment you stop trying to write and start learning how to force claude to do it for you.
"you can't make money with digital products"
sure bro.
i know hundreds of guys making $5k, $10k, $30k a month selling google docs, notion templates, pdf playbooks, and swipe files. most of them can't write. some of them barely speak english. they've never taken a copywriting course.
they figured out one thing everyone else missed.
claude doesn't need you to be a good writer. claude needs you to be a good prompter. and there's a specific way to prompt it that most people never get to because they're still asking it to "write me an ebook about productivity."
that prompt returns garbage. every time. because the model is trained on the average of the internet, and the average of the internet on productivity is dogshit.
here's what most people get wrong.
they treat claude like a ghostwriter. they open a chat. they say "write me a 30-page ebook on [topic]." claude gives them 30 pages of the exact copy you've read on 400 other blogs. they read it, feel disappointed, and decide ai can't write products.
wrong problem. wrong input. wrong output.
claude isn't a writer. claude is a synthesizer. it takes what you give it and reorganizes it into a coherent artifact. garbage in, coherent garbage out. specificity in, sellable product out.
the operator prompt stack
i call it the reverse-engineer stack. five inputs. one output. sellable product in a weekend, sometimes an afternoon.
not "write me an ebook." not "give me 10 chapter ideas." this is different.
variable 1 — the reverse-engineer input
find 3 products in your niche that are already selling. gumroad discover, whop marketplace, stan store trending. sort by copies sold or star count. anything with 500+ purchases is confirmed profitable.
pull the sales page copy. pull the product's table of contents if visible. pull the pricing. pull any preview pages.
paste all of it into claude. every word. don't summarize. don't clean it up. raw input.
this alone puts you ahead of 90% of people using claude to write products. they're trying to invent. you're trying to synthesize what's already working.
variable 2 — the voice input
feed claude 3-5 samples of writing in the voice you want the product to take. could be your own posts, could be a competitor's newsletter, could be a book you loved in the niche.
tell it: "match this voice. same rhythm. same sentence length. same vocabulary. do not use corporate language. do not use hedging."
now claude knows what it's supposed to sound like. before this step, it defaults to the average voice of the internet. after this step, it sounds like whoever you told it to sound like.
variable 3 — the structural blueprint
do not let claude decide the structure. you decide the structure.
give it a table of contents. 6-12 sections. each section title as specific as possible. not "introduction." not "what is [topic]." specific promises with specific outcomes.
example: instead of "chapter 3 — mindset," give it "chapter 3 — the 4 beliefs you need to install before day 1 or the system doesn't work."
structure is 40% of the product. most people skip this step and wonder why the output feels formless.
variable 4 — the constraint stack
now add the constraints. this is where sellable diverges from generic.
word count per section (600-1200 usually). forbidden phrases (leverage, unlock, dive deep, at the end of the day, delve, game-changer). required format (short paragraphs, hard line breaks, lowercase or sentence case). tone directives (confident, no hedging, no filler, no rhetorical questions as transitions). proof requirements (every section must contain one specific example, one specific number, or one specific mechanism).
now claude is fenced in. it can't drift into generic middle-of-the-internet writing because you told it what it's not allowed to do.
variable 5 — the human polish pass
claude will still produce sentences that feel wrong. 4-8 hours of polish. read every paragraph out loud. cut every line that doesn't add new information. rewrite anything that sounds like it came from linkedin.
this is the step nobody talks about. the ai people say "ai wrote this in 10 minutes." they either didn't polish, in which case the product doesn't sell, or they polished and lied about it because "ai wrote it" is better content marketing.
polish is the difference between a product and a document.
what happens today vs what it looks like with the stack
what happens today when someone tries to make a product with claude:
open chat. type "write me a guide on [topic]." get 8 pages of pablum. try to edit. give up. start again. try a different niche. repeat until you conclude claude is overhyped and go back to your job.
what it looks like with the stack:
friday night: pick niche, pull 3 top sellers, extract their structure and copy. saturday morning: run all 5 variables through claude, get first draft in 90 minutes. saturday afternoon: 4-hour polish pass, cover art in midjourney, upload to whop. saturday night: pinned tweet up. product live at $27.
by sunday morning you either have your first sale or you have proof that the niche doesn't work, and you kill it and start again next weekend.
the total cost is a claude subscription and a saturday. the payoff is either $0 or a page that prints for the next 18 months. that math is not a coin flip. that math is a business.
stop asking the wrong question
stop asking "what should i write about?"
start asking "what's already selling that i can reverse-engineer better, faster, cheaper?"
the first question puts you in creative mode, which is where 99% of would-be operators get stuck for 6 months and never ship. the second question puts you in reconnaissance mode, which is what actually pays.
your job isn't to invent. your job is to observe what's working and produce a differentiated version of it. that's what every top gumroad seller is doing. that's what every $10k/mo faceless page operator is doing. they're just too polite to say it out loud.
here's how to actually build one this weekend
step 1 — open gumroad discover. sort by top selling in your niche. write down the 3 products with the most copies sold. document their price, their sales page angle, their table of contents.
step 2 — open claude. paste all 3 sales pages, tables of contents, and any preview content into a single prompt. tell claude: "analyze these three products. identify the shared structural pattern. identify what's missing across all three. identify the strongest hook out of the three."
step 3 — feed it your voice samples. 3-5 pieces of writing you want it to match.
step 4 — give it your custom table of contents. 8-10 sections. specific promises. no generic titles.
step 5 — add the constraint stack. forbidden phrases, word counts, proof requirements, format directives.
step 6 — draft. claude produces the first version. 60-90 minutes.
step 7 — polish. 4-8 hours reading out loud, cutting fluff, tightening voice. this is not optional.
step 8 — cover in midjourney or canva. checkout on whop or gumroad. price at $27-47. one pinned tweet. one landing page in google doc format if you want the lowest-friction sale possible.
ship by sunday night. no exceptions.
three questions to test every product against
would you buy it at $27 if you saw it in your feed today?
can the outcome be described in one sentence a normal person would repeat to a friend?
if it flopped, would you know within 14 days, or would you spend 6 months blaming the algorithm?
if the answers are yes, yes, and 14 days, ship it. if any answer is no, kill it and reverse-engineer a different niche.
12 months from now, two versions of you exist.
version a is still perfecting chapter 3. still googling "how to write a lead magnet." still convinced ai can't write products because the one time they tried, the output was generic. still consuming content instead of shipping product.
version b shipped 7 products. two of them died. one of them prints. that one is enough. the compounding started 8 months ago, and the gap between the two versions is now uncrossable in less than a year.
the window matters because the tooling is the sharpest it's ever been and the crowd hasn't caught up yet. claude fable 5 alone changed what's possible in a saturday afternoon. by q4 2026 there will be a wave of operators who figured this out and the barrier to entry will be a real product with real craft. right now the barrier is a claude subscription and one saturday.
you can pick which version you become.
but the pick is happening today whether you notice or not.
