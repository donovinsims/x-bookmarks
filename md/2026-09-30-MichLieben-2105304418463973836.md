# How to Build a GTM Funnel That Books 100+ Meetings a Month (Our Full 2026 Tech Stack)

## Post

Every B2B company has a GTM funnel. It's everything that happens between a stranger hearing your name for the first time and that same person signing your contract.
Somebody finds you. They leave an email, or they read a few pages and disappear. You work out who's worth a conversation, get on a call and send a proposal.
Those steps look the same at every company. The tooling underneath is where funnels differ: which tool does which job, and how a lead gets from one tool to the next.
So I'm going to show you ours at ColdIQ, tool by tool.
40,000+ people visit coldiq.com every month, and the funnel behind the site books 100+ meetings a month for our new SaaS, almost organically.
I mapped the whole thing on one page. It has 4 stages and 15 jobs, run by 40 tools.
8 of those 40 do 2 or more jobs each, and 3 of those 8 go in before anything else: Claude Code, the ColdIQ MCP and folk, our CRM.
A first build costs about $180 a month in software, plus a $500 deposit for the inboxes.
I'll go through it stage by stage:
Generation: outbound that starts from one signal, one weekly recording cut into 5 outputs, and 4 kinds of search pages.
Capture: 4 paths that end in the same CRM record, silent visitors included.
Management: a scoring skill and a routing table that set the next step for every lead.
Conversion: one call transcript that drafts the proposal and feeds sales training and product.
The build order, and the number to watch at each stage.
For every stage you'll get the jobs, the tools, the steps, the numbers to set and the prompt or file to copy.
 
How the funnel fits together
 
Our funnel has 4 stages:
1. Lead generation gets the right people to notice you. It runs on 2 engines. Outbound means you write to them first, and inbound means they find you through content, search or ads.
2. Lead capture turns an anonymous visit into a name, a company and an email.
3. Lead management decides what each lead is worth and what happens to it next.
4. Lead conversion covers the sales call, the proposal and the contract.
Each stage breaks into jobs, like finding an email, scoring a lead or sending a proposal.
Outbound and inbound feed the same capture and management stages. A newsletter signup, a free-tool user and a booked meeting all land in one CRM and get scored by one set of rules.
 
The terms this guide uses
ICP: ideal customer profile, the kind of company and buyer you sell to best.
CRM: the database where every lead, company and deal lives. Ours is folk.
Signal: an event that suggests a company might need you now, like a funding round.
Enrichment: filling in the missing fields on a record, such as job title or email.
Sequence: a series of messages sent over days or weeks, run by a sequencer.
Lead magnet: something useful you give away in exchange for an email address.
CTA: call to action, the one button or link you want a reader to click.
API: the door a tool opens so other software can use it directly.
MCP server: a connector that lets an AI agent use a tool directly.
Skill: a saved instruction file that Claude Code loads for a matching task.
Webhook: a web address that receives data in real time.
Credits: the prepaid units data tools charge in.

Step 3: check the rules in each region before any outreach
Identifying a visitor and emailing them fall under different rules in different places. This is general information, so check it with your own lawyer before you send.
United States: CAN-SPAM has no opt-in requirement and covers B2B email. It asks for accurate headers, a physical postal address and an opt-out honored within 10 business days.
Canada: CASL asks for consent before you send, express or implied.
United Kingdom: the ICO says PECR's consent rule leaves out corporate subscribers. UK GDPR still covers any named person's data.
European Union: GDPR counts IP addresses and cookie IDs as personal data, and B2B email rules differ from country to country.
RB2B, Vector and Instantly all keep person-level identification to US visitors.
Why it works: in my experience, silent visitors are the largest group on a B2B site, and identification is the one capture path that reaches them.
What it gets you: a card in folk for every captured lead, holding the person, the company, the source and a score. That includes the visitors who never filled a form.

I published a version of this map in April 2025, with the same 4 stages. 11 of its tools are still on the map today, among them Instantly, lemlist, beehiiv, Customer.io, Webflow and Tally.
3 of the swaps changed how the funnel runs. All 3 put the job on a tool Claude Code can reach, through an MCP or an API:
CRM: HubSpot on the 2025 map, folk on this one. Our CRM is the source of truth, so it has to be flexible and come with a strong MCP.
Sales calls: Attention and Circleback on the 2025 map, Claap on this one. Claude Code reads Claap's transcripts through its MCP.
Proposals: Qwilr on the 2025 map, PandaDoc and Claude on this one. Claude drafts the proposal from the call transcript, and PandaDoc handles the signature.
 
Where to start
