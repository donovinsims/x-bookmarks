# If I owned a plumbing company today and wanted to dominate Google Maps in my city, here's exactly what I'd do for the f…

## Post

If I owned a plumbing company today and wanted to dominate Google Maps in my city, here's exactly what I'd do for the first 12 months.

(Bookmark this)

First thing I'm doing is sizing up the market.

Plumbing is different from every other home service vertical. The search intent runs hotter than any other trade because a burst pipe at 10pm doesn't wait until morning. That means 70% of my leads come from the Map Pack, not organic search, not my blog, not Instagram. So my whole strategy gets built around winning three spots on a map.

I'd pull up Ahrefs or Google Keyword Planner and look at five of the money keywords:
> "plumber near me"
> "emergency plumber [my city]"
> "drain cleaning [my city]"
> "water heater repair [my city]"
> "sewer line repair [my city]"

These aren't the only keywords worth targeting...They're just the highest-leverage ones for most plumbing markets.

I'd also pull longer-tail high-ticket searches like "tankless water heater installation" and "trenchless sewer replacement." Lower volume, way higher intent. A single trenchless lead can be a $6K to $30K ticket.

I'm also scanning the competition. The top 3 in most cities under 200k sit between 40 and 100 reviews with patchy citation profiles. That's a market winnable in 6 to 9 months. Going head-to-head with a Roto-Rooter franchise and two PE-backed roll-ups carrying 600+ reviews each is a 12-month build instead. Market difficulty doesn't change the playbook, only the timeline.

Day one I'm rebuilding the GBP from scratch.

This is the single biggest lever I have. If I only fix one thing this month, it's this:
> Every secondary category slot filled. The ones that move the needle for plumbers: drainage service, septic system service, water filter supplier, water softening equipment supplier, gas fitter, gas installation service.
> Business name optimized through a legal DBA. I'd file a fictitious name like "[Brand] Plumbing, Drains, and Sewer Repair" and verify it with Google. One client of mine outranked competitors with 4x his review count doing exactly this. Not a trick, a legal entity
> Service area set to cities within a 2-hour driving distance from my shop. Google doesn't trust that you can reasonably serve outside that range and won't rank you there
> Physical address used wherever possible. Service-area-only profiles get smoked by physical-address profiles in local search
> 50+ geo-tagged photos uploaded, refreshed monthly. Photos of my team at real job sites. Two photos per job, ops person uploads on a set day each month
> Every service listed individually with 100-word descriptions. Drain cleaning, water heater, sewer, emergency, repiping, trenchless, disposal, backflow
> All main services also added under the Products section of GBP. Most plumbers don't even know this section exists, let alone use it. Another ranking signal you're leaving on the table

Reviews are where I'd put consistent energy month after month.

Here's what nobody tells home service owners about reviews. My competitor sitting on 400 total reviews who stopped generating them six months ago is completely beatable by me with 120 reviews and 32 in the last 30 days. Google weighs recency as much as total count.

So my system runs like this:
> QR code cards in every truck linked directly to my Google review URL
> Every tech trained to ask in person right after the job: "If I did a good job for you today, would you mind scanning this QR code and leaving us a quick Google review? Just takes 20 seconds."
> $25 paid out per review that mentions the tech's first name
> Automated SMS follow-up at 3 hours for anyone who didn't leave one on site
> Personal calls from me to past happy customers during month one
> 24-hour response on every review, positive and negative

One client of mine went from 5 reviews a month to 35 in 60 days running this exact system. That single shift moved them from position 14 to top 3 in their primary city.

Website gets restructured to actually rank.

Most plumbing sites are built backwards...one "services" page with bullets for 8 different services, zero location pages, and a homepage that says "your trusted local plumber since 2003" and not much else. Google can't rank a Swiss Army knife page for 8 different keywords.

Here's the structure I'd build:
> Homepage focused on the primary city plus the main service
> One dedicated service page per service offered, not a single "services" page with a list of 8 things
> Each service page 1,200 to 1,800 words minimum with FAQs, photos, pricing context, and proper schema
> One location page for every surrounding city I actually serve, written from scratch and never spun from a template. Local landmarks, neighborhood names, city-specific plumbing issues like hard water, slab foundations, or pre-1970 galvanized pipes
> Elite internal linking structure connecting every service page to every relevant location page. This is how Google understands that you offer drain cleaning across Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tempe, and the rest of your service area using one website
> NAP identical across every page, footer, contact form, and schema markup
> Mobile PageSpeed score above 70. Below 50 will cost you 2 to 3 spots in the Map Pack alone

Conversion rate matters just as much as the rankings.

Phone number top right with click-to-call wired to the dialer on mobile, not a modal. Form fields kept to name, phone, and service. Trust signals above the fold like review count, years in business, license number, service area map. Every extra form field costs about 10% of conversions.

Most home service sites convert under 1%. Well-built ones convert 3 to 5%. Same traffic, three to five times the leads. If your phone is quiet, the cheaper fix is usually the website, not more SEO.

Citations and backlinks are how I'd build trust faster than my competitors.

For citations, I'm getting listed on at least 80 directories with identical NAP. Yelp, BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Nextdoor, Yellow Pages, Foursquare, Home Depot Pro, the local chamber of commerce. One mismatch like a missing suite number, a different abbreviation, or a dash format change removes some trust with Google.

Backlinks are where home service businesses get scammed.

A vendor offering 500 backlinks for $99 is selling you a Google penalty. Real link building in a competitive market costs $500 to $1,500 a month. I've watched clients spend $300 a month for a year and add four legitimate backlinks total. The agency isn't necessarily scamming them. $300 a month just doesn't buy real link building.

The links worth building:
> "Partnered" company links. Direct outreach to other contractors and service businesses in my market (or in markets across the country) for legitimate link partnerships. Some of the highest-quality, most relevant links I can build, and almost nobody does this
> Supplier pages like Ferguson and local wholesalers
> Chamber of commerce, PHCC, state plumbing board
> Local news features through HARO, Qwoted, or direct outreach to local reporters
> Youth sports sponsorships ($300 to $500 gets you a .org backlink and trains AI models on your brand)
> Guest posts on home service blogs
> University trade program partnerships

Press releases are the cheat code that builds backlinks AND triggers AI search visibility at the same time.

I'd publish one a month for anywhere between $50 to $400 each depending on the distribution service. Topics that actually get pickup are a new service launch, a charitable donation, an annual emergency call data report, or response coverage during a weather event. The topic almost doesn't matter. What matters is my business name showing up next to "plumber" and my city across news sites and aggregators.

That's how I show up in ChatGPT.

Most of my competitors aren't even thinking about AI search yet. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini are getting asked "who's the best plumber in [city]" thousands of times a week and the businesses showing up in those answers are the ones with consistent press release history, strong GBP signals, and listicle placements.

So I'd run a 90-day AI sprint:
> Month 1: 2 press releases, 3 useful answers in r/[mycity] and r/[mycity]Homeowners as the owner (not the business), pitch 3 local "best plumber in [city]" listicles
> Month 2: 2 more press releases, 3 more Reddit answers, follow up on the listicle pitches
> Month 3: audit which LLMs are now citing me and double down on what's working

A client of mine ran this exact sprint and showed up in ChatGPT recommendations within about 60 days. His competitors had zero AI presence.

Here's the timeline of actual results:

Month 1: foundation built. GBP rebuilt from scratch, all service pages and location pages live, 80+ citations cleaned and built, review system running with techs trained

Months 2 to 4: quick wins and ranking movements start showing up. Keywords I wasn't ranking for at all start appearing in positions 10-20. Reviews ramping past 25 a month

Months 4 to 7: Map Pack movement, ideally top 5 in my primary city for the primary keyword across a decent radius

Months 7 to 12: top 3 in primary city Map Pack for a 5 to 10 mile radius, organic search starting to kick in for long-tail and secondary keywords, AI search citations starting to show up

Smaller markets with weak competitors compress this timeline to 4 to 6 months. Going against PE-backed competitors in Phoenix or Atlanta stretches it closer to a full 12.

The playbook stays exactly the same regardless. Tougher markets just take longer.

The biggest mistake I'd avoid is hiring a cheap agency and expecting any of this to actually happen.

Run the full stack properly for 12 months and you'll own your city.
