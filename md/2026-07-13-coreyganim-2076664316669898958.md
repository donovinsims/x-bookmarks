# I'm copying a local AI event model that makes my friend $11K a month.

## Post

I'm copying a local AI event model that makes my friend $11K a month.

It costs him $4,000 a year to run. He's directly attributed $80,000 in revenue to it over 7 months.

I'm adapting his exact playbook for Charlotte, and I had Claude generate the entire launch plan from one prompt. Venue, format, marketing, follow-up, all of it.

Here's the entire model:

1. Write the prompt like a job description. I told Claude "you are my operator for this quarterly rock" and demanded finished deliverables, not advice. One recommendation per decision, no hedging.

2. Feed it your business context first. Claude knows I sell AI assessments, knowledge base builds, and managed agents. So every event decision ladders back to driving leads for those services.

3. Find the gap in your market. Charlotte has a big monthly AI meetup for developers. There's nothing for non-technical business owners, and there are way more of them than devs.

4. Copy a working model. My friend Andrew runs the same style event for developers an hour away. 40 attendees a month, $80K attributed in 7 months. I'm stealing it word for word for a different audience.

5. Define success before event one. Three events this quarter. 30 attendees by event three. Attribution tracked from day one. At least one closed services client by September 30th.

6. Claude researched the venue too. It ranked Charlotte co-working spaces by capacity, AV, and parking. I booked the top pick on the spot. $120 for two hours.

7. The format is 90 minutes. 20 minutes of networking, one simple live AI demo, more networking, then a soft CTA offering a free 15 minute AI assessment. No pitch deck, no pressure.

8. You don't need an audience to fill it. Claude found 9 local Facebook groups for business owners, one with 38,000 members. Post value first, then invite the group owners to co-host.

9. Attribution happens at registration. The Luma form takes under 60 seconds and asks one money question: what's the number one thing you want AI to fix? That answer tells me who's hot before they walk in.

10. Claude wrote the SOP so I don't run it. My chief of staff gets a checklist from 5 weeks out to 2 days out. Events two and three should require zero input from me.

Two things that make this work:

1. Don't invent, copy. Andrew's model already prints $11K a month. Adapting a proven system to an underserved audience beats a new idea every time.

2. Prompts are delegation tools. I generated this once, handed it to my chief of staff, and she runs the whole thing end to end. Build it so all you have to do is show up.
