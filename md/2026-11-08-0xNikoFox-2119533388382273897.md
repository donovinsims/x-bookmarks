# I built a website agencies charge $8,000 for in one evening with Claude. Here's the exact system.

## Post

I recorded every prompt, every mistake and every fix, then turned it into a playbook you can copy tonight.

The site in this article is for a premium dental clinic in Miami. It looks like a design studio spent a month on it. It took one evening, and the only things I paid for were a Claude subscription and a domain.

Let's go.

First, the honest part

What's real:

Claude Code writes the whole site from scratch based on your description. No template, no builder, no "every Squarespace site looks the same" problem.

Claude Code comes with the Claude Pro plan ($20/month in the US). You don't need anything more expensive for this.

The two design skills I use are free. frontend-design is made by Anthropic and quietly stops Claude from using the most overused AI fonts and layouts. UI/UX Pro Max is a community skill that gives Claude dozens of styles, palettes and font pairings to choose from.

What's not real:

"$8,000" is what agencies charge for a site like this. It's not a promise of what you'll get paid. The build is the easy part now. Selling it is your job, and I'll show you how below.

The prompts are a starting point, not a vending machine. The result depends on how specific you are and how many times you say "no, make it better."

The 7 tells of a cheap AI website

Before you build anything, learn to spot what makes a site look like it was generated in 30 seconds. Clients can't explain it, but they feel it.

Inter font everywhere

Purple-to-blue gradient in the hero

Centered headline + three feature cards with emoji icons

Generic stock photos of smiling people pointing at laptops

Copy like "Welcome to our world-class solution"

Nothing moves, or everything bounces

On a phone, it's just the desktop version squeezed

Save this list. You'll use it to grade your site later. If your site has zero of these, it already looks more expensive than 90% of the web.

Step 3. Let Claude interview you first

Most people skip this, and it's why their sites look generic. Before the design, write a brief. You don't have to write it yourself:

My answers for the clinic, in short: patients pay a lot and are nervous about it. The site has to feel calm, clean and expensive, like a boutique hotel, not a hospital. No teeth close-ups, no blue-and-white "medical" look.

Now Claude knows the business better than most freelancers would after a kickoff call.

Step 4. Steal the feeling, not the design

Step 7. From "professional" to "expensive"

Here's the biggest mistake: fixing things one at a time. It burns tokens and the site starts to look patched together.

Describe the feeling you want and ask for a batch:

My batch: soft paper grain on the background, headlines that fade in line by line, thin gold lines that draw themselves between sections, slow parallax on photos, and a cursor that turns into a small "view" circle over the treatments.

Then go section by section. Find the 2 flattest ones and give each one interaction:

If it's too much, say so: "half as fast, half as visible." Restraint is what makes it expensive.

Step 8. Photos: the one thing Claude can't do alone

Best option: real photos of the business. For a clinic, 20 minutes with a phone in good daylight beats any stock library. Drop them into the folder and tell Claude what each one is and where it goes.

No photos yet? Generate them. Don't write the image prompt yourself, because Claude already knows the whole project:

Paste them into any image generator you like. Put the results in the folder and ask Claude to place them.

One rule: if you use AI photos for a real business, never present them as real staff or real patients. Interiors and details, yes. Fake doctors, no.

How this turns into money

The build is no longer the hard part. Getting clients is.

What works best for local businesses is a spec site. Pick a local business with an outdated site (dentists, clinics, restaurants, gyms, real estate agents) and build them a new homepage before they ask. Record a 2-minute screen recording: "Here's your current site, here's what it could look like." Send it to the owner.

Typical prices freelancers charge (ranges, not promises):

Local business one-pager: $1,500–$5,000

Agency-level custom site with motion: $5,000–$15,000

Monthly care plan (updates, hosting, small changes): $100–$300/month

The most underrated line on that list is the care plan. Ten small clients at $150/month is $1,500 a month for work that takes a few hours.

A realistic start: your first paid client in a few weeks if you send messages every day. Not in a few days.
