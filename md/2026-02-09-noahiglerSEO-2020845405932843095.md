# If I owned an interior remodeling company in Denver, here's exactly how I'd rank #1 on Google and get more calls:

## Post

If I owned an interior remodeling company in Denver, here's exactly how I'd rank #1 on Google and get more calls:

(Bookmark this strategy)

There's ~3,000 people searching for remodeling services across the Denver metro each month.

Kitchen remodels, bathroom renovations, basement finishing.

That's a lot of $30k-$150k projects sitting on the table.

The 3 businesses ranking in the Map Pack right now:
> FBC Remodel - 118 reviews (2/month) - 4.7 stars
> All Pro Remodel - 59 reviews (0/month) - 4.6 stars
> Colorado Homes and Design - 32 reviews (1/month) - 4.8 stars

Read that again.

The #1 spot in a metro of 2.9 million people has 118 reviews and is adding 2 per month.

All Pro got zero reviews last month. Colorado Homes and Design got one.

For context, I've analyzed remodeling markets in Tampa, Seattle, and other major metros where the leaders sit at 350+ reviews with 10 coming in monthly.

Denver isn't even close to that level of competition.

This is one of the softest remodeling markets I've looked at relative to the metro size.

Here's the strategy I'd run:

1/ Start with an exact match business name.

None of the top 3 are using their target keywords in their business name.

FBC Remodel, All Pro Remodel, & Colorado Homes and Design.

All branding, zero keyword targeting.

If I'm starting fresh, I'd name my GBP something like: [Brand] Kitchen and Bathroom Remodeling Denver.

You still have a branded, professional feel, but...

Google gives a ranking boost when your business name matches what people actually search. And "kitchen remodeling Denver" alone gets 250 searches per month.

This has to be your legal business name or DBA. Filing one in Colorado costs under $50 and takes about a week. Google cross-references against official records when they verify your profile.

This one change alone puts you ahead of every competitor currently ranking.

2/ Get a physical location near your target customers.

This matters more than most remodelers realize.

Google heavily weights proximity to the searcher. If your GBP is in a random industrial park south of the city and someone searches from Cherry Creek, you're probably not showing up.

I'd rent a small office in a strip mall near the highest value neighborhoods.

Without knowing the area too well, I'd say somewhere around the Wash Park, Cherry Creek, or Hilltop corridor where homeowners are actually spending $50k+ on kitchen remodels.

A 400-500 sq ft space costs $600-$800 a month. That's 1/5 the price of one small bathroom job.

But it means Google trusts you as a real local business instead of just another service area profile. Physical location profiles outrank service area profiles almost every time.

3/ Review velocity is where I'd spend most of my energy.

The top 3 combined are getting 3 reviews per month total.

That's nothing.

If you can hit 15-20 reviews per month, math takes over quickly. In 6 months you'd have more reviews than FBC Remodel's entire history.

Here's the part most remodeling companies get wrong.

They only ask for reviews after completed projects. You're finishing maybe 5-10 jobs per month depending on project size. That caps your review potential before you even start.

The move is to treat every consultation and estimate as a reviewable interaction.

You're doing 20-30 free consultations per month. Even if the homeowner doesn't hire you, they still got a thorough walkthrough of their space, ideas for their project, and a detailed quote.

Train your estimators to ask on the spot right after a solid consultation.

"If this consultation was helpful, would you mind scanning this QR code to leave us a quick Google review? Takes 20 seconds."

Respond to every review within 24 hours. Work in keywords naturally.
"Thanks for trusting us with your kitchen remodel consultation in Wash Park. Glad we could help you think through the layout options for your bungalow."

This system alone can get you from 3-5 reviews per month to 15-20. And at the rate the current leaders are going, you'll pass them faster than in almost any other market I've analyzed.

4/ Build service pages that actually go deep.

Most remodeling websites have one general "services" page with a list of bullet points.

Google can't figure out what you specialize in when everything is crammed together.

Build a dedicated page for each service you offer:
> Kitchen remodeling
> Bathroom remodeling
> Basement finishing
> Whole home renovation
> Aging in place modifications
> ADU construction

Each page should go deep on that specific service. Process breakdown, pricing context, real project photos from Denver homes, and FAQs.

For the basement finishing page specifically, lean into Denver's market.

"Basement finishing Denver" gets 350 searches per month, more than any other remodeling keyword in this market. Denver homes almost universally have unfinished basements, and finishing one is one of the highest ROI projects a homeowner can do here. Write about that.

Talk about egress window requirements, ceiling height considerations in older Denver homes, and how altitude affects material choices like flooring adhesives.

That level of specificity is what separates a page that ranks from one that sits on page 3.

Your H1 on each page should use the target keyword word-for-word in the order people search it. "Kitchen remodeling Denver" not "Denver kitchen remodeling." The service + city format has higher search volume almost every time.

5/ Location pages for the suburbs are where the extra money is.

Denver's metro is massive. Aurora alone has 400k people, Lakewood has 160k, Arvada has 120k, etc.

Most remodeling companies optimize for "Denver" and ignore everything else.

Build a dedicated location page for each city you want to rank in. Denver, Lakewood, Arvada, Littleton, Aurora, Centennial.

Each page needs unique content about that specific area.

Arvada's older neighborhoods near Olde Town have 1950s-60s ranch homes where kitchen layouts are cramped and bathrooms haven't been touched in decades. That's a different remodeling conversation than Centennial, which has 80s and 90s two-stories where the original finishes are just now aging out.

Mention specific neighborhoods.

Include a driving directions map embed.

Talk about the actual housing stock and renovation needs in that area.

Page title format: Bathroom Remodeler in Arvada, CO | [Brand].

Optimize for the primary GBP category in the title, then use semantic keywords in your H1 and body content.

Don't copy-paste the same content with city names swapped.

Google catches that and none of your pages will rank.

6/ Internal linking connects everything.

Service pages link to relevant location pages. Location pages link back to service pages. Everything connects to your homepage.

Google follows these connections to understand your site hierarchy. For local businesses, internal linking structure matters more than most people realize.

If your kitchen remodeling page mentions that you serve Arvada, link to the Arvada location page. If your Arvada location page talks about kitchen renovations, link back to the kitchen remodeling service page.

This is free and takes an afternoon to set up properly.

7/ Local backlinks and digital PR.

For link building, I'd start with the relationships you already have.

Reach out to cabinet suppliers, countertop fabricators, flooring companies, and tile showrooms you work with. Most of them have "preferred contractor" or "partners" pages on their websites.

These links carry weight because they look like genuine business relationships to Google.

Join the Denver Metro Chamber of Commerce and any suburb-specific chambers where you want to rank.

Then there's digital PR, which is where nobody in this market has any presence.

Publish press releases once a month through a distribution service like EIN Presswire. The topic doesn't matter.

Each release gets picked up by news aggregators and indexed across dozens of sites. This builds backlinks for your Map Pack rankings and trains AI models like ChatGPT and Gemini to associate your brand with "remodeling" and "Denver."

People are asking ChatGPT "who's the best remodeling contractor in Denver" right now.

If your business name doesn't show up anywhere in its training data connected to remodeling and Denver, you don't get recommended.

The top 3 competitors have almost no digital PR presence. The bar is on the floor.

8/ Houzz matters for remodeling specifically.

A lot of homeowners browse Houzz project galleries before they ever Google a contractor. It's basically a visual search engine for home renovation.

Build out a full portfolio with real before and after photos organized by project type. Kitchen remodels, bathroom renovations, basement transformations.

Most Denver remodelers either skip Houzz entirely or have a half-built profile with 5 photos. A strong Houzz presence is free lead generation that compounds over time.

Reality check:
> FBC Remodel has 118 reviews with 2 per month velocity.
> All Pro has 59 reviews and got zero last month.
> Colorado Homes and Design has 32 reviews.

This is genuinely one of the most approachable remodeling markets I've looked at.

Get to 100+ reviews with 15-20 per month velocity.

Build location pages for the major suburbs.

Add local backlinks and monthly digital PR that none of your competitors are doing.

You could crack the top 3 in 6 months.
