# We installed a review system for a window repair and replacement company that now gets about 60 Google reviews a month.

## Post

We installed a review system for a window repair and replacement company that now gets about 60 Google reviews a month.

It starts with the project manager asking in person and ends with the office manager calling every customer who still hasn't left one.

The project manager asks after the job is complete:

“Would you mind leaving us an honest Google review? It helps other homeowners know what to expect when they hire us.”

Every customer gets the same ask. Nobody is told what rating to leave, and unhappy customers aren't removed from the process.

If the review doesn't come in after the in-person request, the automation starts.

> The customer gets a short SMS with the direct Google review link.

> An email follows with the same simple request and link.

> Once a review appears, that customer is removed from the follow-up process.

If the review still hasn't been left, the customer gets added to a Google Sheet with their name, phone number, project manager, job type, completion date, and the GBP connected to that job.

Every Friday, the office manager works through the sheet and calls each customer personally.

The call is simple:

“I wanted to make sure everything went well with your window project. If you have a minute, would you mind leaving us an honest Google review? I can text you the link again.”

The project manager earns $25 when the review comes through from the in-person ask or automation.

If the office manager's call produces the review, the office manager gets the same $25 incentive.

The homeowner never gets paid or offered anything. The incentive stays inside the company and rewards the staff member whose follow-up produces the review.

At roughly 60 reviews a month, the company spends about $1,500 on a system that keeps the request personal, follows up when people forget, and gives the office a final list nobody can ignore.
