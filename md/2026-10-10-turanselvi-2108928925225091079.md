Understanding algorithms
TikTok, Reels and Shorts stopped distributing content based on who follows you.
They distribute based on what performs. Every clip you post gets shown to a small test audience first. If it holds attention, the platform pushes it to a bigger one.
If it doesn't, it dies, and your follower count barely changes the outcome either way.
Two things to take from this:
The first few seconds decide everything.
Most viewers drop off in the opening moment if the hook doesn't catch them, and the algorithm is watching that exact drop-off to decide whether your clip lives or dies.
And every single clip is a fresh test.
One video gets one shot at the algorithm.
A hundred videos get a hundred shots. The platform doesn't punish you for posting a lot, it rewards whichever clips perform no matter how many you put out.
 
The anatomy of a clip that goes viral
A clip lives or dies on the first two seconds, so that's where the work goes.
Open on the thing the viewer already cares about.
No slow intro, no logo, no "hey guys." You have one second before they're gone.
The rest is mechanical.
Captions on every clip.
Length between 20 and 60 seconds.
Hard cuts, no dead air.
 

The math: what a billion views actually breaks down to
1,000,000,000 views ÷ 60 days = 16.7 million views a day.
At an average of 5,000 views per clip, that's about 3,340 clips a day.
Spread across 250,000 creators, that's roughly one clip per creator every 75 days.
A billion views is not one viral moment you pray for.
It's a flood of small and medium clips, posted constantly, across a huge number of accounts.
Why one account can never get you there
This is the wall every brand eventually hits.
You can understand the algorithm perfectly and still lose, because a single account can only post so much before the reach flattens.
The answer is not a farm of fake accounts.
The answer is a network of real creators, each posting your clips to their own real audience.
Hundreds of accounts, hundreds of angles, hundreds of small tests running at once.
That is the entire difference between a brand doing 50,000 views a month and one doing tens of millions.
The loop that compounds
The whole system is one loop you run forever.
Push out a wide spread of clips with different hooks and angles.
Watch what performs over the first 24 to 48 hours.
Kill the angles that flopped with no sentiment behind them, and make ten more versions of the ones that hit.
Every week your average climbs, because you're feeding the winners and starving the losers.
You stop guessing what works.
The clips tell you, and you just make more of that.
Why you only pay for what performs
Old-school reach made you pay up front and hope.
You bought the ad or the placement and ate the cost whether it landed or not.
The model that gets you to a billion is the opposite.
You pay per view the clips actually generate.
A creator whose clip hits gets paid, a creator whose clip flops doesn't, and the risk sits on performance instead of on you. That is the only reason posting at this kind of volume is affordable in the first place.
