# My 14 years of SEO knowledge and 2800+ tweets combined into one article.

## Post

If you’ve been burned by SEO before, you’re not alone. Most advice is written for SEOs - not for business owners. This article exists to fix that.

This is not a list of useless tactics.

Most SEO content fails because it treats optimization as a checklist.

Do this. Then this. Then wait.

That approach assumes unlimited time and zero pressure.

Local businesses don’t operate that way.

What follows are operating rules - the patterns that decide whether a business ranks, gets calls, and stays visible over time.

These points are ordered on purpose

SEO advice usually fails because everything is presented as equally important.

It isn’t.

Some mistakes quietly kill rankings. Others don’t matter at all.

The sections below are sequenced the way I evaluate and fix local businesses in the real world - starting with the decisions that have the highest impact and moving toward refinements.

Read them in order.

I’ve seen this SEO confusion so often that I ended up speaking about it on a TEDx stage.

https://youtu.be/2B79SIsRP0w?si=0DsXJi3X_jOf3xaD

I. “We’re family-owned for 20 years” isn’t a ranking factorGoogle doesn’t rank stories.
It ranks signals.

Longevity only helps if it shows up as:

reviews

mentions

engagement

consistency

If your history doesn’t translate into measurable trust, Google ignores it.

II. Your primary Google Business Profile category is one of the strongest local ranking signals.

It tells Google what you actually are.

If it’s wrong, everything downstream suffers.

Secondary categories support. Primary category defines.

III. Most local businesses don’t have a single page targeting their money keywords

If Google doesn’t see a page clearly built to serve a query, it won’t rank you for it.

Blog posts don’t substitute for service pages.

Homepages don’t either.

Each core service needs its own page.

Clear. Specific. Intentional.

IV. You can’t rank in a city you don’t mention

Serving an area operationally is irrelevant.

SEO requires explicit geographic confirmation.

Cities must appear in:

headings

body content

internal links

contextual references

If they don’t, Google assumes you don’t serve them.

V. Your homepage should sell one thing first

Most homepages try to sell everything.

That dilutes relevance.

Your homepage should:

lead with your primary service

reinforce location

route users toward conversion

Focus beats variety.

VI. If your phone number isn’t clickable on mobile, fix that first

Local SEO is mobile-first by default.

Most searches end in a call.

Anything that adds friction between intent and contact costs you leads.
Every day.

VII. A two-second delay can cost a significant share of conversions

Not because users are impatient. 

Because urgency searches demand immediacy.

Speed builds trust.

Slowness introduces doubt.

VIII. You don’t need backlinks from Forbes. You need backlinks from your city

Prestige doesn’t equal relevance.

Local backlinks:

confirm geographic legitimacy

reinforce topical alignment

outperform national mentions for local intent

Relevance compounds.

Prestige doesn’t.

IX. Blog posts don’t convert. Service pages do

Blogs educate.

Service pages sell.

If resources are limited, prioritize revenue-producing pages.

Content should support conversion paths - not replace them.

X. Listings are not citations

Listings give visibility.

Citations give consistency.

Accurate NAP across trusted directories builds baseline trust.

Inconsistencies create doubt.

Doubt suppresses rankings.

XI. Install call tracking

If you don’t track calls, you’re guessing.

Rankings without attribution are vanity metrics.

Call tracking shows what actually generates revenue.

XII. Use real photos

Stock photos weaken trust - for users and Google.

Real photos:

prove legitimacy

show activity

signal freshness

Before-and-after photos work because they show outcomes, not branding.

XIII. Ask for reviews immediately after the job

Waiting doesn’t work.

Fresh reviews outperform old ones.

Recency beats volume.

A steady review loop signals an active, trusted business.

XIV. Make your GBP offers do real work

Offers increase:

engagement

clicks

commercial relevance

Inactive profiles stagnate.

Active profiles compound.

XV. If your service area covers the whole country, you serve nowhere

Precision beats breadth.

Define only where you actually operate.

This strengthens geographic confidence.

XVI. Put your CTA above the fold

Don’t make people scroll to call you.

Local intent is impatient by nature.

Meet it immediately.

XVII. Street names and neighborhoods beat big brands

Hyper-local details:

confirm relevance to Google

create familiarity for users

Big brands struggle here.

Local businesses win.

XVIII. Most SEO audit PDFs are uselessly expensive.

SEO audits don’t fail because they’re wrong - they fail because they’re unactionable.

A real SEO audit should answer three questions, clearly and fast:

What is broken?

What is holding rankings back right now?

What should be fixed first — and what can wait?

If an audit doesn’t tell you where the bottleneck is, it doesn’t matter how detailed it is.
