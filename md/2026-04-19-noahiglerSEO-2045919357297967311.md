# How Google Decides Which 3 Businesses Show Up in The Map Pack.

## Post

How Google Decides Which 3 Businesses Show Up in The Map Pack.

(Bookmark this)

---

PROXIMITY

1. Google measures the distance between the searcher and your business address. If you're 15 miles away and a competitor is 2 miles away, you're already at a disadvantage before anything else is factored in.

2. This is why physical location profiles outperform service area profiles almost every time. A real address anchored in your target city changes the math entirely.

3. "Near me" searches default to proximity more than any other factor. You don't optimize for "near me" as a keyword. You optimize by being physically closer to the searcher.

4. Expanding your reach beyond your immediate radius requires additional GBP locations. One profile can only stretch so far no matter how good your SEO is.

---

RELEVANCE

5. Your primary category is one of the strongest relevance signals Google uses. "Air conditioning repair service" will outrank "HVAC contractor" for AC-specific searches every single time.

6. Your business name matters more than most people think. A name with your target keyword in it tells Google exactly what you do before they even look at anything else.

7. Your website needs to reinforce what your profile claims. If your GBP says you do AC repair in Dallas but your website has no AC repair page and no Dallas location page, Google sees a disconnect.

8. Internal linking between service pages and location pages is how Google understands which services you offer in which areas. Without it, Google is guessing.

9. Services listed on your GBP with detailed descriptions help Google match you to specific long-tail searches. If "tankless water heater installation" isn't listed, you're invisible for that search.

10. Attributes like "24/7," "emergency service," or "veteran-owned" also help Google match you to specific intent searches. Someone searching "emergency plumber open now" is more likely to see profiles that have those attributes filled out.

11. The actual words in your reviews act as relevance signals too. A review that says "great AC repair in Plano" helps Google connect your profile to that keyword and city. Most people focus only on review count and velocity but the content of the review matters for matching you to searches.

---

PROMINENCE

12. Review count and velocity combined are one of the biggest prominence signals. A business getting 15 reviews a month with 80 total will outrank a business with 300 total getting 1 a month.

13. Backlinks from local businesses, suppliers, and industry directories signal to Google that your business is established and trusted in the area.

14. Citations with consistent NAP across 50+ directories tell Google your business is legitimate. One wrong phone number on a random listing can create confusion that drags down your rankings without you ever knowing.

15. Press releases and local news mentions build authority that helps both Google rankings and AI search visibility at the same time.

16. Behavioral signals on your profile matter more than people realize. Google tracks click-through rate from the map pack, direction requests, calls from the listing, and photo views. A profile that people click on, engage with, and call from consistently tells Google it deserves to stay in that top 3.

17. Website speed on mobile over cellular data affects bounce rate which feeds back into those engagement signals. If your site takes 6 seconds to load on a phone, people are bouncing before they ever call and Google notices that pattern over time.

---

These three factors interact with each other. You can overcome a proximity disadvantage with strong enough relevance and prominence signals, and that's exactly what good local SEO does.

But you can't outrank a closer competitor who ALSO has good SEO. That's why multi-location expansion is the long-term play for any business that wants to dominate beyond a 5 to 15 mile radius.
