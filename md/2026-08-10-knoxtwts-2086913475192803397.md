# how to master AI marketing in 30 days (the exact roadmap)

## Post

forty ad accounts run through my machine every morning, on about four hours of my day.
nothing about that is talent. i didn't get faster at the work, i stopped doing the work by hand.
that swap is the whole lesson, so let me name what actually fills the gap before anyone sells you something else. 
> not a creative testing framework
> not a thousand-ai-ads-a-day pipeline
> not targeting tricks (there is no targeting anymore, the algorithm took it)
> not a dashboard you stare at
> definitely not an agency retainer
which at most sizes is a subscription tax on not wanting to look at your own numbers.
it's a folder of text files that do the work while you sleep.
get your eye rolls in now, because we're done joking. 
everything below is the actual build, four weeks (or 4 hours - you decide), and you're getting the complete files... not descriptions of files, the files. 
paste them into claude code and they run.

week 1, days 1 to 7: the foundation and the researcher
first, the two laws. everything else in this article obeys them.
repeat after me: agents propose, they never act. i gave an early agent pause rights on live ads. it was right most days, then it killed a good ad on a bad monday, and i shut the whole thing off. an agent that acts gets one mistake. an agent that proposes gets to be wrong every week and keep its job. and an agent with budget control is a donation.
second law: numbers live in files, never in prompts. so day 1 is not a prompt, it's a file. create a folder called marketing-department, and inside it write thresholds.md while you're still rational:
 
if you're newer to this, here's what those lines are actually saying.
cost per lead is what one enquiry costs you, spend divided by leads. 
the first line kills anything that ate 25 euros without producing a single one. that's a dud, and duds don't recover, they just keep eating.
the 2x line kills ads doing twice as badly as your own recent normal... not the industry's normal, yours, which is why the baseline gets updated weekly and never from memory. 
frequency is how many times the same person has already seen the ad, and past 3.5 you're not reaching new people anymore, you're taxing the same ones.
and the floor stops the opposite mistake, which costs beginners more than all the other mistakes combined: judging an ad on 9 euros of data. under 3x your target cost per lead in spend, the ad hasn't answered yet.
what this file does psychologically is the real feature. the kill decision moves from you-at-11pm, staring at a red number after a bad day, to a call you made calmly weeks ago. you stop negotiating with yourself. you just read the list.

week 2, days 8 to 14: the creative line
the herd approach here is free-prompting an image model. "make me a professional ad." what comes back is the plastic look, a melted logo, text you can't read at thumbnail, and then the herd concludes ai creative doesn't work. the model was never the problem. the freedom was.
one fact before the file, because the whole design hangs on it: since meta's andromeda update the auction groups near-identical ads and scores them as one entry. five versions of one poster with swapped headlines is one ad wearing five outfits, and you paid five times for one test. variety is the currency. this file manufactures variety on purpose:
 
days 11 to 12, render. the hookup first: creative-line.md never touches an image model, it writes concepts.md. on day 11 you tell claude code "render every concept in concepts.md through the kie api, key is in .env" and it writes the loop itself, batches the renders, files the outputs by concept number. you review images over coffee, you don't build infrastructure.
route by price, never by taste: the cheap model class (nano banana) runs a few cents an image, so all forty render overnight for a couple of dollars. survivors get re-rendered at 2k, roughly forty cents each, and only what's about to ship ever touches the expensive model.

days 13 to 14, cull to twelve with your coffee and be violent about it. a killed concept cost you four cents. a weak one that goes live costs real budget and a piece of your nerve.

week 3, days 15 to 21: the account reader
week three is where the money stops leaking, and it starts with the least glamorous thing in this article.
day 15, naming. every ad gets one string: concept_angle_format_iteration, vocabulary held in one file. an account full of img_4471_copy can only tell you which filename won. named ads tell you which idea won, across every account you touch. rename everything live before you build the agent, or the agent reads garbage and reports garbage.

week 4, days 22 to 30: the spy, then the wiring
days 22 to 24, the spy. complete file, paste as spy.md:
 
setup is two tools you already own from day 0: the apify facebook ad library scraper for their live ads, and firecrawl for their landing and pricing pages, whose free tier covers a handful of competitors weekly. both pulls land as dated folders, this week next to last week, and the spy diffs the two. the monday cron does the pulling before the spy ever wakes up. and the ad-library half of this is already wired inside autoady too... competitor creatives arrive on tap, with the winning-ad breakdowns next to them, while the scraper route stays for the landing pages.
the no change line decides whether future-you still opens this file in march. a report that pads a quiet week teaches you it's optional, and two of those means you stop reading. one that says no change in five words has earned the read on the week a competitor drops their price.
and since a report is only as good as the moves it triggers, here's how to read a diff. an ad that vanished after six days means the angle failed, note it so you never test it with your own money. an ad passing thirty days means it pays, study every choice in it. a price that moved on their landing page means your own offer conversation now has a deadline of about a week.

three lessons in those lines, each of which cost me an evening.
first, the tool list is a hard allowlist, and a tool the file needs but you didn't list does not produce an error. the run sits silently forever, writes nothing, and exits clean. an empty morning with no error message is almost always this line, not your prompt.
headless runs bill the claude subscription you already pay for, not api credits. mine kept working through a week when the api balance was at zero.
and put a cheap script in front of the model wherever you can... a python pass that checks whether there's anything worth reading and exits early, so no model wakes up for an empty day.
one decision guide before the final stretch, because nobody should build all four at once. already running ads: start with the account reader, it pays for itself the first morning. no ads live yet: start with the researcher, everything downstream eats what it produces. doing client work: start with the spy, because a monday diff on their competitors reads like magic and costs you a cron line.
days 29 to 30, run the whole loop once, end to end, on one account. mine the language, generate the batch, launch named, read the two lists, diff the competitors. that's not really it, though. the loop only compounds if you feed the files: every kill you disagree with tightens thresholds.md, every winning ad's first six words go back into the brief. agents run the loop. files learn.
