# 10 Obsidian Web Clippers You’re Probably Not Using

## Post

Most people use 1 of the 10 things Obsidian Web Clipper can do.

They click the purple icon, dump the whole page into a Clippings folder, and never open that note again.

That is using 10% of it.

If you save 10 pages a day and spend 6 minutes cleaning up each one, you're losing 5 hours a week to copy, paste, and reformat.

In your first week you can realistically get that down to about 1 minute a page and win back close to 4 hours.

Here are the 10 templates, with the exact code you can paste in tonight.

Save this. You will use it every day.

What Web Clipper actually is

Web Clipper is a free browser extension from the Obsidian team, and the easiest way to think about it is as a small assistant that reads a webpage and fills out a form you designed yourself.

That form is called a template.

The blanks in it are called variables, which are little placeholders like {{title}} or {{url}} that get swapped for the real page title and link when you clip.

There is also an optional feature called Interpreter, which lets an AI model read the page and answer questions you write in plain English, then drop those answers straight into your note.

Interpreter works with almost any language model provider, including options that run privately on your device.

What this means for you is that the note arrives already summarized, tagged, and filed, so there's nothing left to clean up by hand.

BEGINNER

1. Build "Keeper," your clean-save template

This is the template everything else builds on. It saves the readable article text along with the details you'll want later, like who wrote it and when.

2. Add "Skim," the 3-bullet summary

Here's where Interpreter comes in. Anything you write inside {{"quotes"}} gets sent to the AI as a question about the page.

3. Set up "Marker," which saves only your highlights

Web Clipper has a highlighter built in.

You mark the lines you care about on the page, and the template saves only those lines, so your notes contain what you actually thought was important.

4. Turn on "Switchboard," so the right template picks itself

Most people pick a template by hand every time.

You don't need to, because template triggers can automatically select a template based on the current page URL or schema.org data.

Schema.org data is hidden labelling that many websites use to describe their content, like marking a page as a recipe or a product.

One setting catches people out here.

The first match in your template list decides which template is used, and if a page doesn't match any trigger rule, Web Clipper uses the first template in your list.

Drag Keeper to the top so it becomes your safe default.

INTERMEDIATE

5. Save recipes with "Chef," minus the life story

Recipe sites label their ingredients with schema data, so Web Clipper can grab those directly. For the steps, it's more reliable to ask Interpreter.

6. Log papers with "Reader" before you forget why you saved them

Web Clipper is great for capturing a "reading intent" for a paper, which means you record why you wanted to read it at the moment you found it.

7. Review any tool in one click with "Tester"

This one is for anyone who writes about or buys software. Clip a tool's landing page and you get a comparison card straight away.

The verdict stays manual on purpose, because an AI summary of a landing page only tells you what the company claims.

8. Collect leads with "Scout"

Set the behavior to append to an existing note so every company you clip lands in one running Leads list.

PRO

9. Mine content ideas with "Miner"

Every template has an Interpreter context box, which is the background instruction the AI reads before it answers your questions.

This is where you give it a proper job description.

10. Go private with "Vault," running AI on your own laptop

If you'd rather not send pages to an outside AI company, Interpreter can use local models, which offer greater privacy and offline compatibility.

The setup is to install Ollama (a free app that runs AI models on your own computer), then add it as a provider in Interpreter settings.

Keep the model small. The Obsidian team recommends small models for Web Clipper because they are faster and perform fairly accurately for this task.

The time math

That 1,500 figure is simply 10 clips a day for 6 months, and every one of those notes has already been summarized and filed.

The honest part

Web Clipper is not for everyone right now.

The template settings are fiddly, and the first hour feels like filling out forms about forms. Interpreter needs an API key from an AI provider (a password that lets the extension use a model you pay for), and each clip costs a small amount unless you run a local model.

Small models get things wrong, especially on long or messy pages, so anything with numbers in it should be checked against the original. Pages behind paywalls or heavy scripts sometimes clip badly or come through half empty.

If you don't already use Obsidian, installing a whole note app just for this is a lot to take on.

It's built for people who read a lot online and are tired of losing what they read.

The trade-off is one evening of setup in exchange for hours back every month.
