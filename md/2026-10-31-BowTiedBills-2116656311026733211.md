# 5 Ways To Make More Money As A Local Business...

## Post

A lot of local businesses try to make more money the same way: 
Spend more on ads.
That plan makes Google and Meta more money than it makes the business owner.
Every year the clicks cost more, and the leads stop the day the budget does.
My background is engineering: 
I worked on ranking and search algorithms at a FAANG company before starting the agency.
These days my SEO agency helps rank local businesses, and 50+ of the law firms I've worked with are now #1 on Google.
These are the 5 things I'd do first to make a local business more money, ranked from the one that pays the most to the one that's quickest to fix.
The 5 ways:
Rank in the top 3 on Google Maps and page 1
Get more reviews, every month
Stay open longer
Show up in AI answers
Stop competing on price
 
Rank in the top 3 on Google Maps and page 1:
 
This is the biggest one by far, and it's the reason the other 4 work better.
Where the clicks go.
Search "divorce lawyer near me" and look at the page.
At the top, you'll usually see a couple of ads, then the map pack with 3 businesses, then the normal results.
From what I've seen across my clients:
The ads get about 5 to 10% of the clicks
The 3 businesses in the map pack get about 61%
Everyone else fights for what's left
So the map pack is the most valuable real estate you can have online as a local business.
The jump at the top is huge.
Going from #3 to #2 in the map pack typically doubles your leads.
Going from #2 to #1 doubles them again.
Below the top 3, you get almost nothing.
Why it beats ads.
Ads are renting.
The day you stop paying, the leads stop.
A #1 ranking keeps bringing calls in every month, and those calls close easier, because people who find you at #1 already trust you.
You can also hold more than one spot.
With a strong map listing and a page 1 website, you can take up 2 or 3 positions on the same search, instead of paying for 1 ad.
What it did for one of my clients.
A traffic ticket firm across three California markets was spending $50K a month on ads.
My team got them to #1 in all 3 markets.
Their organic clicks went up 4x, to 4,800 a month from 1,200, and the ad budget came down to $5K a month.
That's $45K a month back in the business.
Your ranking changes depending on where the searcher is standing.
Google gives a different result depending on where the person searching is.
A local rank tracker shows this as a grid, about 100 square miles around your office, with a different position at every point.
This matters because a lot of searches happen away from your office.
Car accidents happen on the highway, divorce searches happen at home, and a DUI can happen anywhere.
A firm that only ranks at its own front door loses to the firm that ranks where people are actually searching.
Where to start.
Set your Google Business Profile's primary category to the service you want most (for a law firm, the practice area, never just "law firm")
Fill out every service you offer, one by one
Get your business name, address and phone number identical on 80 to 100+ directories
Build 1 page on your website for each service, and 1 for each town you serve
Add a post to your profile 2 or 3 times every week, using real photos of the people and the office
Answer the questions in the Q&A section on your profile
If a lot of your market speaks Spanish, set up your profile and website in Spanish too
1 profile can't rank for everything.
If you run 2 very different services out of one office, like criminal and family law, one profile will struggle to rank for both.
Pick the primary category for the one you want more of.
If you have the space, separate units or floors can each get their own legitimate profile.
The tedious part that most agencies skip.
Citations are your business name, address and phone number listed on other websites, like legal directories and local listings.
Google cross checks them, and if they don't match, it trusts your business less.
The most common problem I see: 
The business changed its phone number or added a partner years ago, and half the directories still show the old details.
Fixing 80 to 100 listings is boring, which is exactly why so many businesses plateau at position 7 or 10.
How long it takes.
The first 30 days usually bring an uptick in clicks and calls.
Around month 3, you can be #1 in a 20 to 25 square mile radius around your office.
In very competitive cities, give it closer to 6 months.
 
Get more reviews, every month:
Reviews make you money twice.
They help you rank, and they decide who gets the call once someone sees the map pack.
Velocity beats count.
What Google weighs most is how many reviews came in over the last 90 days, more than your all time total.
I've seen a firm sitting on 356 reviews rank above a competitor with 756.
40 reviews from 5 years ago tells Google nothing about whether you're still good today.
Reviews decide the click.
Once someone sees the map pack, they compare the 3 businesses fast.
The star rating, the number of recent reviews and whether the owner replies all go into that decision.
A business at #2 with fresh 5 star reviews can take the call from a #1 with old ones.
How many you need.
The target is somewhere between 5 and 20 new reviews a month, with more needed in competitive cities.
The system.
Ask at the moment the customer is happiest (end of the job, case closed, after the appointment)
Text or email the actual review link, so it takes them one tap
Follow up 2, 3 or 4 times, because people mean to do it and then forget
Automate the follow-ups, so nobody on your team has to remember
Reply to every review, good or bad, within 24 to 48 hours
Multiple locations.
If you have more than 1 office, send reviews from your strongest location's clients to the newest one.
They're real reviews from real clients, and they help the new office catch up much faster.
 
Stay open longer:
 
This is the easiest money on the list, and almost nobody does it.
Hours are a ranking signal.
Try a search like "personal injury lawyer near me" late in the evening.
Google pushes businesses that are open right now higher in the results.
If you close at 5, you're invisible to everyone searching in the evening, and a lot of people search after work.
The fix.
Someone else can answer the phone for you.
Hire an after hours call service, then change your business hours on Google to match.
Now someone with a problem at 9pm reaches a person, instead of your competitor.
How to set it up.
Pick a call service that can take details and book a callback, or book straight into your calendar
Give them a short script with your services and the questions to ask
Update your hours on your Google Business Profile, your website and every directory, so they all match
Check the call notes every morning and call back the warm ones first
Why it's worth it.
Someone searching at 9pm often has an urgent problem.
The business that picks up first has the best shot at the job, and at night, far fewer businesses are picking up.
What it did for one firm.
A multi-attorney law firm I work with took this tip, among others.
In about 6 weeks, they signed ~20 new clients across 3 attorneys, and started hiring a paralegal to keep up.
They told me the business hours change was one of the things that made the difference.
 
Show up in AI answers:
More of your customers are asking ChatGPT or Google's AI Overviews who to call before they ever scroll through search results.
So a new place to get recommended has opened up.
Where AI gets its answers.
AI tools pull from your website and from the top rated business profiles on Google and Yelp.
So the same things that make you rank on Google also make you show up in AI answers.
Search now has 3 layers.
Organic results, which take the longest to move
The map pack, which moves the fastest
AI answers, like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews
A business that wins the first 2 usually shows up in the 3rd.
How to get recommended.
Get your Google Business Profile ranking first (see #1)
Keep the reviews coming (see #2)
Claim and fill out your Yelp profile, because Yelp matters a lot for ChatGPT
Spell out on your website what you do, for whom, and in which towns
Ask ChatGPT who the best [your service] in [your city] is, and see who it names today
One sign it's working.
That same multi-attorney firm also came up #2 on ChatGPT for their practice area.
Another client of mine was told by a stranger in a different state that they came up top 3 when they asked AI.
All of that came from doing #1 and #2 properly.
 
Stop competing on price:
 
The last one costs nothing, and it changes who you work with.
Low prices bring the WORST customers.
When you compete on price, you attract the customers who complain the most, pay the latest and leave the fastest.
For your own sanity, never compete on price.
What to do instead.
Set a minimum job or case size, and don't go below it
Make it clear that anything outside the original scope costs extra
Put your effort into getting found by the customers who want the best, instead of the cheapest
Your price also tells people who you are.
The cheapest option in town is rarely the one people trust with a divorce, a car accident or their teeth.
A clear minimum, with what's included, tells the right customers you're the serious option.
How this ties back to SEO.
When your leads come from ads, you're competing with every other ad on the page, and price becomes the easy way to win.
When your leads come from a #1 ranking, they already trust you before the first call.
People who trust you argue a lot less about price.
Then close the leads you get.
A big part of this is still you being good at what you do.
My job is to bring the leads, and the business has to close them.
You'd be surprised how many businesses can't close layup deals from people who already found them at #1.
 
The order I'd do them in:
If you only do one, do #1.
Ranking in the top 3 on Maps and page 1 is the thing that makes reviews, longer hours, AI answers and better pricing all pay off more.
If you want quick wins this week, do #3 and #5, because they take a day to set up.
Then build #2 into how your business runs, because reviews compound every month you keep them coming.
Inbound from search is the cheapest customer you'll ever get, and it keeps coming long after the work is done.
